# SSoT: Phases of Task Execution

## Core Concept

Task execution is not a singular event but a complex cognitive process comprised of five distinct phases: Initiation, Encoding, Execution, Performance Monitoring, and Completion. This "universal cognitive model" is supported by research in neuroscience and clinical psychology (including the ACT-R model), which identifies specific brain networks and cognitive resources required for each stage. Understanding this granularity is essential for diagnosing and managing executive function disorders like ADHD, which disrupt the entire chain rather than just the starting point.

## Details

### 1. Initiation (Activation)

- Definition: The phase of "getting started." It involves organizing materials, estimating time requirements, prioritizing, and generating sufficient motivation to overcome inertia.
- Neuroscience: Relies on superior medial prefrontal regions to provide the necessary "energization" to switch from a resting state to an active state.
- Timing: According to the ACT-R model, the perception-cognition-action cycle for simple tasks takes approximately 260–390ms, though complex initiation involving memory retrieval takes substantially longer.

### 2. Encoding (Planning)

- Definition: Occurs immediately following initiation. The brain performs a perceptual encoding of the task's specific demands to formulate a plan of approach.
- Function: Processes _what_ needs to be done and maps it to a sequence of actions.

### 3. Execution (Sustained Attention)

- Definition: The active performance of the work. It requires maintaining focus over time (sustained attention) and utilizing working memory to hold task-relevant information online while shifting focus as necessary.
- Neuroscience: Engages a right-lateralized network involving the prefrontal cortex and parietal areas.

### 4. Performance Monitoring

- Definition: A continuous, dynamic process of tracking progress and detecting errors. It involves adjusting attention or behavior in real-time to meet situational demands.
- Neuroscience: Generated primarily in the medial frontal cortex, specifically the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC), which is responsible for conflict monitoring and error detection.

### 5. Completion

- Definition: The final stage involving finishing the work, verifying accuracy and completeness (checking), and formally transitioning away from the activity.

## Conflicts/Nuances

### The Impact of ADHD

ADHD is often misunderstood as solely an initiation problem ("procrastination"), but it disrupts all five phases due to a theorized Neuroenergetic Deficit (chronic neural energy insufficiency) and dopamine hyposensitivity.

- Initiation: Dopamine deficits lead to "ADHD Paralysis," where action is impossible until an emergency overrides the deficit.
- Sustained Attention: Requires significantly higher cortical activation to compensate for baseline deficits.
- Monitoring: Research shows reduced Error-Related Negativity (ERN) and Error Positivity (Pe) signals, indicating a breakdown in both automatic and conscious error detection.
- Completion: Long-term projects often fail due to the inability to maintain planning and motivation over time.

### Subjective Experience vs. Clinical Model

- The "Surface Tension": Subjectively, initiation is often felt not as a planning step but as a physical barrier or heavy weight.
- Friction of Perfection: Detailed phases like "Encoding" can become traps; the perceived effort of doing a task "properly" (perfect planning) increases friction, leading to paralysis.
- Strategic Focus: While all phases are real, focusing intervention solely on Initiation is often the highest-leverage strategy. Establishing a "starting habit" can sometimes bypass the need for complex encoding in the moment.
