# SSoT: The Honeyman Method (Adaptive Routines)

## Core Concept

The Honeyman Method is a flexible routine framework designed to accommodate the fluctuating energy levels and executive function capacity common in neurodivergent individuals (ADHD, Autism) and those with chronic illness. It replaces the "all-or-nothing" perfectionism trap with a three-tiered system (Ideal, Most Likely, and Minimum). By pre-planning different versions of the same routine, the method ensures that habit consistency is maintained even on low-capacity days, transforming routine adherence from a binary pass/fail state into a spectrum of valid success options.

## Details

### The Three Tiers of Routine

The framework scales intensity while maintaining the core structure of the habit:

- Ideal Routine (Maximum Energy/Motivation):
    - Context: For "best-case scenario" days where motivation and energy are at their peak.
    - Content: The comprehensive, aspirational version including all desired activities (e.g., 1-hour walk, cooked breakfast, journaling, full grooming).
    - Purpose: Clarifies values and demonstrates what is possible under optimal conditions. It is a template, not a source of guilt.
- Most Likely Routine (Average Energy/Baseline):
    - Context: For typical, unremarkable days.
    - Content: A realistic middle ground where tasks are shortened or simplified (e.g., 20-minute walk, toast/cereal, basic essentials).
    - Purpose: Acts as the sustainable foundation used most frequently to build long-term habit momentum.
- Minimum Routine (Low Energy/Executive Dysfunction):
    - Context: For days of severe depletion, sensory overload, or "survival mode."
    - Content: Bare-bones essentials stripped of all non-necessities (e.g., letting the dog out instead of walking, quick hygiene, staying in pajamas).
    - Purpose: Preserves the neural pathway of the routine and prevents complete habit collapse. Completing this tier counts as a "win" for the day.

### Implementation and Selection

1. Pre-Planning: All tiers must be written down in advance. This eliminates the "cognitive load" and decision fatigue of trying to improvise a plan when energy is already low.
2. Daily Tier Selection: A brief morning assessment is used to ask, "What is my energy level today?" Selecting a tier immediately clears the path for execution and removes the need for internal negotiation throughout the day.
3. Adaptive Scaling: The same model is applied to both morning and evening routines to provide predictable "anchors" for the day.

### Strategic Benefits

- Consistency through Adaptation: Because the routine never "breaks" (it just scales down), individuals avoid the cycle of "starting from scratch" after a difficult period.
- Self-Compassion: The method reframes the Minimum tier as intelligent resource allocation rather than failure. It validates variable capacity as a reality to be accommodated rather than a character flaw to be overcome.
- Structure + Flexibility: It satisfies the dual neurodivergent need for predictable structure (Autistic need) and the freedom to adapt to internal states (ADHD need).

## Conflicts/Nuances

- Interoception Challenges: Success depends on the ability to accurately assess one's internal energy state, which can be difficult for individuals with poor interoceptive awareness.
- Definition of "Minimum": The tiers are highly subjective. A "Minimum" for one person (e.g., making the bed) might be an "Ideal" for someone else depending on their specific disability or current circumstances.
- The "Productivity" Trap: There is a risk of users feeling pressure to always reach for the "Ideal" tier. The framework emphasizes that the "Most Likely" tier is the actual baseline, and choosing "Minimum" is an active, successful choice.
