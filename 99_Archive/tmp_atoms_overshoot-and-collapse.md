## Atoms

### Atom 001: Overshoot and Collapse: Delayed Negative Feedback Causes a System to Exceed Then Crash Below Its Carrying Capacity
- Kind: mechanism
- Statement: A system undergoing exponential growth within a finite environment, where the negative feedback signalling resource scarcity is delayed, tends to overshoot its carrying capacity; the overshoot degrades the underlying resource base itself, so the system does not stabilise gently but collapses to an equilibrium permanently lower than the original carrying capacity.
- Scope & Conditions: Distinct from ordinary balancing-loop correction (SoT - Systems Thinking) specifically because the delay allows the resource base itself to be damaged before correction arrives — the target the system is trying to stabilise around has moved.
- Evidence: "Systems that undergo unchecked exponential growth within finite environments, coupled with delayed feedback mechanisms, tend to overshoot their carrying capacity. This overshoot severely degrades the underlying resource base, leading not to a gentle stabilisation, but to a rapid systemic collapse towards a permanently diminished equilibrium."
- Implications:
    - A system that looks fine right up until collapse is not evidence against this mechanism — overshoot is structurally invisible until the resource base is already damaged.

### Atom 002: Removing a Negative Feedback Loop Can Cause Ecological Overshoot (the Kaibab Deer Case)
- Kind: definition
- Statement: When natural predators were removed from the Kaibab Plateau, a critical negative feedback loop on the deer population disappeared, allowing exponential population growth that overshot the habitat's carrying capacity, consumed vegetation down to the roots, and caused a population crash to a level far below the original carrying capacity.
- Scope & Conditions: A concrete instance of Atom 001's general mechanism; real ecosystems usually have multiple overlapping feedback loops, so removing one does not guarantee total collapse.
- Evidence: "Removing natural predators removes a critical negative feedback loop, allowing a deer population to grow exponentially. The population overshoots the habitat's carrying capacity, consuming vegetation down to the roots. The resulting permanent degradation of the food supply causes a massive population crash to a level far lower than the original carrying capacity."
- Implications:
    - Removing a regulating constraint (a predator, a review step, a rate limit) doesn't just remove friction — it can remove the very signal that would have prevented overshoot.

### Atom 003: A Cultural Ritual Can Function as a Negative Feedback Loop Preserving Ecological Equilibrium
- Kind: definition
- Statement: The Tsembaga people of New Guinea historically regulated pig populations through periodic ritual pig feasts, an embedded cultural practice that functioned as a negative feedback loop culling the population before it stripped the rainforest of resources — disrupting the ritual removes the feedback loop and exposes the ecosystem to overshoot.
- Scope & Conditions: Illustrates that negative feedback loops preserving a system's carrying capacity need not be biological or mechanical — cultural practices can occupy the same functional role.
- Evidence: "The Tsembaga people of New Guinea historically maintained ecological equilibrium through ritualistic pig feasts. This cultural practice acts as an embedded negative feedback loop, periodically culling the pig population before it can strip the rainforest of its resources."
- Implications:
    - When assessing why a system stays stable, check for non-obvious regulating mechanisms (rituals, norms, informal practices) before assuming stability is automatic or requires no maintenance.

### Atom 004: The Purpose of a System Dynamics Model Is to Make Assumptions Explicit, Not to Predict Outcomes
- Kind: claim
- Statement: Computer models of complex systems are not predictive black boxes meant to output future dates or numbers; their real purpose is to force the modeller to make assumptions about causal relationships and feedback loops explicit, which improves the mental models humans use to understand the system.
- Scope & Conditions: Applies to system dynamics models specifically (e.g. World3 / Limits to Growth); doesn't claim all quantitative models are non-predictive, only that treating prediction as the point misreads their main value.
- Evidence: "Computer models of systems are not predictive 'black boxes' meant merely to output future dates or numbers. Rather, their purpose is to force researchers to make their assumptions about causal relationships and feedback loops explicit, thereby improving the internal 'mental models' humans use to understand complex systems."
- Implications:
    - Criticising a system dynamics model for a wrong date or number misses the point; the right critique targets whether its causal assumptions were made explicit and are defensible.
