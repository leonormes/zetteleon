my wife still thinks about the accounts the money is in. We have a joint account and a personal account each. When she got paid into her personal account it shows up in ynab as ready to assign. She moved it to the shared account. but it doesn't matter where the money is. What matters is the plan. How can I explain this to her? Or get her to adapt her mental model of this budgetting?

To help your wife adapt her mental model to the YNAB methodology, you can reframe the relationship between bank accounts and the budget using these structural frameworks.

## 1\. The Envelope Metaphor (The "Where" vs. The "What")

The most effective mental model for YNAB is the **envelope system**.

- **The Budget (Envelopes):** These represent the **purpose** of the money (e.g., Rent, Groceries, Personal Spending).
- **The Accounts (The Safe):** Your bank accounts are simply the "safes" where the cash is stored.
- **The Logic:** Moving money from a personal account to a joint account is like taking a stack of cash out of a small safe and putting it into a larger safe. It doesn't change what is written on the envelopes. YNAB doesn't care which "safe" a dollar is in; it only cares which "envelope" it belongs to.

## 2\. The Total Pile Concept

YNAB operates on the principle of **Zero-Based Budgeting**, which treats all available funds as one unified "pile of cash".

- **Ready to Assign (RTA):** When she receives her pay, YNAB sees those new dollars and adds them to the "Ready to Assign" pile immediately.
- **The Intent:** Moving the money to the shared account is a redundant administrative step for the budget. The moment the money hits *any* linked account, it is already "ready" to be given a job.

## 3\. Reframing Account Boundaries

If she values the "personal" aspect of her account, suggest shifting that boundary from the **account level** to the **category level**:

- **Yours, Mine, and Ours:** Instead of using separate accounts to define personal money, create a "Personal Spending" category group.
- **The Benefit:** This allows you to keep all money in a single joint "pile" (which simplifies management and reduces decision fatigue) while still protecting her autonomy through a dedicated budget category.

## 4\. The "Neutral Third Party" Strategy

Explain that the budget is a **neutral third party** that keeps the conversation grounded in the plan rather than the mechanics of where money came from.

- **The Shift:** Encourage her to look at **category balances** instead of **account balances** when deciding if she can afford something.
- **The Result:** This replaces the "Can I afford this?" question (based on account balance) with "Does this move us closer to our goal?" (based on the category plan) .

By focusing on the **categories** as the source of truth, you move away from the "timing-bills-to-paychecks" dance and toward a state where the plan—not the account location—dictates your financial reality.

how can we make it even more simple and concrete? My wife doesn't think in abstractions or metaphors

To make this concrete and eliminate abstraction, shift the focus from "where the money is" to the **functional rules** of the system.

Stop using metaphors like envelopes or safes. Instead, use these literal, logic-based operational rules:

## 1\. The "Only One Number" Rule

Teach her to ignore the bank balance entirely. In this system, the bank balance is a "ghost" number.

- **The Rule:** Before buying anything, check the **Category Balance** in YNAB.
- **The Logic:** If the "Groceries" category says £50, she has £50 to spend on food. It does not matter if she uses the personal card or the joint card to pay for it. The transaction will reduce that £50 balance regardless of which account it came from.

## 2\. The "Immediate Job" Rule

The friction occurs because she sees money in "Ready to Assign" and feels it isn't "home" yet.

- **The Action:** The moment her pay appears in YNAB (even in her personal account), she must **assign it to a category immediately.**
- **The Logic:** Once that money is assigned to "Mortgage" or "Utilities," it is "gone" from her personal spending power. It has a job. Moving it to the joint account becomes a redundant administrative task that changes nothing in the software.

## 3\. The "Tap and Map" Method

If she is worried about which account pays for what, use this literal mapping:

- **Personal Account:** Use for personal categories (e.g., "Her Clothes," "Lunch with Friends").
- **Joint Account:** Use for shared categories (e.g., "Rent," "Council Tax").
- **The Concrete Reality:** YNAB merges these into one list. If she spends £10 from her personal account on "Groceries" (a joint category), the "Groceries" balance drops by £10. The system self-corrects. You do not need to move money to "match" the spending.

## 4\. Remove the Friction (The Technical Fix)

If the manual movement of money is causing the mental load, simplify the plumbing:

- **Stop the Transfer:** Ask her to stop moving the money to the shared account for one month as a "system test."
- **Observe the Data:** Show her that at the end of the month, the "Total Available" in your categories exactly matches the "Total Cash" across all three accounts combined.
- **The Result:** The math is always true, regardless of the physical location of the pounds.

## Summary for Her

> "The bank account is just where the money **sits**. The YNAB category is where the money **works**. If you look at the bank app to see what we can afford, you are looking at the wrong data source."
