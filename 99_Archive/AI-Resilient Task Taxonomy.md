Based on the provided sources, the **AI-Resilient Task Taxonomy** is a classification of human roles that will survive and thrive by shifting from the "Capital Economy" (CE) logic of efficiency to the "Inspiration Economy" (IE) logic of capacity and human essence.

This taxonomy argues that as AI automates execution, human value migrates to **defining problems, catalyzing empathy, and orchestrating complex narratives**—a transition closely aligned with the "Human 3.0" concept of moving from "task-execution" to "idea-origination".

## The Economic Context: Capital vs. Inspiration

To understand the taxonomy, one must understand the economic shift described in the sources:

- **The Capital Economy (CE):** Operates on **"Supply vs. Demand."** In this model, human labor is valued for efficiency and replaceability. AI is the ultimate optimizer of this equation, successfully automating execution, routine analysis, and mass production.
- **The Inspiration Economy (IE):** Operates on **"Capacity vs. Demand."** Here, career security comes from expanding the capacity to inspire, navigate ambiguity, and create emotional resonance. In this model, AI is not a replacement but a partner that handles computational heavy lifting, while humans define the "terrain of the game".

## The AI-Resilient Task Taxonomy

The sources identify specific categories of tasks that are resilient to automation because they leverage intrinsic human capacities that AI cannot easily replicate:

### 1. Problem Definers (vs. Problem Solvers)

Because AI excels at solving well-defined problems, value shifts to those who can identify and frame the *right* problems.

- **Roles:** "Chief Question Officer," "Problem Framing Strategist," and "Ethical Systems Designer".
- **Function:** Instead of providing answers, these individuals design the questions an organization explores. They leverage AI to generate options, but the human provides the intent and ethical judgment.
- **Connection to Telos:** This aligns with The Telos Method's foundational layer of identifying a **"Problem"** before a **"Mission."** The human defines the "Problem" (e.g., lack of secure software), and the AI assists in the execution strategies to solve it.

### 2. Empathy and Connection Catalysts

While AI can analyze sentiment, it cannot build genuine trust or "feel".

- **Roles:** "AI-Augmented Therapist," "Empathy-Driven Experience Curator," and "Community Weaver".
- **Function:** These roles navigate complex social dynamics and foster culture. An AI might suggest therapeutic techniques based on data patterns, but the healing relationship relies on human shared experience.

### 3. Strategic Narrative Designers & Visionaries

AI provides probabilistic forecasts, but human intuition is required for "leaps of logic" and strategy in the face of incomplete data.

- **Roles:** "Fusion Strategist," "Creative Visionary," and "Negotiator".
- **Function:** These workers craft the overarching "why" for a brand or movement. They interpret AI outputs and blend them with geopolitical awareness or market intuition to make bold bets. This mirrors the "Human 3.0" concept where humans must become "originators of ideas" rather than just executors.

### 4. Physical and Craft Masters

In a digital world, the demand for unique, high-touch physical craftsmanship increases as it represents irreplaceable human capacity.

- **Roles:** Artisans, live performers, and personal trainers who provide real-time, adaptive feedback.
- **Function:** The value lies in the "imperfection," story, and human touch, which distinguishes the output from mass-produced AI content.

## Implementation: The Augmentation Stack

The sources suggest that thriving in this taxonomy requires building a **Personal AI Infrastructure (PAI)** or "augmentation stack" to handle the non-resilient tasks.

- **Offloading Execution:** By using frameworks like **LangGraph**, **n8n**, or **CrewAI**, humans can offload the "CE" tasks (routine workflows, data scraping, email sorting) to autonomous agents. For example, a "Researcher" agent can perform multi-source investigations, while the human acts as the "Architect" (Strategic Narrative Designer) who decides *what* to research.
- **Context Engineering:** To be an effective "Problem Definer," humans must practice "Context Engineering"—curating the information (prompts, tools, history) given to the AI to ensure it stays aligned with the user's mission. The human provides the "Strategic Guardrails" (Policy), while the AI executes the action.

## Summary of the Shift

|Economy|Capital Economy (Legacy)|Inspiration Economy (Resilient)|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Logic**|Supply vs. Demand|Capacity vs. Demand|
|**Human Role**|Replaceable Executor|Irreplaceable Creator/Strategist|
|**Primary Value**|Efficiency & Speed|Empathy, Framing, & Ambiguity Nav.|
|**AI Relationship**|Competitor (Replacement)|Partner (Amplifier)|
|**Example Role**|Data Entry / Routine Analyst|Chief Question Officer / Fusion Strategist|

In this new context, a "job" is no longer a set of predefined tasks provided by an employer, but a "Telos"—a personal mission defined by the individual, executed by a swarm of AI agents, and guided by human empathy and strategic vision.
