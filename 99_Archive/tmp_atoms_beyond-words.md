### Atom 002: The Symbol Grounding Problem

- Kind: definition
- Statement: The symbol grounding problem is the difficulty that words are arbitrary symbols whose meaning depends on a relationship to experience.
- Scope & Conditions: The source illustrates it with the word "red", which means nothing without the experience of seeing red. It is presented as a challenge for purely language-based understanding.
- Evidence: "the symbol-grounding problem highlights how words derive meaning from their relationship to experience. The word "red" means nothing without the experience of seeing red, creating a circular challenge for pure language-based understanding." (L300)
- Implications:
    - Understanding built only from language faces a circularity.
    - The source applies this to language models, which lack the embodied experience that anchors human concepts (L354).

### Atom 003: Language Discretises Continuous Experience

- Statement: Language maps a continuous range of experience onto a finite set of words, so nuance is lost in the mapping.

- Implications:
