### Atom 001: Classical Rationalism Grounds Knowledge in Reason, Not the Senses
- Kind: claim
- Statement: Rationalists such as Descartes and Plato hold that the senses are deceptive and that true knowledge comes from pure reasoning, logic, and innate ideas rather than observation.

- Implications:

### Atom 004: Hume's Inductive Skepticism Denies a Rational Guarantee for Extrapolated Patterns
- Kind: claim
- Statement: Hume pointed out that observing a pattern in the past (such as the sun rising) gives no rational, logical guarantee that the pattern will continue in the future.
- Scope & Conditions: A skeptical consequence internal to classical empiricism (Atom 003); the classic statement of the problem of induction.
- Evidence: "Hume... pointed out that just because we have observed a pattern in the past (like the sun rising), we have no rational, logical guarantee that it will continue in the future."
- Implications:
    - Motivates every later attempt (falsificationism, reliabilism, tracking theory) to ground scientific confidence in something other than naive induction.

### Atom 007: Constructive Empiricism Targets Empirical Adequacy, Not Truth About Unobservables
- Kind: definition
- Statement: Van Fraassen's constructive empiricism argues that science does not need to describe the hidden, unobservable structures of the world; instead, the goal of science is merely to be "empirically adequate," meaning its theories accurately predict observable phenomena.
- Scope & Conditions: A modern alternative to scientific realism; softer than logical positivism (Atom 005–006) in that it doesn't deny unobservables meaning, only remains agnostic about their existence.
- Evidence: "constructive empiricism argues that science does not need to describe the hidden, unobservable structures of the world. Instead, the goal of science is merely to be 'empirically adequate.'"
- Implications:
    - Gives instrumentalism a precise technical target ("empirical adequacy") rather than a vague slogan.
