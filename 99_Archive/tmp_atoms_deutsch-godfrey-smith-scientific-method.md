### Atom 001: Deutsch Rejects Empiricism for Conjecture-Led Theory Formation
- Kind: claim
- Statement: Deutsch holds that scientific theories originate as bold conjectures from human imagination, not as generalisations derived from sensory experience, so experience is used to choose between pre-existing guesses rather than to read theories off nature.
- Scope & Conditions: Deutsch's Popperian account of theory-genesis specifically; contrasts with Godfrey-Smith's modernised empiricism, which treats observation as a source of knowledge.
- Evidence: "theories are not 'derived' from nature, nor are they extrapolations of repeated observations... they are bold conjectures created by the human imagination... we invent theories to solve problems and then use experience merely to choose between these pre-existing guesses"
- Implications:
    - Reframes observation's role from "source of theory" to "arbiter between theories."
    - Sharpens the existing account of induction's limits by naming the alternative, conjecture-first account of where theories actually come from.

### Atom 002: Godfrey-Smith's Confirmation Objection to Strict Falsificationism
- Kind: claim
- Statement: Godfrey-Smith argues that if theories can never be confirmed by evidence, there is no rational basis for relying on well-tested theories over untested ones, illustrated by a bridge-design example.
- Scope & Conditions: A direct critique of Popper/strict falsificationism's refusal to give confirmation any epistemic role.
- Evidence: "if we cannot increase our confidence in a theory through testing, then it would be no more rational to build a bridge using a well-tested design than it would be to use a brand-new, completely untested design"
- Implications:
    - Motivates naturalistic empiricism as a repair to falsificationism, not a wholesale rejection of it.
    - Sets up a direct tension with Deutsch's non-confirmation justification for the same kind of reliance (see Atom 009).

### Atom 003: Duhem-Quine Holism Complicates Simple Falsification
- Kind: mechanism
- Statement: Because a prediction follows from a theory only in conjunction with background assumptions and instruments, an unexpected observation can indict a background assumption or measuring device instead of the theory under test, so falsification is rarely a clean single-theory verdict.
- Scope & Conditions: Applies whenever a test result is used to falsify a theory; known as "holism about testing" or the Duhem-Quine thesis.
- Evidence: "simple falsification rarely works in practice because of 'holism about testing'... an unexpected observation might mean the main theory is wrong, or it might just mean a background assumption or measuring instrument is faulty"
- Implications:
    - Names a specific mechanism by which theory-choice becomes underdetermined at the moment of testing, not only at the moment of comparing finished theories.

### Atom 004: A Good Explanation Is Hard to Vary
- Kind: definition
- Statement: Deutsch defines a good explanation as an assertion about reality whose details play a specific, functional role, such that they cannot be easily swapped out without destroying the explanation's force.
- Scope & Conditions: Deutsch's core criterion for scientific quality; intended to replace predictive success or bare falsifiability as the primary target of a good theory.
- Evidence: "A 'good explanation' is an assertion about reality that is hard to vary—meaning its details play a highly specific, functional role in the explanation and cannot be easily swapped out"
- Implications:
    - Gives a concrete test for genuinely explanatory (realist) depth, beyond simply wanting to understand mechanics.
    - Distinguishes a theory that explains from one that merely fits the data, relevant wherever multiple theories fit the same evidence.

### Atom 006: Theory Support Comes From the Reliability of the Testing Procedure
- Kind: claim
- Statement: Godfrey-Smith holds that an observation supports a theory because of the reliability of the procedure—such as randomised sampling or experimental intervention—in which the observation was embedded, not from the observation in isolation.

### Atom 007: Science's Division of Labour Lets Individual Scientists Be Dogmatic
- Kind: claim
- Statement: Godfrey-Smith argues the scientific method works not because every scientist is personally open-minded and self-critical, but because the community divides the labour: individual scientists can be quite dogmatic about their own theories while rival scientists eagerly do the critical testing for them.
- Scope & Conditions: Godfrey-Smith's naturalistic, sociological account of science, contra Popper's demand that individuals themselves be constantly self-critical.
- Evidence: "individual scientists can actually be quite dogmatic about their own theories; the scientific method works because the community distributes the labor, and rival scientists will eagerly do the critical testing for them"
- Implications:
    - Relocates the open-mindedness requirement from the individual scientist to the community structure.
    - Supports treating consensus survival as evidence of adversarial testing having occurred, even when individual researchers are personally biased.

### Atom 009: Deutsch and Godfrey-Smith Converge on Rational Reliance From Different Grounds
- Kind: claim
- Statement: Facing the same "why is it rational to rely on a well-tested theory" dilemma that motivates Godfrey-Smith's confirmation objection, Deutsch argues we are justified in relying on a well-tested theory not because past observations "confirmed" it, but because it is the only rationally tenable explanation left once bad or unexplained alternatives are ruled out.
- Scope & Conditions: Directly ties Deutsch's tower-jumping example to Godfrey-Smith's bridge-building example as two answers to the same underlying question.
- Evidence: "Deutsch argues that we are justified in relying on the prevailing theory of gravity not because past observations 'confirmed' it, but because it is the only rationally tenable theory remaining that provides a good explanation, whereas alternative theories provide bad or unexplained assertions"
- Implications:
    - The two thinkers reach practically compatible conclusions (rational reliance on tested theories) via incompatible justifications (best-remaining-explanation vs procedural confirmation).
    - This is a genuine, unadjudicated tension, not a contradiction: both survive once you drop the shared assumption that rational reliance requires positive confirmation.
