## Atoms

### Atom 001: Reassurance-Seeking Relieves Anxiety Without Testing Whether the Feared Outcome Was Real
- Kind: mechanism
- Statement: Checking or information-seeking used as a safety behaviour reduces distress immediately, but because relief arrives before the person can observe whether the feared outcome would have happened anyway, the nervous system credits the safety behaviour with preventing danger — which lowers tolerance for acting under ordinary uncertainty next time and makes the behaviour self-reinforcing.
- Scope & Conditions: Applies to reassurance-seeking and safety-behaviour patterns generally (established in anxiety-disorder research); illustrated in the source with technical over-preparation (researching a system before making a change).
- Evidence: "relief arrives immediately after seeking it, so the nervous system may credit the search with preventing an outcome that was never actually likely... checking or searching can reduce distress rapidly, but because the person does not get to discover that the feared outcome might not occur without the safety behaviour, the behaviour becomes self-reinforcing."
- Implications:
    - The fix is not "stop seeking information" but building in a corrective experience: acting under bounded uncertainty and observing that the feared outcome didn't occur.
    - Explains why over-preparation can coexist with genuine expertise — the loop is about relief timing, not about whether the person's knowledge is objectively good.

### Atom 002: Intolerance of Uncertainty Is Finding Missing Information Intolerable, Not Just Undesirable
- Kind: definition
- Statement: Intolerance of uncertainty (IU) is a tendency to find uncertainty itself aversive or dangerous enough that it must be resolved, rather than merely a preference for clarity; a prospective form of IU — anxious anticipation of future uncertainty — is distinguished in the research from other forms involving paralysis or inhibition in the face of uncertainty.
- Scope & Conditions: A named research construct with sub-forms; high IU can lead people to seek information even where that information has little practical value.
- Evidence: "A useful construct here is intolerance of uncertainty: a tendency to find uncertainty itself aversive, threatening, or difficult to endure. It is not merely preferring clarity... Research distinguishes a prospective form—anxious anticipation of future uncertainty, often associated with worry—from other forms involving paralysis or inhibition."
- Implications:
    - "I need the full picture before I can act" can be IU manifesting as excess information-seeking rather than sound risk assessment.

### Atom 003: Information-Seeking Is Adaptive Only While It Remains Instrumental to an Actual Decision
- Kind: distinction
- Statement: Information-seeking is adaptive when it changes a real decision, prediction, or response capacity (instrumental, contingency, or recovery information); it becomes a safety behaviour rather than sound judgement when its actual function is reassurance or identity-protection — proving certainty is impossible, or proving one isn't inadequate — rather than improving the decision at hand.
- Scope & Conditions: Distinguishes five information types by the question each answers: instrumental ("will this change my next decision?"), contingency ("how will I know if my model is wrong?"), recovery ("what can I do if this fails?"), reassurance ("can I be certain nothing bad will happen?"), and identity reassurance ("can I prove I'm not inadequate?"). Research on anxiety and obsessive-compulsive traits finds a greater willingness to seek even non-instrumental information — information that cannot change the outcome — despite a real cost.
- Evidence: table mapping information type → question → adaptiveness; "the moment information no longer changes the action, its primary function may be emotional relief."
- Implications:
    - A practical test before continuing research: does this next piece of information change what I would do, or only how I feel about what I've already decided?

### Atom 004: Perfectionism's Harm Comes From Contingent Self-Worth and Fear of Failure, Not High Standards Alone
- Kind: distinction
- Statement: Research distinguishes adaptive personal standards from more harmful forms of perfectionism in which standards are socially prescribed, self-worth is made contingent on performance, or errors are treated as evidence of personal deficiency — with fear of failure, rather than high standards per se, being the significant driver of the distressing pattern.
- Scope & Conditions: Socially prescribed and self-oriented perfectionism specifically have been associated with fear of failure and test anxiety in the cited research.
- Evidence: "Perfectionism is not simply high standards. Research distinguishes adaptive personal standards from more harmful forms in which standards are socially prescribed, self-worth is contingent on performance, or errors are treated as evidence of personal deficiency. Socially prescribed and self-oriented perfectionism have both been associated with fear of failure and test anxiety; fear of failure, rather than high standards alone, is an important part of the distressing pattern."
- Implications:
    - Treating "I have high standards" as the target for intervention misses the actual mechanism; the useful lever is the fear-of-failure/contingent-self-worth layer underneath.

### Atom 005: How to Test Whether Information-Seeking Has Become a Safety Behaviour
- Kind: procedure
- Statement: Before continuing to seek information on a topic, ask in sequence: (1) what concrete decision could this specific information change; (2) what new action, rollback, monitoring signal, or escalation path would it enable; (3) if it changes nothing, am I seeking information or seeking relief; (4) can I tolerate the uncertainty for a bounded period (e.g. 20 minutes) while taking a safe, reversible next step instead; (5) is the remaining uncertainty an actual external risk, an identity threat, or both.
- Scope & Conditions: Trigger — about to open another source, re-read documentation, or extend research beyond what a decision plausibly requires. Verification — the test succeeds if it produces either a concrete decision-relevant reason to continue, or a decision to stop and act within a defined boundary instead.
- Evidence: "Before opening another documentation page, ask: 1. What concrete decision could this information change? 2. What new action, rollback, monitoring signal, or escalation would it enable? 3. If it changes nothing, am I seeking information or seeking relief? 4. Can I tolerate the uncertainty for 20 minutes while taking a safe, reversible next step? 5. Is the remaining uncertainty an engineering risk, an identity threat, or both?"
- Implications:
    - Pairs naturally with pre-committing a stopping rule before research begins, so the boundary isn't being negotiated in the moment by whichever mode (curiosity or anxiety) is currently active.
