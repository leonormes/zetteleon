## Directionality of Links

One-Way vs. Two-Way Links: Some links might be one-way (e.g., Note A informs Note B) while others are reciprocal (Notes A and B both enhance each other). Two-way links could have higher magnitude, indicating a more integrated relationship.

Asymmetry: Consider the direction in which the information flows. For example, foundational knowledge may heavily influence other ideas, but the reverse may not be true. A higher asymmetry could mean a stronger dependency.

In your Zettelkasten, magnitude could represent the strength of a connection between two notes.

You could quantify magnitude based on factors like:

Relevance: How directly does one note support, expand, or refute the other?

Frequency of Reference: If a particular link between ideas is referenced frequently, it may have a higher magnitude.

Depth of Dependency: If a concept is foundational to understanding another note, the magnitude could be stronger.
