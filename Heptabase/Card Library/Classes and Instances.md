## Classes and Instances

   Ontologies typically define classes (general concepts or categories) and instances (specific examples or occurrences of those classes). You can apply this to your PKM by distinguishing between general ideas (theory, principles) and specific examples (case studies, examples, applications) within your notes.

   How to Apply It:

- Define classes of notes that represent overarching principles or ideas, and link them to instances that represent specific examples or applications of those ideas.

- For instance, you might have a note on "Graphs in Mathematics" as a general class, and several other notes that are instances of this class, such as "Social Network Graphs," "Biological Networks," etc.

    Example:

   ```markdown
   - [[Graphs in Mathematics]] <!--class--> is the general concept.
   - [[Social Network Graphs]] <!--instance--> is an instance of [[Graphs in Mathematics]].
   - [[Biological Networks]] <!--instance--> is another instance of [[Graphs in Mathematics]].
   ```

    This helps you organize your notes into a structured hierarchy where general principles are connected to their specific applications, ensuring that your knowledge graph captures both abstract concepts and concrete details.
