## Solving Problem 4: "What Does Done Look Like?"

This is the most important question in your brain dump, and it has a specific answer.

### The Definition of Done for a Claim Card

A claim card is **done** (for now) when it has:

1. A sharp one-sentence thesis.

2. A named falsifier.

3. A dated current position.

4. At least one link (to a concept hub, another claim, a source, or an open thread).

5. No more branches your current interest wants to pursue.

That is it. Five boxes. When all five are ticked, the card is *done for this session*. It is not done forever — future-you will return, add evidence, update the dated position, refine the thesis. But *for now*, it is done.

### The Larger "Done"

At a higher level, your PKM as a whole is never "done." It is a living system. The right framing:

> **You are not trying to reach a finished state. You are trying to reach a state where each specific thought you want to pursue has been pursued to the limit of your current interest.**

The goal is not a complete map. The goal is that **when you revisit TDD in six months, you find your previous thinking captured, sharp, and available to be built on** — not a pile of fragments requiring re-derivation.

Done = **recoverable**. Not complete. Recoverable.

### Why This Is Liberating

Because it kills the perfectionism trap. You do not need to fully clarify TDD. You need to capture *today's best thinking* about TDD in a form that *tomorrow-you* can pick up. That's a vastly smaller task.

The scientific method framing from earlier helps here: a scientist doesn't "finish" a research programme. They publish a paper when the current conjecture is stable enough to be useful, then move on. Your claim card is your paper. It is done when it is useful, not when it is perfect.
