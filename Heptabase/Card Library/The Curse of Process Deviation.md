## The Curse of Process Deviation

When people don't apply processes properly, resulting in inefficiency, it is often referred to as "process deviation" or "process non-compliance."

### Process Non-conformance

A process non-conformance is when a process or activity doesn't meet its intended purpose. It can be caused by a number of things, including:

- Not following the procedures outlined in a management system  
- Documented procedures that are not fit for purpose  
- Failure to meet regulatory requirements  

Here are some steps in the non-conformance process:

- Identify the root cause: Determine the source of the failure  
- Document the non-conformance: Record the details and severity of the non-conformance, including the material, problem, disposition, and signature of the person responsible  
- Evaluate the non-conformance: Decide if an investigation is required  
- Take corrective action: Use inspection, testing, rework, repair, or replacement to fix or mitigate the non-conformance  
- Take preventive action: Take action to eliminate the cause of a potential non-conformance before an actual problem exists  
- Verify results: Use analytics to monitor the time taken to respond, the effectiveness of the solution, and the improvements made

#### Implications of Process Nonconformity

1. Impact on Team Morale and Dynamics:

   - Disruption: Introducing work outside the planned Sprint Backlog (Set (D)) can disrupt the team's workflow, leading to frustration and decreased morale.
   - Trust: Frequent nonconformity might erode trust in the prioritization process and the team's ability to plan effectively.

2. Effect on Sprint Goals:

   - Goal Achievement: Adding items from (D) can jeopardize the achievement of Sprint Goals, as the team may need to divert focus from planned tasks.
   - Predictability: It reduces the predictability of sprint outcomes, making it harder to measure progress and success.

3. Capacity Management:

   - Overload: Nonconformity can lead to overloading the team, exceeding the capacity constraint (\\kappa), and causing burnout.
   - Resource Allocation: It complicates resource allocation, as unplanned tasks consume time and effort that were allocated for other priorities.

4. Process Integrity:

   - Deviation from Scrum Principles: Regularly adding items from (D) undermines the Scrum framework, which emphasizes a stable and predictable Sprint Backlog.
   - Continuous Improvement: It can hinder the team's ability to reflect and improve, as the root causes of nonconformity might not be addressed.

5. Quality of Deliverables:

   - Rushed Work: Urgent, unplanned tasks might be rushed, leading to lower quality deliverables and technical debt.
   - Focus on Priorities: It diverts attention from high-priority items, potentially impacting the overall quality of the product.

#### Strategies to Minimize Process Nonconformity

1. Clear Prioritization:

   - Ensure that the Product Backlog is well-prioritized and that the team understands the rationale behind prioritization decisions.

2. Capacity Planning:

   - Regularly review and adjust the team's capacity to accommodate realistic workloads, reducing the need for urgent, unplanned work.

3. Stakeholder Communication:

   - Maintain open communication with stakeholders to manage expectations and minimize last-minute changes.

4. Root Cause Analysis:

   - Conduct retrospectives to identify and address the root causes of nonconformity, implementing changes to prevent recurrence.

5. Buffer Time:

   - Consider including a small buffer in the Sprint Backlog to handle unexpected tasks without disrupting planned work.
