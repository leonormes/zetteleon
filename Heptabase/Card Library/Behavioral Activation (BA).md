## Behavioral Activation (BA)

Originally developed for depression, Behavioral Activation is increasingly recognized for its utility in ADHD, particularly for addressing avoidance, passivity, and low motivation. BA operates on the principle that action can precede motivation; by engaging in activities that are rewarding, pleasurable, or provide a sense of mastery, mood and motivation can improve, creating a positive feedback loop.

For adults with ADHD, BA is adapted to:

- Encourage starting with very small, manageable actions to overcome initial inertia and overwhelm.
- Systematically schedule activities, including those that are enjoyable or aligned with values.
- Incorporate immediate rewards, given the ADHD brain's preference for more immediate reinforcement.
- Crucially, BA emphasizes aligning chosen activities with personal values and goals, making the actions feel more meaningful and less like arbitrary chores. For example, if an individual values creativity, a BA plan might involve scheduling a short art activity before tackling a less appealing administrative task. The CADDI protocol, a CBT approach for ADHD-Inattentive Presentation, specifically incorporates BA to target passivity and procrastination.
