# **The Story of Computer Science with Prof. Jim Al-Khalili**

[Doctor Irving Finkel.md](Doctor%20Irving%20Finkel.md)

The earliest writing we have comes on clay tablets. It comes from Iraq and ancient [Mesopotamia](https://app.heptabase.com/c16a6d60-49a6-4aec-9d1a-6161cbbe31a8/card/390928ce-87e2-490e-9844-f01575ed0f4d). A culture called [Sumerians.md](Sumerians.md)

The Sumerians started off with [pictogram](https://app.heptabase.com/c16a6d60-49a6-4aec-9d1a-6161cbbe31a8/card/69ff3c0b-e676-4a57-839f-b9f159069aae)s but then someone came up with the idea of symbols that represented sound. A new idea. And one that Finkel calls a giant leap for man-kind. The beginning of infinity.

![image.png](image.png)

By combining pictures of sounds the mesopotamians could express in writing any idea they could think of. 

These ideas are awe inspiring to me. Someone at some point came up with this idea. A single thought. A single solution to a problem. Possibly didn’t even seem like much. But it started as a tiny chemical reaction in a humans brain. And it spread and grew into the  written word. A similar moment for me is when Alan Turing hit upon his idea of the ‘computer’. A single brain, with it mysterious depths, producing chemical reactions that are interpetated as thought, coming up with an idea. [Essentially a nothing](https://app.heptabase.com/c16a6d60-49a6-4aec-9d1a-6161cbbe31a8/card/d44c4561-afec-4e2d-b7c8-a43998ac77a0). It doesn’t exist then it somehow does exist.
