## Bessie Sees it Different

Of course. It is wonderful that you have identified this incredible strength in Bessie. You are absolutely right; for many people with ADHD and dyslexia, this ability to make rapid, non-linear connections—to see the whole web of ideas at once—is a genuine superpower.

The traditional school system often rewards linear, "train-track" thinking (A leads to B leads to C), which can be frustrating and invalidating for someone with a "web-based" mind who has already jumped from A to F and seen a fascinating link to M over in another subject.

Helping her understand and harness this is one of the most valuable things you can do. Here is a guide on how you can explain this to her and get her engaged in using it as the advantage it truly is.

---

### Part 1: The Conversation – Framing Her Superpower

The goal here is to explain this concept in a way that is empowering, validating, and makes her feel seen and understood.

1\. Choose the Right Moment

Find a relaxed time when you're not talking about specific homework struggles. This isn't a "we need to fix this" conversation; it's a "I've noticed something amazing about you" conversation.

2\. Use a Powerful Analogy: The Train Track vs. The Mind Web

This visual analogy can be incredibly effective.

You could say something like:

_"I've been thinking about how your brain works, and I think it's amazing. You know how in school, each subject is like its own separate train track? You get on the History train, go from one station to the next, then you get off and get on the English train._

_But I've noticed your brain doesn't work like that. It's not a set of train tracks; it's more like a giant, sparkling spider's web. When you learn something in History, you don't just stay on that one track. Your mind instantly shoots a thread across the web to something you learned in Film, and another thread to an idea from Philosophy, and another to a photo you saw. You see the whole pattern, all at once._

_The school system is built for train-track thinkers. It's simple and organised. There is nothing wrong with your mind web—in fact, it's a much more creative and powerful way of thinking. It's the way inventors, artists, and problem-solvers think. The only challenge is that school isn't always set up to appreciate it. So, let's figure out how we can use your superpower to our advantage."_

3\. Validate Her Frustration

Acknowledge that this mismatch can be tough. Say something like, "It must be really frustrating sometimes when you see a cool connection but the teacher needs you to stay on that one train track. I get it. That doesn't mean your idea is wrong; it just wasn't the right moment for that particular class."

---

### Part 2: Engaging Her – Practical Ways to Use Her Mind Web

Once you've framed it positively, you can introduce activities that celebrate and utilise her thinking style. This turns it from a concept into a practical tool.

1\. Get a Giant Whiteboard or Big Sheets of Paper

Visual and kinaesthetic tools are fantastic for neurodivergent minds.

- The Activity: Put a core topic from one of her subjects in the centre. Let's say, _"Elizabethan England"_ from History. Then, together, literally draw the web.
   - Draw a line to English: "Let's link to Shakespeare. What was happening in the country when he wrote _Romeo and Juliet_?"
   - Draw a line to Art: "Let's find some portraits of Queen Elizabeth I. How did the artist use symbols to show her power?" (This links back to analysing images in Photography/Film).
   - Draw a line to Philosophy & Ethics: "What were the big religious conflicts then? How did that affect people's lives?"
   - Draw a line to Science/Maths: "Who were the explorers? What navigation tools did they use? That's all maths and physics!" This makes her 'jumps' visible and validates them as part of a bigger picture.

2\. Start with Her Interests First

ADHD brains are interest-driven. Use her passion subjects—like Film, Photography, or Art—as the central hub.

- The Activity: Pick a film she loves. Put its title in the middle of the page.
   - Link to History: "When was this film set? What was happening in the world then? Or, when was it _made_? Does it reflect the anxieties of its time (like the Cold War)?"
   - Link to Philosophy: "What's the main ethical dilemma the hero faces? Would you have made the same choice?"
   - Link to Photography/Art: "Let's pause on your favourite shot. Why is it so powerful? Let's analyse the composition, the lighting, the colours, just like a famous photograph or painting."

3\. Play "Find the Hidden Link"

Turn it into a low-pressure game. Give her two seemingly random concepts from her studies and challenge her to find a creative connection.

- Examples:
   - "What's the link between a scene in an action movie (Film Studies) and the concept of photosynthesis (Science)?"
      - _Possible Answer:_ Both are about energy transfer. Photosynthesis converts light into chemical energy for a plant. In an action scene, potential energy (a car teetering on a cliff) is converted into kinetic energy (it falling) to create excitement for the audience.
   - "How can you connect a poem about war (English) to a mathematical graph (Maths)?"
      - _Possible Answer:_ Both can show change over time. A graph shows data rising and falling. A poem can show a soldier's hope rising and falling through its stanzas and language.

4\. Lean into Technology

Her brain works like the internet—hyperlinked and associative. Use that!

- The Activity: When she's studying, encourage controlled "wiki-walks." Let her start on a revision page for, say, the human eye (Science), and then follow her curiosity. She might click on optics, then lenses, which takes her to the history of the camera (Photography), then to a famous photographer (Art). The key is to perhaps set a timer (e.g., 20 minutes of "web-walking") so it feels like a productive exploration rather than getting lost.

By doing this, you are not just helping her with her GCSEs. You are giving her a lifelong gift: the confidence to trust her own mind and the tools to use her unique, brilliant way of thinking to her full advantage.
