## Epistemological Link Types

From the perspective of how knowledge is constructed, you can identify links based on their role in knowledge generation:

- "Synthesis": The link represents a new idea or insight that emerges from the combination of the two notes.
- Example: "Note A (Idea 1)" and "Note B (Idea 2)" lead to "Note C (Synthesis)."
- "Example-of": One note provides a concrete example or case study of a more abstract concept in another note.
- Example: "Note A (Theory of Relativity)" and "Note B (GPS Error Correction)" where Note B is an example of Note A.
- "Evidence-for" or "Supports": One note provides evidence, data, or argumentation that supports the claim made in another note.
- Example: "Note A (Research Study)" provides evidence-for "Note B (Hypothesis)."
- "Refines" or "Expands-on": One note builds on or elaborates the content of another.
- Example: "Note A (Summary of a Concept)" refines "Note B (Original Idea)."
- "Challenges": One note critiques or questions the ideas in another.
- Example: "Note A (Critique of Traditional Economics)" challenges "Note B (Traditional Economics)."
