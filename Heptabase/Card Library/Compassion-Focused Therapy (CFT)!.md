# Compassion-Focused Therapy (CFT):

CFT was specifically developed to address mental health difficulties characterized by high levels of shame and self-criticism. Its central aim is to cultivate self-compassion, which involves three core components

- Treating oneself with kindness and understanding, particularly in times of suffering;

- Recognizing that imperfections and difficulties are part of a shared human experience (common humanity); 

- Maintaining a balanced, mindful awareness of painful thoughts and feelings without over-identifying with them.

Group-based CFT has demonstrated acceptability and efficacy in improving psychological well-being across various clinical presentations, with facilitators and fellow group members playing a vital role in participant engagement and outcomes. For adults with ADHD, who often contend with high self-criticism stemming from chronic challenges, CFT offers a promising avenue. Low self-compassion has been directly linked to poorer mental health outcomes in this population. Actionable principles from CFT that individuals can explore include normalizing their experiences with ADHD, developing a greater understanding of their emotional responses, and actively challenging negative self-talk ("stinking thinking") by externalizing shame and cultivating a compassionate inner voice. The development of self-compassion can serve as a significant protective factor against the psychological distress frequently associated with ADHD.
