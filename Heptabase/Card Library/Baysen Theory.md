# Baysen Theory

[Functional Fixedness.md](Functional%20Fixedness.md) often leads us to build upon existing solutions rather than considering reductive approaches. This relates to Bayesian reasoning in an interesting way:

Functional fixedness can be viewed as having an overly strong prior probability distribution that heavily weights existing use-cases. In Bayesian terms, we're essentially starting with a prior that's too "peaked" around conventional functions, making it difficult for new evidence or possibilities to meaningfully update our posterior beliefs.

For an example, see <!-- type: example -->[Mount the Candle on a Wall.md](Mount%20the%20Candle%20on%20a%20Wall.md).

To use Bayesian thinking to overcome this:

1. Deliberately flatten your prior distribution - consciously acknowledge that your initial assumptions about an object's function are just one possibility among many

2. Increase your likelihood function's sensitivity - actively look for evidence that might support alternative functions, even if they seem improbable initially

3. Use Bayesian updating iteratively - each time you encounter an object, force yourself to update your probability distribution of its possible functions based on the current context, rather than defaulting to your strongest prior

The key Bayesian insight here is that functional fixedness represents a failure to properly update our probability distributions when encountering objects in new contexts.
