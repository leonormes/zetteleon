# Schema Therapy (ST):

Schema Therapy is an integrative approach that targets Early Maladaptive Schemas (EMS)—deeply entrenched emotional and cognitive patterns, typically formed in childhood, that continue to influence an individual's thoughts, feelings, and behaviours in adulthood. It is particularly relevant for adults with ADHD who may have developed core schemas such as "Defectiveness/Shame" or "Failure" as a result of lifelong academic, social, or organizational struggles. ST is considered promising for populations who have not found long-term relief from traditional Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) alone.

The therapeutic process in ST typically unfolds in phases:

1. *Early Phase (Understanding the Shame Schema):* This stage involves exploring the developmental origins of shame, identifying specific triggers for shame-based beliefs, and pinpointing the core maladaptive schemas (e.g., "I am inherently flawed") that perpetuate these feelings.1  

2. *Middle Phase (Challenging and Restructuring):* Here, active work is done to challenge and modify these shame-based beliefs. Techniques include cognitive restructuring (examining and altering distorted thoughts), and experiential exercises such as imagery rescripting (revisiting and emotionally repairing past painful memories), chair work (dialoguing with different parts of the self or representations of others), and role-playing to explore and change maladaptive interpersonal patterns.1 This phase is pivotal in breaking the cycle of shame and self-blame.  

3. *Later Phase (Healing and Reintegration):* The focus shifts towards cultivating self-compassion, acceptance of ADHD as a part of oneself rather than a defect, and integrating new, healthier self-perceptions. Mindfulness practices and continued imagery rescripting help nurture the "wounded inner child" and develop a more balanced and compassionate self-view.1

While research specifically on ST for ADHD is still emerging, it is recognized for its potential in addressing the deep-seated "inherent defectiveness" schema often observed in this population. A scoping review by Vuijk et al. (2024) noted the limited number of studies but highlighted the potential of ST if appropriately adapted for neurodivergent populations.
