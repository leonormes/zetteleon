# Complex from Simple

Complex systems that work are built from much simpler systems that work.

The subcomponents of a complex system that contribute to its overall function are often referred to as "subsystems." These subsystems are typically smaller, simpler systems that perform specific, well-defined tasks within the broader system. When multiple subsystems work together, they create a complex system that has emergent properties—features that arise from the interaction of the parts but are not present in the individual subsystems.

Depending on the context, these subcomponents can be called by other names:

- Modules: Often used in software and engineering, these are self-contained units with defined inputs and outputs.

- Components: A more generic term used across various fields, indicating distinct parts that contribute to the overall system.

- Elements: Used in systems theory, indicating basic units or parts of the system.

- Agents: In multi-agent systems or biological systems, agents refer to independent entities that act and interact with each other.

Each subsystem or component usually works based on simpler rules or principles, and their collective behavior results in the complex outcomes seen in the larger system.
