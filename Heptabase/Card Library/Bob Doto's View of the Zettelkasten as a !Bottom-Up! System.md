## Bob Doto's View of the Zettelkasten as a "Bottom-Up" System

While the sources don't directly quote Bob Doto explaining the Zettelkasten as a "bottom-up" system, they provide ample information on how the Zettelkasten functions in this manner.

**Building from Individual Notes:** The sources consistently describe the Zettelkasten as a system that grows organically from the "bottom-up," starting with individual notes (Zettels) as the foundation. These atomic notes, representing single ideas, are the building blocks upon which the entire structure is built. Instead of starting with a pre-defined structure or hierarchy, you are encouraged to let the structure emerge from the connections you make between these individual notes as your understanding develops.

**Emergent Structure:** The sources use various metaphors to illustrate this emergent structure, such as a rhizome, a lattice, or a network. The key takeaway is that the Zettelkasten does not rely on a fixed, top-down hierarchy. Instead, the structure emerges organically through the linking of notes, creating a flexible and interconnected web of ideas that can evolve.

**Links as the Key to "Bottom-Up" Organisation:** The sources emphasize that links are crucial to the Zettelkasten's bottom-up nature. By linking notes based on their content and your evolving understanding, you create pathways through your ideas that do not depend on a pre-determined structure. The "idea compass," as discussed in our conversation, is a prime example of how the linking process can facilitate bottom-up exploration and organization. By considering various relationships between notes – broader context, sub-components, similarities, and differences – you build a multi-dimensional network of knowledge rather than a rigid hierarchy.

**Structure Notes as a Secondary Development:** While the Zettelkasten prioritizes bottom-up growth, the sources acknowledge the value of structure notes for imposing a hierarchy on the note collection. However, these structure notes are seen as a secondary development, a way to create "maps of content" or overviews of specific themes that emerge from the interconnected network of notes, rather than a primary means of organization.

**Key Points from the Sources:**

- The Zettelkasten "helps you write your own ideas as you go along", building up from individual notes rather than imposing a pre-existing structure.

- The structure of the Zettelkasten is "emergent, building up from the ideas that have been incorporated".

- The Zettelkasten allows you to visualise and manipulate the "heterarchical reality" we live in, creating "provisional structures" through links rather than relying on rigid categories.

- Structure notes can provide a hierarchical overview, but even those are absorbed into the overall "rhizomatic structure" of the Zettelkasten.

In summary, while the sources don't directly cite Bob Doto using the term "bottom-up", they strongly support the concept of the Zettelkasten as a system that prioritizes the organic linking of individual ideas over pre-defined structures, allowing for emergent and flexible knowledge organization.
