Table 1: Comparative Overview of Key Therapeutic Modalities for Deep Change in Adult ADHD

| Therapeutic Modality | Core Theoretical Underpinnings | Primary Targets in Adult ADHD | Key Techniques/Interventions | Evidence Base for Deep/Sustainable Change in ADHD (Summary) | Specific Considerations/Adaptations for ADHD Population |

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) | Psychological Flexibility; Relational Frame Theory | Experiential avoidance, cognitive fusion, lack of values clarity, inaction, shame, low self-esteem | Defusion, acceptance, present moment awareness, self-as-context, values clarification, committed action 11 | Promising for ADHD symptoms, QoL, psychological maladjustment; focuses on living meaningfully with symptoms 11 | Structure home practices carefully to avoid guilt/shame if tasks are difficult to complete due to EF deficits 10; focus on making values tangible and actions concrete. |

| Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) & Adaptations (e.g., Hesslinger Protocol) | Biosocial Theory of Emotion Dysregulation; Dialectics (Acceptance & Change); Behavioral Science | Emotional dysregulation, impulsivity, interpersonal difficulties, distress intolerance, shame, identity confusion | Mindfulness, distress tolerance skills (e.g., TIPP, STOP, self-soothe), emotion regulation skills (e.g., check facts, opposite action), interpersonal effectiveness skills (e.g., DEAR MAN), behavior chain analysis 9 | Effective for ADHD symptoms, executive functioning, QoL, emotional regulation.9 Hesslinger protocol specifically adapted for ADHD.47 | Break down skills into smaller steps; provide repetition and practice; address potential challenges with homework completion; group format can be beneficial but may require management of ADHD-related behaviors. |

| Schema Therapy (ST) | Early Maladaptive Schemas (EMS) resulting from unmet childhood needs; Schema Modes | Deep-seated shame, defectiveness, failure schemas; chronic negative self-perception; maladaptive coping styles; relationship difficulties 1 | Cognitive restructuring, experiential techniques (imagery rescripting, chair work, role-play), limited reparenting, behavioral pattern-breaking 1 | Emerging for ADHD; promising for chronic issues and when standard CBT is insufficient.7 Addresses biographical factors. | Requires adaptation for ADHD cognitive style (e.g., concrete explanations, managing distractibility during experiential work). Longer-term therapy often needed. Access to trained therapists may be limited.7 |

| Compassion-Focused Therapy (CFT) | Evolutionary psychology; Attachment theory; Affect regulation systems; Importance of compassion for well-being | High shame, self-criticism, low self-esteem, emotional dysregulation | Cultivating compassionate mind attributes (wisdom, strength, warmth, non-judgment); compassionate imagery, letter writing, mindfulness; understanding the "tricky brain" 4 | Effective for improving psychological well-being in groups with high self-criticism.13 Self-compassion is a protective factor in ADHD.4 | Group format helpful.15 Focus on psychoeducation about ADHD brain can enhance self-compassion. Address potential difficulties with sustained attention during meditative practices. |

| Existential Therapy / Logotherapy | Focus on ultimate concerns (death, freedom, isolation, meaninglessness); Will to meaning | Lack of meaning/purpose, identity confusion, existential anxiety, feelings of alienation or hopelessness 67 | Socratic dialogue, meaning-centered exercises, exploration of values and responsibility, dereflection, paradoxical intention 70 | Meaning therapies show effects on positive meaning, psychopathology, self-efficacy.72 Relevance for ADHD in re-evaluating life post-diagnosis and finding purpose amidst challenges. | May need to be adapted to be more structured or concrete for some with ADHD. Focus on translating abstract concepts of meaning into tangible, value-driven actions. |

| Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy (MBCT) | Integration of CBT and mindfulness meditation; Focus on changing relationship to thoughts/feelings | Negative thought patterns, rumination, emotional reactivity, inattention, stress 73 | Mindfulness meditation (body scan, sitting, walking), cognitive decentering, psychoeducation on links between thoughts, feelings, and behaviors 73 | Effective for ADHD core symptoms, especially without major comorbidities.74 Improves functioning of Default Mode Network.75 | Requires commitment to regular meditation practice, which can be challenging for ADHD. Adaptations may include shorter practice periods, varied types of mindfulness, and explicit linking to ADHD experiences. |
