## Techniques for Connecting Daily Actions to Deeper Life Goals and Personal Values

A critical component of fostering sustained motivation is helping adults with ADHD connect their daily actions to their broader life goals and deeply held personal values. This transforms mundane or challenging tasks from obligations into meaningful steps towards a desired future.

Engaging in value-driven action offers a powerful antidote to the shame and low self-esteem that frequently plague adults with ADHD.1 When self-worth is primarily tied to performance in areas compromised by ADHD (e.g., consistent organization, flawless productivity), it can be exceedingly fragile. Values, however, are not about achieving perfect outcomes but about the qualities of action an individual chooses to embody. By consciously aligning daily behaviours with deeply held values—such as curiosity, compassion, persistence, or creativity—individuals can cultivate an intrinsic sense of meaning and self-worth that is independent of symptom severity or the attainment of specific external achievements. Even if an ADHD-related challenge arises during a task (e.g., a project takes longer than anticipated due to distractibility), if the effort was guided by a core value (e.g., "striving for excellence" or "making a meaningful contribution"), the experience can still affirm one's identity and purpose. This effectively decouples self-worth from the often-unrealistic expectation of perfect, neurotypical performance, fostering a more resilient and authentic sense of self.
