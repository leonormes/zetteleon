# Concept: Illusion of Explanatory Depth (IOED)

When somebody believes they understand something well, but then try to explain it to someone else only to discover they don’t actually know how to describe it. [2026-04-23.md](../Journal/2026-04-23.md)

##### 2\. Provenance

- **Coined by:** Rozenblit & Keil (2002), *Cognitive Science*, "The misunderstood limits of folk science: an illusion of explanatory depth."

- **Tradition:** Cognitive psychology, metacognition research.

- **Related lineage:** Dunning–Kruger (different — that's about skill, not mechanism), Kahneman's System 1/2, the "feeling of knowing."

##### 3\. The mechanism — *why* the illusion happens

This is the most important section. A concept card without mechanism is just a dictionary entry.

- **Confusing recognition for generation:** Seeing a bicycle daily produces familiarity; familiarity feels like understanding.

- **Abstraction compression:** We store gist-level representations and mistake them for detailed models.

- **No forcing function:** In daily life, nothing tests the depth of our understanding, so the illusion is never punctured.

- **Hierarchical opacity:** We know bicycles "have gears"; we do not know how a derailleur works; we rarely notice the gap.

##### 4\. Diagnostic — how to detect it in yourself

- Try to **explain the thing mechanistically, step-by-step, to an imagined naïve audience**, out loud or in writing.

- The gap between "I get it" and the first place you stutter = the illusion's magnitude.

- Rozenblit & Keil's original protocol: rate understanding → attempt explanation → re-rate. The re-rating drops sharply.

##### 5\. Domains where it bites hardest (for you specifically)

- Software engineering: "I know how HTTPS works" → try explaining TLS handshake from memory.

- Philosophy: "I understand JTB" → try stating Gettier's argument in your own words.

- Meta: "I understand IOED" — *notice this card is itself the test*.

##### 6\. Countermeasures (this is where it connects to your PKM practice)

- **Self-explanation protocol** (Chi et al.) — explain to yourself whilst learning.

- **Feynman technique** — teach it to a 12-year-old in plain language.

- **Generation over recognition** — always write from memory before re-reading.

- **Steel-manning** — forces you to reconstruct, not recognise.

- **Dated position-taking** — commits you to a claim that can be falsified later.

- **Atomic note discipline** — one idea per card, explained in your own words.

→ Each of these deserves its own card. Most are **Practice / Protocol** cards.
