## Solving Problem 3: The Branching Explosion

This is the deepest of your five problems, and I suspect the one generating most of the anxiety. Let me address it directly.

**You are right that branching is infinite in principle.** Every node you create could spawn sub-nodes. This is called the **infinite regress problem** in philosophy, and it is real.

The answer is **not** to find a natural stopping point — there isn't one. The answer is to have an **external stopping rule**:

### The Three Stopping Rules

Stop branching when any of these is true:

1. **The claim is sharp enough to defend.** Can you state it, name its falsifier, and cite its key support? If yes, stop. Further refinement is procrastination dressed as rigour.

2. **You have hit the limit of your current knowledge.** If the next sub-question requires research you haven't done, stop. Log the sub-question as an `Open Thread` and move on. Do not branch into speculation.

3. **You are bored.** Genuinely. If your interest has dropped, your brain is telling you the useful thinking has been extracted. Further branching will be busywork, not insight.

**Rule 3 is the ADHD-friendly one and the one most people ignore.** You said earlier that you are motivated by novelty and interest, not urgency. The inverse is: *when interest dies, the work is done for now*. Your interest is a diagnostic instrument. Trust it.

### The Practical Application

When processing a dumped paragraph, extract maybe 2–4 distinct claims or questions from it. Not 20. Not one per sentence. **The paragraph is not a tree to be exhaustively mapped — it is a rough quarry from which you extract the 3 most useful stones.**

The rest of the paragraph's content is *fine to lose*. You wrote it; the act of writing already did some cognitive work; the surviving 3 claims are the output. Everything else can go.
