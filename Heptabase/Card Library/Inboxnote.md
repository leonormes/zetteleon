It’s a long, slow process, making incremental gains, and it’s very easy to fall back into negative self-talk if you take your eye off it. It may also be helpful to bear in mind that negative self-talk can be a symptom of depression, or of learned thought patterns that can lead to depression, so any work that you are able to do on it is hugely beneficial to your well-being both in the long and short term. Have you done any work or research into catching negative self talk and rewording it (at a very basic level, I often hear kids at work saying “I can’t do this”, which is a form of negative talk which frames difficulties as evidence of one’s own personal inadequacies and failings, and becomes a self-fulfilling prophecy because it encourages them to give up trying. I always get them to at the very least add the word “yet”, and I encourage them to reword it to something along the lines of “This is difficult and it’s taking more time and effort than I expected, but I’m going to keep at it until I get the hang of it”, which acknowledges the difficulty they are experiencing, but clearly labels that difficulty as an aspect of the task, rather than as evidence of personal inadequacy, and also functions as a self-fulfilling prophecy, because it encourages them to persevere until they can do whatever it is. It also gives them an immediate psychological boost, which is often all they need to get back on track with whatever they’re trying to accomplish, plus it gives them a win which is independent of the task, because even if they don’t master it then and there, they still get to feel good about themselves because they didn’t give up, they believe that they’ll get there eventually, and we give them loads of praise for persevering) ?

I’ve also just remembered one of the most helpful techniques I’ve picked up along the way, which is to try to talk to myself as though I was talking to a child, or to a good friend. You would never go up to a kid who was struggling to get the hang of something and say “You can’t do that because you’re not good enough.
