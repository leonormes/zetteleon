# Externalizing Thought

Writing is essential for thinking systematically.

Luhmann saw the act of writing as part of the thinking process itself, not simply a way to record completed thoughts.

Beyond simply capturing thoughts, externalizing them through various methods encourages deeper engagement with the information.

- Annie Murphy Paul, in "The Extended Mind," explains how note-taking, especially in context-rich environments, forces us to discriminate, judge, and select information, leading to more profound processing and potential for new insights.

- Robert Caro's method of creating wall-sized outlines and concept maps illustrates the power of externalizing information on a large scale, enabling spatial reasoning and memory to enhance cognitive work.

- David Allen's recommendation to capture ideas on separate sheets of paper during a mind sweep helps to focus on each item individually and avoid the distractions of a long list.

- David Allen, in "Getting Things Done," stresses that our minds are designed for "having" ideas, not for "holding" them. He advocates for a system of capturing all thoughts, ideas, and commitments as they arise, using tools like notebooks, digital apps, or even voice recorders. This allows for a more relaxed and controlled mental state, knowing that everything is captured and will be revisited at the appropriate time.

- Daniel Levitin, in "The Organized Mind," similarly highlights the limitations of working memory and how offloading information onto external systems can enhance our cognitive capacity. He suggests that writing things down frees up the "rehearsal loop" in our brains, allowing us to focus on other tasks.
