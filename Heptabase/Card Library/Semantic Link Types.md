## Semantic Link Types

Semantic networks and ontologies often define links by the semantic relationship between concepts. You can apply these categories to differentiate relationships between notes based on their content:

- Hierarchical Links:

- "Is-a": One concept is a more specific version or instance of another.

   - Example: "Note A (Graph)" is-a "Note B (Mathematical Structure)."

- "Part-of": One concept is a component or part of a larger whole.

   - Example: "Note A (Neuron)" is part-of "Note B (Neural Network)."

- Associative Links:

- "Related-to": Two concepts are related by association, but not in a hierarchical or causal way.

   - Example: "Note A (Cloud Computing)" is related-to "Note B (Distributed Systems)."

- "Contrasts-with": One concept contrasts with another, often presenting an opposing view.

   - Example: "Note A (Classical Computing)" contrasts-with "Note B (Quantum Computing)."

- Causal Links:

- "Causes" or "Leads-to": One concept causes or influences another.

   - Example: "Note A (Inflation)" causes "Note B (Price Increase)."

- "Depends-on": One concept relies on another for its explanation or existence.

   - Example: "Note A (Server Performance)" depends-on "Note B (Network Bandwidth)."

- Temporal Links:

- "Precedes": One event or idea comes before another in time.

   - Example: "Note A (Set-up)" precedes "Note B (Execution)."

- "Sequential": Two concepts are part of a sequence of steps or ideas.

   - Example: "Note A (Learning Basics)" is sequential to "Note B (Learning Advanced Topics)."
