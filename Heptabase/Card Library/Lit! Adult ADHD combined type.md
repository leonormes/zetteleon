- Claim · Concept · Practice · Source · Person · Question

The right-hand card is none of these. It is a **Literature** card (or "Reference," or "Import" — naming to be decided): a block of externally-sourced material you are going to mine for your own cards later. It is **raw input awaiting processing**, not a finished artefact.

This is a well-known category in Zettelkasten tradition — Sönke Ahrens calls them **literature notes**, distinguished from **permanent notes**. The Zettelkasten community has argued about this split for years. You have recreated the distinction by intuition. That's a good sign.

The critical property: **a literature note is not subject to the five-box "done" test.** It is not expected to have a falsifier, a dated position, or a sharp thesis. It is raw material. Its job is to be *mineable*, not *finished*.

## The Seventh Note Type

I am going to propose adding one type to the taxonomy:

- **Literature** (or `Source-content` if you prefer) — raw material imported from elsewhere (this conversation, a book, a paper, an LLM output) that will be processed into claims, concepts, practices, questions as you work with it.

The rule that makes this work:

> **A Literature card is temporary by design. It exists to be decomposed. Once its contents have been absorbed into permanent cards, the Literature card can be archived or deleted.**

This matters because otherwise Literature cards accumulate forever and clutter the Card Library. They should feel like a workbench, not a display case.

**Visual convention suggestion:** prefix Literature cards with `Lit:` or `Source:` so they sort together and are visually distinct. So the right-hand card becomes something like:

- `Lit: Claude on JTB claim (2026-04-23)` — dated, clearly sourced, clearly temporary.

This small naming discipline makes it obvious, six months from now, that the card is working material, not a finished thesis.
