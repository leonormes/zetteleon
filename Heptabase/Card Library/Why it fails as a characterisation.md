## Why it Fails as a Characterisation

- Gettier (1963) demolished sufficiency with a two-page paper. His counterexamples show cases where S has a justified true belief that P, but through epistemic luck—we would not call it knowledge.
   - Classic case: You see a clock reading 2:00 and form the justified belief it is 2:00. It happens to be 2:00. But the clock stopped 12 hours ago. JTB ✓, knowledge ✗.
- The post-Gettier literature (60+ years of it) has produced a cottage industry of repairs: defeasibility conditions, causal theories (Goldman), reliabilism, safety/sensitivity (Nozick, Sosa), virtue epistemology (Zagzebski), and knowledge-first epistemology (Williamson, who argues knowledge is _primitive and unanalysable_—i.e., belief is analysed in terms of knowledge, not the reverse).
- There is no consensus replacement. The honest summary is: "JTB is necessary but probably not sufficient, and nobody has agreed on the fourth condition."
