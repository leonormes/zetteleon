## Values Clarification (as in ACT)

Acceptance and Commitment Therapy places significant emphasis on values clarification as a cornerstone of meaningful living.21 Values are chosen life directions, qualities of ongoing action that are intrinsically important to the individual (e.g., being a compassionate partner, a creative problem-solver, a lifelong learner). They are distinct from goals, which are specific outcomes that can be achieved. ACT-based interventions guide individuals through exercises to identify and articulate their core values across various life domains (e.g., relationships, career, personal growth, community).21 This might involve reflective questions such as "What do you want to stand for in life?" or "What kind of person do you want to be?".21 While a general values clarification exercise like the one described in 103 (using colored slips of paper for different value domains) is not ADHD-specific, its principles can be adapted. Once values are clarified, the focus shifts to "committed action"—behaving in ways that are consistent with these chosen values, even when faced with ADHD-related challenges or internal discomfort.
