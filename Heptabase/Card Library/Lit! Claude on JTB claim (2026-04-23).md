### Part 2: "Belief is Always belief-that-P, where P is a proposition"

#### What the Claim Gets Right

- This is the dominant view in analytic philosophy: belief is a propositional attitude. Russell, Frege, and most contemporary philosophers of mind treat it this way.
- The grammar of belief ascription ("S believes that P") does push strongly in this direction.

#### Where it is Contested

- Belief-in vs belief-that. "I believe in you" / "I believe in God" resist clean reduction to belief-that. Some philosophers (notably H.H. Price) argued belief-in is sui generis; others reduce it, but the reduction is non-trivial.
- Credences, not binary belief. Formal epistemologists (Jeffrey, Joyce) argue the fundamental doxastic state is a degree of confidence in \[0,1\], not a binary attitude toward a proposition. On this view, "belief-that-P" is a coarse approximation.
- Dispositionalism (Schwitzgebel). Beliefs are clusters of behavioural, cognitive, and phenomenal dispositions—not discrete propositional attitudes. A person can be "in-between believing" in ways propositionalism cannot capture.
- Fragmentation (Lewis, Stalnaker, Elga). Agents hold inconsistent "belief-fragments" used in different contexts—one doxastic system, not one propositional attitude.
- Non-propositional representations. Map-like or imagistic mental representations (Camp, Rescorla) may play belief-like functional roles without being propositional.
- Pre-linguistic cognition. Do infants and animals have beliefs? If yes and they lack propositional contents, propositionalism fails. If no, propositionalism looks anthropocentric.
- What is a proposition anyway? Even granting propositionalism, "proposition" is contested—Russellian structured entities, Fregean senses, sets of possible worlds (Stalnaker), or sentences in a language of thought (Fodor). The claim smuggles in metaphysical commitments it does not declare

## Building a Dialectical Card: JTB

Good instinct. The Illusion of Explanatory Depth (IOED) is defeated specifically by forcing yourself to _generate_ structure, not just _recognise_ it. Reading about Gettier feels like understanding Gettier. Reconstructing the argument from memory, in your own words, with its weakest link exposed—that is understanding.

What you want is not a note. It is a claim under adversarial pressure, maintained over time.

### The Principle: Why Most PKM Fails at Epistemics

Most people treat PKM cards as containers for facts. That is fine for reference but useless for conviction. For a contested philosophical claim, the card must be a living dialectic—a structured argument map where:

- The claim is interrogated, not stored.
- Evidence is weighted, not just listed.
- Your own position is forced to commit and is dated (so you can see it evolve).
- The card exposes what you would need to learn or experience to _change your mind_.

This is closer to Rationalist "double-crux" notes or Tiago Forte's "Intermediate Packets" than to a Zettelkasten atomic note. Borrow from all three.

### The Card Structure

Here is a template. Use it verbatim for this first card, then adapt.

#### `Claim: Knowledge is justified true belief`

##### 1\. Steel-manned Statement of the Claim

> One sentence. The strongest, most charitable version. No strawmen.

_Example:_ "To know that P is to believe that P, where P is true and one's belief is adequately justified—and these three conditions are jointly sufficient."

Why this matters: If you cannot state the claim in its strongest form, you cannot honestly argue against it. This step alone kills 80% of IOED.

##### 2\. Provenance

- Who holds/held it? (Plato, _Theaetetus_; orthodox view until 1963)
- In what tradition? (Analytic epistemology)
- Why does anyone believe it? (The intuitive pull of each condition individually)

##### 3\. Arguments FOR (each as a Sub-bullet, not a paragraph)

- Necessity of truth: You cannot know a falsehood. _(Intuition pump: "I knew the Earth was flat"—no, you believed it.)_
- Necessity of belief: You cannot know P whilst disbelieving P. _(Edge case: Radford's unconfident examinee—does she know or merely believe?)_
- Necessity of justification: Lucky guesses are not knowledge. _(Intuition pump: correctly guessing a lottery number.)_
- Parsimony: Three clean conditions, each independently motivated.

##### 4\. Arguments AGAINST (the Core of the card)

- Gettier counterexamples → JTB is not _sufficient_.
   - State one case in your own words (do not copy).
   - → _Link to card: `Gettier Cases`_
- Williamson's knowledge-first: Knowledge may be primitive; belief is analysed _in terms of_ knowledge, not the reverse. → _Link: `Knowledge-First Epistemology`_
- Reliabilism's challenge to "justification": Maybe what matters is the reliability of the belief-forming process, not internal justification. → _Link: `Reliabilism`_
- Virtue epistemology: Knowledge is true belief arising from intellectual virtue. → _Link: `Virtue Epistemology`_

##### 5\. Attempted Repairs (the post-Gettier landscape)

- No-false-lemmas (Clark)
- Defeasibility conditions (Lehrer & Paxson)
- Causal theory (Goldman)
- Safety (Sosa) / Sensitivity (Nozick)

   _One line each. You are mapping the terrain, not mastering it yet._

##### 6\. The Crux (IOED-killer)

> What is the single question that, if answered, would settle this for me?

_Example:_ "Is there a fourth condition that rules out epistemic luck without collapsing into question-begging?" If yes → JTB is reparable. If no → we need a different framework entirely (Williamson).

This field is non-negotiable. It forces you to locate the load-bearing disagreement, not just list positions.

#####7. My position—dateded

> As of 23 April 2026: I think JTB captures three necessary conditions but is not sufficient. I am tentatively drawn to \[reliabilism / safety / knowledge-first\] because \[reason\]. I am uncertain about \[X\].

Re-date when it changes. Keep the old entries. The diff is the learning.

##### 8\. Open Threads

- Things I do not yet understand.
- Papers I have not read.
- Counterexamples I cannot handle.

### Why This Structure Works Against IOED

- Section 1 (steel-man) forces generation, not recognition.
- Section 4 (against) forces you to _explain_ objections, not merely namedrop them.
- Section 6 (crux) is the killer move: IOED thrives on vague gestures at complexity ("well, it's complicated"). A crux demands you commit to _what specifically_ is unresolved.
- Section 7 (dated position) creates accountability. You cannot hide behind "I'm still thinking about it" forever.
- Section 8 (open threads) is where genuine humility lives—and where your next cards get born.
