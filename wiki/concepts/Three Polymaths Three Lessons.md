# Three Polymaths Three Lessons

Three historical polymaths — **Ibn al-Haytham**, **Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz**, and **Richard Feynman** — each developed a distinct strategy for mastering multiple fields. Their techniques converge on a shared principle: *understanding is not passive recognition; it is active reconstruction*.

## Minimum Viable Understanding (MVU)

Learning is a **generative** act, not a consumptive one. You do not know something until you can reconstruct it from memory — in your own notation, defending it against your strongest objections.

## Working Knowledge

### Ibn al-Haytham: Steelmanning

> "Seek objective truth rather than defending your identity."

Construct the strongest possible counter-argument to your own beliefs. If you can dismantle your own position, you gain a more accurate worldview.

- Opposite of *strawmanning*.
- Forces **reconstruction over recognition**.
- Directly applicable to PKM: before committing a claim to a note, attempt to demolish it yourself.
- Primary countermeasure to [[Concept! Illusion of Explanatory Depth (IOED)|IOED]].

### Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz: Intuitive Notation and Diagrams

> "The notation we use determines how well we understand the concept."

Transform every concept into a diagram, model, or symbol that a stranger could understand from memory.

- Leibniz's calculus notation ($\int$, $dy/dx$) survived because it encoded physical meaning and relationships.
- **Test:** If you cannot draw a concept from memory, you have not mastered it.
- The act of creating notation is itself a generative act — you compress understanding into a symbolic form.
- Foundation: [[Mental representations take multiple forms]] — visual, symbolic, and logical representations each encode different facets of understanding.

### Richard Feynman: The 12 Favorite Problems

> "Keep a dozen of your favorite problems constantly present in your mind... Every time you hear a new trick or result, test it against each of your twelve problems."

Maintain a dynamic list of ~12 open questions spanning scales — from fundamental to playful.

- Each new piece of information becomes a "key" tested against 12 "locks".
- Creates a **vector for learning** — transforms passive fact-gathering into active solution-seeking.
- Enables **cross-pollination**: a spinning cafeteria plate → electron orbit equations → Nobel Prize.

## Current Understanding

### Meta-Principle: Reconstruction Over Recognition

All three strategies share a single epistemic stance: **understanding is demonstrated by generation, not recognition**. This is the unifying thread — steelmanning tests your ability to reconstruct opposing views; notation forces you to reconstruct concepts symbolically; the 12 problems method forces you to reconstruct knowledge actively against open questions.
