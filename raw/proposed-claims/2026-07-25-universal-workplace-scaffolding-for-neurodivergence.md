## Supporting Context

In contemporary workplaces, reactive medical models of accommodation—which rely on formal diagnoses and individual requests—often fail because many neurodivergent individuals remain undiagnosed or fear disclosing their condition due to a lack of psychological safety [source: raw/2026-07-25-bbc-adhd-workplace-consequences]. As Professor Amanda Kirby observes, a medical model of providing tailored adjustments is inadequate because "nobody fits into neat boxes," and targeted training focused simply on individualized condition rules is effective long-term only when embedded structurally [source: raw/2026-07-25-bbc-adhd-workplace-consequences]. In contrast, businesses that introduce universal adaptations across their entire workforce—such as default flexible working, visual communication signals like desk "busy lights," and optimized meeting structures—eliminate the burden of masking and disclosure while lifting cognitive performance across the entire team [source: raw/2026-07-25-bbc-adhd-workplace-consequences] [inference]. This approach aligns with existing organizational strategies for neurodivergent talent, reinforcing that systematic environmental design out-scales individual exception handling (see [[Organizations Can Support ADHD Employees Through Culture and Accommodations]]).
