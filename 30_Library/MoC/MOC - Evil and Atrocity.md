## MOC - Evil and Atrocity

> [!hint] Start Here
> [[Card's Atrocity Paradigm Identifies Evil via Three Necessary Co-conditions]]—_The organising test. Harm is evil only if it is culpable, reasonably foreseeable and intolerable; missing any one drops it to wrongdoing, negligence or tragedy._

- Up: [[MOC - Character and Virtue]] (§7, Adjacent Traditions)

## Purpose

The vault holds a small cluster of atomic claims about evil that were never given a home of their own. They share one analytical stance: treat evil as a category you can test for, not a feeling. This map orders them so the test comes first and the supporting claims hang off it.

## 1. The Three Conditions

The Atrocity Paradigm splits into its parts:

- [[Evil Requires Both Culpability and Foreseeability — Without These It Is Tragedy]]—_Conditions one and two: the agent is responsible and the harm was predictable from their position._
- [[Evil Harm Is Intolerable Harm That Destroys the Conditions for a Decent Life]]—_Condition three: the harm crosses a threshold where it forecloses a tolerable life rather than merely impeding it._

## 2. Diagnostic Features (Not Extra Conditions)

Properties that typically accompany evil but are not part of Card's three necessary conditions. Use them as markers, not as tests:

- [[Evil Structurally Requires a Radical Power Asymmetry Directed at the Defenceless]]—_Look for radical imbalance of power first; its absence suggests a fair fight or a mutual wrong._
- [[Evil Attacks the Shared Moral Community Itself Not Merely a Norm Within It]]—_What separates evil from ordinary wrongdoing: it attacks the framework, not a rule inside it._
- [[Objectification Treats Persons as Instruments and Is a Core Property of Evil]]—_Treating persons as disposable means recurs across Kant to Card._

## 3. How Ordinary People Take Part

- [[Evil Arises from Malice or Thoughtless Banality — Motivation Is Not Determinative]]—_Foreseeability, not motive, decides the classification; Arendt's Eichmann is the second pathway._
- [[SoT - Bonhoeffer's Theory of Functional Stupidity]]—_A route by which ordinary people come to satisfy the conditions._

## 4. Responding

- [[Rejection As Moral Response]]—_Collective rejection as a counterweight to harmful power dynamics; it cites the moral-community claim above._

## 5. Structural Analogy

- [[The Traditional Definition of Knowledge is Justified True Belief]]—_Another classic tripartite necessary-conditions framework; the Atrocity Paradigm mirrors its shape. An analogy, not a dependency._
