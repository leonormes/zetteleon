This note summarizes the reasons why task initiation is so difficult for individuals with ADHD, and the behavioural interventions that can help.

## Why Task Initiation Is Difficult

- Executive Dysfunction: [[Executive Dysfunction - The Root of Analysis Paralysis|Executive dysfunction]] impairs planning, prioritizing, and organizing tasks.
- Low Dopamine Reward: [[Dopamine reward pathway dysfunction causes ADHD task initiation difficulty|Low dopamine in the reward centers]] makes tasks feel unmotivating.
- Interest-Based Nervous System: The [[The ADHD nervous system is primarily interest-driven|ADHD nervous system is interest-based]], requiring tasks to be novel, urgent, or meaningful.
- Emotional Barriers: [[MOC - Emotional Dysregulation|Emotional barriers]] like anxiety and overwhelm can block initiation.
- Time Blindness: [[Time Agnosia Impairs Planning for Developers with ADHD|Difficulty perceiving the passage of time]] makes planning and starting tasks harder.
- Activation Cost: [[The Activation Cost of Tasks is Higher in ADHD|The mental energy required to start a task is higher]].

## Behavioural Interventions

[[Behavioral Interventions for ADHD Compensate for Neurobiological Deficits|Effective behavioral interventions compensate for these neurobiological differences]].

- Micro-Stepping: [[Micro-Stepping Reduces Cognitive Load for Task Initiation|Breaking tasks into micro-steps]] lowers the activation cost.
- External Cues: Using [[Environmental and Physical Hacks for ADHD Focus|external cues]] like timers and alarms bypasses unreliable internal timing.
- Behavioural Momentum: [[Chaining Starter Tasks Creates a Momentum Ramp|Starting with an easy task]] can build momentum for harder ones.
- Reward the Start: [[Rapid Feedback Loops are Essential for ADHD Motivation|Rewarding the start of a task]], not just the finish, provides immediate reinforcement.
- Social Structures: [[Body Doubling for ADHD Focus|Body doubling]] and accountability partners provide external motivation.
