## 5. Franchise Deep Dive: The War of the Worlds

There are three primary television series adaptations of _The War of the Worlds_. Each approaches H.G. Wells’s source material using a distinct narrative architecture: a direct legacy sequel, a faithful period implementation, and a modern deconstruction.

### 1. War of the Worlds (1988–1990)

**Framework:** Legacy System Extension (Sequel to the 1953 Film)
**Network:** Syndicated
**Duration:** 2 Seasons (44 Episodes)

This series functions as a direct continuation of the 1953 George Pal film rather than the original novel. It operates on the logic that the aliens (the Mor-Taxans) did not die from bacteria but merely entered a state of suspended animation.

- **The Narrative Model:** The series shifts the genre from "invasion war" to "covert infiltration." The aliens awaken decades later and, lacking their heavy machinery (tripods), resort to body-snatching and guerrilla tactics.
- **Key Mechanic:** The show utilises a procedural format. A small, specialised task force (The Blackwood Project) tracks and neutralises alien cells to prevent a second full-scale invasion, mirroring the "monster of the week" structure common in late-80s television.

### 2. The War of the Worlds (2019)

**Framework:** Faithful Historical Implementation
**Network:** BBC One
**Duration:** 3-Part Miniseries

This adaptation attempts to restore the original "source code" of the novel by retaining the Edwardian period setting (approx. 1905), unlike most adaptations which port the story to modern America.

- **The Narrative Model:** It adheres to the classic invasion structure—cylinder landing, heat rays, and the collapse of societal order—but introduces a dual-timeline structure. It intercuts the invasion with a post-apocalyptic future ("The Red World") to examine the long-term ecological and psychological impact of the event.
- **Key Mechanic:** The focus is on the collision between Edwardian arrogance and superior technology. It critiques the rigidity of the British class system and empire, using the Martians as an unstoppable external disruptor that renders social hierarchy obsolete.

### 3. War of the Worlds (2019–2022)

**Framework:** Modern Distributed Deconstruction
**Network:** Fox / Canal+
**Duration:** 3 Seasons (24 Episodes)

This series (created by Howard Overman) strips away the "tripod" imagery entirely, refactoring the premise into a character-driven survival drama set in present-day Europe.

- **The Narrative Model:** The "war" is over almost immediately. An extraterrestrial signal kills the vast majority of humanity instantly, leaving scattered survivors. The antagonists are not giant machines, but Boston Dynamics-style biomechanical dogs and humanoid entities.
- **Key Mechanic:** It prioritises psychological tension and moral ambiguity over spectacle. The core logic explores the blurred lines between human and alien, eventually revealing a causal loop (bootstrap paradox) that redefines the aliens' motivation and origin.

### Summary of Architecture

| Series          | Core Concept            | Setting       | Antagonist Form           |
| --------------- | ----------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------- |
| **1988 Series** | **Covert Infiltration** | Late 80s USA  | Body-snatchers / Humanoid |
| **2019 (BBC)**  | **Period Fidelity**     | Edwardian UK  | Giant Tripods (Classic)   |
| **2019 (Fox)**  | **Post-Apocalyptic**    | Modern Europe | Robot Dogs / Humanoids    |

## 12. Franchise Deep Dive: Mad Max

The _Mad Max_ series operates as a collection of **"Campfire Myths"** rather than a strict, linear history. This legend-based model explains why the character remains roughly the same age despite decades passing between films.

### The Chronological Timeline

1. **Mad Max (1979)**
   - **Setting:** Mid-apocalypse (societal decay).
   - **Context:** Max is a highway patrolman in a failing world. Origin story.
2. **Mad Max 2: The Road Warrior (1981)**
   - **Setting:** Post-apocalypse (approx. 3 years later).
   - **Context:** Global war and oil collapse. Max is now a scavenger.
3. **Mad Max Beyond Thunderdome (1985)**
   - **Setting:** Deep post-apocalypse (approx. 15 years after _Road Warrior_).
   - **Context:** Primitive tribal structures (Bartertown).
4. **Furiosa: A Mad Max Saga (2024)**
   - **Setting:** Pre-_Fury Road_ (covers 15–20 years).
   - **Context:** Direct prequel tracking Furiosa's rise from kidnapping to Imperator.
5. **Mad Max: Fury Road (2015)**
   - **Setting:** The furthest point in the timeline.
   - **Context:** Max entangled in Furiosa's mission to rescue Immortan Joe’s wives.

### System Architecture: Summary of Logic

| Feature | The Legend Model (Myth) |
|:--- |:--- |
| **Continuity** | Technology (phones/newer cars) shifts forward in time for newer films. |
| **Persistence** | Character details (like the Interceptor) "reset" between stories. |
| **Source of Truth** | The storyteller (survivors) determines the details of the legend. |
