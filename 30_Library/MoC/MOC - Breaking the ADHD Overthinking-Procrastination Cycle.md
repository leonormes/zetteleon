## MOC - Breaking the ADHD Overthinking-Procrastination Cycle

> Inclusion criteria: Atomic notes explaining the psychological drivers of the ADHD overthinking-procrastination cycle and the feedback-based strategies designed to break it.

### Introduction: From Analysis Paralysis to Actionable Progress

For many professionals with ADHD, the internal experience is like [[The ADHD Brain as a High-Performance Engine with a Faulty Transmission|a high-performance engine with a faulty transmission]]. A powerful intellect generates endless ideas, but impaired executive functions fail to translate that energy into action. The solution is not more willpower, but an external system of tangible feedback to provide the traction needed to convert intellectual energy into forward momentum.

---

### Part 1: The Vicious Cycle—Deconstructing the Psychology

The cycle of overthinking and procrastination is a predictable loop created by the core traits of ADHD.

- 1.1 Executive Dysfunction: [[Executive Function Challenges are Central to ADHD|Deficits in planning, prioritising, and initiating action]] lead to a cognitive gridlock. When faced with complexity, the brain defaults to [[ADHD Overthinking is a Substitute for Action|overthinking as a substitute for action]], creating a painful loop of overwhelm and avoidance that results in [[ADHD Paralysis is the Inability to Start a Task Until it Becomes an Emergency|ADHD paralysis]].
- 1.2 Working Memory Challenges: Significant deficits in the brain's mental "RAM"—see [[ADHD Working Memory Deficits Create a Compulsive Re-Planning Loop]]—lead to the "out of sight, out of mind" phenomenon. This fosters a lack of self-trust and results in an anxiety-fuelled, compulsive re-planning loop.
- 1.3 Dopamine-Seeking Behaviour: The brain's interest-based nervous system, driven by dopamine deficiency, gives rise to [[Tool tinkering is a form of productive procrastination|productive procrastination]]—engaging in useful but non-essential tasks that provide an immediate dopamine hit not available from the primary, long-term goal. See also [[Experiential Information Avoidance]] for the avoidance of feedback-generating action specifically.
- 1.4 Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD): To avoid the extreme emotional pain of perceived failure, many with [[Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD)|RSD develop a perfectionistic trap]], where compulsive over-planning is used as a shield against criticism. This quest for a flawless plan inevitably leads to paralysis.

---

### Part 2: The Antidote—Engineering Feedback for the ADHD Brain

The solution is to build an external system that provides the specific feedback the ADHD brain needs.

- 2.1 Harnessing Real-World Data: It is critical to distinguish between two information types. [[Type 1 Information is Theoretical and Addictive for Overthinkers|Type 1 Information (passive, theoretical)]] feels safe but fuels paralysis. Type 2 Information (active, empirical) feels risky but provides the immediate, real-world feedback necessary for progress. The core strategy is to intentionally shift from gathering Type 1 to generating Type 2 information.
- 2.2 The Power of Immediacy: [[Rapid Feedback Loops are Essential for ADHD Motivation|The ADHD brain requires rapid feedback loops]] to maintain engagement. Shrinking the "idea-to-feedback" loop using techniques like Test-Driven Development or interactive shells provides a continuous stream of small dopamine rewards.
- 2.3 Making Progress Visible: To bypass working memory deficits, it is essential that [[Externalizing Progress Makes it Tangible and Motivational|progress is externalised and made visible]]. Systems like Kanban boards or a "Done" list act as an external brain, providing a persistent and motivating record of accomplishment.
- 2.4 The Kaizen Engine: The Kaizen philosophy of using micro-victories functions as a dopamine feedback system. Breaking large tasks into the smallest conceivable steps provides a steady stream of low-stakes "wins" that build confidence and momentum.

### Part 3: Actionable Plan—Immediate Strategies

These techniques translate the principles into a practical, immediate toolkit.

- 3.1 The 5-Minute Action: Use [[The 5-Minute Action Overcomes Initiation Barriers|a timer to commit to just five minutes of work]] on an avoided task, lowering the perceived effort and bypassing emotional resistance.
- 3.2 Define a "Micro-Victory": Break a task down to its smallest verifiable unit of success (Kaizen method).
- 3.3 Externalise Everything: Use a 'brain dump' to offload all mental clutter onto a trusted external system, freeing up finite working memory.

> Brain Dumping Offloads Cognitive Load to an External System is not yet an atomic note.

- 3.4 Time-Box Research: Employ [[Time-Boxing Research Prevents Productive Procrastination|time-boxing to set firm boundaries on passive information gathering]], forcing a pivot from learning to doing.
- 3.5 Create a "Done" List: Keep a 'Done' list to create a factual, past-focused record of success, which directly combats the negative self-talk fuelled by RSD and poor working memory.
