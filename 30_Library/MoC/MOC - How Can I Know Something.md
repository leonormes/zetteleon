## Minimum Viable Understanding (MVU)

The guiding question, in your own words from the session that built this map: how can I know something, what does that even mean, and how can I build up logical arguments for my beliefs and opinions? This hub orders the vault's epistemology notes as a sequence of claims and counter-claims, so the progress of human understanding on the question can be read in order instead of rediscovered each time a new note is captured.

## 2. How the Question Moved

Each step fixed a problem left by the last and created a new one. This is the same shape as the book-based version in [[MOC - Theory and Reality (Godfrey-Smith)]], widened to the whole subject.

1. Reason or experience? Rationalists and empiricists disagree about the source ([[Classical Rationalism Grounds Knowledge in Reason, Not the Senses]], [[Classical Empiricism Treats Experience as the Sole Source of Knowledge]]).
2. Experience cannot guarantee the future. Hume's Fork and the problem of induction limit empiricism, and Kant adds a third category and a boundary ([[The Problem of Induction Means Past Regularity Cannot Logically Guarantee Future Regularity]], [[Kant's Synthetic A Priori Challenges Hume's Fork by Claiming a Third Knowledge Category]]).
3. What counts as knowing at all? The justified true belief analysis is attacked from three sides ([[The Traditional Definition of Knowledge is Justified True Belief]], [[Gettier Problems Challenge the Traditional Definition of Knowledge]]).
4. If certainty is out, what makes science special? Positivism gives way to falsification, then to history, then to doubt about any fixed method ([[Falsifiability Distinguishes Science from Dogma]], [[Kuhn's Four-Stage Model of Paradigm Collapse]], [[Feyerabend's Epistemological Anarchism Denies Any Fixed Scientific Method]]).
5. What is science aiming at? Prediction against explanation ([[Deutsch Rejects Instrumentalism Because Prediction Is a Means, Not the Purpose, of Science]]).
6. If scientists are biased, why trust it? The answer moves from individuals to structure ([[Science Is Trustworthy Because Its Structure Corrects for Bias, Not Because Scientists Are Less Biased]]).
7. How should one person hold beliefs given all this? As graded, revisable hypotheses with the burden on the claimant ([[Probabilistic Thinking Treats Beliefs as Hypotheses With Confidence Levels]], [[Hitchens Razor Places the Burden of Proof on the Claimant, Not the Sceptic]]).

## 5. How to File a New Capture

1. Find the question in section 1 that the note answers. If none fits, that is a sign a new hub is needed.
2. Write the note as a proposition and give it the right type, claim or concept.
3. Record any disagreement as `contradicts`, any evidence as `supports`, and any lineage as `extends` or `revises`, in the note itself.
4. Add one line to the matching hub's table or list, and one `synthesizes` edge if the note is a core claim there.
5. Run `edge_lint.py --path` on the note before leaving it.
