## MOC - Shame

> Core Question: How do we recognize, deconstruct, and move beyond the secondary shame of neurodivergence?

Shame is a deeply personal and painful emotion that stems from a negative self-evaluation. For the ADHD brain, shame is often not a primary emotion but a secondary pathology developed from years of "unexplained underachievement" and social friction. (See: [[SoT - Social Friction Coefficient]])

---

### 1. The Architecture of Shame (The "Roots")

The development of shame in ADHD is a constellation of reinforcing mechanisms:

- [[30_Library/100_zettelkasten/Misinterpretation as Character Flaws|Misinterpretation as Character Flaws]]: Executive dysfunction (lateness, forgetting) is wrongly labeled as "laziness" or "selfishness" by authority figures. (See: [[SoT - Authority-Competence Asymmetry]])
- [[30_Library/100_zettelkasten/Distorted Negative Self-Image|Distorted Negative Self-Image]]: The individual internalizes these labels, viewing themselves as fundamentally broken or "defective."
- [[30_Library/100_zettelkasten/Unexplained Underachievement|Unexplained Underachievement]]: The gap between potential and output reinforces the belief of inherent flaw.
- [[MOC - Emotional Dysregulation]]: The inability to self-soothe keeps the individual trapped in negative emotional loops.
- [[30_Library/100_zettelkasten/Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD)|Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD)]]: The intense biological pain of perceived rejection makes shame feel existentially threatening.

Healing requires moving from a "Deficit Model" (fixing flaws) to a "Strength-Based Model" (optimizing the engine).

- [[SoT - ADHD Self-Compassion & Strengths]]: The canonical source for reframing and recovery.
- [[SoT - ADHD Self-Compassion & Strengths#4. Protocols for Self-Compassion|Cultivating Self-Compassion for ADHD]]: Moving from self-judgment to understanding.
