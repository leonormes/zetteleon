## GCSE Mathematics 2028—Strategic Plan

Student: Year 9 → Year 11

Specification: Pearson Edexcel Level 1/2 GCSE (9–1) in Mathematics—code 1MA1

Tier: Foundation (grades 1–5 available)

First assessment: Summer 2028

Plan written: May 2026

---

### 1. Targets—The Grade Arithmetic

Foundation tier is marked out of 240 total across three papers. Recent Edexcel 1MA1 Foundation boundaries:

| Grade             | June 2024 | June 2025 | % equivalent |
| ----------------- | --------- | --------- | ------------ |
| 5 (strong pass)   | 175       | 175       | ~73%         |
| 4 (standard pass) | 142       | 144       | ~60%         |
| 3                 | 103       | 105       | ~43%         |
| 2                 | 65        | 67        | ~28%         |
| 1                 | 27        | 29        | ~12%         |

> Anchor: Grade 4 is the universally-recognised "pass" floor—colleges and sixth forms require it. Grade 5 ("strong pass") is the realistic ceiling on Foundation and the entry standard for most non-STEM A-levels.

The Grade 4 → 5 jump is ~33 marks (≈14% of the total). That is the single most useful target boundary to anchor the entire two years on.

### 2. The Pathway Implication

Foundation caps at grade 5. Consequences for post-16:

| Pathway | Typical requirement | Open on Foundation? |
|---|---|---|
| A-level Maths | Grade 6 or 7 | ❌ Closed |
| A-level Sciences (Physics/Chemistry/Biology) | Grade 6 | ❌ Closed |
| A-level Economics, Psychology, Geography | Grade 5 (sometimes 6) | ⚠️ Borderline |
| Humanities, languages, arts, social sciences | Grade 4–5 | ✅ Open |
| BTECs, T-Levels, apprenticeships | Grade 4 | ✅ Open |

Action: One conversation with her maths teacher: _"Is Foundation locked in, or could she move to Higher if she puts in the work?"_ Confirm the door is closed before optimising for Foundation. Then file this section away.

### 3. Scope—What's In, What's Out

#### Topic Weighting on Foundation

| Topic | Weighting | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Number | 22–28% | High ROI |
| Ratio, Proportion & Rates | 22–28% | High ROI |
| Algebra | 17–23% | Core mid-tier |
| Geometry & Measures | 12–18% | Lower priority |
| Statistics & Probability | 12–18% | Lower priority |

Number + Ratio ≈ half the paper. Percentage change, compound measures (speed, density, unit pricing), ratio-sharing, fractions, and basic algebra are where Foundation is won or lost.

#### Assessment Objective Weighting

- AO1 (routine procedures): 50%
- AO2 (reasoning): 25%
- AO3 (problem-solving): 25%

Half the marks are pure procedural fluency. Drilling has very high return at Foundation—much higher than at Higher tier.

#### Topics She Does NOT Need to Learn

The bold-type content in the spec is Higher-only and out of scope:

- Surds, algebraic fractions, completing the square, quadratic formula, iteration
- Sine/cosine rule, circle theorems, vector proofs
- Histograms with unequal class widths, conditional probability, Venn-diagram probability
- Exponential/trig graphs, equation of a circle, gradients of curves
- Function notation (inverse/composite)

Roughly a third of the Higher syllabus eliminated. Smaller scope means deeper practice on what remains.

### 4. Strategic Principles

#### Principle 1—School First, home Study as Force-multiplier

The single highest-ROI move is to mirror what school is teaching _right now_. Parallel curricula double cognitive load. Home study should be reactive to the school's sequencing.

#### Principle 2—Worked Examples before Productive Struggle

Cognitive Load Theory (Sweller): for novices, worked examples beat unguided struggle. Productive failure only outperforms once foundations are robust. Sequence is:

1. Study a worked example
2. Imitate with a near-identical problem
3. _Then_ attempt unfamiliar problems

#### Principle 3—Number + Ratio First, Everything Else Second

Half the marks live here. Time allocation should reflect that. Algebra is the third pillar; Geometry/Stats/Probability are mop-up.

#### Principle 4—Paper 1 Fluency is Non-negotiable

Paper 1 is non-calculator. Long multiplication, long division, fraction arithmetic, percentages of quantities, decimal arithmetic—all by hand, all automatic. Examiner reports flag this every year. Times tables to 12×12 must be 100% automatic.

#### Principle 5—Formula Sheet Provided, but Train as if it Isn't

A formula sheet is currently provided in the exam (DfE/Ofqual confirmed through 2027; likely to continue for 2028 but not guaranteed). Train her to know the formulae cold anyway. The sheet is a _speed tool_ under time pressure, not a memory crutch.

#### Principle 6—The Error Log is the Single Most Underused Tool

Every mistake she makes goes into one notebook: the question, what she did wrong, the correct method, and a re-attempt a week later. This becomes her personal revision curriculum.

### 5. The Five-Phase Plan

#### Phase 0—Diagnose (Now → End of June 2026)

Output: a topic-by-topic heat-map against the spec, scored Red/Amber/Green.

1. Download June 2024 Foundation papers (1F, 2F, 3F) and mark schemes from `qualifications.pearson.com`.
2. Sit Paper 1F under timed conditions (90 mins, no calculator).
3. Self-mark using the mark scheme.
4. Score each topic in the spec (pages 5–11) as Red (struggled), Amber (partial), or Green (confident).
5. Identify the 3 highest-priority Red topics in Number or Ratio.

#### Phase 1—Align (June → September 2026)

1. Confirm with the school: which scheme of work, which textbook, which platform (Sparx/MathsWatch/Hegarty/other) they use.
2. Get the Y10 curriculum map for September.
3. Pick one home-study platform (see §6).
4. Set up the error log (a physical A5 notebook works best).
5. Buy a Casio fx-83GTX scientific calculator—this is the standard at Foundation.

#### Phase 2—Co-pilot Y10 (September 2026 → July 2027)

- Weekly rhythm: Mirror the school's topic with one Corbettmaths "5-a-day" practice sheet (Foundation tier).
- Saturday morning: 30 minutes of mixed retrieval—random topics from earlier in the year via Maths Genie or Dr Frost.
- Every mistake: into the error log. Re-attempt a week later.
- End-of-half-term: 30-minute topic review of whatever school covered, against the spec.

#### Phase 3—Mock-driven Y11 Autumn (September → December 2027)

- Y11 mocks usually fall in November. They are the forcing function for synthesis.
- Every fortnight: one full past paper under timed conditions.
- After each paper: mark-scheme analysis—_what working did the examiner want, not just the answer._
- Heat-map refreshes monthly. Topics drifting from Green back to Amber get prioritised.

#### Phase 4—Spaced Revision (January → May 2028)

- Cycle through the error log on Anki or paper flashcards. Daily 15 minutes.
- Final 6 weeks: one past paper per week, minimum.
- Final fortnight: only Red and Amber topics, no new material.

### 6. Tools

#### Primary platform—pick ONE

| Tool | Strength | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Maths Genie | Explicit Foundation paths, grade-rated 1–5, videos + worksheets + answers | The default starting point |
| Corbettmaths | "5-a-day Foundation" daily mixed practice; clear 5-min topic videos | Daily retrieval habit |
| Dr Frost Maths | Free, vast question bank, filterable by Edexcel + Foundation + topic | Targeted weakness drilling |

> Two creates choice paralysis. Pick one as primary. Add a second only if she explicitly hits its limits.

#### Supporting Resources

- Past papers: 1MA1 has been examined every year since 2017. That's ~24 Foundation papers (8 years × 3) plus specimens and sample sets. A finite, plannable resource.
- MathsBot (`mathsbot.com`): exam-style question generator, useful for topic-targeted drilling.
- Pearson formula sheet: download the current Foundation Exam Aid PDF from `qualifications.pearson.com` so she practises with the actual document.

#### Avoid

- TikTok maths influencers. Algorithmic chaos, no spec alignment.
- Buying expensive revision guides before the diagnostic. Wait until you know what she actually needs.
- Switching platforms more than once a term.

---

### 7. The First Four Weeks—Tactical Focus

Before the formal plan kicks in, three things give the highest immediate return:

1. Paper 1 arithmetic fluency—long multiplication, long division, fraction operations, percentages of a quantity, decimal arithmetic. By hand. Every day, 10 minutes.
2. Times tables to 12×12—if not 100% automatic, this is the single highest-ROI fix possible.
3. Ratio basics—sharing into parts (e.g. £60 in ratio 3:2), scaling recipes, currency conversion.

Everything else waits for the diagnostic.
