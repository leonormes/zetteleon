## Reinforcing Feedback Loop

A reinforcing feedback loop (positive feedback) is an amplifying mechanism in which a change in a system stock feeds back to produce further change in the same direction, driving exponential growth or runaway collapse. ^def

### Scope & Conditions

Governs compounding phenomena such as interest, network effects, viral spread, and self-fulfilling beliefs. Unchecked reinforcing loops eventually hit physical or resource limits, where a balancing loop takes over.

### Evidence

Extracted from [[SoT - Systems Thinking]] (Meadows and Open University synthesis). No verbatim book quote has been verified for this seed note yet.

### Implications

- Small initial differences can compound into extreme divergence over time.
- Interrupting a destructive reinforcing loop requires either breaking the causal link or introducing a stronger counter-signal.
