## The Clarify Stage Is the Executive Decision-Making Bridge Between Stuff and Action

The Clarify stage—the second of GTD's five stages (Capture, Clarify, Organise, Reflect, Engage)—is the critical executive decision-making bridge that transforms a raw, undifferentiated inventory of "stuff" into actionable projects and usable information. Clarifying is not execution: it is the act of assigning meaning and categorical fate to each captured item. Without this stage, the gap between inbox and action remains full of ambiguity that drains cognitive energy and produces paralysis.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to every item in a capture container before it can enter the organisational system. The stage must be treated as a discrete, dedicated act—not merged with either the Capture phase that precedes it or the Organise phase that follows. Its value depends on regularity and completeness.

### Evidence

> "the 'Clarify' (or processing) stage functions as the critical executive decision-making bridge… where you transform that vague inventory into actionable projects and usable information."

### Implications

- Clarifying is about deciding meaning, not immediately executing the work; treating the two as the same is the root failure of most to-do list systems.
- It serves as a mandatory filter between raw input and the organisational system—nothing enters Organise without first passing through Clarify.
