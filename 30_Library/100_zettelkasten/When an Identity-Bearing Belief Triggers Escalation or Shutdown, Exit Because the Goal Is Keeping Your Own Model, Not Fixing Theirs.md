## When an Identity-Bearing Belief Triggers Escalation or Shutdown, Exit Because the Goal Is Keeping Your Own Model, Not Fixing Theirs

When a belief is load-bearing for a person's identity and they escalate or shut down under critique, the critic should exit and not press on. The goal is to keep one's own model intact, not to repair theirs. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies only when two things hold: the belief carries the person's identity, and the person escalates or shuts down. It does not apply to a calm disagreement. The exit can be courteous and need not concede the point, for example "I look at the data differently, but I understand why you value that approach." Exiting does not mean the critique was wrong, and it may be read as withdrawal.

### Evidence

The core is the author's own reasoning, first worded in an AI conversation (Gemini, 2026-07-13) captured in a HEAD note no longer found under that name, so that origin is unverified. No source in the library tests when to stop. The claim rests on [[A Critique of a Belief That Is Fused to Identity Is Received as an Attack on the Person]]: when each further point is received as an attack, continuing cannot improve the discussion.

### Implications

- Decide the exit condition before the conversation: an identity-bearing belief plus escalation or shutdown.
- The aim is limited. You are keeping your own view and your composure, and not winning or changing theirs.
- Exiting leaves the question open and does not end the relationship.

### Falsifier

- Evidence that people change an identity-bearing belief more often when the critic keeps pressing through escalation or shutdown than when the critic exits.
