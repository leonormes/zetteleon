## Predictive Power Verifies a Theory via Its Outputs, Not Its Proofs

A scientific theory can be verified by observing that the technologies built on it function reliably—GPS accounting for general relativity, semiconductors relying on quantum mechanics—without the observer needing to understand or personally verify the underlying mathematics.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to theories with engineered, real-world applications. It establishes functional reliability, not metaphysical truth—a theory can work for practical purposes while later being superseded by a more accurate one (see [[Newtonian Physics as an Effective Theory]]).

### Evidence

> "You don't have to understand the underlying mathematics of a theory to verify that it works; you just have to look at the outputs… if the engineers who built the GPS network didn't account for [general relativity], your navigation apps would drift by miles every day."

### Implications

- Non-experts can rationally trust a theory without following its proofs.
- The flat-earth model fails this test specifically because it predicts nothing and builds nothing that works—it cannot forecast a solar eclipse, let alone launch a satellite.
