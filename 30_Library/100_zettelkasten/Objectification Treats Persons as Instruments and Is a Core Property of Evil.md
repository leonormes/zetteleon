## Objectification Treats Persons as Instruments and Is a Core Property of Evil

In the context of evil, objectification involves treating persons purely instrumentally—as objects, means, or category-members—while disregarding their subjective experience, agency, and inherent worth. This is not merely a psychological attitude of the perpetrator; it has observable manifestations in how agency is exercised: persons are rendered disposable, interchangeable, or transparent to the perpetrator's purposes.

### Scope & Conditions

Identifiable through the observable reduction of a person to their utility or categorical membership (their race, role, or vulnerability class). The analytical focus is on the structure of the act, not the internal state of the perpetrator—objectification can occur without conscious awareness or malicious intent.

### Evidence

> "analytical standpoint, evil involves treating persons as objects, instruments, or means rather than as beings with inherent worth."

### Implications

- Dignity violation has observable, analysable manifestations—objectification is not merely a subjective experience of the victim but a describable structural property of the perpetrator's actions.
- Treating individuals as disposable is a core, recurring descriptor of evil that appears across philosophical frameworks from Kant to Card.
