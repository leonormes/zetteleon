## Put-Away Dependency Cascade

A put-away dependency cascade is the chain reaction in which returning one item to its home forces you to move other items, then to open or restructure the storage behind them, until a small task has become an overhaul. ^def

### Scope & Conditions

Applies in homes where storage is full or fiddly, and most strongly when executive capacity is low, because each extra obstacle is a fresh decision and a fresh physical step.

### Evidence

> "To break this **dependency cascade**—where putting away a single item threatens to spiral into a multi-hour overhaul—the domestic architecture must structurally decouple **daily putting away** from **deep storage reorganisation**."
> (AI chat summary pasted 2026-11-24, not checked against a primary source)

The chat credits the 5 Things Tidying Method and Cassandra Aarssen (Clutterbug) for related ideas. I have not checked either, so confidence is low.

### Implications

- The first link in the chain is the place to cut it: do not open the crowded cupboard.
- Naming the pattern lets the family say "that is a cascade" instead of arguing about effort.
