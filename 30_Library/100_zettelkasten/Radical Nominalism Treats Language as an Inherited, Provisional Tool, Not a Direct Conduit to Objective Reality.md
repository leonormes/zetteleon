### Radical Nominalism Treats Language as an Inherited, Provisional Tool, Not a Direct Conduit to Objective Reality

Radical nominalism (as argued by John Gray) holds that language and conceptual frameworks are not direct conduits to an absolute, objective reality; they are inherited, provisional tools — likened to a spider's web — that serve functional purposes but should not be reified as universal truths.

#### Scope & Conditions

A position in philosophy of language distinct from realism about universals or concepts; the "spider's web" metaphor emphasises that the framework is spun by and useful to the organism using it, not a neutral window onto the world.

#### Evidence

> "Radical Nominalism: Language and conceptual frameworks are not direct conduits to an absolute, objective reality. Instead, they are inherited, provisional tools (likened to a spider's web) that serve functional purposes but should not be reified as universal truths."

#### Implications

- Treating a conceptual framework as if it directly mirrors reality (rather than as a useful, revisable tool) is the specific move this position warns against.
