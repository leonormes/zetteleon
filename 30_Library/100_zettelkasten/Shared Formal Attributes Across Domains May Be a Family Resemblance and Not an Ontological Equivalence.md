## Shared Formal Attributes Across Domains May Be a Family Resemblance and Not an Ontological Equivalence

The concept-lattice analysis shows that several domains share the structural-truth and complexity-reduction attributes at the same level, which is a fact about the lattice. Whether that reflects a genuine equivalence between the domains or a useful family resemblance is a separate question the analysis does not settle. The SoT's statement that the isomorphism "is not an analogy" therefore goes further than the lattice alone shows.

### Scope & Conditions

- This challenges the strength of the cross-domain claim and not its usefulness. A family resemblance can still guide design.
- The attributes in the lattice were synthesised from topic description fields. Shared attributes can therefore reflect how the descriptions were written, which is a weaker basis than the SoT's phrase "mathematically derived" suggests.
- The limit is the SoT's own first tension, so this note records a doubt the SoT already holds.

### Evidence

> "The FCA analysis establishes that DS, CI, DC, AM, and PK share M4 and M10 at C4. This is a structural fact about the concept lattice. Whether the lattice is capturing a genuine ontological equivalence or a useful but ultimately domain-specific family resemblance is a deeper question."
> ([[SoT - Structure is Truth is a Unifying Axiom Across Formal Systems]], §7 Tensions & Gaps, T1, emphasis markers omitted)

> "To scale the context, 10 higher-order meta-attributes were synthesised from the topic description fields (not keywords). Each attribute is present in at least two topics."
> ([[SoT - Formal Context (Applied Formal Methods)]], §3 Phase 2, emphasis markers omitted)

### Implications

- Treat cross-domain transfer as a source of hypotheses to test in each domain, and use the tests in [[How to Test Whether a Structure Is Constitutive or Merely Representational]] domain by domain.
- A stronger demonstration would give an explicit structure-preserving mapping between two domains and not a shared attribute label.
