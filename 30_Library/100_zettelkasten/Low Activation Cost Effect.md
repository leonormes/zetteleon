## Low Activation Cost Effect

Reducing the activation cost of digital tasks (e.g., sending an email or Slack message) leads to a higher frequency of incoming requests and increased context switching. Paradoxically, as the effort required to initiate a task drops, the total volume of task arrivals increases, resulting in greater cognitive exhaustion and less uninterrupted time for deep work.

### Scope & Conditions

Commonly observed in modern digital communication environments and AI-assisted task workflows.

### Evidence

> "Digital tools (Email, Slack, AI) lower the 'activation cost' of tasks. Paradoxically, reducing friction does not result in more free time; it results in a higher frequency of task arrivals."

### Implications

- Lowering internal and external friction can lead to a state of chronic overwhelm rather than increased efficiency.
- Focused, uninterrupted work time becomes a scarcer and more valuable resource as the cost of interruption decreases.
