## Need for Cognition, Need for Cognitive Closure, and Intolerance of Uncertainty Are Related but Distinct Constructs

Need for cognition (enjoyment of sustained, effortful thinking), need for cognitive closure (wanting a firm answer and disliking ambiguity), and intolerance of uncertainty (distress specifically about unclear future outcomes or risks) overlap but are not identical—a person can be high on one without being equally high on the others.

### Scope & Conditions

A four-to-six-construct comparison in the source also includes competence need, contingent self-worth, and rejection sensitivity as neighbouring but distinct constructs.

### Evidence

> "Need for cognitive closure is typically defined as wanting a firm answer and disliking ambiguity. Intolerance of uncertainty is more specifically about difficulty bearing an uncertain future or possible negative outcomes. They overlap but are not identical."

### Implications

- "I need a firm answer" (closure) and "I can't tolerate not knowing what might go wrong" (IU) are different complaints and may call for different responses even when they co-occur.
