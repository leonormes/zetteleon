## Shared Parental Agreement Must Precede Unilateral Routine Enforcement

Enforcing a household routine rule unilaterally before both parents have agreed to it creates a visible tug-of-war in front of children and undermines the routine. Resolving differences privately in the "back-end" turns enforcement from personal policing into a shared, neutral system boundary. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to any shared household routine, chore check-in, or privilege boundary affecting both parents and children—especially when one partner carries disproportionate physical, mental, or emotional labour, or partners hold differing Process-versus-Immediacy priorities.

### Evidence

> "Switching phones off unilaterally while she switches them back on is a tug of war in front of the children. Agreement has to come before enforcement."
> ([[Household Routines Proposal]], §4)

### Implications

- Audit who carries existing physical, mental, and emotional load (whole-family ownership) and recognise Process-versus-Immediacy differences before proposing new routine demands.
- Resolve disagreements privately in the "back-end" so children meet a unified "front-end" without mixed signals or parental splitting.
- Establish joint parental consent before activating impersonal structural defaults (device-level limits or neutral `Work = Access` gates), testing changes as small, reversible joint trials ([[Household Routine Changes Should Be Small Joint and Reversible Experiments]]).
