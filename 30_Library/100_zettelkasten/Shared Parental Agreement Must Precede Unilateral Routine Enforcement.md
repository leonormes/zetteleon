## Shared Parental Agreement Must Precede Unilateral Routine Enforcement

Enforcing a household routine rule unilaterally before both parents have agreed to it creates a visible tug-of-war in front of children and undermines the routine.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to any shared household rule affecting both parents and children, especially when one partner already carries disproportionate bedtime or household labour.

### Evidence

> "Switching phones off unilaterally while she switches them back on is a tug of war in front of the children. Agreement has to come before enforcement."
> ([[Household Routines Proposal]], §4)

### Implications

- Check who carries the existing physical and mental load before proposing new routine demands.
- Joint consent is a prerequisite for any device-level or structural enforcement.
