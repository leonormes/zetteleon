## Moral Silencing Suppresses Conscience and Enables Evil

A distinctive psychological mechanism enabling evil—proposed by Eve Garrard—is the systematic suppression or elimination of moral responsiveness. The perpetrator psychologically silences the considerations that should function as absolute barriers to action: empathy, recognition of the victim's humanity, or basic ethical constraints. This silencing can operate through apathy, deliberate empathy suppression, or ideological restructuring that renders victims "conceptually invisible."

### Scope & Conditions

Confidence is medium—this is a philosophical proposal rather than an empirically validated psychological mechanism. The claim is analytical: identifying moral silencing as a descriptive property that distinguishes evil from ordinary wrongdoing. Manifests differently across cases: in some, active suppression; in others, ideological substitution (reframing victims as non-persons).

### Evidence

> "the perpetrator psychologically silences considerations that should function as absolute barriers to action… a systematic suppression or elimination of moral responsiveness."

### Implications

- Evil involves an active or systematic bypass of conscience—not merely a failure to act morally but a structural disabling of the moral feedback mechanism.
- Victims are often rendered "conceptually invisible" through ideological or bureaucratic reframing—a process that precedes and enables the harmful act.
