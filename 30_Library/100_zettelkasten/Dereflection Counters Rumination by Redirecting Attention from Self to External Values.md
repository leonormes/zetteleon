## Dereflection Counters Rumination by Redirecting Attention from Self to External Values

Dereflection is Frankl's logotherapeutic technique for breaking self-focused anxiety and hyper-reflection loops. Rather than trying to suppress anxious thoughts or analyse them further — both of which intensify the self-focus — the technique redirects attention outward toward an external goal, a person who needs help, or a value the individual is committed to. The loop is interrupted not by fighting it from within but by changing the direction of attention entirely.

### Scope & Conditions

Effective specifically for self-focused anxiety and hyper-reflection ("performance anxiety," "am I doing this right?," anxious self-monitoring). Less applicable to anxiety with a clear external cause that requires practical problem-solving. Works best when attention *on* the self is the mechanism sustaining the distress, not merely accompanying it.

### Evidence

> "Frankl's dereflection technique helps counter the rumination and self-focused anxiety… Instead of obsessing over problems, you redirect attention toward external goals and values."

### Implications

- Uses external focus to bypass internal feedback loops of anxiety; the relief comes from genuine re-direction, not suppression.
- Particularly effective for ADHD hyperfocus directed inward — redirecting toward values provides both an interrupt and a replacement engagement.
