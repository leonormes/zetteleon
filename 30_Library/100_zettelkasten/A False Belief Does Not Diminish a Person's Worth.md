## A False Belief Does Not Diminish a Person's Worth

Summary: Holding a false belief makes a person mistaken about that belief, not wrong as a human being—humanity is non-negotiable and holds constant worth even when the opinion on the table is worthless.

Details: In the axiomatic framework, one incorrect premise flags the entire person as compromised, and dismissal follows. Separating person from proposition establishes the critical boundary: evaluate ideas with full severity, hold worth constant. This is also cognitively cheaper—being the arbiter of who is right or wrong _as a human being_ requires constant defence and categorisation, whereas evaluating claims alone drops the friction dramatically. Regret about past dismissiveness is functional telemetry: you can only see the old system's rigidity from outside it.
