## The RISA Framework Pre-Screens a Disagreement for Being Real, Important, Specific, and Aligned Before Engaging

Before entering a disagreement, check it against four questions—Real (is this genuine or based on a misunderstanding?), Important (does it justify the time and effort?), Specific (is it concrete enough to make progress on?), and Aligned (do participants share a broadly compatible goal for the discussion?)—to avoid spending effort on disagreements that are unreal, unimportant, vague, or working at cross purposes.

### Scope & Conditions

Trigger—about to enter or already stuck in a disagreement. Verification—a "no" on Real or Specific suggests clarifying the actual disagreement first; a "no" on Important suggests disengaging; a "no" on Aligned suggests naming the mismatch in goals before continuing, since no amount of evidence resolves a disagreement where the parties want different things from the conversation.

### Evidence

> "Real: Is the disagreement genuine, or is it based on a misunderstanding? Important: Is the issue significant enough to justify the time and effort? Specific: Is the disagreement sufficiently clear and concrete to make progress? Aligned: Do the participants share a broadly compatible goal for the discussion?"

### Implications

- Alignment is the hardest of the four to assess honestly, since people frequently conceal (even from themselves) that they want validation or victory rather than resolution.
