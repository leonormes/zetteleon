## ADHD Impairs Execution at the Point of Performance Rather Than Knowledge

ADHD is not a deficit of knowing what to do, but a disorder of self-regulation and executive execution at the point of performance—failing to initiate or sustain action in the moment it matters. Because prospective memory (remembering a task) and activation (crossing the initiation threshold) are distinct bottlenecks, a reminder can fire in full view while the person remains unable to start.

### Scope & Conditions

Grounded in Russell Barkley's self-regulation model, Thomas Brown's six executive clusters (Activation, Focus, Effort, Emotion, Memory, Action), and Miyake and Friedman's executive-function taxonomy. Applies across work, household, and self-care tasks in adult ADHD.

### Evidence

This claim synthesises §1.1 of [[SoT - Prosthetic Executive Function]] alongside [[Executive Function Challenges are Central to ADHD]], [[Clinical Models Recognize Activation as a Core Impairment in ADHD]], and [[ADHD Causes Task Initiation Deficits Due to Dopamine Hyposensitivity]].

### Implications

- Educational or knowledge-based interventions fail when the bottleneck is real-time execution rather than ignorance.
- Effective scaffolds must bridge the activation gap at the physical time and place of performance rather than merely delivering more reminders.
