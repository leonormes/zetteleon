## Conceptual Integration Networks Have Four Typologies Distinguished by Shared Framing

Fauconnier and Turner classify conceptual integration networks into four types: Simplex (one input supplies an organising frame, the other supplies values to fill it), Mirror (both inputs share the same organising frame, enabling direct comparison), Single-scope (inputs have different frames but only one is projected into the blend), and Double-scope (inputs have different frames and the blend incorporates parts of each, yielding a genuinely novel emergent structure).

### Scope & Conditions

Double-scope blending is the only type that produces a truly novel, interdisciplinary emergent structure rather than a comparison or a framework application.

### Evidence

> "Double-scope: Both input spaces have different organizing frames, and the blended space incorporates parts of each, yielding a highly novel, emergent structure."

### Implications

- When authoring a bridge note between two domains, naming which of the four types is being attempted clarifies whether the goal is comparison (Mirror), framework transfer (Single-scope), or genuine new synthesis (Double-scope).
