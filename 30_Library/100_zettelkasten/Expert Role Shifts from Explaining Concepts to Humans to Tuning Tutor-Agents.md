## Expert Role Shifts from Explaining Concepts to Humans to Tuning Tutor-Agents

The role of the human expert is shifting from directly explaining concepts to learners to distilling knowledge into agents that then serve as personalised, infinitely patient tutors. The new high-value skill is not the ability to teach but the ability to encode a domain into its minimal, computable essence—the kind of compression that produces a 200-line implementation capable of teaching a complex system.

### Scope & Conditions

Requires the expert to be capable of reducing a complex domain to its irreducible structure—a prerequisite that is itself a form of mastery. Confidence is medium—this is a directional claim about role evolution, not an observed shift. Does not imply direct human explanation becomes worthless; it becomes more valuable as a quality signal for tutor-agent calibration.

### Evidence

> "The role of the human expert is shifting from explaining concepts to humans to explaining them to agents… the agent can then serve as a personalised, infinitely patient tutor for any number of human learners."

### Implications

- Knowledge distillation (reducing a domain to minimal, teachable form) becomes a primary high-value skill as agents take over the delivery layer of education.
- Content creation shifts from "write a course" (optimised for human reading) to "tune a tutor-agent" (optimised for machine-mediated adaptive delivery).
