## Developing an Idea Differs From Presenting a New One

Three tests distinguish _developing_ an idea from _presenting a new one_, applied to a piece of writing that reworks an existing argument:

1. Shifted focus—has the argument moved from the _evidence_ for a thesis to the _nature_ of the thing the thesis is about, without changing the thesis itself?
2. Broadened scope—does it extend the same claim to cases the original didn't cover (e.g. a human-only argument extended to non-human cases)?
3. Added dimension—does it introduce a new axis (e.g. a developmental or causal account) the original was silent on, without contradicting it?

If one or more of these hold and the core thesis is unchanged, the result is a sophisticated paraphrase: the underlying claim still passes the paraphrase-invariance test in [[Paraphrasing Demonstrates the Independence of Meaning from Language]]—it survives being re-expressed with new focus, scope, or dimension. It only counts as a genuinely _new_ idea if the core thesis itself changes, not merely its evidence, scope, or framing.
