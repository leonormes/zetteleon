## The Iceberg Model

The Iceberg Model is a systems thinking framework that layers reality into four levels of depth: visible events (react), recurring patterns over time (anticipate), underlying system structures (design), and foundational mental models or beliefs (transform). Deeper layers are less immediately visible than surface events but hold far greater leverage over long-term system behavior. ^def

### Scope & Conditions

Used as a diagnostic and sense-making heuristic across organizational, social, and personal problem-solving. It does not specify quantitative causal equations on its own, but directs attention away from reactive event-level fixes toward structural and paradigmatic causes.

Extracted from [[SoT - Systems Thinking]] (Minimum Viable Understanding section); no verbatim book quote is attached yet.

### Implications

- Effective diagnosis moves downward from isolated incidents to trend patterns, structural incentives, and shared assumptions.
- Interventions at the structure and mental-model layers prevent entire classes of recurring events rather than patching one incident at a time.
