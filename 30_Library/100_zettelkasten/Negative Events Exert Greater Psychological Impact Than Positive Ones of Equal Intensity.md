## Negative Events Exert Greater Psychological Impact Than Positive Ones of Equal Intensity

Negative events, information, and experiences exert a greater psychological impact and capture more attention than neutral or positive ones of equal intensity. Evolutionary accounts attribute this asymmetry to ancestral selection pressures where missing a threat (predator, danger, social rejection) carried a higher survival cost than missing an opportunity. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- **Applies:** Across attention, memory consolidation, and social-threat appraisal when comparing negative vs positive stimuli of equal objective magnitude.
- **Weakens or reverses:**
  - **In-group motivated reasoning:** Partisan or identity-protective motivation can suppress negativity bias toward in-group falsehoods ([[Polarization Suppresses Negativity Bias Toward In-Group Lies]]).
  - **Positivity / fading-affect effects:** Over longer autobiographical intervals, positive memories can decay more slowly than negative ones in non-clinical populations.
