## Evidence - Ahrens on Luhmann Overview Notes Reached From the Index as Entry Points Into a Topic

> These are notes directly referred to from the index and usually used as an entry point into a topic that has already developed to such a degree that an overview is needed or at least becomes helpful.

The same passage says such a note can collect links to other relevant notes with a short indication of what to find on each, that these notes "help orientate oneself within the slip-box", and that Luhmann collected up to 25 links on them. They do not have to be written in one go, since links can be added over time.

Source: Sönke Ahrens, _How to Take Smart Notes_.

### What it Supports

The answer in [[Finding Thoughts]]: you return to a line of thought through the index, land on an overview note, and follow its links, adding new links there as the thinking develops.

### What it Does not Show

This is one author's account of one practitioner's method. It supports the structure (index, overview note, links), not the note's stronger claim that linking is the _simplest_ mechanism or that it works reliably for an ADHD reader.
