## Knowledge Requires Justification Because a True Belief Reached by Guesswork Is Not Knowledge

A true belief reached by guesswork is not knowledge, however strongly it is held, because the believer had no adequate basis for it. Knowledge therefore requires that the believer is entitled to the belief, which philosophers call justification. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to propositional knowledge. It says that some adequate basis is needed. It does not say what the basis is, and that is the most disputed part of the whole analysis:

- Internalists hold that the knower must be able to reach the basis from the inside ([[Internalism Requires Conscious First-Person Access to Justification]]).
- Externalists hold that a reliable process can supply it without the knower seeing it ([[Goldman Reliabilism Grounds Knowledge in a Reliable Belief-Forming Mechanism]]).
- Some philosophers dispute that justification is required at all and replace it with another condition.

### Evidence

One book gives the reasoning in plain terms:

> "A belief that’s just a hunch or a guess doesn’t carry with it a great likelihood of being true, no matter how strongly it’s held."
> (Martin 2014, ch. 3, PDF p. 33) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/298/EPUB?open_at=toc:3%20Justification%20and%20Gettier%20problems)

The same passage adds that such a belief might be true, but if so it would not be called knowledge, because the believer lacked an adequate basis. That is a point about ordinary cases of luck, and it does not settle what the basis must be or whether the basis alone is enough.

### Implications

- The requirement separates knowing from guessing correctly, so a quiz answer that is right by luck is not knowledge.
- The requirement alone does not close the gap, because a belief can be justified, true and still right by luck. That is the point [[Gettier Problems Challenge the Traditional Definition of Knowledge]] presses.

### Falsifier

- A clear case in which a person knows a proposition on no basis at all, such as a guess that happens to be right and that most people would still call knowledge.
