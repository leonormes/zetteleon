## Thoughts Are Bundled with Phenomenological Qualities

When thoughts occur in consciousness, they are not bare propositions. They arrive bundled with rich phenomenological qualities—emotional resonance, a felt sense of significance or discovery, and live associative networks—that exist independently of the semantic content itself.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to first-person, real-time conscious thinking prior to externalisation. Once a thought is written down and revisited later, the accompanying affective state has usually subsided, exposing the bare proposition.

### Evidence

This claim is derived from phenomenological observation and synthesis across the pre-linguistic cognition and metacognitive calibration notes in the vault rather than a single external quotation.

### Implications

- Internal thoughts can feel profound or groundbreaking even when their underlying propositional content is ordinary or flawed.
- Evaluating an idea solely by how compelling it feels in the moment conflates the experience of thinking with the quality of the thought.
- Externalising thoughts in writing separates the felt experience from the claim so each can be judged on its own terms.
