## Everyday Stressors Like Poor Sleep and Loneliness Can Impair Executive Function Without a Diagnosis

Summary: Ordinary lifestyle and psychosocial factors are sufficient on their own to degrade executive functioning, independent of any diagnosis.

Details: Executive dysfunction is usually discussed as a symptom of conditions such as ADHD, autism, or dementia, but clinical guidance also identifies everyday factors as capable of impairing executive function in anyone: chronic stress, social loneliness, poor sleep, and lack of physical exercise. Executive function is therefore not solely determined by diagnostic status—it fluctuates with everyday physical and emotional state in the general population.
