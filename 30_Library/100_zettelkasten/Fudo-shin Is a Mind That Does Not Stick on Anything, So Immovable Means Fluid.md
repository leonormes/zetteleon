## Fudo-shin Is a Mind That Does Not Stick on Anything, So Immovable Means Fluid

Fudo-shin, the immovable mind, is often mistaken for rigidity. It names a mind that does not stop or stick on anything, whether fear, surprise or a particular technique, so being immovable means being endlessly fluid. A mind that stops on anger is trapped, and one that flows freely cannot be pinned down.

### Scope & Conditions

- The idea of a mind that does not stop on any one object is most closely associated with the Zen monk Takuan Soho and his letters to the swordsman Yagyu Munenori, a contemporary of Musashi. The SoT places it in Musashi's emotional philosophy. Which of them the vault should credit has not been checked.
- It is about attention and affect not getting fixed. It does not mean ignoring danger or never committing to a course of action.
- The existing note [[Fudo The Path of Immovability]] describes the state as "unshakable composure". That wording leans toward the rigid reading this claim corrects.

### Evidence

> "Often mistaken for rigidity, Fudo-shin actually means a mind that does not "stop" or "stick" on anything (fear, surprise, or specific techniques)."
> ([[SoT - Miyamoto Musashi's Philosophy on Mastering Emotions]], §2B Fudo-shin)

> "To be "immovable" means to be endlessly fluid. If your mind stops on anger, you are trapped. If it flows freely, you cannot be pinned down."
> (same section)

### Implications

- The failure it guards against is sticking, which in daily life looks like rumination, where a thought or a slight is replayed and the mind cannot move on. [[The DMN as a Demon - Negative Rumination in ADHD]] describes that pattern in ADHD.
- The alternative to a stuck mind is not a calm one. It is a mind in motion that gives every stimulus its moment and then moves on, which is close to the skill of [[Dereflection Counters Rumination by Redirecting Attention from Self to External Values]].
