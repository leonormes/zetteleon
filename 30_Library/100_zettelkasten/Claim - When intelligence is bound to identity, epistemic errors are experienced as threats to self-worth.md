## Minimum Viable Understanding

When "being intelligent" becomes a core component of a person's identity (Contingent Self-Worth), every intellectual insight carries double freight: the idea itself, and evidence for the verdict "I am intelligent." Consequently, being wrong is no longer just a gap in knowledge; it is an indictment of the self.

## Mechanisms

- [[Sociometer Theory Treats Self-Esteem as a Monitor of Perceived Social Acceptance|Sociometer Theory]]: Self-esteem functions as a gauge of perceived social value. Craving recognition for deep insights is the gauge seeking confirmation of social worth. [depends_on:: [[Sociometer Theory Treats Self-Esteem as a Monitor of Perceived Social Acceptance]], strength=4, confidence=high]
- Dweck's Mindset: This vulnerability is common in individuals praised early for _being smart_ rather than for their _effort_ (fixed mindset vs. growth mindset).
- Amplification by [[Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD)]]: A nervous system primed to scan for rejection will seize upon intellectual validation as profound relief. Recognition lands like a drug, making the individual highly defensive against perceived intellectual failure.

## Antidotes

One practical countermeasure is to deliberately universalize the insight: appending the thought "and this is also true of most people." This deflates the identity-verdict, allowing the idea to survive or die on its own merits without threatening the self.
