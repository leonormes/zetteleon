## Feyerabend's Epistemological Anarchism Denies Any Fixed Scientific Method

Feyerabend argued that there is no such thing as a fixed scientific method—his doctrine was "anything goes"—and that strict methodological rules hinder scientific progress.

### Scope & Conditions

The most radical position in the historical/anarchistic cluster, going further than Kuhn or Lakatos by denying that any stable method or structure governs science at all.

### Evidence

> "Feyerabend argued that there is no such thing as a fixed scientific method… His doctrine was 'anything goes.'… He believed strict methodological rules hinder scientific progress."

### Implications

- Positions Feyerabend as the terminus of the historical-method critique that began with Kuhn's incommensurability.
