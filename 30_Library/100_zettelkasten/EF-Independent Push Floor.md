## EF-Independent Push Floor

An EF-independent push floor (Tier 1 prosthetic floor) is an external scaffold whose activation energy is supplied by the physical environment, automation, or another human rather than the user's own executive function. It stands in sharp contrast to a Tier 2 point-of-performance aid (pull tool), which waits passively for the user to remember, open, maintain, and obey it.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to compensating for adult ADHD executive dysfunction across activation, focus, memory, and error monitoring. Pull tools remain useful for working-memory offloading and micro-step decomposition *after* engagement has already begun, but cannot serve as the primary floor.

### Evidence

Defined in §2 of [[SoT - Prosthetic Executive Function]], contrasting Tier 1 push mechanisms (direct debits with failure alerts, physical path cues, scheduled human body-doubling) against Tier 2 pull tools (task managers, notes vaults, visual timers).

### Implications

- Classify every workflow intervention by who supplies the activation energy before relying on it for critical obligations.
- Pair automated push defaults with visible external failure alarms so silent input failures (such as expired cards) cannot go unnoticed.
