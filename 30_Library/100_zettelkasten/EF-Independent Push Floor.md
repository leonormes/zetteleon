## EF-Independent Push Floor

An EF-independent push floor (Tier 1 prosthetic floor) is an external scaffold whose activation energy comes from the physical environment, automation, or another human rather than the executive function of the user. It contrasts with a Tier 2 point-of-performance aid (pull tool), which waits passively for the user to remember, open, maintain, and obey it. ^def

### Scope & Conditions

Applies when designing or selecting external scaffolding for adult ADHD across activation, memory, focus, and error monitoring. Tier 2 pull tools remain useful for working-memory offloading and step decomposition once engagement has begun, but cannot act as the primary floor when initiation or prospective memory fails.

### Evidence

Synthesised from existing vault notes: §2 and §4 of [[SoT - Prosthetic Executive Function]], which contrast Tier 1 push mechanisms (automated defaults with visible failure alarms, physical path cues, and scheduled human accountability) against Tier 2 pull tools (task managers and uncoached apps), grounded by [[External Scaffolds Fail Through Circularity When They Require Executive Function to Operate]] and [[Human Accountability Outperforms Solo Digital Tools for ADHD Task Activation]]. No direct source quotation is used here.

### Implications

- Classify every proposed workflow or tool by whether activation energy lives outside the user before relying on it for critical obligations.
- Pair automated defaults with visible external failure alarms so silent upstream breaks are surfaced automatically.
