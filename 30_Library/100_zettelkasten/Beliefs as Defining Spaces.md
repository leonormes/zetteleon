## Beliefs as Defining Spaces

A belief is a room you build, not a fact you hold. [[The Map is Not the Territory|Beliefs, like all mental models, are maps]] [depends_on:: [[The Map is Not the Territory]], confidence=high]—so the question worth asking is not "is this belief objectively true?" but "does this belief expand or contract my space of possible action?"

### Scope & Conditions

Applies one level down from where pragmatism judges truth claims generally: not to external factual claims, but to the beliefs a mind holds about itself and its own capacities. [implements:: [[Pragmatic Truth Focuses on Utility Over Absolute Correctness]], confidence=high]

### Evidence

Own reasoning, illustrated with two worked examples of the same mechanism:

- A Limiting belief ("I am lazy") builds a room with no windows: the brain stops searching for solutions outside it, and the space of available responses collapses toward zero.
- An Empowering belief ("I have an Interest-Based Nervous System") builds a hallway with many doors: the brain searches for solutions compatible with the new frame, and the space of workable strategies expands.

### Implications

- Held onto too literally, a belief stops being a tool and becomes the territory itself—the map gets mistaken for the ground. [depends_on:: [[Mistaking the Map for the Territory]], confidence=medium]
- A flawed model becomes a hidden ceiling on what a person believes is possible. [supports:: [[Flawed Mental Models Limit Mastery]], confidence=medium]
