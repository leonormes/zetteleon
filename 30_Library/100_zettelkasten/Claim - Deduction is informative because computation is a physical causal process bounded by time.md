## Minimum Viable Understanding

The "Scandal of Deduction" asks: if the conclusion of a valid deductive argument is already fully contained within its premises, how can deduction ever be informative or surprising? The solution from physics resolves this by recognizing that computation and reasoning do not exist as eternal Platonic entities outside of spacetime. Instead, they are physical, causal processes.

## Mechanisms

- Computation is Communication: Whether in a Turing machine head reading symbols off a tape, or neurons firing in a brain, computation requires the physical transmission of state.
- Causality Takes Time: Because communication is a causal process bound by the laws of physics, a message cannot be received before it is sent. Therefore, a conclusion cannot be known until the stepwise computations on the premises are finished.
- The Illusion of Triviality: Some deductions feel instantaneous only because the vast amount of computation required to produce our conscious perceptions is hidden from us (as proposed in Blind Brain Theory).
