## Minimum Viable Understanding (MVU)

ADHD is increasingly understood not merely as a deficit of attention, but as a neurodevelopmental disorder of self-regulation and executive function (EF). Executive functions are the "brain's management system"—the set of mental skills that allow us to set goals, manage time, and execute tasks. In ADHD, these systems are dysregulated, leading to a performance gap where the individual "knows what to do, but cannot do what they know."

---

## 1. The 6 Domains of Executive Function (ADHD Context)

According to the Barkley and Brown models, EF challenges in ADHD can be grouped into six primary clusters:

1. Activation: Difficulty organizing, prioritizing, and initiating tasks. This often manifests as chronic procrastination or "paralysis" until a deadline becomes an emergency.
2. Focus: Difficulty sustaining attention and shifting focus when necessary. This isn't just "distractibility," but an inability to regulate the "zoom" of attention.
3. Effort: Difficulty regulating alertness, sustaining effort, and processing speed. This includes the "trough phase" exhaustion after periods of hyperfocus (see [[Executive Function Impairment During ADHD Trough Phase]]).
4. Emotion: Difficulty managing frustration and modulating emotions. ADHD brains often experience "Emotional Dysregulation," where feelings are felt with overwhelming intensity.
5. Memory: Impairments in Working Memory—the ability to hold information in mind while working with it. This leads to losing the "thread" of a task or forgetting instructions mid-execution.
6. Action: Difficulty monitoring and self-regulating physical or mental activity (impulsivity). This is the "broken brakes" of the race car brain.

## 2. Neurobiological Underpinnings

These challenges are rooted in the brain's physical architecture and chemistry:

- Prefrontal Cortex (PFC): The "CEO" of the brain, responsible for EF, is often under-activated in ADHD.
- Dopamine Dysregulation: Low tonic levels of dopamine in the PFC make it difficult for the brain to signal the "importance" of non-novel tasks, leading to an Interest-Based Nervous System (IBNS).
- The Glitchy Switch: Dysregulation between the Task Positive Network (TPN) and Default Mode Network (DMN), making it hard to stay "on task."

## 3. Practical Implications

- The Performance Gap: The primary struggle is not a lack of knowledge or skill, but the inability to _apply_ that knowledge consistently at the "point of performance."
- Metacognitive Blindness: Deficits in "thinking about thinking" (metacognition) can prevent someone from recognizing _why_ they are stuck, often leading to self-blame and the belief that they are "lazy" or "stupid" (see [[Metacognitive Deficits in ADHD Create Practical Impairments]]).
