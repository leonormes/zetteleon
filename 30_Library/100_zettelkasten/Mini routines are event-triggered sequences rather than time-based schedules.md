## Mini Routines Are Event-Triggered Sequences Rather Than Time-Based Schedules

Summary: A mini routine is structurally defined by what starts it — the completion of an activity or a natural life event, not a clock time. This is the specific mechanism that lets event-based routines eliminate the psychological burden of clock-watching and adapt to day-to-day variation, rather than a side benefit of "flexibility" in general.

Details: A time-based schedule asks "is it 7:00am yet?" — a question whose answer is binary and externally imposed, and which generates anxiety every time the answer is "not yet" or "too late." An event-triggered mini routine instead asks "has the prior step finished?" — tying the next action to a natural life event (finishing a meal, completing a work block, waking up) rather than to a position on a clock. The sequence itself, not the clock, is the timer.

This is why event-based structures are reported to reduce time-pressure anxiety and work with natural attention patterns: there is no schedule to fall behind on, only a sequence to continue or pause. The structure travels with whatever the day actually does, instead of requiring the day to match a fixed timetable.
