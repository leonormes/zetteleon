## Social Media Now Requires a Sobriety Approach Rather Than a Craftsman Approach

Modern algorithmic social media (e.g. TikTok, Instagram Reels) has shifted from a communication utility to an engineered dopamine delivery mechanism. Newport's earlier "craftsman" approach—evaluate pros and cons of each platform, keep those with net value—is no longer sufficient because the platforms' reward engineering makes moderate, intentional use neurologically unstable. The appropriate model is now "sobriety": hard access restrictions that starve the short-term dopamine circuits rather than trying to manage them.

### Scope & Conditions

Specifically applies to algorithmically-driven, infinite-scroll platforms. Does not necessarily apply to intentional, bounded communication tools (e.g. a family WhatsApp group or a professional forum). The distinction is whether the platform's design goal is maximising engagement time or facilitating a specific communication task.

### Evidence

> "Newport argues it must now be approached more like a 'sobriety' issue, focusing on heavily restricting access to dumb down your phone and starving short-term dopamine circuits." [29:22]

### Implications

- Moderate usage is often impossible due to addictive design—the craftsman model assumes the tool is neutral, but these platforms are adversarially designed.
- Successful management requires hard environmental constraints (device restrictions, app blocking) rather than nuanced selection or "mindful use" intentions.
