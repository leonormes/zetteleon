### Historical Recurrence Means Recurring Mechanisms, Not Identical Events

Claims that "history repeats" or that a current situation resembles a past one are useful as anti-utopian warnings, but risk flattening important differences — recurring motives (status competition, resource conflict, fear, identity, domination, the search for meaning) can operate through radically different institutions and technologies, so recurrence should mean recurring mechanisms or patterns, not identical events.

#### Scope & Conditions

A corrective for historical-analogy arguments generally; the burden is on the arguer to show the pattern has genuinely recurred (shared mechanism) rather than merely resembles a past case on the surface.

#### Evidence

> "Recurring motives—status competition, resource conflict, fear, identity, domination, and the search for meaning—can operate through radically different institutions and technologies... Recurrence should therefore mean recurring mechanisms or patterns, not identical events."

#### Implications

- When evaluating a historical analogy, ask whether it identifies a shared causal mechanism or merely an evocative surface resemblance — these support very different strengths of conclusion.
