## Minimum Viable Understanding (MVU)

Communication is coordination, not transmission: words do not carry thoughts from one brain to another, but act as instructions for the listener to reconstruct meaning through their own experiential filter (history, culture, values). Because no two experiential filters are identical, misunderstanding is the default state unless speakers actively model what the listener knows, anchor abstractions in shared concrete experience, and iterate through feedback and repair.

## 1. The Mechanic of Communication

Communication begins with an internal mental state that cannot be copied directly into another mind. Instead, the speaker emits linguistic stimuli—pointers that trigger local reconstruction inside the listener's own [[Experiential Filter]]—across an unavoidably lossy channel.

## 2. The Source of Misunderstanding

Because every listener decodes stimuli through a different filter, misunderstanding is not an exceptional failure mode—it is the structural baseline. Speakers routinely underestimate this gap because their own words feel transparent against their tacit context (the Curse of Knowledge), and teams compound the error when shared vocabulary masks incompatible mental models.

## 3. Strategies for Shared Understanding

Overcoming default misunderstanding does not mean achieving identical mental states; it means building enough functional overlap to coordinate action within a shared activity.

![[Shared Understanding Is Sufficient Functional Overlap Rather Than Identical Mental States]]

![[Meaning emerges from language games]]

### 3.1 Establishing Common Ground

Before introducing complex or abstract claims, communicators can locate shared reference points—grounded first in common human biology and universal experiences, and then calibrated deliberately to the listener's world.

![[Shared biological architecture enables communication]]

![[Common experiential anchors ground abstract concepts]]

![[How to Establish Common Ground Using I-CARE]]

### 3.2 Bridging the Expert-Novice Gap

When speaker and listener differ sharply in domain experience, shared vocabulary breaks down completely unless the expert unpacks compressed abstractions into concrete examples and scaffolds onto what the novice already knows.

![[Experts Must De-Compile Abstractions Into Concrete Examples for Novices]]

### 3.3 Active Repair

Even with careful calibration, first-pass encoding drops context. Reliable communication therefore depends on continuous feedback loops and an explicit assumption of divergence until verified.

![[Communication requires iterative approximation]]

![[How to Repair Misunderstanding During Communication]]

> Key Insight: Effective communication is the collaborative construction of a temporary shared language, grounded in the intersection of two experiential worlds.
