## A Simple Note-Taking Structure Lets Complexity Build Up at the Content Level

Keeping the structure of a note-taking system as simple as possible lets complexity build up in the content, where it is wanted, instead of in the way the notes are organised. The source is Sönke Ahrens, How to Take Smart Notes.

### Scope & Conditions

Ahrens is talking about the structure of the whole note-taking system, and he contrasts it with sorting notes into topics and sub-topics. Reading this as a reason for single-idea notes is an extension and not his own statement: single-idea notes are one way of keeping each unit simple.

### Evidence

> "Most people try to reduce complexity by separating what they have into smaller stacks, piles or separate folders. They sort their notes by topics and sub-topics, which makes it look less complex, but quickly becomes very complicated."
> (Ahrens, How to Take Smart Notes, chapter "Everything You Need to Know", section "Complexity")

> "The best way to deal with complexity is to keep things as simple as possible and to follow a few basic principles. The simplicity of the structure allows complexity to build up where we want it: on the content level."
> (Ahrens, How to Take Smart Notes, chapter "Everything You Need to Know", section "Complexity")

### Implications

- Sorting notes into stacks and sub-topics makes a collection look simpler while making it harder to use and less likely to produce surprising connections.
- A structure with few principles leaves room for the content, and the connections in it, to grow.
