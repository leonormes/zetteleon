## Interactive Visual Artifacts Speed Human Review of Complex AI-Generated Designs

A design decision described in prose forces the reviewer to reconstruct its structure mentally—which components relate to which, what depends on what—from a linear sequence of sentences. The same decision rendered as an interactive diagram (a whiteboard-style visual with explicit boxes, arrows, and hierarchy) puts that structure directly in front of the reviewer instead of asking them to infer it. The claim is specifically about review speed for complex, structurally-rich designs—not a general claim that visuals are always better than text, and not evidenced with any measurement in the source beyond the qualitative claim itself.

This connects to the general principle that different content shapes suit different mediums: a design with real hierarchical or relational structure is arguably mis-served by prose, which is a poor medium for conveying structure regardless of how well-written it is.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies specifically to complex system designs with genuine hierarchical or relational structure worth visualizing—a simple, linear decision likely doesn't benefit from this treatment, and the overhead of generating an interactive artifact may not be worth it for anything but genuinely complex designs.
