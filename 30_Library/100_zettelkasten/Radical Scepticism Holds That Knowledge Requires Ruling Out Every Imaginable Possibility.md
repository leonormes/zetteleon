## Radical Scepticism Holds That Knowledge Requires Ruling Out Every Imaginable Possibility

Radical scepticism sets the bar for knowledge at excluding every imaginable alternative. On that bar almost no empirical claim counts as known. The source chat treats it as internally consistent but too costly.

### Scope & Conditions

The chat describes the position by its consequence and names no holder, so this note records the position and not a particular philosopher. The chat observes that it does not single out one case such as ghosts: it removes nearly all empirical knowledge.

### Evidence

> "If knowledge required ruling out every imaginable possibility, you couldn’t know your car is where you parked it."
> (AI chat transcript pasted by Leon, 2026-10-23, section "Ghosts, fairies, and knowing that something doesn’t exist")

> "That position exists: it’s radical scepticism. It’s internally consistent, but it doesn’t single out ghosts. It abolishes almost all empirical knowledge."
> (same section)

### Implications

- If it is right, "I can at most believe a thing does not exist, never know it" is true of everything, not just of ghosts or gods.
- The disagreement with fallibilism is about the bar for knowledge, not about the evidence in any one case.

- [[Fallibilism Holds That Knowledge Requires Good Justification, Not Certainty]]—tension, not contradiction: it keeps knowledge by lowering the bar from certainty to good justification.
