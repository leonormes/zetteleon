title: What is information
type: concept
---
Data that has been processed, organized, structured, or presented in a given context to make it meaningful and useful. Information answers the basic questions of "who," "what," "where," and "when." This definition treats information as a form of [[Information as a Perceivable Pattern]] [extends:: [[Information as a Perceivable Pattern]]].

---

## Directly Related Concepts

The raw material for information is data. The process of turning data into information often involves [[Applying Previous Knowledge]] [depends_on:: [[Applying Previous Knowledge]]] and context to reveal hidden patterns. This transformation is a key step in the DIKW pyramid, eventually leading to knowledge (see [[Information vs Knowledge]] [extends:: [[Information vs Knowledge]]]).

A clear, practical example of this is the [[SoT - Protocol Data Units (PDU)]] [supports:: [[SoT - Protocol Data Units (PDU)]]], where the name and structure of data change based on its context within a networking model.

## Extending the Idea

The importance of context cannot be overstated. A failure to maintain perspective leads to [[Myopic Understanding]] [supports:: [[Myopic Understanding]]], where the meaning of information is lost.

Furthermore, our ability to interpret information is not neutral; it is shaped by [[Knowledge-Related Biases]] [depends_on:: [[Knowledge-Related Biases]]].

This semantic view of information stands in contrast to the mathematical view, as explored in [[The Conflict Between Semantic and Shannon Information]] [extends:: [[The Conflict Between Semantic and Shannon Information]]] and [[Shannon's Information Theory - Information as Uncertainty]] [contradicts:: [[Shannon's Information Theory - Information as Uncertainty]]].
