## The Impressions Test Traces an Abstract Concept Back to Its Originating Sense-Impression or Discards It as Meaningless

To determine whether a suspicious or highly abstract concept has actual meaning, trace it back to the specific sense-impression from which it was supposedly derived; if no such impression exists, treat the concept as meaningless rather than granting it the benefit of the doubt.

### Scope & Conditions

Trigger—encountering vague, theoretical, or heavily academic terminology. Historically Hume's own method, not a universal linguistic law; over-rigid application can wrongly discard moral, aesthetic, or philosophical concepts that aren't derived from a single physical impression.

### Evidence

> "To determine if a suspicious or highly abstract concept has actual meaning, one must trace it back to the specific impression from which it was supposedly derived. If no such impression exists, the concept is meaningless."

### Implications

- A practical jargon-detector: ask a speaker using an abstract term (e.g. "hegemony") to name the concrete experience that generates it; inability to do so is itself informative.
