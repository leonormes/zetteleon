## Deutsch Rejects Empiricism for Conjecture-Led Theory Formation

Deutsch entirely rejects empiricism—the doctrine that we derive our theories from sensory experience. Theories are not "derived" from nature, nor are they extrapolations of repeated observations; they are bold conjectures created by the human imagination. We do not learn by "reading" the Book of Nature: we invent theories to solve problems and then use experience merely to choose between these pre-existing guesses.

### Scope & Conditions

Deutsch's Popperian account of theory-genesis specifically; contrasts with Godfrey-Smith's modernised empiricism, which treats observation and experience as the fundamental source of knowledge.

### Evidence

> "theories are not 'derived' from nature, nor are they extrapolations of repeated observations... they are bold conjectures created by the human imagination... we invent theories to solve problems and then use experience merely to choose between these pre-existing guesses"

### Implications

- Reframes observation's role from "source of theory" to "arbiter between theories."
- Sharpens the vault's existing account of induction's limits by naming the alternative, conjecture-first account of where theories actually come from.
