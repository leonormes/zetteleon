## Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus establishes the inverse relationship between differentiation and integration. It unifies the two primary branches of calculus by showing how a total quantity can be reconstructed if the exact rate of change is known at every point.

### Scope & Conditions

The theoretical bridge connecting the concepts of "Change" (derivatives) and "Accumulation" (integrals).

### Evidence

> "…showing how you can theoretically reconstruct the entire height of a mountain if you are given the exact steepness (slope) at every single point, tying directly into the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus."

### Implications

- Allows for the evaluation of definite integrals using antiderivatives.
- Provides the formal mathematical justification for the relationship between instantaneous change and cumulative growth.
