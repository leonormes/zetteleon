## Observation Is Theory-Laden Rather Than Raw and Neutral

What counts as "the data" is never a pure, assumption-free readout of the world. The instrument used, the concepts brought to the measurement, the calibration procedure, and the background assumptions about what matters all shape the observation before it reaches the scientist. This is why scientists must validate their measurements and make their assumptions explicit rather than treating data as self-evidently neutral.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to any empirical observation, from a thermometer reading to a dashboard metric—not only to contested or qualitative fields.

### Implications

- Two people can "observe the same thing" and disagree, because their instruments, calibration, or background concepts differ—the disagreement can be genuinely about the measurement, not just its interpretation.
- Making assumptions and calibration explicit is a precondition for a measurement being checkable by someone else.
