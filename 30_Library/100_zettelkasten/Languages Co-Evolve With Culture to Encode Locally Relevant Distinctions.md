## Languages Co-Evolve With Culture to Encode Locally Relevant Distinctions

Languages evolve alongside their communities to lexicalise locally relevant distinctions and compress shared background assumptions into idioms and social scripts. This cultural scaffolding lets speakers coordinate without stating every assumption explicitly, while creating divergence across cultural boundaries.

### Scope & Conditions

- Applies to vocabulary specialisation, idioms, and implicit social scripts within a shared linguistic community.
- Influences what distinctions are easy to name and attend to without imprisoning underlying cognitive capacity ([[Claim - Language serves as a tool for communicating thoughts rather than determining them]]).

### Evidence

> "Cultural co-evolution with language creates systems of shared assumptions and background knowledge. Languages develop within communities to express culturally relevant distinctions and relationships."
> ([[Beyond Words]], line 326)

### Implications

- Intra-cultural communication is compressed because shared scripts (such as what "meeting for coffee" entails) do not need to be spelled out.
- Cross-cultural misunderstanding arises when speakers use identical surface words routed through different cultural models.
