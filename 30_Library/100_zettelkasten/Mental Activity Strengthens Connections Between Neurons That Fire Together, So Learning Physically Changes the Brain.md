## Mental Activity Strengthens Connections Between Neurons That Fire Together, So Learning Physically Changes the Brain

When neurons fire together they strengthen their existing connections and form new ones, so mental activity leaves a physical trace in the brain. Turning information into knowledge is therefore a physical change in the learner, and it cannot happen by the information merely being present. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- It is a claim about the neural mechanism of learning in general (Hebbian plasticity). It does not say which regions store which knowledge, or that every connection formed is useful.
- Repetition and attention matter because the change follows activity: neurons that do not fire together do not strengthen their link, and connections weaken when they go unused.
- The claim describes the mechanism of knowledge formation in the brain. It does not settle what knowledge is, which [[The Traditional Definition of Knowledge is Justified True Belief]] and the notes around it debate.
- The earlier version of this note also said that new information is integrated with existing cognitive structures and that sleep and reflection consolidate it. Those two points now sit in [[Prior Knowledge Organized as Schemas Provides the Foundation for New Learning]] and [[Spacing and Sleep Consolidate New Connections Into Long-Term Memory, So Cramming Leaves Them Weak]].

### Evidence

Two books state the principle. Hanson, a neuropsychologist, writes:

> "when neurons fire together, they wire together—mental activity actually creates new neural structures"
> (Hanson 2015, ch. 1, PDF p. 22) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/358/EPUB?open_at=toc:Chapter%201.%20The%20Self-Transforming%20Brain)

He ties the saying to Hebb (1949) and LeDoux (2003) and gives the example of London taxi drivers, who develop a larger hippocampus. Barrett says the same of connections:

> "Connections become more or less numerous, and they become stronger when neurons fire together and weaker when they don't."
> (Barrett 2019, PDF p. 34) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/466/EPUB)

### Implications

- Learning needs repeated, active engagement with the material, because the connection is strengthened only when the neurons are active together. A single passive exposure does little.
- The same mechanism explains why habits and mental patterns can be changed, which is the subject of [[The Brain Changes Its Structure and Function in Response to Experience Throughout Life]].
- The mechanism fits a note-taking system only by analogy. A link in a vault is not a synapse, so the claim supports practices such as active recall and linking, and it does not prove that a vault works like a brain.

### Falsifier

- Evidence that learning in healthy adults occurs without lasting change in synaptic strength or structure, or that firing together does not strengthen connections in the regions involved.
