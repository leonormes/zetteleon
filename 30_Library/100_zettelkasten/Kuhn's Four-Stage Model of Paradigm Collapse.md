## Kuhn's Four-Stage Model of Paradigm Collapse

Thomas Kuhn (_The Structure of Scientific Revolutions_, 1962) argued that science does not progress through slow, linear accumulation of facts, but through long stretches of stability punctuated by sudden, violent intellectual revolutions, following a predictable lifecycle:

1. Normal Science—the current paradigm works well; scientists use it as a toolkit and blame the equipment or their own methodology when an experiment fails, not the theory.
2. Model Drift—anomalies accumulate that the paradigm cannot explain; they are initially dismissed or patched over with increasingly convoluted sub-theories (16th-century astronomers adding epicycles to keep the Earth at the centre of the solar system is the canonical example).
3. Model Crisis—the patches stop working; anomalies become too numerous or central to ignore, the consensus loses predictive power, and the field fragments into speculative alternatives.
4. Model Revolution—a new paradigm that explains almost everything the old one did _and_ resolves the crisis-causing anomalies replaces it.

### Scope & Conditions

Describes the sociology of paradigm change, not a claim about any single paradigm's truth.

### Evidence

> "He mapped this out as a predictable lifecycle… Normal Science… Model Drift… Model Crisis… Model Revolution."

### Implications

- Anomalies are patched over long before they are taken as falsification—a field doesn't drop a paradigm at the first sign of trouble.
- A field's current stage in this cycle can be diagnosed from how it treats its own anomalies.
