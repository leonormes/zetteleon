## Diverting Income on Receipt Creates a Felt Scarcity That Lowers Spending Without Willpower

Diverting part of income as soon as it arrives, from the river of income and bills into a separate lake, makes the available balance feel smaller. Lifestyle then adjusts to the remaining flow without significant willpower. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Assumes the diverted money is moved immediately and is not treated as available to spend, as in a zero-out method. The size and durability of the effect are untested here.

### Evidence

Carried over from the earlier prose version of [[SoT - Financial Philosophy and Spendfulness]], which called this the Money River Model; no outside source is cited.

### Implications

- Spending can be adjusted by changing what looks available instead of relying on restraint.
