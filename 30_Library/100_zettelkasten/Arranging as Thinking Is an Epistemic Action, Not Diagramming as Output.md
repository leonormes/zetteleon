## Arranging as Thinking Is an Epistemic Action, Not Diagramming as Output

"Diagramming as output" is the retrospective creation of a static visual representation of something already understood. "Arranging as thinking" is the active, in-progress manipulation of partially formed conceptual material in 2D space to discover structure rather than depict it. Moving cards, clustering nodes, and drawing connections are epistemic actions (Kirsh and Maglio, 1994): physical actions that simplify cognitive computation rather than advancing a goal-state directly.

### Scope & Conditions

The distinction matters most when evaluating whether a visual tool is useful for synthesis-in-progress versus communication of a finished idea. Conflating the two leads to underusing canvas tools during active thinking.

### Evidence

> "Moving cards, clustering nodes, drawing connection arrows, and forming visual containment structures… are not administrative gestures—they are epistemic actions in Kirsh and Maglio's (1994) framework: physical actions that simplify cognitive computation rather than advancing a goal-state."

### Implications

- Spatial manipulation during synthesis is itself cognitive work, not a substitute for or precursor to thinking.
- A canvas should be evaluated as a thinking medium during active synthesis, separately from its value as a final communication artefact.
