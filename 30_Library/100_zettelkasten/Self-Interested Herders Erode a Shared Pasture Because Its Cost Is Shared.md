## Self-Interested Herders Erode a Shared Pasture Because Its Cost Is Shared

In Garrett Hardin's 1968 parable of a shared pasture, each herder gains most of the benefit of adding an animal to the flock while the cost of supporting it is spread across all herders. If every herder acts on self-interest, the commons is eroded and nothing is left for anyone.

### Scope & Conditions

Hardin's parable; the commons supports many animals but not infinitely many; every herder faces the same incentive.

### Evidence

> "By adding an animal to her herd, she receives a substantial benefit when she sells the animal at market. However, the cost of supporting that animal is shared by all who use the commons. Thus, the herder gains a lot, but pays only a little, by adding an additional animal to her herd."
>
> "If each herder acts according to her self-interest, the commons will be completely eroded, and there will be nothing left for anyone."
> (Joshua Greene, Moral Tribes, Chapter 1: The Tragedy of the Commons)

### Implications

- Individually rational choices can destroy a resource that everyone depends on.
- The pattern generalises to any cooperation problem where personal and collective interest are only partly aligned.
