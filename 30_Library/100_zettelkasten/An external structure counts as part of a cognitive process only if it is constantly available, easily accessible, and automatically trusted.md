## An external structure counts as part of a cognitive process only if it is constantly available, easily accessible, and automatically trusted

An external structure counts as part of a cognitive process only when it is constantly available, easily accessible, and automatically endorsed without deliberate verification. These conditions are generic to extended cognition rather than specific to ADHD. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- **Generic, not clinical:** Applies equally to a neurotypical person's notebook (Clark and Chalmers's Otto) and to an ADHD task scaffold.
- **Contested metaphysics:** Critics (such as Adams and Aizawa's coupling-constitution objection) reject literal constitution; a functional reading still holds that violating any of the three conditions breaks reliable reliance.

### Evidence

> "Elements of the world outside may effectively act as mental "extensions," allowing us to think in ways our brains could not manage on their own."
> (Annie Murphy Paul, _The Extended Mind_, summarising Clark and Chalmers)

- Grounded in [[The Parity Principle - Functional Equivalence in Cognition]] and [[SoT - The Extended Mind]]. *(No Calibre book page citation attached yet.)*

### Implications

- **Design checklist:** Any vault, task view, or capture inbox that fails availability, speed of access, or user trust ceases to function as part of the cognitive loop.
