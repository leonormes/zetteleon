## Working With a Zettelkasten Is a Practice of Decomposition and Reassembly

Working with a zettelkasten disassembles a writer's structured thoughts into separate atomic ideas, networks them, and reassembles them later in new ways. The source is Bob Doto, A System for Writing.

### Scope & Conditions

Doto's account of how note-making repurposes ideas; he ties the "mosaic of quotations" image to Julia Kristeva's view that written texts are built from other texts.

### Evidence

> "Working with a Luhmann-style zettelkasten is, in part, a practice of decomposition, where a writer's previously structured thoughts are disassembled, to be networked in the slip box, and reassembled later in new ways as a "mosaic of quotations.""
> (Doto, A System for Writing, section "Atomicity Allows You to Repurpose Ideas")

> "Theses are unwound from their original focus to yield separate, atomic ideas that can be reimagined, reinterpreted, and, ultimately, repurposed for new work."
> (Doto, A System for Writing, section "Atomicity Allows You to Repurpose Ideas")

### Implications

- An idea is separated from the argument it came in, so it can serve a different argument.
- Splitting an author's thesis into atoms is a productive step, not damage to the source.
