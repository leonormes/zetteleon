Negative thinking patterns often encompass various cognitive distortions, such as mind reading and selective abstraction.

Both are shortcuts, not evidence-gathering: mind reading treats an inferred guess about another person's reaction as settled fact, and selective abstraction locks onto one negative detail and discounts everything that would complicate it. Neither requires anything new to be learned—each just launders an assumption into a conclusion.
