## Ideas Exist beyond Linguistic Expression

Ideas and mental concepts exist as internal representations that precede and transcend their expression in any particular language or symbolic system. Having an "aha" moment before finding the words—or recognising the same concept across different languages—shows that the underlying idea is independent of specific linguistic forms.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to conceptual, spatial, and embodied understanding rather than purely syntactic or formal-symbol manipulation. Language still shapes attention and refines thought once articulated, even though it is not the primary substrate of the idea.

### Evidence

This claim is derived from cognitive synthesis across the pre-linguistic cognition and thought-language notes in the vault rather than a single external quotation.

### Implications

- Difficulty articulating a thought does not mean the underlying insight is absent; it signals a translation gap between internal representation and words.
- Testing comprehension requires checking whether someone can apply or paraphrase an idea, not merely repeat its wording.
- Non-linguistic mediums such as music, diagrams, and movement can express nuances that prose drops.
