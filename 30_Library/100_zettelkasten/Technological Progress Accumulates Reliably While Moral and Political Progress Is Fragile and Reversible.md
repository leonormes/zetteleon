### Technological Progress Accumulates Reliably While Moral and Political Progress Is Fragile and Reversible

Scientific knowledge and technology tend to preserve earlier gains and can be built upon reliably, but ethical and political achievements — rights, norms, peace, institutional competence, public trust — do not behave the same way and can weaken or disappear within a few generations, because they depend on continued enforcement, legitimacy, and public support rather than being self-maintaining.

#### Scope & Conditions

The narrower, empirically defensible core of John Gray's argument, distinct from the stronger and less supported claim that moral/political progress is simply a myth or illusion — measurable gains in health, education, and liberty are real even though they are uneven and reversible.

#### Evidence

> "Scientific knowledge and technology often preserve earlier gains: a technique, discovery, or engineering capability can be transmitted and built upon. Ethical and political achievements do not behave as reliably. Rights, norms, peace, institutional competence, and public trust can weaken or disappear within a few generations."

#### Implications

- A useful warning against assuming new technology automatically produces wiser politics — the two kinds of "progress" don't transfer into each other.
