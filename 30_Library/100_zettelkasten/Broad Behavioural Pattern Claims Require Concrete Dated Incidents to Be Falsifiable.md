## Broad Behavioural Pattern Claims Require Concrete Dated Incidents to Be Falsifiable

Asserting that a partner's behaviour is a pervasive pattern is unfalsifiable and invites attribution error unless grounded in at least three concrete, dated incidents. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to interpersonal disagreements where one person feels a recurring pattern exists; even dated incident lists remain vulnerable to confirmation bias.

### Evidence

> "\"It's a pattern across everything\" is unfalsifiable until I list incidents. List three, with dates: [...]"
> ([[Household Routines Proposal]], §4)

### Implications

- Requiring three dated examples prevents emotional reasoning from turning isolated frustrations into character judgements.
- Keeps partner conversations anchored to observable data rather than sweeping generalisations.
