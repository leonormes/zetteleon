## Embrace Your True Expenses (YNAB Rule 2)

Rule 2 treats infrequent but predictable bills, such as insurance, car repairs, holidays and annual fees, as monthly costs. Each is divided by the months remaining and funded gradually, so £600 a year becomes £50 a month. The bill then arrives as a planned withdrawal and not as a shock to the current month.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to expenses that are irregular in timing but foreseeable in kind. It needs [[Give Every Dollar a Job (YNAB Rule 1)]] to supply the money, because instalments can only be assigned from cash already held. It does not cover true emergencies of unknown size, which belong to a separate resilience buffer.

### Evidence

This is the household's working method, set out as the second of four rules in [[SoT - Family Financial Wellness]]. The vault holds no outside source for it yet, so the claim rests on that operational record and on the reasoning above.

### Implications

- Large bills stop competing with groceries and rent in the month they fall due.
- Prioritising true-expense funding over aggressive debt payoff keeps the household from sliding back into credit, as the Payday Protocol states.
- Each true-expense category is a named job, which makes the Stability question in Rule 1 concrete.
