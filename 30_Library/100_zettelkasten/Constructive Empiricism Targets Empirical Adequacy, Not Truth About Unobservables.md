## Constructive Empiricism Targets Empirical Adequacy, Not Truth About Unobservables

Van Fraassen's constructive empiricism argues that science does not need to describe the hidden, unobservable structures of the world; instead, the goal of science is merely to be "empirically adequate," meaning its theories accurately predict observable phenomena.

### Scope & Conditions

A modern alternative to scientific realism; softer than logical positivism in that it doesn't deny unobservables meaning, only remains agnostic about their existence.

### Evidence

> "constructive empiricism argues that science does not need to describe the hidden, unobservable structures of the world. Instead, the goal of science is merely to be 'empirically adequate.'"

### Implications

- Gives instrumentalism a precise technical target ("empirical adequacy") rather than a vague slogan.
