## Voters Trade Democratic Norms for Partisan Victory

A significant portion of the electorate will knowingly choose a candidate who shares their partisan goals but behaves dishonestly or undemocratically over an opposing candidate who is honest and adheres to democratic norms, and this trade-off intensifies as affective polarization rises.

### Scope & Conditions

Based on Milan Svolik's experimental research on democratic backsliding, using forced-choice designs. Voters are not shown to forget or fail to notice the dishonesty—they consciously demote honesty to a secondary priority.

### Evidence

> "A highly significant portion of the electorate is perfectly willing to trade off democratic principles and basic honesty for partisan interests… When affective polarization is high, the perceived cost of letting the opposing party win vastly outweighs the moral cost of voting for a manipulative, dishonest, or corrupt in-group candidate."

### Implications

- Democratic backsliding can proceed through informed, deliberate voter choice rather than deception or ignorance.

### Library Sources

- _Enlightenment Now: The Case for Reason, Science, Humanism, and Progress_ (Steven Pinker), Ch. 14—discusses "democratic backsliding" directly, including how episodes of reversal are sometimes used as "a convenient pretext for dictators"—complements this atom's voter-choice framing with the macro/historical pattern of backsliding episodes, though Pinker's chapter is not specifically about Svolik's partisan-tradeoff experiments.
