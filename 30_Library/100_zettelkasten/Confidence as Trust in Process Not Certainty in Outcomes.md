## Confidence as Trust in Process Not Certainty in Outcomes

Durable confidence under uncertainty is grounded in trusting one's capacity to sense, decide, and adjust rather than in guaranteeing a specific outcome in advance. Like a navigator relying on checkpoints and course correction rather than a flawless initial forecast, process-based confidence decouples self-worth from being immediately right and attaches it to iterative learning competence. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to personal agency, skill acquisition, and decision-making in domains where outcomes cannot be guaranteed ex ante.

### Evidence

Conceptual synthesis grounded in pre-linguistic calibration from physical skills ([[Embodied Confidence Comes From Direct Feedback in Physical Skills]]) and volatility-driven adaptation ([[Anti-Fragility Means Systems That Benefit From Volatility]]); no verbatim quotation note is attached (`evidence_links: []`).

### Implications

- Measure self-trust by responsiveness to feedback and recovery from errors rather than by prediction accuracy.
- Anchor daily execution in repeatable process habits so confidence compounds across iterations ([[Valuing the Learning Process Over External Outcomes Sustains Motivation]]).
