## Claim—ADHD Perfectionism is a Maladaptive Compensation

> [!claim] Statement
> Perfectionism in ADHD often arises not as high standards but as an attempt to compensate for a history of underachievement—and because the ideal is unreachable, it reliably produces failure, self-criticism and renewed system-seeking.

### Supporting Evidence

- The report (citing ADDitude) links the pattern to fear of failure and [[Rejection-Sensitive Dysphoria]].
- The compulsion to reach an ideal organised state generates pressure; missing it reinforces negative self-talk—a self-perpetuating loop.

### Mechanism

- Unattainable standard → predictable shortfall → shame/RSD spike → either paralysis or a hunt for "the system that will finally fix this".

### Counter-positions

- Standards themselves are not the problem; the problem is rigidity and all-or-nothing scoring of outcomes.
- For some, high standards are genuinely productive and not compensatory.

### Crux

- Whether the perfectionism is _driven by_ anticipated shame (compensatory) or is an independent disposition that merely co-occurs with ADHD.

### Falsifier

- If reducing shame/RSD (e.g. via self-compassion work) left the perfectionistic behaviour unchanged, the compensation account would be weakened.
