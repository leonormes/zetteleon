title: Discreteness as a Precondition for Counting
type: claim

For the act of counting and the concept of number to be meaningful, the universe must contain distinction or discreteness. We must be able to perceive separate, distinct things.

Counting requires the ability to identify an object and distinguish it from its background and from other objects. It is the fundamental difference between what is discrete (countable, like apples) and what is continuous (an unbroken whole, like a flow of water).

Without separation, there is no quantity. This is explored in [[The Continuum Thought Experiment]].

### Further Reading (Personal Library)

- [An Essay Concerning Human Understanding, 1690 — John Locke, Ch. XVI "Of Number"](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/371/EPUB)—_Locke's classical account of number as built by repeating the idea of a single, distinct "unit"—the historical philosophical root of this note's claim._
- [Encyclopedia of Mathematics — James Stuart Tanton, entry "discrete"](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/1154/PDF)—_gives the formal mathematical definition of discreteness (no intermediate values between elements) that this note's "apples vs. flowing water" contrast is pointing at._
- [Elements of Programming — Alexander A. Stepanov & Paul McJones, §5.9 "Concepts and Their Models"](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/306/EPUB)—_formalises "discreteness" as the axiom (no element between 0 and 1) that makes a structure isomorphic to the natural numbers—the precise mathematical cash-value of this note's claim._
- [The Princeton Companion to Mathematics — Gowers, Barrow-Green & Leader (eds.), "Set Theory"](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/1137/PDF)—_describes the counting process itself as assigning a number to each object in turn, exactly once—illustrating why the objects must already be distinguishable before counting can begin._
- [The Blank Slate — Steven Pinker, Ch. 13 "Out of Our Depths"](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/1571/EPUB)—_corroborates the cognitive half of the claim: humans have an innate "number sense" for registering exact quantities of small numbers of distinct objects._
