## Morality Evolved Through Competition Between Groups

Cooperation evolves only if individuals prone to cooperation outcompete those who are not. So insofar as morality is a biological adaptation, it evolved not only to put Us ahead of Me but to put Us ahead of Them.

### Scope & Conditions

Holds insofar as morality is a biological adaptation. Greene notes that much of morality seems unrelated to intergroup competition, and he does not assume group selection in making the argument.

### Evidence

> "Cooperation evolves only if individuals who are prone to cooperation outcompete individuals who are not (or who are less so)."
>
> "insofar as morality is a biological adaptation, it evolved not only as a device for putting Us ahead of Me, but as a device for putting Us ahead of Them."
> (Joshua Greene, Moral Tribes, Chapter 1, section "The Function of Morality")

### Implications

- We are moral beings only because our morally minded ancestors outcompeted their less morally minded neighbours.
- Moral instincts carry a built-in bias toward the home group.
