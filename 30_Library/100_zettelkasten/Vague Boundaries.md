## Vague Boundaries

The lack of clear-cut criteria for categorizing concepts that exist on a continuum.

## Core Examples

- [[Sorites Paradox]]—_the classic philosophical puzzle demonstrating this problem._
- [[The Concept of a Character Ideal]]—_the existing note this broken link was likely pointing at (redirected during refresh): Aristotle's golden mean, where character traits sit on a spectrum between two flanking defects (courage between cowardice and recklessness) with no sharp boundary._

## Mathematical Implications

- [[The Continuum Thought Experiment]]—_explores the limiting case: what happens without any boundaries at all, rather than merely a fuzzy one._
- [[Discreteness as a Precondition for Counting]]—_shows the opposite case: mathematics requires clear, sharp boundaries for counting to be defined at all._

### Further Reading (Personal Library)

- [The Organized Mind — Daniel J. Levitin, "Why Categories Matter"](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/689/EPUB)—_distinguishes hard-boundary categories (a triangle) from fuzzy-boundary categories (friendship), the same distinction this note names._
- [The Blank Slate — Steven Pinker, Ch. 13 "Out of Our Depths"](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/1571/EPUB)—_states the exact three-way distinction this vault's cluster of notes maps onto: "the mind distinguishes between a fuzzy boundary and no boundary at all."_
- [Encyclopedia of Mathematics — James Stuart Tanton, entry "fuzzy"](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/1154/PDF)—_gives the formal mathematical treatment of vague category membership: fuzzy-set theory's degrees of membership between 0 and 1._
- [The Art of Logic — Eugenia Cheng](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/418/EPUB)—_connects vague boundaries to fuzzy logic and continuous truth values, beyond simple true/false._
- [Guide to Good Reasoning — David C. Wilson, Ch. 5 "Specifying"](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/459/EPUB)—_practical heuristic for handling vague boundaries in argument: stop arguing over where the line is and ask why the line matters._
