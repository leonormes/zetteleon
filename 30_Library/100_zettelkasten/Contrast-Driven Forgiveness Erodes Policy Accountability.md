## Contrast-Driven Forgiveness Erodes Policy Accountability

Because politicians can rely on voters excusing lies and policy failures when contrasted against a frightening alternative, elections lose their function as referenda on governing performance and become tribal roll calls instead, removing the incentive to govern honestly or effectively.

### Scope & Conditions

Presented as the downstream, systemic consequence of the contrast effect, affective polarization, and moral licensing operating together over repeated election cycles—not a standalone mechanism. Confidence set to medium because the source frames this as a synthesised conclusion rather than citing a single dedicated study, unlike the more directly evidenced atoms above.

### Evidence

> "Because politicians know definitively that voters will excuse their lies, manipulations, and legislative failures as long as they are contrasted against a terrifying alternative, there is little incentive to govern effectively or honestly."

### Implications

- The erosion is self-reinforcing: less policy accountability produces more room for elite negative-representation strategies, which further entrenches contrast-driven forgiveness.
