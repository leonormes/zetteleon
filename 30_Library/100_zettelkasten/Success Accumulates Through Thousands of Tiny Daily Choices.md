Success or failure accumulates from thousands of tiny daily decisions—moments of choosing, not merely repetitions of an established action. Each is a fork where the alternative was live and available; the outcome is the tally, not any single vote.

Compounded over time, these micro-decisions produce transformation out of proportion to any one of them. The corollary is that the leverage sits in consistent small choices rather than extreme, overnight change.

## The choice/repetition Distinction

This note deliberately occupies the _choice_ half of an axis it shares with [[Success is the Result of Disciplined Repetition]]:

- Repetition—doing the same thing again, where the behaviour is already established and the work is maintenance. Failure mode: monotony.
- Choice—the moment of deciding, where an alternative is genuinely available and the behaviour is not yet automatic. Failure mode: [[Akrasia is Acting Against One's Better Judgment|akrasia]].

The two demand different interventions. Repetition is defended by systems and environment design; choice is defended by identity framing and pre-commitment. Conflating them is why "just be more consistent" advice fails at the point of decision.
