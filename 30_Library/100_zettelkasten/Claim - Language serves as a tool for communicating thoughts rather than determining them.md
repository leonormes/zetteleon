## Minimum Viable Understanding
The Sapir-Whorf Hypothesis (Linguistic Relativity) originally suggested that the language one speaks sets limits on the concepts one can think of. However, modern linguistics rejects strong linguistic determinism. Because languages are tools shaped by human agency and communicative needs, humans adapt their language to describe new experiences and perceptions, rather than being cognitively imprisoned by their existing vocabulary.

## Core Arguments
- **Denotation aids discrimination:** Having specific labels (like specific words for colors or emotions) allows people to easily identify and distinguish between similar concepts. The lack of a label makes it harder to think discretely about that concept, but not impossible.
- **Language is driven by need:** When humans encounter a novel experience (e.g., a new flavor), they adapt their language to describe it. The perception comes first, and the language evolves to communicate it.
- **Cognitive compatibility:** Accepting strict linguistic determinism would incorrectly imply that humanity possesses over 7,000 incompatible cognitive systems based on the 7,000+ languages currently spoken.
