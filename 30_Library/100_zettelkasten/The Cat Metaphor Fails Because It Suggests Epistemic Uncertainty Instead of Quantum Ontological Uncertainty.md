## The Cat Metaphor Fails Because It Suggests Epistemic Uncertainty Instead of Quantum Ontological Uncertainty

Humans have no everyday experience of macroscopic objects existing in an undecided, overlapping state, so when told about a cat sealed in a box, we default to classical probability: the cat is already dead or already alive, and the "superposition" is just our own ignorance of which. That is epistemic uncertainty—a gap in information about a fact that is already settled. Quantum superposition is ontological uncertainty: the universe itself has not decided, and the particle has no definite state until an interaction forces one. Because the cat metaphor cannot distinguish these two kinds of uncertainty, it misleads people into thinking quantum mechanics is about incomplete information rather than an unsettled state of reality.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies specifically to the cat-in-a-box framing (a closed, unobserved system); the failure is structural to the "hidden box" device, not to quantum mechanics itself.

### Implications

- A good teaching metaphor for superposition must make the undecided state itself observable, not hidden behind a barrier that invites a "we just don't know yet" reading.
- The distinction between epistemic and ontological uncertainty is the actual concept being taught—naming it directly may do more pedagogical work than any metaphor.
