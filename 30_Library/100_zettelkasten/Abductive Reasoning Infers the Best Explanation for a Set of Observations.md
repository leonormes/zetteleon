## Abductive Reasoning Infers the Best Explanation for a Set of Observations

Abductive reasoning ("inference to the best explanation") starts from a set of data or circumstances and formulates the hypothesis that best accounts for those facts; it is the primary reasoning method used in medical diagnosis and investigative work, and—unlike deduction—never guarantees its conclusion, only proposes the most plausible one available.

### Scope & Conditions

The "best" explanation is bounded by the reasoner's current knowledge and imagination—a more accurate explanation may exist outside their awareness, so an abductive conclusion should remain a working hypothesis, not a settled fact.

### Evidence

> "Abduction, or 'inference to the best explanation', involves examining a set of data or circumstances and formulating a hypothesis that best accounts for those facts. This is the primary reasoning method used in medicine (diagnostics) and detective work… The 'best' explanation is limited by the observer's current knowledge and imagination."

### Implications

- Diagnostic and troubleshooting work (medical, technical, or otherwise) is structurally abductive, not deductive—the goal is the best available account, not a proof, so holding it loosely is correct practice, not a weakness.
