## Science Is Trustworthy Because Its Structure Corrects for Bias, Not Because Scientists Are Less Biased

Given that [[Human Cognition Evolved for Social Survival, Not for Tracking Objective Truth|cognition evolved for belonging and survival rather than accuracy]] [depends_on:: [[Human Cognition Evolved for Social Survival, Not for Tracking Objective Truth]]], no individual—including a scientist—escapes that machinery by force of will or expertise. What makes science preferable to a belief system built on the same fallible cognition is not that its practitioners are more objective people. It is that the method is deliberately built never to rest on anyone's individual objectivity:

1. It is falsifiable. A scientific claim states in advance what evidence would count against it, rather than being defended by inventing new excuses when challenged.
2. It relies on evidence rather than on the confidence with which a belief is held—the same subjective feeling of certainty that a flat-earther and a physicist both experience is not what settles the question.
3. It is never treated as definitively, finally true. Every result stays open to revision, which means the system is designed to find and correct its own errors rather than to protect them.

These three properties mean a community of equally bias-prone people can still converge on reliable knowledge, because the correction happens at the level of the system—open publication, adversarial replication, peer review, competing labs with an incentive to prove each other wrong—not at the level of any single mind being unusually rational.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to institutional science with functioning peer review, open publication, and competitive incentives to overturn established results. It is a claim about why the method is structurally trustworthy, not a claim that every individual finding, scientist, or institution calling itself "scientific" meets this bar in practice.

### Implications

- Preferring science is not the same as trusting scientists to be right or unbiased; it is trusting a process that does not require them to be.
- A claim is not owed trust merely for having a credentialed source behind it—see [[Trusting a Scientific Authority Feels Psychologically Identical to Trusting a False One]]. What earns trust is the falsifiable, evidence-based, revisable structure, which is checkable independently of any one person's authority.
- The same reasoning gives a concrete answer to "why prefer science over a system some successful, sincere people believe instead": the rival system is judged by whether it shares these structural properties, not by comparing whose adherents feel more confident.
