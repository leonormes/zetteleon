## Considering Consequences Before Acting Reduces Avoidable Long-Term Risks

Considering likely consequences before acting reduces avoidable risks and improves long-term outcomes. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- Deliberation has costs, and delay can itself be a risk.

### Evidence

> "Prudent individuals are farsighted and deliberative."
> (Seligman 2011, "Appendix: Signature Strengths Test", PDF p. 251) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/294/EPUB?open_at=toc:Appendix:%20Signature%20Strengths%20Test)

The quotation describes prudent people and does not measure outcomes. The effect on risk comes from the earlier Prudence note and is my own reasoning.

### Falsifier

- If deliberating before comparable choices does not reduce later avoidable harm, the claim is wrong.
