## Students Rate Identical Feedback Higher When They Believe It Comes from a Human

Students perceive the utility and quality of identical feedback as significantly higher when told it originates from a human instructor rather than an AI, regardless of the actual content. The source attribution—not the substance—drives the perceived value. This is a social judgement, not a content judgement: the relational signal (a human invested time in my work) carries independent value that the information alone cannot replicate.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to instructional feedback and student motivation dynamics. The finding concerns perceived quality and motivational impact, not objective quality of the feedback itself. The effect size may vary with task domain, student age, and the degree of personal relationship with the instructor.

### Evidence

> "students rated identical feedback significantly higher when told it originated from a human instructor rather than an AI [21:38]"

### Implications

- Human-in-the-loop remains critical for the perceived legitimacy of evaluation—AI feedback may be informationally equivalent but relationally deficient.
- Learning remains a fundamentally social process; the relational dimension of feedback (someone cared enough to evaluate me) is a distinct input from the informational dimension (here is what was wrong).
