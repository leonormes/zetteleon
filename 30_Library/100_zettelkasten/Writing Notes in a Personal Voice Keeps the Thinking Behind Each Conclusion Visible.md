## Writing Notes in a Personal Voice Keeps the Thinking Behind Each Conclusion Visible

PKM notes written in a personal first-person voice, with the questions and feelings left in, keep the thinking behind each conclusion visible to the writer later. An academic register tends to strip that out and leave only the result. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- Applies to notes that record your own thinking, such as journals, workbench notes and the commentary around an idea.
- It does not require feelings in every note. A claim card can state its proposition plainly and keep the voice for scope, doubts and reasons.
- Practical forms are writing as I, keeping unanswered questions, and recording reactions such as confusion or surprise.
- Voice is a different test from own words. Own words checks comprehension, while voice keeps the path by which you got there.

### Evidence

> "This is the personal connection that every reader wants to make with a writer; if we care about the writer we'll follow him into subjects that we could have sworn we never wanted to know about."
> (Zinsser and Zinsser 1988, ch. 7, PDF p. 97) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/747/EPUB)

> "Such humanity is so rare in the prose of academics that it glitters when it appears"
> (Zinsser and Zinsser 1988, ch. 10, PDF p. 151) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/747/EPUB)

Zinsser is writing about prose for other readers, so these quotations support only the value of a human voice. The claim that a personal voice keeps your own reasoning visible in private notes is my own reasoning.

### Implications

- Keep the question that prompted a note, even before it has an answer.
- Record doubt or surprise when it explains why the idea mattered.
- A note collection written this way is a record of discovery as well as a store of results.

### Falsifier

- Return after some months to matched notes written in a personal and in an impersonal register. If you reconstruct the reasoning no better from the personal ones, the claim is wrong.
