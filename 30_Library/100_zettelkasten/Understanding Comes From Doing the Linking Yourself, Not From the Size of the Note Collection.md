## Understanding Comes From Doing the Linking Yourself, Not From the Size of the Note Collection

Understanding in a zettelkasten comes from doing the thinking and linking yourself, not from the size or density of the note collection. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- Applies where the aim of the notes is to understand ideas. A collection kept only for lookup can be useful at any size.
- The linking counts only when you have to decide why two ideas connect.
- The notes support the thinking. This claim does not say they play no part in it.

### Evidence

> "Writing, taking notes and thinking about how ideas connect is exactly the kind of elaboration that is needed to learn."
> (Ahrens 2022, ch. 10, PDF p. 126) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/704/EPUB?open_at=toc:10%20Read%20for%20Understanding)

> "Making these connections deliberately means building up a self-supporting network of interconnected ideas and facts that work reciprocally as cues for each other."
> (Ahrens 2022, ch. 11, PDF p. 138) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/704/EPUB?open_at=toc:11%20Take%20Smart%20Notes)

Ahrens supports learning through deliberate connection. That the size or density of the collection is poor evidence of understanding is my own reasoning.

[supports:: [[The sophistication is a bug not a feature]], confidence=medium]

### Implications

- A large, densely linked graph view is not an achievement in itself.
- More automation, tagging or graph sprawl can happen without any idea being better understood.
- Judge a link by whether you can say why it exists.

### Falsifier

- Compare people who make and justify their own links with people given an equally large set of ready-made links. If they understand and apply the ideas no better, the claim is wrong.

### Steelman

> "I, of course, do not think everything by myself."
> (Ahrens 2022, ch. 1, PDF p. 28) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/704/EPUB?open_at=toc:1%20Everything%20You%20Need%20to%20Know)

Ahrens reports this as the answer Luhmann gave about his productivity, adding that the thinking happened mainly within the slip-box. On this view the collection is a partner in thought, so its size and structure may matter more than this claim allows.
