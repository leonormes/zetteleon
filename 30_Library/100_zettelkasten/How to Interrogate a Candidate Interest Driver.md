## How to Interrogate a Candidate Interest Driver

For any interest or interest bundle, test a proposed psychological driver by working through seven questions in sequence: (1) what does this interest let me do, experience, or avoid; (2) what moment within it feels most alive or satisfying; (3) what elements are shared across otherwise unrelated interests; (4) which similar-looking activities fail to hold me, and what is missing from them; (5) what conditions make the interest stronger or weaker; (6) what would I predict I would enjoy next if this driver were real; (7) what evidence would make me downgrade the explanation.

### Scope & Conditions

Trigger—a candidate driver (e.g. "love of pattern") has been proposed to explain an interest bundle and needs testing rather than just narrative plausibility. Verification—the driver survives if a predicted next-interest is later confirmed and no strong disconfirming case turns up.

### Evidence

> "For any interest bundle, ask: 1. What does each interest let me _do_, experience, or avoid?… 7. What evidence would make me downgrade the explanation?"; "if you hypothesise that maths and music are both driven by 'love of pattern,' test whether you also enjoy pattern-rich activities that lack mastery progression, aesthetic satisfaction, or personal relevance. If not, 'pattern' may be too broad."

### Implications

- Turns a plausible-sounding driver narrative into a falsifiable claim before it is trusted.
- The worked example (testing "pattern" against non-mastery, non-aesthetic pattern-rich activities) is the template for narrowing an over-broad candidate driver into a more specific bundle.
