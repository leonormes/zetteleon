## Negative Partisanship

Negative partisanship is a mode of political identity in which a voter's allegiance is driven more by fear, hatred, and distrust of the opposing party than by genuine affection for their own party's platform or positions.

### Scope & Conditions

A downstream consequence of [[Affective Polarization]]; becomes the dominant driver of voting behaviour under high polarization, overshadowing assessments of candidate quality or competence.

### Evidence

> "A voter's political identity is driven significantly more by an intense hatred, fear, and distrust of the opposing party than by any genuine affection for their own party's platform."

### Implications

- Voting becomes a defensive act against the out-group rather than an endorsement of the in-group's platform.

### Related

- [[Claim - Beliefs often function as belonging cues to secure group membership rather than as truth-seeking theses]]—extends: negative partisanship is that belonging-cue dynamic expressed specifically as opposition to an out-group rather than affiliation with an in-group.
- [[Affective Polarization]]—shared mechanism: negative partisanship is the behavioural/identity consequence of affective polarization's emotional asymmetry.
