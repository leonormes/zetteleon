Habit 4: Think Win-Win is the first habit of the Public Victory and the foundation for effective interpersonal leadership. It is a frame of mind and heart that constantly seeks mutual benefit in all human interactions. Win-Win means that agreements or solutions are mutually beneficial and satisfying. It builds on the self-mastery of the Private Victory—you cannot negotiate from abundance until you've done the work of [[Habit 3 - Put First Things First]]. _New connection: this backward link wasn't present in the note before._ [extends:: [[Habit 3 - Put First Things First]], confidence=medium]

This habit is built upon the Abundance Mentality—the paradigm that there is plenty out there for everybody. It stands in contrast to the Scarcity Mentality, which sees life as a zero-sum game.

## The Six Paradigms of Human Interaction

1. Win-Win: The only truly viable option in interdependent realities.
2. Win-Lose: The authoritarian approach: "I get my way; you don't get yours."
3. Lose-Win: The "doormat" paradigm: "I lose, you win." This is worse than Win-Lose because it has no standards.
4. Lose-Lose: The philosophy of a highly dependent person, or of war.
5. Win: Thinking only of getting what you want, without regard for others.
6. Win-Win or No Deal: If a mutually beneficial solution can't be found, you agree to disagree agreeably. No deal.
