title: Never Return an Item to the In-Tray Once Picked Up for Clarification
---

Once an item has been picked up from the "in" container for clarification, it must not be returned without a decision having been made about its fate. Returning an item undecided resets the cognitive work already invested, adds it back to the pile to be reconsidered again, and—crucially—drains psychological energy through repeated non-decision. The no-return rule eliminates the loop of processing the same ambiguous items multiple times.

## Scope & Conditions

Applies from the moment an item is physically or digitally "picked up" for clarification. The rule protects the entire clarification session from becoming a cycle of deferral. Acceptable exits for an item are: Trash, Reference, Incubate (Someday/Maybe), or advancement to an actionable state—never back into "in."

## Evidence

> "Never put anything back into 'in': Returning an item to your 'in' tray without deciding its fate drains psychological fuel and creates 'decision fatigue'."

## Implications

- Eliminates the loop of re-processing the same ambiguous items; each item is processed exactly once per cycle.
- Protects psychological energy by forcing a binary decision—the discomfort of deciding is always less than the cumulative cost of repeated non-decision.
