## Worth Debating Splits Into Factual Resolution and Relational or Philosophical Value, and Conflating Them Causes Confusion

"Worth debating" carries two different senses that are easy to conflate: worth debating to establish factual truth (where testability, per Alder's razor, matters), and worth discussing for philosophical, emotional, relational, or imaginative value (where it doesn't)—treating an untestable question as pointless, or a factual dispute as merely a matter of perspective, both come from collapsing this distinction.

### Scope & Conditions

Surfaces as an apparent tension whenever a testability-based filter (like Alder's razor) is paired with an argument that unresolvable questions can still be worth exploring—the tension dissolves once the two senses of "worth" are named separately.

### Evidence

> "Alder's razor recommends setting aside claims that cannot be tested, while the conclusion argues that unresolvable questions can still be worth discussing for the sake of wonder, reflection, and mutual understanding. This is not necessarily a contradiction, but it means the video is using 'worth debating' in two different senses."

### Implications

- Before applying a testability filter to end a conversation, check which sense of "worth" is actually at stake—factual resolution or something else—since the filter only bears on the first.
