## Communication Is Coordination Through Reconstruction, Not Direct Transmission

Language does not transfer thoughts directly from one brain to another; it transmits instructions for reconstruction. Words act as external stimuli that the listener decodes through their unique [[Experiential Filter]]—past history, culture, and values—to build a new meaning in their own mind.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to all natural-language communication between human minds, where meaning is constructed locally by each participant rather than copied across a wire. Formal machine protocols can copy bitstreams losslessly, but human interpretation of those symbols remains reconstructive.

### Evidence

This claim synthesises the vault's communication and cognitive-reconstruction notes rather than quoting a single external source, grounded in [[Words Are Pointers, Not Containers of Meaning]] and [[Knowledge Transmission Is Reconstruction, Not Copying]].

### Implications

- Repeating the same words louder or more slowly cannot fix a failed transmission if the listener lacks the experiential referents needed to reconstruct the intended meaning.
- Effective communicators design stimuli for the listener's reconstruction process rather than assuming their own mental state has been packaged into the words.
