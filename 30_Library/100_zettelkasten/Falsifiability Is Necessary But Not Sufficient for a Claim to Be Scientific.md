## Falsifiability Is Necessary But Not Sufficient for a Claim to Be Scientific

Popper's falsifiability criterion is a genuinely useful discipline—try to state in advance what would prove your claim wrong—but treating it as the _whole_ test for what counts as science overstates it. A claim can technically admit a falsifying observation and still be poor science if the measurement is uncalibrated, the causal design doesn't rule out confounders, the statistical reasoning is weak, the claim is incoherent with established evidence, or it has never been exposed to community scrutiny.

### Scope & Conditions

Qualifies, rather than replaces, the Popperian demarcation criterion. Falsifiability remains the property that makes a claim testable at all; this note names the further conditions needed for a test of it to actually be good science.

### Implications

- Passing "is this falsifiable?" is a necessary check, not a certificate of scientific quality.
- When evaluating a claim, ask about measurement quality and causal design in addition to whether it is falsifiable in principle.
