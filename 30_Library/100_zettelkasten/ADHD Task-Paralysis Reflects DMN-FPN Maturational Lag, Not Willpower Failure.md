### ADHD Task-Paralysis Reflects DMN-FPN Maturational Lag, Not Willpower Failure
Large-sample resting-state fMRI research, including the ADHD-200 sample (n=757), shows ADHD brains have diminished anticorrelation between the Default Mode Network and Task Positive Network, with measurable maturational delay in DMN connections to the frontoparietal network (FPN). This partial co-firing, rather than full TPN engagement or full DMN suppression, is the direct neuroanatomical cause of task-paralysis.

#### Scope & Conditions
Grounded in multi-site resting-state fMRI studies of ADHD populations. The FPN is specifically implicated because it generates task-control signals for unpractised situations, so its immature connectivity impairs the brain's ability to configure itself for a new task on demand.

#### Evidence
> "ADHD is characterised by diminished anticorrelation, including increased DMN–VAN connectivity and DMN–FPN maturational lag... Task-paralysis is a direct consequence of partial co-firing: neither full TPN engagement nor full DMN suppression is achieved."

#### Implications
- Task-paralysis should be treated as a measurable network-topology failure requiring environmental/behavioural workarounds, not a motivation problem solved by willpower or guilt.
- Interventions should target the transition into TPN-dominant states directly (somatic triggers, implementation intentions) rather than trying to suppress DMN rumination through effort alone.
