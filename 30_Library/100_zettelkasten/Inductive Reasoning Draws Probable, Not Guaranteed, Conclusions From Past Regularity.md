## Inductive Reasoning Draws Probable, Not Guaranteed, Conclusions From Past Regularity

Inductive reasoning uses past experience to infer future outcomes, on the premise that nature is generally predictable—but unlike deductive reasoning, an inductive conclusion is only ever probable, never logically guaranteed, and can be overturned if the underlying circumstances change or new evidence emerges.

### Scope & Conditions

Completes the standard reasoning triad alongside deduction (guaranteed conclusions from premises) and abduction (best available explanation)—distinct in what kind of conclusion each is entitled to claim.

### Evidence

> "Inductive arguments use past experiences to infer future outcomes, functioning on the premise that nature is generally predictable. Unlike deductive reasoning, inductive conclusions are only ever probable, not logically guaranteed… Even highly probable inductive conclusions can be proven false if the underlying circumstances change or new evidence emerges."

### Implications

- Treating an inductive conclusion (e.g. "this has always worked before") with the same confidence as a deductive one is a category error that overstates what past regularity can license.
