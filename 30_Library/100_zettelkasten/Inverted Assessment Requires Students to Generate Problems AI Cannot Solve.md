## Inverted Assessment Requires Students to Generate Problems AI Cannot Solve

To verify comprehension in an age of AI-generated answers, invert the assessment: require students to generate complex problems that AI cannot solve, then defend the logic of those problems in an oral examination. The student demonstrates mastery by showing they can navigate the frontier of AI capability—which requires deeper understanding than merely solving a problem that AI can also solve.

### Scope & Conditions

A high-rigour alternative to standard problem-solving assignments. Highly resource-intensive due to the requirement for oral examination with qualified instructors. Scales poorly without high instructor-to-student ratios. Most effective in STEM and formal reasoning domains where "AI cannot solve" has a clear operational meaning.

> "Inverting the Assessment: The method of having students invent mathematical problems that AI cannot solve, followed by oral examinations [09:36]"

### Implications

- Effectively neutralises automated submission via LLMs by requiring the student to demonstrate understanding at the boundary of AI capability.
- The cognitive requirement for generating a problem AI cannot solve is categorically higher than solving a problem AI can—it requires a mental model of both the domain and the limits of AI.
