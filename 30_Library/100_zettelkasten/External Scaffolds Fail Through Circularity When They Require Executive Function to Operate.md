## External Scaffolds Fail Through Circularity When They Require Executive Function to Operate

Most purported "cognitive prosthetics" fail for adult ADHD because they are circular: they demand the very executive functions—remembering to check, maintaining lists, resisting snooze blindness, and initiating action—that ADHD impairs. Unguided mental-health and productivity apps illustrate this collapse with median 15-day retention around 3.9%, and offloaded systems are abandoned first under stress or fatigue.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to standalone pull-based tools (task managers, uncoached apps, manual calendars) when used without external human accountability or automated push triggers. Does not deny that cognitive offloading helps once a person is actively engaged or supported by a coach.

### Evidence

This claim synthesises §1.3 of [[SoT - Prosthetic Executive Function]], citing Baumel et al. (2019) on app retention, Risko and Gilbert (2016) on metacognitive offloading cost, NICE NG87 guidelines, and [[Task Management Systems Have Limited Efficacy for ADHD Productivity]].

### Implications

- App abandonment should be treated as a structural mismatch with EF-dependent pull design rather than a personal moral failure.
- Any scaffold claiming to compensate for executive dysfunction must be audited for how much daily executive function it consumes to stay alive.
