### Graded Exposure Reduces a Safety Behaviour by Ranking Situations From Mildly to Strongly Uncomfortable and Working Upward

To reduce a safety behaviour without abandoning genuine safeguards, build a hierarchy of situations ranked from modestly to strongly uncomfortable, each reducing one specific safety behaviour while retaining real controls, and work upward — starting at a level that creates discomfort but remains safe and manageable, then repeating each level until anxiety becomes more manageable before advancing.

#### Scope & Conditions

Trigger — a specific, named safety behaviour (over-preparation, reassurance-seeking, checking) has been identified and needs reducing. Verification — anxiety at a given level becomes more manageable with repetition before moving to the next; NHS graded-exposure guidance recommends beginning with the lowest-ranked feared situation.

#### Evidence

> "Create a hierarchy, from modestly uncomfortable to strongly uncomfortable situations where you reduce one safety behaviour but retain real safeguards... NHS graded-exposure guidance recommends beginning with the lowest-ranked feared situation, repeating it until anxiety becomes more manageable, and then working upward."

#### Implications

- Attempting to skip straight to the hardest instance of a safety behaviour (e.g. a high-stakes production change) rather than starting low on the hierarchy is a common way graded exposure gets abandoned.
