## Storage in a Shared Household Should Fit the Visibility and Detail Preferences of Each User

People differ on whether they need items visible or hidden and whether they sort broadly or finely, so one house style will suit some members and fail others. Each zone should match its main user, with a stated compromise where a space is shared. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to shared homes where members have different cognitive or sensory needs. It is an inference from the two axes of the taxonomy, not a claim made by the source.

### Evidence

This is my own inference from [[Clutterbug Storage Taxonomy]] and the two tensions between its axes. The source chat only offered to explore combining styles for shared households and gave no evidence, so confidence is low.

### Implications

- A parent's preferred style should not be assumed to suit a child with different needs.
- Shared spaces such as the kitchen need an explicit compromise.
