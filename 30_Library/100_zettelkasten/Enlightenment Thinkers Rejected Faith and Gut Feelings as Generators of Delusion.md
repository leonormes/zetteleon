## Enlightenment Thinkers Rejected Faith and Gut Feelings as Generators of Delusion

Enlightenment thinkers insisted on applying the standard of reason and not falling back on generators of delusion such as faith, dogma, authority and gut feelings. The source is Steven Pinker, Enlightenment Now.

### Scope & Conditions

Pinker's account of what the Enlightenment thinkers had in common; the full list he gives is faith, dogma, revelation, authority, charisma, mysticism, divination, visions, gut feelings and the hermeneutic parsing of sacred texts.

### Evidence

> "If there's anything the Enlightenment thinkers had in common, it was an insistence that we energetically apply the standard of reason to understanding our world, and not fall back on generators of delusion like faith, dogma, revelation, authority, charisma, mysticism, divination, visions, gut feelings, or the hermeneutic parsing of sacred texts."
> (Enlightenment Now, Chapter 1: Dare to Understand!)

### Implications

- Faith, authority and intuition are treated as sources of error, not as alternative standards of justification.
- Reason is the standard against which beliefs are checked, not one input among equals.
