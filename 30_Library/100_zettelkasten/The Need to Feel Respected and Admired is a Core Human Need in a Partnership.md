Summary: A fundamental human need often prioritized in long-term relationships is the need to feel respected and admired by one's partner.

Details: This is not about being put on a pedestal, but about feeling competent and trusted in one's domains, having one's efforts noticed and valued, and feeling that one's partner still respects and chooses them.

## Tensions

- The title joins two things: respect and admiration. [[Mutual Respect in a Partnership Involves Valuing Opinions Speaking Kindly and Honouring Boundaries]] treats respect as a baseline owed to any partner, but admiration is earned appreciation of particular qualities and can be withheld without disrespect. The note does not say whether the need is for the baseline, the appreciation, or both. UNSURE: the note is a bare assertion with no source; the sociometer edge supports the general mechanism, not the specific claim about partnerships.
- Sociometer theory says rejection lowers self-esteem more than acceptance raises it. If that asymmetry holds here, the absence of respect costs more than its presence gives, which would make this need behave more like a floor than a goal. Interpretive, not tested in the vault.
