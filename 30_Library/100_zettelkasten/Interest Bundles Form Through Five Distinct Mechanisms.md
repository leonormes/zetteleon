## Interest Bundles Form Through Five Distinct Mechanisms

Interests that co-occur in one person (an "interest bundle") can be explained by at least five distinct, non-exclusive mechanisms: shared cognitive ingredients (the activities reward the same kind of mental operation, e.g. pattern detection), shared reward dynamics (the same challenge → feedback → improvement loop), shared values or drivers (the activities satisfy the same underlying want, e.g. mastery or belonging), shared environments and social pathways (co-exposure via household, school, peers, or online communities), and identity/network effects (adopting one identity lowers the friction for trying adjacent domains).

### Scope & Conditions

A bundle is evidence worth investigating, not proof of a single cause. Absence of an interest is informative but ambiguous—it can mean genuine lack of fit, or lack of access, wrong timing, or a high entry cost, so absence alone should not be read as disproof of a candidate driver.

### Evidence

> "There are at least five mechanisms… A bundle is not proof of one cause, but it is a useful clue… absence is informative but ambiguous—it can also mean 'I never had access,' 'I met it at the wrong time,' or 'the entry cost was too high.'"

### Implications

- Before proposing a psychological "driver" to explain a bundle, first check whether shared environment or shared cognitive demands already explain it more cheaply.
- Gives a checklist for auditing any interest cluster: which of the five mechanisms actually applies here, and is more than one operating at once?
