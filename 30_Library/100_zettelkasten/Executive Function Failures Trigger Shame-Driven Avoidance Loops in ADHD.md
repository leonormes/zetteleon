## Executive Function Failures Trigger Shame-Driven Avoidance Loops in ADHD

Executive-function slips—such as forgetting a commitment or missing a deadline—frequently trigger a self-reinforcing shame-procrastination cycle. Interpreting a neurological capacity limit as a moral flaw ("I am broken or lazy") creates anticipatory anxiety toward the next task, driving avoidance for short-term mood repair and further confirming the negative self-belief. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies when executive-function difficulties are evaluated through moralised character labels rather than capacity constraints. Self-compassion and externalised scaffolding interrupt the cycle at step 2 by reframing failures as empty working memory rather than bad character.

### Evidence

This claim synthesises §2.1 of [[SoT - ADHD Neurology & Core Concepts]] and connects to [[Misinterpretation as Character Flaws]], [[Productive vs Destructive Shame]], and [[Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD)]].

### Implications

- Addressing chronic procrastination requires breaking the shame-avoidance emotional loop, not adding harsher self-criticism.
- Reframing setbacks as hardware capacity limits neutralises destructive shame without excusing action.
