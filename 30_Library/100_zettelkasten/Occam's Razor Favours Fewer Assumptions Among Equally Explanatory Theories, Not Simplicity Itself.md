### Occam's Razor Favours Fewer Assumptions Among Equally Explanatory Theories, Not Simplicity Itself

Occam's razor states that when competing explanations account for the available evidence equally well, the one requiring fewer additional assumptions is generally preferable — it does not mean the simplest explanation is always true, and complexity is not automatically evidence against a theory.

#### Scope & Conditions

Only discriminates between explanations that already fit the evidence comparably; a more complex explanation is preferable when the evidence actually requires it. Most useful when comparing explanations of similar explanatory power and predictions, not as a blanket preference for simplicity.

#### Evidence

> "when competing explanations account for the available evidence equally well, the one requiring fewer additional assumptions is generally preferable. This does not mean that the simplest explanation is always true, nor that complexity is automatically evidence against a theory."

#### Implications

- Citing "Occam's razor" to dismiss a more complex explanation is only valid if the simpler explanation actually accounts for the same evidence equally well — otherwise the razor doesn't apply.
