## Overshoot and Collapse: Delayed Negative Feedback Causes a System to Exceed Then Crash Below Its Carrying Capacity

A system undergoing exponential growth within a finite environment, where the negative feedback signalling resource scarcity is delayed, tends to overshoot its carrying capacity; the overshoot degrades the underlying resource base itself, so the system does not stabilise gently but collapses to an equilibrium permanently lower than the original carrying capacity.

### Scope & Conditions

Distinct from ordinary balancing-loop correction ([[SoT - Systems Thinking]]) specifically because the delay allows the resource base itself to be damaged before correction arrives—the target the system is trying to stabilise around has moved.

### Evidence

> "Systems that undergo unchecked exponential growth within finite environments, coupled with delayed feedback mechanisms, tend to overshoot their carrying capacity. This overshoot severely degrades the underlying resource base, leading not to a gentle stabilisation, but to a rapid systemic collapse towards a permanently diminished equilibrium."

### Implications

- A system that looks fine right up until collapse is not evidence against this mechanism—overshoot is structurally invisible until the resource base is already damaged.
