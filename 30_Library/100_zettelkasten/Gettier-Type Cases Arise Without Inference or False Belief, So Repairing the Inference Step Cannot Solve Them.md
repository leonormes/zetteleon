## Gettier-Type Cases Arise Without Inference or False Belief, So Repairing the Inference Step Cannot Solve Them

Gettier-type cases can be built in which the believer makes no inference and holds no false belief, so a repair aimed at how justification passes from one belief to another cannot solve the problem. The luck lies in the link between the evidence and the fact, not in the reasoning. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to the first repair after 1963, which blocks the original cases by denying that justification carries over to everything a justified belief entails. Two cases show its limit:

- Max sees two realistic decoy ducks on a lake and takes them for real ducks. He is a good duck-spotter, so he is justified, and there are real ducks on the far side, so he is right. He does not know it, and he drew no inference from a false premise.
- Henry drives through a county full of barn facades and sees the one real barn. He is justified and right, and he forms no false belief about it.

A defender can reply that perceptual belief hides an unconscious inference through a false premise such as "I am not being deceived". The reply is contested, and it leads to the over-strong condition in [[Requiring Knowledge to Rest on No False Belief Fails Because Trivial False Beliefs Do Not Destroy Knowledge]].

### Evidence

Two books give the cases. Martin says of Max:

> "There isn’t any inference here of the sort that occurs in the Jack/Jim case."
> (Martin 2014, ch. 3, PDF p. 37) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/298/EPUB?open_at=toc:3%20Justification%20and%20Gettier%20problems)

Nagel says of Henry:

> "Henry isn’t actually forming any false beliefs as he makes his judgement about that one real barn"
> (Nagel 2014, "Box 3 The causal theory of knowledge", PDF p. 71) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/1573/EPUB?open_at=toc:Box%203%20The%20causal%20theory%20of%20knowledge)

### Implications

- Luck has to be excluded in some other way: by the reliability of the mechanism, by what the belief tracks, or by a condition on the facts around the believer.
- The cases move attention from the justification itself to the relation between the justification and the truth.

### Falsifier

- A convincing argument that Max and Henry do know, or that their cases hide a false belief that can be identified without making ordinary knowledge impossible.
