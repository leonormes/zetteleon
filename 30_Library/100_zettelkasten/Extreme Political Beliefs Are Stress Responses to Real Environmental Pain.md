## Extreme Political Beliefs Are Stress Responses to Real Environmental Pain

Many extreme political beliefs, especially scapegoating ones, are stress responses and not positions derived by logic: real distress from a degraded environment combines with a simple narrative that misattributes its cause. The distress is real even when the diagnosis is wrong. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to scapegoating political beliefs that blame a visible outside group or factor. It is a tendency, not a law: the title is flat but the claim covers many cases, not all. It uses the ecological model, where the environment is the soil and the available narrative is the fertiliser. Examples of soil are failing public services, stagnant wages, housing shortages and lost community cohesion. Calling a diagnosis "wrong" needs its own check.

### Evidence

This is the author's own reasoning, and the examples of soil are the author's. Its first wording came from an AI conversation (Gemini, 2026-07-13) captured in a HEAD note no longer found under that name, so that origin is unverified. No source cited here shows that stress or environmental decline causes extreme beliefs. One book supports only the mechanism by which a complex failure becomes a single simple story:

> "The measure of success for System 1 is the coherence of the story it manages to create."
> (Kahneman 2012, ch. 7, PDF p. 106) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/53/EPUB?open_at=toc:7.%20A%20Machine%20for%20Jumping%20to%20Conclusions)

Fast thinking aims for a coherent story, not an accurate one, which fits a mind that settles on a single visible cause when faced with multi-variable failure.

### Implications

- Separate the distress from the diagnosis: treat the distress as real, and examine the diagnosis.
- More facts alone are unlikely to shift the belief while the underlying stress continues.
- The person has real symptoms of a degraded environment but often lacks the information to see its root causes, so the response is compassion for the distress without agreeing with the diagnosis.

### Falsifier

- Evidence that people holding extreme political beliefs show no more stress or hardship than comparison groups, or that relieving the hardship leaves the beliefs unchanged.
