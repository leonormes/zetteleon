## Minimal Components

The system should have very few moving parts. Instead of multiple apps, tags, complex folder structures, and intricate linking, it focuses on one or two core tools or methods that are incredibly straightforward.

## Intuitive Workflow

The process of capturing, organising (if at all), and retrieving information should be so obvious that it requires almost no conscious thought or recall of specific rules. If you have to stop and remember "How do I file this?" or "What tag should I use?", the friction is already too high.

## Tensions

- [[SoT - Conservation of Complexity]]—Tesler's Law says complexity cannot be destroyed, only relocated, which reads as a challenge to "aggressively reject complexity." The assumption that differs: this note targets the *user-facing friction surface*—how many tools, choices, and rules a person must hold in mind at the moment of capture, filing, or retrieval—while Conservation of Complexity targets *total system complexity accounting*, which includes structure, defaults, and automation the user never sees. Both can hold: hiding complexity behind fewer visible moving parts is compatible with that complexity persisting elsewhere in the system's design (e.g. a sparse index, per [[Keyword Index Provides Sparse Entry Points]], trades retrieval simplicity for inferential work on the reader). The same tension is already flagged inline at [[SoT - Principles for Peaceful Productivity]]'s Principle 10 (`[contradicts:: [[SoT - Conservation of Complexity]]]`, strength=2).
- [[Digital Productivity Paradox]] / [[Jevons Paradox of Attention]] / [[Low Activation Cost Effect]]—this cluster documents friction reduction *backfiring*: lowering the cost of sending a request increases the volume of incoming demand and cognitive exhaustion. The assumption that differs: those notes describe an *open, multi-party* channel (email, Slack, AI-assisted requests), where lowering friction lets other people generate more demand on your attention. This note describes a *closed, single-user* system (your own capture/organise/retrieve loop), where no other party can exploit the freed capacity. The backfire mechanism—freed capacity gets absorbed by new demand—has no external demand generator to act on in a personal PKM system, so the two claims are not in genuine conflict; they apply to different topologies of who benefits from the reduced friction.
