## Individual Interpretation Creates Different Knowledge from the Same Information

Two people can create different knowledge from the same information because knowledge formation is an active process of interpretation, not passive reception. Each person filters input through their prior experiences, existing mental models, cognitive patterns, and biases, leading to different connections and conclusions from identical source material.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to how individuals integrate, weight, and apply information within their own mental models and personal context. Does not imply that objective truth or empirical verifiability is arbitrary—only that the resulting actionable understanding differs per person.

### Evidence

This claim is derived from cognitive synthesis across the information-to-knowledge and experiential-filter notes in the vault rather than a single external quotation.

### Implications

- Sharing identical facts or documentation does not automatically align two people's mental models.
- A personal knowledge system derives its unique value from linking information to lived context rather than storing raw facts.
- Persuasion and teaching must address the listener's existing frame of reference rather than merely increasing information volume.
