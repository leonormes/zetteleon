## A Behavioural Experiment Tests a Feared Prediction Under Safe Conditions Instead of Arguing With It

Rather than trying to talk yourself out of an anxious belief, state the belief as a specific, falsifiable prediction, then design a low-risk action that tests it directly—retaining real safety controls but deliberately reducing the safety behaviour in question—and compare what was predicted against what actually happened.

### Scope & Conditions

Trigger—a recurring anxious belief (e.g. "if I act without complete understanding, I'll be exposed as incompetent") that has never actually been tested against evidence. Verification—the experiment records the specific prediction, what safety behaviour was withheld, and whether the predicted consequence occurred.

### Evidence

> "A behavioural experiment tests a prediction, rather than arguing with it endlessly… Prediction: 'The change will fail or I will be unable to cope.' Measure: Did the anticipated failure occur? Did the existing controls detect it? Could you recover?"

### Implications

- A belief that has survived years of avoidance untested is not thereby confirmed—it has simply never been checked; a single well-designed low-risk experiment can do more than years of reasoning about it.
