## Scandal Spillover Contagion Versus Contrast

A political scandal produces one of two opposite trust effects on other politicians: in low-polarization environments it spreads distrust to the whole political class (contagion), while in highly polarized, rivalrous, multilevel environments it can instead increase trust in the scandal-maker's opponents (contrast).

### Scope & Conditions

Demonstrated experimentally using the UK's "Partygate" scandal and its effect on trust in rival Scottish Parliament politicians—Westminster's norm violation increased, rather than contaminated, trust in the opposing tier of government.

### Evidence

> "Severe norm violations at one level of government did not cause a contagion of distrust toward the rival politicians. Instead, because the Westminster leader was acting so egregiously, trust and approval for the opposing Scottish politicians actively increased."

### Implications

- The same scandal can either damage or repair the reputations of unrelated politicians depending purely on the surrounding polarization level, not on anything those other politicians did.
