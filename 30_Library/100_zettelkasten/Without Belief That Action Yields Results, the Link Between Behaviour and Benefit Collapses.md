## Without Belief That Action Yields Results, the Link Between Behaviour and Benefit Collapses

When the conviction that actions will produce results is missing, the connection between behaviour and benefit collapses. People then quit well short of their true physiological or psychological limits.

### Scope & Conditions

Follows from [[The Motivation Triangle]]. The claim about quitting short of "true physiological or psychological limits" is the summary's assertion and is not backed by a cited study.

### Evidence

> "Without the foundation of belief, the connection between behavior and benefit collapses, leading people to quit far short of their true physiological or psychological limits."
> (Nir Eyal, Beyond Belief, via an LLM summary; chapter and page not captured)

### Implications

- Explains the limiting belief "Incentives alone are enough to motivate".
- Locates one cause of quitting in belief rather than in effort or reward.
