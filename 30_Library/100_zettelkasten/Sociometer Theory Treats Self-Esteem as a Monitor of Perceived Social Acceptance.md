## Sociometer Theory Treats Self-Esteem as a Monitor of Perceived Social Acceptance

Sociometer theory holds that self-esteem functions partly as a monitor of whether one is valued and included by relevant others, rather than as a standalone trait; rejection tends to lower self-esteem more strongly than acceptance raises it.

### Scope & Conditions

Does not claim every competence concern is fundamentally social, but explains why competence often becomes socially loaded (competence → reliability → respect → acceptance → felt safety in the group).

### Evidence

> "Self-esteem is also closely tied to perceived social acceptance in sociometer theory. The theory treats self-esteem partly as a monitor of whether one is valued and included by relevant others; rejection tends to lower it more strongly than acceptance raises it."

### Implications

- Gives a mechanism for why being wrong in front of others can feel disproportionately threatening: it isn't only about the error, it's a live read on the sociometer.
