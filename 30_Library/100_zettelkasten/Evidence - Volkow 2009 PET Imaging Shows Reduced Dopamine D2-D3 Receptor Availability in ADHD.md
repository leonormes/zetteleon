A PET imaging study found dopamine D2/D3 receptor availability was 11.8% lower in the caudate and 35.7% lower in the midbrain of adults with ADHD compared to controls, directly linking receptor availability to attention and motivation. Because dopamine functions as the brain's "start signal" for action, insufficient receptor availability means the signal to begin a task often fails to clear the initiation threshold—even though other cognitive processes such as thinking and analysing continue unimpaired. This gives a neurobiological basis for the "engine that keeps running while the gears fail to engage": the analytical/cognitive engine stays intact while the action-initiation pathway is impaired.

Source: Volkow ND, Wang GJ, Newcorn JH, et al. "Evaluating dopamine reward pathway in ADHD: clinical implications." *JAMA*. 2009;302(10):1084-1091.
