## Popper Held That Preferring the Best-Tested Theory Is a Rational Critical Preference, Not a Probability Claim

Popper's own reply to the induction objection is that preferring the best-tested theory is a rational critical preference. It is the theory that has survived criticism, and nothing is claimed about its being more probably true. Later defenders add that critical rationalism does say which predictions are rational.

### Scope & Conditions

This is a secondhand report from an AI chat. The chat says some defenders hold that the rational predictions are those made in ways that have so far withstood criticism and would be easy to criticise if wrong, and that demanding more justification restarts the regress. The chat also notes that one paper it does not name calls the objection the Achilles heel of Popper's method and argues that both Popper's and Musgrave's responses fail.

### Evidence

> "Popper’s own reply: preferring the best-tested theory is a rational critical preference. It’s the one that has survived criticism, with no claim that it’s more probably true."
> (AI chat transcript pasted by Leon, 2026-10-23, section "Wesley Salmon’s criticism of Popper")

> "some argue that critical rationalism does specify which predictions are rational: those made in ways that have so far withstood criticism and that would be easy to criticise if they went wrong. Demanding more justification than that restarts the regress from your last question."
> (same section)

### Implications

- The reply declines to give a reason for relying on a survivor and calls the preference rational anyway, so the dispute is over what counts as a reason.
- It makes "survived criticism" carry the weight, which is why the severity of the criticism matters.
