## Changing System Goals and Paradigms Provides Higher Leverage Than Adjusting Parameters

Interventions in complex systems differ sharply in leverage: tweaking numerical constants or parameters produces minimal lasting change, redesigning feedback loops and information flows yields moderate leverage, and changing the system goal or underlying paradigm transforms overall behavior. High-leverage interventions alter what the system is optimizing for rather than pushing harder on existing loops. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to complex adaptive, organizational, and socio-technical systems with entrenched feedback structures. While higher-leverage points produce larger behavioral shifts, they also encounter stronger institutional and cognitive resistance than parameter adjustments.

### Evidence

Extracted from [[SoT - Systems Thinking]] (Current Understanding and Section 4B, Leverage Points, summarizing Donella Meadows); no verbatim book quote is attached yet.

### Implications

- Reform efforts focused solely on budget percentages or numerical targets rarely change entrenched system outcomes.
- Clarifying or redefining a system purpose—such as shifting from short-term output to long-term sustainability—reorients downstream rules and behaviors automatically.
