## Callosotomy Can Produce Divergent Simultaneous Intentions

Summary: After the corpus callosum is severed, the two hemispheres can pursue incompatible goals at once — in one reported case the left hemisphere said it wanted to be a draftsman while the disconnected right hemisphere spelled out "racing driver."

Details: Callosotomy is the surgical severing of the corpus callosum, usually to stop severe epilepsy spreading across the brain. Once the hemispheres can no longer exchange information directly, they can express different preferences simultaneously, and in the acute post-surgical phase the hands may even work against each other. Harris uses the divergent-career case to argue that a single organism can host two distinct centres of intention, straining the idea of one indivisible will.

#### Evidence

> "a young patient's left hemisphere stated he wanted to be a 'draftsman,' while his disconnected right hemisphere used letter cards to spell out 'racing driver'."

#### Implications

- A single body can display more than one apparent locus of preference.
- The intuition of a single "chooser" is not guaranteed by the biology.
