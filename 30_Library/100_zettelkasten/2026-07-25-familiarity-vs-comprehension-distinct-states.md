Familiarity is knowing _that_ something happens—push the lever, the water goes down. Comprehension is knowing _why_ it happens—the lever lifts the flapper valve, breaking the seal, triggering the siphon. Most of daily life runs fine on familiarity alone, but familiarity fails completely the moment you need to fix, debug, or improve a system: you cannot debug something you only know by label.

## Evidence

> "Familiarity (Prediction): Knowing that X happens… Comprehension (Causality): Knowing why X happens… You cannot debug a system you only know by label."—[[SoT - Illusion of Explanatory Depth (IoED)]]

## Steelman

In many practical domains, reliable prediction is functionally sufficient—an operator who can predict system behaviour accurately may never need causal comprehension to use the system effectively, so the distinction may matter less than claimed outside debugging-specific contexts.
