title: Intellectual Growth Is Non-Rivalrous but Educational Credentials Are Positional

Two goods are being conflated under one word. Understanding is non-rivalrous: my grasp of a proof does not consume any of yours, and a population in which everyone understands more is straightforwardly better off. A credential is positional by construction: a degree signals rank, and rank is defined relationally. If everyone has a first-class degree, no one does.

Educational institutions supply both simultaneously, which is why the distinction is so easily lost. But the competitive dynamics people experience in education—the anxiety, the gaming, the ranking—belong entirely to the positional half. They are not a property of learning. They are a property of the sorting mechanism bolted onto it.

This is what licenses Hitz's egalitarian claim: intellectual life is a sanctuary _because_ the good it trades in cannot be made scarce.

## Steelman

Attention and time are rivalrous even if understanding is not, and elite institutions supply concentrated access to teachers, peers and libraries that genuinely is scarce. Treating intellectual life as costlessly available to all understates how much of it is downstream of resources that are distributed exactly as unequally as credentials are.
