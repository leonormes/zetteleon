## Over-Optimized Systems Trade Systemic Resilience and Redundancy for Fragile Efficiency

Systems that maximize narrow efficiency by eliminating slack, diversity, and redundant pathways become brittle when operating conditions shift or unexpected shocks arrive. By contrast, diverse and redundant systems appear wasteful during calm periods because they carry spare capacity, yet that buffer is what preserves systemic stability under stress. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to supply chains, software architectures, organizations, and personal workflows operating in environments with non-zero volatility or tail risks. Under perfectly predictable, static conditions, redundancy imposes pure overhead without immediate payoff.

### Evidence

Extracted from [[SoT - Systems Thinking]] (Section 5, Tensions and Gaps: Efficiency vs. Resilience); no verbatim book quote is attached yet.

### Implications

- Treating every unutilized buffer or backup pathway as pure waste increases systemic fragility.
- Designing for resilience requires explicitly valuing slack and diversity as shock absorbers rather than optimizing solely for peak throughput.
