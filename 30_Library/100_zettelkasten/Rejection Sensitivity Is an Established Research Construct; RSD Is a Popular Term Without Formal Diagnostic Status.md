## Rejection Sensitivity Is an Established Research Construct; "RSD" Is a Popular Term Without Formal Diagnostic Status

Rejection sensitivity—anxiously expecting, readily perceiving, and reacting intensely to potential rejection—is an established research construct; "Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria" (RSD) is widely used in ADHD communities to describe especially intense rejection-related pain, but it is not currently a distinct DSM diagnosis with universally accepted criteria or a standard validated assessment measure, so the lived phenomenon can be real without the specific "RSD" label being a settled clinical mechanism.

### Scope & Conditions

The useful distinction is graded: "I experience intense pain around perceived rejection" is a valid experience report; "I show a pattern consistent with rejection sensitivity" is a reasonable hypothesis; "RSD is a fully established diagnosis explaining this" overstates current evidence; "my drive is only trauma or avoidance" is also an overstatement in the other direction.

### Evidence

> "Rejection sensitivity is an established research construct… 'Rejection sensitive dysphoria' is widely used in ADHD communities… it is not currently a distinct DSM diagnosis with universally accepted diagnostic criteria or a standard validated assessment measure. The lived phenomenon may be real and important; the caution is simply not to treat the label as a fully settled, ADHD-specific scientific mechanism."

### Implications

- Using "RSD" as a personal working description of experience is reasonable; citing it as an established, distinct neurological diagnosis is not currently supported.
