## Plantinga Reformed Epistemology Holds That Belief in God Can Be Properly Basic

Plantinga argues that belief in God can be properly basic, meaning warranted without inferential evidence. He puts it in the same class as belief in other minds or in the reliability of your own memory. On this view "my belief is my evidence" is not what a sophisticated theist says.

### Scope & Conditions

This is a secondhand report from an AI chat, not read from Plantinga. The chat says the folk version, "belief is the evidence", is not what most theists claim, and that philosophers of religion appeal to arguments, testimony and religious experience. Attacking the folk version is, in the chat's words, attacking a straw man.

### Evidence

> "Plantinga’s reformed epistemology. He argues belief in God can be properly basic: warranted without inferential evidence, in the same way as belief in other minds or in the reliability of your memory."
> (AI chat transcript pasted by Leon, 2026-10-23, section "Is there knowledge about God, or only belief?")

> "Most theists don’t say “belief is the evidence.” That’s the folk version. Philosophers of religion appeal to arguments, testimony and religious experience. Attacking the folk version is attacking a straw man."
> (same section)

### Implications

- An argument that targets "belief as its own evidence" does not touch this position.
- If other minds and memory are accepted as basic, the question becomes why belief in God is excluded from the class, which is a separate argument.
