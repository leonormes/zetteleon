## Each Decision About Where an Item Goes, Which Category It Belongs To and How to Reach Its Container Drains Executive Bandwidth

Every placement decision, about where an item goes, which category fits it and how to reach its container, draws on executive bandwidth. When that bandwidth is low, the item stays on a surface instead. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to routine put-away in a home, most strongly for people with executive-function difficulties. The size of the drain is an assumption of the source, not a measured figure.

### Evidence

> "Every time an individual must decide *where* an item goes, *which* sub-category it belongs to, or *how* to physically access its container, it drains prefrontal executive bandwidth."
> (AI chat summary pasted 2026-11-24, not checked against a primary source)

### Implications

- Storage design should remove placement decisions, not only add capacity.
- A failed put-away can be a decision-load problem, not a motivation problem.

### Falsifier

- If adding categories and compartments made put-away no slower or less likely to be abandoned, the claim would be wrong.
