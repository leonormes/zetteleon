## Latour Treats Scientific Facts as Socially Constructed Through Networks of Power

Latour argued that empirical "truth" is often just a resource scientists use in debates to gain status, and that scientific facts are socially constructed through networks of power and cooperation.

### Scope & Conditions

A stronger, more constructivist sociology-of-science claim than Merton's; truth itself, not just norms, is explained sociologically.

### Evidence

> "Latour pushed further, arguing that the empirical 'truth' is often just a resource scientists use in debates to gain status, and that scientific facts are socially constructed through networks of power and cooperation."

### Implications

- Denies that "truth" refers to anything beyond the social process that produces consensus—a different assumption from every realist position in this same source material.

### Related

- [[Merton's Reward System Drives Scientific Norms]]—shared mechanism: both are sociology-of-science accounts, but Latour extends the sociological explanation from norms to truth itself.

### Tensions

[contradicts:: [[Deutsch Rejects Instrumentalism Because Prediction Is a Means, Not the Purpose, of Science]], confidence=medium]

- [[Deutsch Rejects Instrumentalism Because Prediction Is a Means, Not the Purpose, of Science]]—that note holds science's purpose is to explain "the fabric of reality," presupposing a truth that exists independent of social process; this note holds truth is itself a resource produced by networks of power. Not a hard contradiction: the assumption difference is whether "truth" denotes something mind-independent (realism) or is constituted by social process (constructivism)—both accounts are coherent once that assumption is named.
- [[Science's Division of Labour Lets Individual Scientists Be Dogmatic]]—that note explains scientific self-correction as adversarial testing converging on a truth the community didn't invent; this note holds the "truth" reached is itself a social product of the same adversarial process, not something the process discovers. A tension over whether convergence tracks a mind-independent fact or manufactures one, not a direct logical contradiction.
