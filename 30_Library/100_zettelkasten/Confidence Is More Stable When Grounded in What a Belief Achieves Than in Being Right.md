## Confidence Is More Stable When Grounded in What a Belief Achieves Than in Being Right

Because objective rightness is an unattainable standard, confidence rests more stably on the utility of a belief than on a brittle claim to objective truth. The leading question moves from "Is this belief true?" to "Is this belief useful, and does it lead to a better outcome?" ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to confidence in a belief or course of action where objective certainty is unavailable. Usefulness is shown by experience and evidence that the course of action is effective and aligned with the principles a person has chosen.

### Evidence

This is a conceptual synthesis drawing on pragmatist epistemology ([[Pragmatism Defines Truth by Practical Consequences]]); no direct external quotation is attached.

### Implications

- Confidence is built and revised through experience of what works, so it does not depend on first proving a belief right.
- Attention moves towards systems that work and away from abstract goals.
