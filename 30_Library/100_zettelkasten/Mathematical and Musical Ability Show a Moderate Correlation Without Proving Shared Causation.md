## Mathematical and Musical Ability Show a Moderate Correlation Without Proving Shared Causation

A moderate positive association has been found between latent mathematical and musical ability (r = 0.41), but this is a correlation, not proof that either skill causes the other; broader evidence that music instruction specifically transfers to mathematics ability is inconsistent or limited.

### Scope & Conditions

Correlational finding; do not read as evidence of a direct causal or shared-mechanism link without further qualification.

### Evidence

> "Recent work found a moderate positive association between latent mathematical and musical abilities, r = 0.41, but this is an association rather than proof that either skill directly causes the other. Other reviews find that music instruction may benefit certain spatial-temporal abilities, while broad transfer to mathematics is inconsistent or limited."

### Implications

- A commonly cited "maths and music are linked" claim should be stated as correlational, not causal.
- Useful caution against over-interpreting any single [[Interest Bundles Form Through Five Distinct Mechanisms|interest-bundle]] correlation as proof of a shared cognitive mechanism.
