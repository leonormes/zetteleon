## Catharsis Theory Is False—Venting Anger Rehearses and Reinforces Aggressive Pathways Rather Than Resolving Them

Expressing anger physically (shouting, breaking objects) does not resolve the emotion; it rehearses and reinforces the neural and behavioural pathways of aggression, contrary to the popular "catharsis" idea that anger must be let out to heal. This is supported by both Buddhist teaching and modern psychological research.

### Scope & Conditions

Debunks a widespread pop-psychology myth; aligns with established neuroscientific and psychological consensus rather than being a novel or contested claim.

### Evidence

> "Expressing anger physically (e.g., shouting, breaking objects) does not resolve the emotion but rather rehearses and reinforces the neural and behavioural pathways of aggression."

### Implications

- "Let it out to feel better" is actively counterproductive advice for anger specifically—practising the aggressive response strengthens it for next time.
