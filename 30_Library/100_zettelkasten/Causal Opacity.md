## Causal Opacity

Causal opacity is the state of a mental model in which an unexamined mechanism—a cognitive black box—is falsely tagged as transparent and understood. The opacity stays invisible during routine recognition and surfaces only when a person is forced to simulate, explain, or build the underlying causal chain. ^def

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to mental models of complex systems—mechanical, economic, or technical—where surface labels are familiar enough to suppress further enquiry. It does not describe deliberate abstraction in which an operator consciously treats a subsystem as a black box while knowing its internal mechanics are unexamined.

### Evidence

Derived as a conceptual synthesis from the heuristic-substitution mechanism in [[2026-07-25-heuristic-substitution-creates-ioed]] and the empirical gap documented in [[Evidence - Sloman and Fernbach Find the Illusion of Understanding Is Robust Across Topics]]. No primary-source quotation defines the term verbatim; it names the structural defect produced when recognition replaces causal audit.

### Implications

Because a causally opaque model is tagged as known, routine use never triggers self-correction. Debugging, repairing, or innovating on a system requires forcing the black box open through externalised explanation or construction ([[Active Creation Surfaces Hidden Gaps and Dissolves the Illusion of Explanatory Depth]]).
