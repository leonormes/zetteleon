## Hume's Fork Holds That All Valid Knowledge Is Either Analytic or Synthetic, With Nothing Else

Hume's Fork divides all valid knowledge into exactly two categories—analytic truths (logic, mathematics, definitions, which tell us nothing new about the physical world) and synthetic truths (matters of fact derived from direct experience)—and holds that knowledge of the world cannot be obtained through reason alone.

### Scope & Conditions

A historically specific epistemological claim, not a linguistic law; one of the most contested binaries in epistemology (see Kant's rebuttal).

### Evidence

> "All valid knowledge falls into exactly two categories: analytic truths (logic, mathematics, definitions, which tell us nothing new about the physical world) and synthetic truths (matters of fact derived from direct experience). Knowledge of the world cannot be obtained through reason alone."

### Implications

- A claim that fits neither category (not definitional, not experientially verifiable) is, on this view, not knowledge at all—which is the basis for the impressions test.
