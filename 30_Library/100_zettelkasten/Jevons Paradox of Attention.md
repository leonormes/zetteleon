## Jevons Paradox of Attention

The Jevons Paradox of Attention states that increased efficiency in digital communication and AI tools leads to an increase in the total consumption of human attention. As tools make it easier and faster to solicit attention (e.g., through low-friction messaging), the resulting efficiency does not create more free time; instead, it generates new "demands" that fill the freed-up capacity, making attention an even scarcer resource.

### Scope & Conditions

A specific application of resource economics to digital knowledge work and the attention economy.

### Evidence

> "The 'Digital Productivity Paradox' described is a specific application of the Jevons Paradox… to human attention."

### Implications

- Tool-driven efficiency can be counter-productive if it results in a net increase in the volume of distractions and requests.
- Protecting attention requires structural constraints rather than simply adopting more efficient tools.
