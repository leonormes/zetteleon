## Archery Shot Process - Faults and Diagnostics

This note aggregates the common archery errors, their mechanical causes (including "leading causes"), and suggestions for correction derived from the complete Archery Shot Process system.

### 1. Vertical Spread (Scattering Downrange)

- Leading Cause: Head position or anchor shift ([[Archery Shot Process - Anchor]]). Even a few millimetres of shift drastically alters the "rear sight" of the archer.
  - _Validation Note:_ This is the exclusive leading cause of vertical spread across the system.
  - _Suggestion:_ Press the index finger firmly against the jawbone with no gaps for bone-to-bone contact. Establish and maintain a fixed, pre-set head position.
- Cause: Changing finger pressure distribution on the string ([[Archery Shot Process - Setup]]).
  - _Suggestion:_ Maintain a consistent finger hook (deep in the first joint) and lock forearm flexor tension.

### 2. Lateral Spread (Left or Right Misses)

- Cause: Grip torque (twisting the bow hand right or left) ([[Archery Shot Process - Setup]]).
  - _Suggestion:_ Keep fingers completely relaxed ("jelly fingers", "sick sparrow"). Form a relaxed Y-shape with the bottom three fingers curled in. Never use a "death grip" wrapping all four fingers around the riser.
- Cause: Off-Center Pressure in the grip ([[Archery Shot Process - Setup]]).
  - _Suggestion:_ Direct pressure perfectly down the center line of the lower grip, directly behind the wrist joint.
- Cause: Rushing the release and lacking back tension (letting go using only arm and finger muscles) ([[Archery Shot Process - Expansion]]).
  - _Suggestion:_ Squeeze the draw elbow around the spine, ensuring continuous expansion through the clicker.
- Cause: Floating head position (bow not remaining vertical) ([[Archery Shot Process - Anchor]]).
  - _Suggestion:_ Keep the head perfectly still against a vertical reference. Ensure the string touches exact reference points (center/right of nose and chin).

### 3. Plucking (Draw hAnd pUlls outward away from fAce)

- Cause: "Feathering" (hovering on the edge of the jaw with zero backward load) ([[Archery Shot Process - Anchor]]).
  - _Suggestion:_ Settle firmly into the anchor with active backward pressure against the jawline.
- Cause: Hooking on the fingertips (introduces massive isometric tension on sensitive skin pads) ([[Archery Shot Process - Setup]]).
  - _Suggestion:_ Set a deep hook (close to the joint lines) to place the string nearer the skeletal line of force.
- Cause: Excess tension in the hand, forearm, and biceps ([[Archery Shot Process - Release]]).
  - _Suggestion:_ Transfer load to the scapular retractors (back muscles). Treat the release as a passive relaxation of the fingers.

### 4. Collapsing (Draw hand/elbow mOves fOrward)

- Cause: Stopping the pulling motion / freezing right before the shot (fatiguing against bow weight) ([[Archery Shot Process - Expansion]] & [[Archery Shot Process - Release]]).
  - _Suggestion:_ Maintain two-way fluid motion (pushing toward the target and pulling backward). The release must be a dynamic continuation.
- Cause: Slipping hook causing sudden loss of tension ([[Archery Shot Process - Setup]]).
  - _Suggestion:_ Keep flexor tension locked on the hook until the exact moment of passive release.
- Cause: Front shoulder yielding backward (Shoulder Collapse at Full Draw) ([[Archery Shot Process - Pre-draw]]).
  - _Suggestion:_ Reach and extend the bow shoulder continuously towards the target. Establish true skeletal alignment ("Barrel of the Gun") via torso rotation during pre-draw.

### 5. Floating Anchor Point & Inconsistent Draw Length

- Cause: Rushing the anchor / shooting too fast ([[Archery Shot Process - Anchor]]).
  - _Suggestion:_ Take time to feel tight, consistent facial contact before releasing. Settle into the anchor in one fluid motion.
- Cause: Allowing the front shoulder to roll or yield backward ([[Archery Shot Process - Pre-draw]]).
  - _Suggestion:_ Rotate the torso to lock the skeletal structure. Do not just push the shoulder forward.

### 6. Erratic Arrow Flight & String Interference

- Cause: Side Anchor (anchoring too deep on the face), which physically blocks the string's natural inward oscillation upon release ([[Archery Shot Process - Anchor]]).
  - _Suggestion:_ Maintain a slightly forward anchor point with minimal face contact.
- Cause: Subconscious Flinching of the bow arm inward to avoid being hit ([[Archery Shot Process - Follow-through]]).
  - _Suggestion:_ Trust natural bow rotation. Applying center-line grip pressure causes the bow's weight distribution to naturally spin and pull slightly right (for RH archers) providing total clearance.
- Cause: String Slap against the forearm due to a straight, fully wrapped "death grip" locking the arm's range of motion ([[Archery Shot Process - Setup]]).
  - _Suggestion:_ Change grip to a relaxed 45-degree V-shape and internally rotate the bow arm to naturally roll the elbow out of the string's path.

### 7. Wide, "Flappy" Release (Hand fLies oUtward)

- Cause: Actively using extensor muscles to claw or force the hand open ([[Archery Shot Process - Release]]).
  - _Suggestion:_ Slowly relax the fingers while continuously pulling backward with shoulder tension. Allow the bowstring to push the passive fingers out of the way.
