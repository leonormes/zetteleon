## Process the Top Item First and One at a Time to Prevent Emergency Scanning

When clarifying a capture container, always process the top item first and move through items one at a time. "Emergency scanning"—scanning the inbox for urgent or interesting items to process out of order—is a failure mode that creates distraction, undermines the habit of making firm decisions, and allows less attractive items to accumulate indefinitely. Sequential, top-first processing forces equal attention on every item and prevents the cognitive switching overhead of constantly re-evaluating which item to process next.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies during any dedicated clarifying session for all items in a capture container. The rule is an operating constraint, not a moral absolute—genuine emergencies may warrant exception—but the default must be strict to prevent "emergency scanning" from becoming the habit.

### Evidence

> "Process the top item first… Process one item at a time: This prevents distracting context-switching and forces the requisite attention to make a firm decision."

### Implications

- Treating all items as equal prevents urgent or attractive items from causing chronic stagnation of everything else.
- Focusing cognitive resources on a single discrete decision at a time produces better, firmer decisions than multi-tracking.
