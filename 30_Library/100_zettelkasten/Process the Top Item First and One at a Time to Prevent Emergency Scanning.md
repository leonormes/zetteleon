## Clarifying a Capture Container in Fixed Order Prevents Selective Processing

Clarifying a capture container in a fixed order (such as oldest-first or queue order), without choosing which item to tackle next, prevents the selective scanning through which urgent or attractive items crowd out less appealing items and leave them to accumulate. This claim governs item ordering and no-selection, not single-item concurrency or inter-type context switching. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- **Applies:** During dedicated clarifying sessions over a bounded capture container using a fixed ordering rule (note: in newest-first views, "top-first" becomes last-in-first-out and starves older items unless ordered oldest-first or drained completely).
- **Emergencies excluded by definition:** Urgent scanning belongs outside the clarifying session; scanning an inbox for emergencies is triage, not clarifying.
- **Weakens or fails:** When a time-critical item is buried in a very large backlog, when items are strongly batchable by context or type ([[Task Batching is Grouping Similar Tasks to Reduce Context Switching]]), or when backlog size exceeds single-session capacity.

### Evidence

> "Process the top item first… Process one item at a time: This prevents distracting context-switching and forces the requisite attention to make a firm decision."

- **Annotation:** Unverified, ellipsis-stitched quote with no source citation in the vault. For fixed-order processing, it supports only the narrower claim that every item must receive the requisite attention to reach a decision. *(No Calibre book quotation attached yet.)*

### Implications

- **No-selection rule:** Require every item in the fixed queue to receive a decision rather than cherry-picking by urgency or appeal; spin off concurrency and decision-quality claims separately.
