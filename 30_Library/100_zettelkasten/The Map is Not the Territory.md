## The Map is Not the Territory

Summary: The mental model "The Map is Not the Territory" states that our mental models, beliefs, and perceptions are simplified maps of reality, not reality itself. [depends_on:: [[The noumenal-phenomenal distinction limits knowability]]] [extends:: [[Maps as Simplified Abstractions]]]

Details: This concept, coined by Alfred Korzybski, is a fundamental principle of intellectual humility. It acknowledges that our understanding of the world is always an abstraction and is therefore inherently incomplete and potentially flawed. [extends:: [[Mistaking the Map for the Territory]]] The goal is not to create a perfect map, but to create a useful one and, crucially, to remember to update the map whenever we encounter new terrain (evidence) that contradicts it. [extends:: [[Map Analogy Illustrates Abstraction Tradeoffs]]] This reduces the pressure to be "right" and increases adaptability.
