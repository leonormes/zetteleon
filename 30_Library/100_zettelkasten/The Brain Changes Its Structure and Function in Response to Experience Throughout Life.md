## The Brain Changes Its Structure and Function in Response to Experience Throughout Life

The brain alters its physical structure and function in response to experience, and this [[Neuroplasticity|neuroplasticity]] continues throughout life. Learning is therefore the brain reshaping itself, and a person is not fixed by early neural wiring. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- Changes are usually tiny and cumulative over years; dramatic cortical remapping (such as blind visual cortex processing sound) is rare.
- Upper neural regions are far more plastic than lower ones regulating bodily functions (Hanson).
- Asserts lifelong plasticity, not that every change helps or every adult trait alters easily.
- Concept definition: [[Neuroplasticity]]; synaptic mechanism: [[Mental Activity Strengthens Connections Between Neurons That Fire Together, So Learning Physically Changes the Brain]].

### Evidence

Barrett states that synaptic changes continue across life:

> "These changes are examples of what scientists call plasticity, and they occur throughout your life."
> (Barrett 2019, PDF p. 34) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/466/EPUB)

Hanson notes their typical scale:

> "Usually, the results are tiny, incremental alternations in neural structure that add up as the years go by."
> (Hanson 2015, ch. 4, PDF p. 75) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/358/EPUB?open_at=toc:Chapter%204.%20Taking%20in%20the%20Good)

Textbook summaries, not primary studies.

### Implications

- Sustained practice reshapes the brain, so early wiring does not fix skill or habit ([[Self-Change Refutes the Determinism of the Plant Metaphor]]).
- Study methods succeed by driving neural change, favouring active techniques ([[SoT - Active Learning Techniques]]).
- Indiscriminate adaptation lets harmful habits settle in alongside deliberate learning.

### Falsifier

- Evidence that adult brain structure and function remain static despite experience, or that learning occurs without neural change.
