## The Methods of Science Are a Paradigm of How to Achieve Reliable Knowledge

The methods of science, which are skepticism, fallibilism, open debate and empirical testing, are a paradigm of how to achieve reliable knowledge. The source is Steven Pinker, Enlightenment Now.

### Scope & Conditions

Pinker's reading of what the Enlightenment thinkers took from the escape from ignorance and superstition, which showed how mistaken conventional wisdom could be.

### Evidence

> "To the Enlightenment thinkers the escape from ignorance and superstition showed how mistaken our conventional wisdom could be, and how the methods of science—skepticism, fallibilism, open debate, and empirical testing—are a paradigm of how to achieve reliable knowledge."
> (Enlightenment Now, Chapter 1: Dare to Understand!)

### Implications

- Reliability comes from the method, not from who holds the belief.
- Open debate and skepticism are treated as parts of the method, not threats to it.
