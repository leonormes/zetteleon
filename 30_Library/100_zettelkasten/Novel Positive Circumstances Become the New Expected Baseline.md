## Novel Positive Circumstances Become the New Expected Baseline

Hedonic adaptation occurs because once-novel positive circumstances become the new expected baseline against which subsequent outcomes are judged. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies primarily to circumstantial and positional improvements (income, possessions, completed milestones) once they stabilise. Adaptation is slower or incomplete for severe negative events (such as chronic disability or long-term unemployment) and for varied intrinsic activities.

### Evidence

Extracted as the causal mechanism from [[Hedonic Adaptation to Achieved Goals Is a Biological Certainty, Not a Character Failing]] and reinforced by reward-prediction-error neuroscience in [[Dopamine Neurons Encode Reward Prediction Error, Not Pleasure]], where fully predicted outcomes cease generating a reward signal.

### Implications

- Once a gain is normalised into the expected baseline, maintaining the status quo feels neutral rather than rewarding, while losing it feels like a loss.
- Useful as a standalone premise for arguments about lifestyle creep, reward prediction error, and why external milestones fail to satisfy permanently.
