## Split-Brain Confabulation Reveals a Post-Hoc Interpreter

Summary: When a word is flashed only to a split-brain patient's right hemisphere, the speaking left hemisphere reports seeing nothing, yet on being shown the correct object it invents a plausible reason for the choice—revealing that the verbal self narrates behaviour it did not cause.

Details: In the classic demonstration, "EGG" is shown to the right hemisphere; the patient's left hand (right hemisphere) selects an egg from behind a partition while the left hemisphere, which controls speech, denies having seen anything. Asked _why_ the egg was chosen, the left hemisphere confabulates—"I had eggs for breakfast." The mechanism is the reverse of introspection: the speaking self does not access the true cause and instead manufactures a coherent story after the fact. This is Harris's central empirical hook for arguing that our sense of a single narrating self is a construction.

### Evidence

> "the speaking left hemisphere will 'confabulate' a plausible excuse, such as having had eggs for breakfast."

### Implications

- Verbal self-reports about one's own motives can be fabrications, not readouts.
- The felt unity of "I decided" can survive even when the deciding was done elsewhere.
