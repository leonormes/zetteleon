## Cognitive-Emotional Feedback Loop

The Cognitive-Emotional Feedback Loop is a self-reinforcing psychological cycle in which emotion shapes a mental model, the mental model filters how reality is perceived, the filtered perception triggers matching emotion, and that emotion further validates and entrenches the model. Because the feeling confirms the model and the model predicts the feeling, biases, phobias, and avoidance spirals can sustain themselves without external reinforcement. ^def

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to affective cognition, belief entrenchment, anxiety, and self-reinforcing behavioral patterns. The loop remains closed until an external or deliberate counter-signal—such as a strong prediction error—interrupts the cycle.

### Evidence

Extracted from [[SoT - Systems Thinking]] (Section 4A, The Cognitive-Emotional Feedback Loop); no verbatim book quote is attached yet.

### Implications

- Explaining away a phobia or cognitive bias with pure logic often fails because the emotional signal continually re-validates the underlying model.
- Breaking an entrenched loop requires generating experiences that produce unmistakable prediction error rather than relying on abstract reassurance.
