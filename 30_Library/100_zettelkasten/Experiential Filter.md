## Experiential Filter

An experiential filter is the unique interpretive lens formed by a person's past history, culture, and values through which every linguistic stimulus is decoded into meaning. Because no two people share an identical life history, every listener constructs a slightly—or radically—different meaning from the same words.

### Scope & Conditions

Operates at both the individual level (personal memories, expertise, emotional associations) and the collective level, where culture acts as a massive shared experiential filter governing norms such as eye contact, silence, and directness.

### Implications

- Two listeners can hear the exact same sentence and walk away with incompatible conclusions without either acting in bad faith.
- Cross-cultural and cross-disciplinary communication fails predictably when participants treat their own filter as neutral or universal.
