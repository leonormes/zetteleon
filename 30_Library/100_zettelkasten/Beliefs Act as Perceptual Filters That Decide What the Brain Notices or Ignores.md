## Beliefs Act as Perceptual Filters That Decide What the Brain Notices or Ignores

Because not all environmental stimuli can be processed, beliefs act as perceptual filters that dictate what the brain notices or ignores. Changing a belief shifts the attentional spotlight and can reveal opportunities, solutions or relationship nuances that were previously invisible.

### Scope & Conditions

The first of the source's three powers of practical beliefs, Attention (the power to see), alongside [[Beliefs Set Expectations That Shape Felt Experience Through the Experience Loop]] and [[Beliefs Foster an Internal Locus of Control That Sustains Perseverance in Uncertain Environments]].

### Evidence

> "Beliefs act as perceptual filters. Because we cannot process all environmental stimuli, our beliefs dictate what our brain notices or ignores."
> (Nir Eyal, Beyond Belief, via an LLM summary; chapter and page not captured)

### Implications

- Changing a belief can surface solutions that were invisible under the old one.
