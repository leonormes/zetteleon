## A Drive Explains Why a Domain Holds Attention and Is a Different Kind of Node From a Theme

A theme is a structural, descriptive mechanism found in the knowledge itself, such as feedback. A drive is a hypothesised psychological explanation for why certain themes keep recurring, such as competence. A faceted PKM schema that adds a drive facet to domain and theme must keep the two as separate kinds of node, even where they overlap.

### Scope & Conditions

Domain and theme classify what a note is about. A drive explains why a domain holds attention, so it is a hypothesis about the person and not a property of the notes. Not every theme is a candidate driver, and not every candidate driver reduces to one theme: a single drive can operate through several different themes at once.

### Evidence

> "A theme such as feedback may connect archery and DevOps conceptually. A drive such as competence may help explain why feedback-rich activities hold attention. Theme and drive can overlap, but they are not the same kind of node."
> (A Portable Interest and PKM Knowledge Graph, project note)

### Implications

- The Portable Interest project treats conflating theme and drive as a modelling error, not a simplification.
- A candidate can sit ambiguously across the line. First-Principles Modelling is currently handled as a candidate driver because it purports to explain the pull of several domains, not only to describe a pattern in them.
