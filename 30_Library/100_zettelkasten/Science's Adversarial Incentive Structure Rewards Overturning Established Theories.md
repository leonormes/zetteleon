## Science's Adversarial Incentive Structure Rewards Overturning Established Theories

Science is not a unified establishment protecting the status quo—it is a highly competitive arena. Researchers are professionally and financially rewarded for successfully tearing down established ideas, so when a consensus forms, it is because many hyper-competitive attempts to break the idea have failed, not because everyone agreed to hold hands.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to institutional science with functioning peer review and career incentives for novel results.

### Evidence

> "If a young physicist could definitively prove that Einstein was wrong about gravity, they wouldn't be silenced—they would win a Nobel Prize, secure lifelong funding, and go down in history."

### Implications

- Consensus survival is evidence of having withstood adversarial attack, not evidence of conformity.
- A field's incentive structure is itself diagnostic of whether its consensus can be trusted.
