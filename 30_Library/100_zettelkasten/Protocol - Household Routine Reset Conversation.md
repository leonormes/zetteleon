> Trigger: a household routine is stuck in a stalemate between the parents, and one parent wants to propose a fresh joint start.

## Minimum Viable Understanding (MVU)

Run this when a routine has become a standoff. Repair first, then agree one small joint change, then review it. Every step is yes or no, so it works under stress. The reasoning lives in the linked claims, not here.

---

## 1. Preconditions

Do not start until each line is true.

- [ ] It is not bedtime and not straight after a row. See [[De-escalating Partner Conversations Requires Redirecting Drift Without Naming Fallacies Aloud]].
- [ ] I have stopped any withdrawal of shared upkeep and resumed my share. See [[Withdrawing Household Cooperation as Leverage Turns System Disagreements Into Conditional Penalties]].
- [ ] I have written my ownership sentence with no "but" (the slot in [[Household Routines Proposal]] §1).
- [ ] I have checked who carries the load for the routine in question, and I am not asking for more from the person already carrying most of it. See [[Shared Responsibility in a Partnership Includes Physical Mental and Emotional Labor]] and [[Protocol - Household Zone Standards & Pet Care SOPs]] §2.
- [ ] If I plan to describe a pattern, I have three dated incidents written down. See [[Broad Behavioural Pattern Claims Require Concrete Dated Incidents to Be Falsifiable]].

---

## 2. Steps

1. [ ] Open with the ownership sentence. Say nothing else first.
2. [ ] Ask "what do you think is going wrong?" Restate her answer in my words and get a yes before I speak.
3. [ ] Offer the shared-goals list as a question. Ask which she wants and what she would add.
4. [ ] State the proposal as data, impact, proposal, check ([[SoT - The Data-Impact-Proposal-Check Framework]]).
5. [ ] Propose one routine element only, jointly chosen, for two weeks. See [[Household Routine Changes Should Be Small Joint and Reversible Experiments]].
6. [ ] Agree what "held consistently by both of us" means, in writing, before the trial starts.
7. [ ] Let her pick one thing she wants from me. Agree it.
8. [ ] Agree the review date and write it in the family calendar.
9. [ ] Agree enforcement before switching anything on. No unilateral device or rule changes. See [[Shared Parental Agreement Must Precede Unilateral Routine Enforcement]].

## 3. Verification

- [ ] One routine element is agreed, with a start date, a review date and a written meaning of "held consistently".
- [ ] She picked one thing, and it is on my list.
- [ ] I resumed my share of upkeep before the conversation, not as a trade for it.
- [ ] At the review, both of us say whether the change worked, and the answer is recorded against the claim it tests. For a bedtime routine that claim is [[Consistent Calm Bedtime Routines Reduce Childhood Bedtime Conflict]].
