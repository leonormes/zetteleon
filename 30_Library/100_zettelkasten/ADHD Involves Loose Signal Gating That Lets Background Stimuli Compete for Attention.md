## ADHD Involves Loose Signal Gating That Lets Background Stimuli Compete for Attention

Unlike neurotypical sensory and attentional filtering that suppresses background noise automatically, the ADHD brain operates with loose signal gating. Competing sights, sounds, and internal thoughts arrive with equal priority, requiring continuous manual effort to maintain focus on a chosen target. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to everyday sensory and cognitive environments where multiple cues compete for working memory. High-interest or hyperfocus states can temporarily narrow the gate, though often at the cost of missing external time or context cues.

### Evidence

This claim synthesises the Signal Gating hardware constraint in §1.1 of [[SoT - ADHD Neurology & Core Concepts]] and aligns with [[ADHD Involves an Inability to Regulate Attention]] and [[ADHD Causes Sustained Attention Deficits Requiring Higher Cortical Activation]].

### Implications

- Relying on willpower to ignore a noisy environment drains executive capacity rapidly; environmental friction removal is far cheaper.
- Loose gating explains why open-plan distractions or unmuted notifications derail task continuity even when motivation is high.
