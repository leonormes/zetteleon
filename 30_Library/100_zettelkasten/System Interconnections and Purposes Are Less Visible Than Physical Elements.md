## System Interconnections and Purposes Are Less Visible Than Physical Elements

A system consists of elements, interconnections, and a purpose, yet its physical elements are far easier to see and measure than the information flows and emergent purposes that actually drive its behavior. Because interconnections and goals are largely invisible, observers default to adjusting visible parts even when real leverage lies in relationships and purpose. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies whenever human observers diagnose complex organizations, software architectures, or social systems using surface inspection. Physical or interface changes are immediately tangible, whereas shifts in incentives, information delays, or implicit goals require longitudinal observation of actual behavior.

### Evidence

Extracted from [[SoT - Systems Thinking]] (Working Knowledge and Section 5, The Visibility Problem); no verbatim book quote is attached yet.

### Implications

- System audits should map information flows and observed behavior rather than inventorying components alone.
- A system's true purpose must be inferred from what it consistently produces rather than from stated mission statements.
