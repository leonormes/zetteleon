## Writing in Own Words Distinguishes Comprehension from the Illusion of Knowledge

Rebuilding an idea in your own words tests whether you understand it or only recognise it. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- Applies to ideas with reasons or mechanisms that can be explained. A practical or tacit skill may be real and still hard to put into words.
- The test counts only if you rebuild the idea without leaning on the wording of the source.
- It is a test of understanding. Whether rephrasing also deepens learning is a separate claim.

### Evidence

> "It is the simplest test: We tend to think we understand what we read – until we try to rewrite it in our own words."
> (Ahrens 2022, ch. 8, PDF p. 89) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/704/EPUB?open_at=toc:8%20Let%20the%20Work%20Carry%20You%20Forward)

> "Before trying to explain something, people feel they have a reasonable level of understanding; after explaining, they don’t."
> (Sloman and Fernbach 2017, ch. 1, PDF p. 23) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/707/EPUB?open_at=toc:ONE%20What%20We%20Know)

### Implications

- Treat a feeling of recognition as untested until you have explained the idea.

![[Gaps in an Own-Words Explanation Show Where Understanding Is Missing#^claim]]

### Falsifier

- Compare people who rebuild an idea in their own words with people who only reread it. If success at rebuilding predicts performance on new problems no better than confidence after rereading, the claim is wrong.
