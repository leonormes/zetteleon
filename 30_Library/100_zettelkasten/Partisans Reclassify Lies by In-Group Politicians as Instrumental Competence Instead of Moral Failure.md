## Partisans Reclassify Lies by In-Group Politicians as Instrumental Competence Instead of Moral Failure

When partisans see their side as locked in an existential contest with an extreme opponent, they relabel a politician's lies from a moral failing into a skill needed to win. The lie is no longer judged as dishonesty, so it escapes the moral penalty it would normally carry. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to voters who see the other side as a serious threat and the contest as one that must be won. It is a proposed mechanism, not an observed one: it says how an in-group lie escapes punishment, and the same outcome could come from other routes, such as ignoring the lie. It is about how the act is judged, not whether the lie is noticed. Nothing here shows it holds for politicians outside the voter's own side or for people outside politics.

### Evidence

The claim comes from an AI research summary (titled "Political Contrast Effect Research") whose wording could not be traced to a book, so the mechanism is unverified. One book supports only the general point that conclusions are reached first and justified afterwards, which is what a relabelling would be:

> "Intuitions come first, strategic reasoning second."
> (Haidt 2012, "Introduction", PDF p. 12) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/1639/EPUB?open_at=toc:Introduction)

Haidt does not describe this particular relabelling.

### Implications

- The loyal supporter may sincerely call a lie "competence" and not "dishonesty", so a charge of hypocrisy misses how they see it.
- Naming the relabelling directly, by asking whether they would call the same act a skill if the other side did it, tests it more than quoting the lie again.

### Falsifier

- Evidence that partisans who excuse in-group lies still describe them as moral failings, so they excuse without relabelling, or that the relabelling does not appear under high threat.
