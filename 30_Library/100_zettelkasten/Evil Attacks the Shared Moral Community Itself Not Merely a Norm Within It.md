## Evil Attacks the Shared Moral Community Itself Not Merely a Norm Within It

Ordinary wrongdoing violates particular norms _within_ a shared moral framework—it is a transgression that the framework itself is designed to address. Evil differs by attacking the _framework itself_—the foundational assumption of common humanity and mutual recognition that makes moral community possible. This is why evil produces horror and not merely condemnation: it threatens the conditions under which moral response is possible at all.

### Scope & Conditions

A philosophical distinction of degree and target rather than kind. The claim is that evil's distinctiveness lies in its target—not a rule within the moral system but the preconditions of the system's existence. Confidence is medium—this is a phenomenological/philosophical observation rather than an empirical claim.

### Evidence

> "Ordinary wrongdoing violates particular norms within a shared moral framework; evil attacks the framework itself, the basic assumption that we share a common humanity."

### Implications

- The characteristic emotional response to evil (horror, not just condemnation) is explained by the attack on foundational social assumptions—it reveals the fragility of the moral order itself.
- Evil's social destructiveness extends beyond its direct victims to destabilise the community's capacity for mutual trust and recognition.
