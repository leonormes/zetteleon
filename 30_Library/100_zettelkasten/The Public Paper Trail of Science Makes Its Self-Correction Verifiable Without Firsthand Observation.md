## The Public Paper Trail of Science Makes Its Self-Correction Verifiable Without Firsthand Observation

You don't have to have sat in a lab to trust that scientific self-correction happens, because it leaves a massive public trail. Researchers who disagree publish competing papers, publicly replicate and debunk each other's work, and leave visible peer-review comments on pre-print servers and databases of retracted papers. Science is, in this sense, the open-source repository of human knowledge—its "commit history" is entirely visible.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to institutional science with open publication norms. The analogy is deliberate: open-source software offers the same reassurance via public issue trackers, without requiring anyone to have watched the maintainers write the code.

### Evidence

> "Science is the open-source repository of human knowledge… You can go online right now and read the brutal peer-review comments on pre-print servers, or look at databases of retracted papers where flawed research was caught and publicly thrown out."

### Implications

- Trusting the process doesn't require trusting individual scientists' character—the trail is independently checkable by anyone.
- Systems lacking this visible correction trail (dogma, closed groups) structurally cannot offer the same reassurance, regardless of how sincere their adherents are.
