## Recording One Idea Per Note Trains Succinct Writing in Plain Language

The habit of recording one idea per note trains a writer to express ideas succinctly and in plain language. The source is Dan Allosso, How to Make Notes and Write.

### Scope & Conditions

Allosso's claim about the effect of the habit on the writer, as distinct from its effect on retrieval or connection.

### Evidence

> "Getting in the habit of recording one idea per note makes the ideas easier to find, and trains us to write succinctly, in plain language."
> (Allosso, How to Make Notes and Write, chapter on writing as thinking)

### Implications

- The constraint is a writing exercise as well as a filing rule.
- Succinct notes are cheaper to reread and to reuse.
