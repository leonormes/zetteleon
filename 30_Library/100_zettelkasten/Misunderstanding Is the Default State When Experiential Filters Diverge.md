## Misunderstanding Is the Default State When Experiential Filters Diverge

Misunderstanding is the default state of communication, not an anomaly, because it arises automatically whenever the experiential filters of speaker and listener diverge. Speakers experience an illusion of clarity—the Curse of Knowledge—because their own words feel transparent against the rich tacit context in their own head, while cultural and experiential differences lead listeners to decode the same signal differently.

### Scope & Conditions

Holds whenever speakers and listeners differ in expertise, cultural norms (such as directness, silence, or eye contact), or personal history. Routine exchanges within tightly coupled communities can mask this baseline because long-shared habits temporarily align their filters.

### Evidence

This claim synthesises the vault's communication and cognitive-bias notes rather than quoting a single external source, grounded in [[Communicating Depends on Modelling What the Listener Already Knows]] and [[Communication Is Inherently Lossy Like Image Compression]].

### Implications

- Communicators should treat successful alignment as an achievement requiring active verification rather than an effortless baseline.
- Assuming you have been misunderstood until proven otherwise prevents silent divergence from compounding into later conflict or execution errors.
