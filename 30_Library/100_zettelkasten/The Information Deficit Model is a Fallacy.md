## The Information Deficit Model is a Fallacy

The "information deficit model"—the assumption that providing people with more facts or clearer explanations will cause them to change their behaviour—is a consistently debunked fallacy. While information is necessary, it does not bridge the intention-action gap; interventions that redesign context, environmental cues, friction, and shared systems outperform repeated explanation.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to sustained behaviour change in habits, household coordination, and interpersonal dynamics where stress, cognitive load, or competing priorities already exist. Information alone only moves behaviour when two conditions hold: high certainty (trust in the source and model) and immediate urgency of consequences.

### Evidence

This claim is derived from behavioural-science synthesis across the habit-design, choice-architecture, and household-governance notes in the vault rather than a single external quotation.

### Implications

- Explaining harder or repeating logical arguments to a partner rarely shifts household or financial habits, and often triggers defensiveness when received as lecturing.
- Replace in-the-moment persuasion with environmental defaults, low-friction choice architecture, and pre-agreed systems negotiated away from conflict.
- Check whether resistance stems from an emotional/stress state or differing experiential filters before offering any system fix.
