## Chronic Wanting and Constant Striving Fail to Produce Lasting Fulfilment

Chronic wanting and constant striving fail to produce lasting fulfilment. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies primarily to extrinsic, positional striving (wealth, status, image, external validation) rather than intrinsic, mastery-driven engagement (such as deliberate skill practice or contribution). Rests on an implicit bridging assumption about what fulfilment requires (specifically, whether lasting fulfilment is incompatible with ongoing desire) and notes that hedonic adaptation partially erodes rather than completely resets every outcome.

### Evidence

Conclusion supported by three separable premises in the graph: partial reward decay through hedonic adaptation (supply side), paired with convergent desire-replenishment from external systems and habitual comparison (demand side).

### Implications

- Separating the conclusion from its three premises lets each mechanism be tested, refined, or reused independently in other arguments.
- Distinguishing extrinsic treadmill striving from intrinsic mastery prevents over-generalising the failure of fulfilment to all effort.

### Falsifier

- Longitudinal evidence that extrinsic, positional striving produces compounding, non-adapting baseline fulfilment, or that intrinsic mastery striving also undergoes full hedonic reset.
