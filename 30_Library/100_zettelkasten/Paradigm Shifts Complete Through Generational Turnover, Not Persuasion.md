## Paradigm Shifts Complete Through Generational Turnover, Not Persuasion

Kuhn famously noted that a new scientific truth does not triumph by convincing its opponents—it triumphs because its opponents eventually die, and a new generation grows up already familiar with it. Paradigms often change "one funeral at a time." The established guard, whose careers and reputations are built on the old model, tend to resist; the shift is instead catalysed by a younger or adjacent generation adopting the new framework because it offers a cleaner, more productive way to work.

### Scope & Conditions

A corollary of [[Kuhn's Four-Stage Model of Paradigm Collapse]]; describes the sociological mechanism by which the Model Revolution stage actually completes, once a viable replacement exists (see [[A Paradigm Only Collapses When a Viable Replacement Exists]]).

### Evidence

> "a new scientific truth does not triumph by convincing its opponents, but rather because its opponents eventually die, and a new generation grows up that is familiar with it—paradigms often change 'one funeral at a time.'"

### Implications

- Expecting a paradigm shift to complete through argument alone, within the career span of its opponents, is likely to be disappointed.
