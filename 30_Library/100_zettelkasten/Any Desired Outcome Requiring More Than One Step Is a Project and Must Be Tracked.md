## Any Desired Outcome Requiring More Than One Step Is a Project and Must Be Tracked

In GTD, any commitment that takes more than one action step to complete is classified as a "Project" and must be listed on a dedicated master Projects list. This single-step threshold is a deliberate structural decision: it prevents multi-step commitments from being disguised as simple tasks, ensures every open loop above the task level has a home, and guarantees that a weekly review can verify the entire landscape of active commitments. Without this rule, complex outcomes silently disappear into vague task entries and are never completed.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies at the desired-outcome definition step within the Clarify stage, after an item has been determined to be actionable. The rule is definitional and categorical—there is no "small project" exception. Even a two-step commitment (e.g., "Draft proposal; send to client") becomes a tracked project.

### Evidence

> "If the commitment takes more than one step to complete, it becomes a 'Project' and must be tracked on a master Projects list."

### Implications

- Prevents complex commitments from being lost as single tasks—the master Projects list is a complete inventory of all multi-step open loops.
- Ensures a high-level review of all active commitments is possible; the Projects list is the unit of review in the weekly GTD reflection stage.
