## Habitual Comparison Keeps Desire Active

Habitual social comparison converts personal goals into zero-sum positional contests, keeping desire active regardless of absolute progress. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to domains governed by relative rank, status, or positional goods (credentials, income, social visibility), especially when modern networks scale comparison from small tribes to millions. Applies less to non-rivalrous, atelic mastery where progress is measured against one's own prior baseline.

### Evidence

Synthesised from vault claims on automatic social comparison, Dunbar-scale evolutionary mismatch in status anxiety, and the zero-sum nature of positional goods compared with non-rivalrous intrinsic growth.

### Implications

- Absolute gains fail to close the desire gap whenever the reference group shifts upward alongside them.
- Reusable as a demand-side premise in arguments about status anxiety, credentialism, self-compassion, and hedonic treadmills.

### Falsifier

- Evidence that upward social comparison across large reference groups does not increase dissatisfaction or renewed striving once basic needs are met.
