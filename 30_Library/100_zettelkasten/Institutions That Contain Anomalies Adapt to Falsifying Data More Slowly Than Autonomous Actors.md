## Institutions That Contain Anomalies Adapt to Falsifying Data More Slowly Than Autonomous Actors

Institutions built to contain and catalogue paradigm-breaking phenomena prioritise systemic stability and rigid categorisation over epistemic accuracy, so they adapt to falsifying data more slowly than autonomous actors. The source's example is the Institute set against the Stalkers.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to containment institutions facing paradigm-breaking anomalies. The source rates its own confidence at 8/10 but gives no basis for the rating, and the case rests on fiction, so treat the claim as a working hypothesis.

The source's counterargument: institutions supply the resources, data aggregation and peer review that turn anecdotal, local survival tactics into systematic, durable knowledge.

The source's falsification criterion: the claim fails if a heavily bureaucratised containment institution shows a consistently faster time-to-adaptation to a novel anomaly than decentralised, direct-contact operators.

### Evidence

### Implications

- Speed of adaptation and durability of knowledge can trade off against each other.
- Autonomous actors hold the freshest anomaly data but cannot store it durably.
