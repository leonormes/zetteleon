## A Next Action Must Be the Absolute Next Physical Visible Activity Required to Move a Situation Forward

A "Next Action" in GTD is not a project label, a category, or a vague directive—it is the single, absolute next physical and visible activity that must happen to move a situation forward. "Set meeting" is not a next action; "Draft email to Bob proposing three meeting times" is. The physical, visible, and immediate character of the definition is what prevents the psychological gap between intention and action—and what eliminates the executive function load of re-deciding "where do I start?" at the moment of doing.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to any item determined to be actionable during the Clarify stage. The definition must hold for both simple tasks and complex projects—a project's only executable touchpoint at any given time is its current next action. Vague or compound directives are not next actions and must be further broken down.

### Evidence

> "You must define the absolute next physical, visible activity required to move the situation forward… must be translated into the physical reality of 'draft email to Bob'."

### Implications

- Vague directives (e.g., "set meeting," "handle client issue") are rejected as incomplete—they require one more decision before they can become action.
- Reducing friction at the point of execution by pre-loading the physical entry point is the core mechanism by which GTD converts intention into reliable action.
