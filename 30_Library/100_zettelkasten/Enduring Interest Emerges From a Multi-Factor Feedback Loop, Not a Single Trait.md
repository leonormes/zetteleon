### Enduring Interest Emerges From a Multi-Factor Feedback Loop, Not a Single Trait

Enduring interest in an activity is generated by an interacting feedback system — temperament and ability, early experience, perceived competence, emotion, identity, opportunity, social reinforcement, and the kinds of reward a person finds meaningful — rather than by one hidden "true passion" or trait.

#### Scope & Conditions

Applies to explaining why the same activity produces durable engagement in one person and not another; not a claim that any single causal factor is sufficient alone.

#### Evidence

> "A person's interests are usually not driven by one hidden 'true passion' or a single trait. They emerge from a feedback system between temperament and ability, early experience, perceived competence, emotion, identity, opportunity, social reinforcement, and the kinds of rewards a person finds meaningful."

#### Implications

- Explaining "why do you like X" by naming one trait or one cause is likely incomplete.
- The same activity can be differently rewarding to different people because the felt experience of doing it differs, not because the activity itself has fixed appeal.
