## The Prefrontal Cortex Provides Executive Control Over Action

The prefrontal cortex exerts top-down executive control over attention, working memory, impulse inhibition, and goal-directed action. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

This is the standard model of human executive control, stated at the level of function rather than mechanism. The prefrontal cortex acts through distributed fronto-striatal and cerebellar circuits, so it regulates these functions without being their only seat. The claim would be weakened if the same functions were shown to run intact on non-frontal circuits alone.

### Evidence

No verbatim source quotation supports this card yet. It is the baseline premise that [[Executive Function]] and [[Prefrontal Cortex Catecholamine Insufficiency Impairs Executive Control in ADHD]] both depend on, so a neuroscience source stating it directly is still needed.

### Implications

- Executive dysfunction reflects concrete circuit and neurochemical constraints rather than lack of desire or intelligence.
- Where control over planning, initiation or emotional modulation fails, the bottleneck is a candidate for external compensation, such as offloading working memory to a scaffold.
