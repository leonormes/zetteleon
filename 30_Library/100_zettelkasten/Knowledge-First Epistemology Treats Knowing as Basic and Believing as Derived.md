## Knowledge-First Epistemology Treats Knowing as Basic and Believing as Derived

Knowledge-first epistemology, led by Timothy Williamson, treats knowing as the basic state and believing as derived from it. It therefore rejects the project of analysing knowledge as belief plus truth plus further conditions, and offers the long failure of that project as its reason. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- The note reports the position and does not endorse it. It is a minority approach to the problem that the other notes in this set try to solve by adding conditions.
- The argument turns on explanatory order. Every case of knowing is a case of believing, but belief is not the simpler building block merely because it is more common. The comparison offered is a perfect circle, which is rarer than rounded things and still more basic.
- It accepts that knowledge entails truth and belief. It denies that knowledge can be built up from them.

### Evidence

One book states the idea and names its source:

> "What if knowing is the fundamental idea, and believing is a spin-off from it?"
> (Nagel 2014, "Can knowing be analysed at all?", PDF p. 75) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/1573/EPUB?open_at=toc:Can%20knowing%20be%20analysed%20at%20all%3F)

The same passage names Williamson as the leader of the movement and gives his reason: philosophers have not produced a satisfactory analysis of knowledge in terms of belief plus further factors, possibly because the concept of knowledge is more basic than the concept of belief.

### Implications

- The Gettier problem is not solved but dissolved: no analysis is needed, so no missing condition has to be found.
- The cost is that "knowledge" is left undefined, and the approach has to show what it explains in place of a definition.

### Falsifier

- A fourth condition that is agreed to close the Gettier gap without new counterexamples, which would restore the case for analysing knowledge in terms of belief.
