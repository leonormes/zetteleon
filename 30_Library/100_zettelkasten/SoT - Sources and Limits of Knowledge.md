## Minimum Viable Understanding (MVU)

The oldest split in the subject is about where knowledge comes from. Rationalists say reason ([[Classical Rationalism Grounds Knowledge in Reason, Not the Senses]]) and empiricists say experience ([[Classical Empiricism Treats Experience as the Sole Source of Knowledge]]). Hume's Fork then limits what each can deliver, the problem of induction limits what experience can guarantee, and Kant answers by adding a third category and a boundary around what can be known at all. Modern notes in the vault carry the same limit forward as the claim that every model is a simplification.

## 1. Claim and Counter-claim, in Order

| Step | Claim | Counter-claim | What moved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source of knowledge | [[Classical Rationalism Grounds Knowledge in Reason, Not the Senses]]: the senses deceive, so true knowledge comes from reason and innate ideas. [[Descartes Requires Infallible "Clear and Distinct" Ideas for Rigorous Knowledge]] is the strict form. | [[Classical Empiricism Treats Experience as the Sole Source of Knowledge]]: the mind starts as a blank slate and experience is the only source. | The question became what experience can and cannot supply. |
| Test of meaning | [[The Impressions Test Traces an Abstract Concept Back to Its Originating Sense-Impression or Discards It as Meaningless]]: a concept with no originating impression is meaningless. | (none recorded) | A working procedure came out of empiricism. |
| Kinds of knowledge | [[Hume's Fork Holds That All Valid Knowledge Is Either Analytic or Synthetic, With Nothing Else]]: logic and definitions tell us nothing new about the world, matters of fact come from experience. | [[Kant's Synthetic A Priori Challenges Hume's Fork by Claiming a Third Knowledge Category]]: some claims are informative about the world and knowable without experience. | A third category appeared that the two-way split cannot hold. |
| Reach of experience | [[Inductive Reasoning Draws Probable, Not Guaranteed, Conclusions From Past Regularity]]: experience supports probable conclusions. | [[The Problem of Induction Means Past Regularity Cannot Logically Guarantee Future Regularity]]: no number of confirming cases proves the generalisation will keep holding. | Empirical knowledge is permanently probable, never logically certain. |
| Limit of knowability | [[The noumenal-phenomenal distinction limits knowability]]: we know reality as experienced, not reality as it is. | (none recorded) | The limit is built into the knower, not just the evidence. |

## 2. How the Limit Shows Up in the Vault's Own Thinking

- [[SoT - Reality, Models, and the Limits of Accuracy]]—_all models are wrong, some are useful: understanding consists of simplified representations._
- [[Human Understanding is Inherently Myopic]]—_limited attention and working memory give an incomplete view that we mistake for the whole._
- [[Observation Is Theory-Laden Rather Than Raw and Neutral]]—_even the data is shaped by instruments and assumptions before it reaches us._
- [[Deutsch Rejects Empiricism for Conjecture-Led Theory Formation]]—_a modern counter to empiricism: theories come from conjecture and experience only chooses between them._

## 3. Working Knowledge

- Rationalism and empiricism are better read as emphases than as a winner and a loser: the Kant step exists because neither pure form held. (inferred)
- Inductive conclusions are always revisable, which is why later positions in [[SoT - What Makes Science Knowledge]] look for ways to justify reliance without certainty.
- The impressions test is a cheap first filter for a suspicious abstract concept. (inferred)

## 4. Tensions & Gaps

- No note records a rationalist reply to the empiricists, or Plato's position beyond the one-line attribution in the rationalism note.
- The link from Hume's Fork to the problem of induction is not written down. Both are in the vault but no note says one produces the other.
- The impressions test has no counter-claim recorded, so it currently reads as settled.
