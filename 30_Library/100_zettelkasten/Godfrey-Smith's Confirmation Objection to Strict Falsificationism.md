## Godfrey-Smith's Confirmation Objection to Strict Falsificationism

Godfrey-Smith is highly critical of Popper's refusal to accept that theories can be confirmed by evidence. He points out a major flaw in strict falsificationism: if theories can never be confirmed, we have no rational basis for relying on them. He uses the example of building a bridge—if we cannot increase our confidence in a theory through testing, then it would be no more rational to build a bridge using a well-tested design than it would be to use a brand-new, completely untested design.

### Scope & Conditions

A direct critique of Popper/strict falsificationism's refusal to give confirmation any epistemic role.

### Evidence

> "if we cannot increase our confidence in a theory through testing, then it would be no more rational to build a bridge using a well-tested design than it would be to use a brand-new, completely untested design"

### Implications

- Motivates naturalistic empiricism as a repair to falsificationism, not a wholesale rejection of it.
- Sets up a direct tension with Deutsch's non-confirmation justification for the same kind of reliance.
