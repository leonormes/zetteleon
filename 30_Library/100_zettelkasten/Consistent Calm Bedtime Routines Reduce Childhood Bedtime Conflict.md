## Consistent Calm Bedtime Routines Reduce Childhood Bedtime Conflict

Holding a consistent, calm evening bedtime routine across both parents reduces bedtime conflict with children. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Hypothesis proposed for testing over a 3–4 week dual-parent trial; does not hold if underlying separation or bedtime anxiety drives resistance independently of routine structure.

### Evidence

> "Claim A–A Consistent, Calm Bedtime Routine Reduces Bedtime Conflict for Rae ... Falsifier: routine held consistently by _both_ of us for 3–4 weeks with no improvement or worse → my model is wrong or incomplete (e.g. anxiety is driving the 'mum must sleep here' demand)."
> ([[Household Routines Proposal]], §3 Claim A)

### Implications

- Both parents must pre-define what consistent adherence means before evaluating the routine.
- Persistent conflict after 3–4 weeks of consistent application points to an emotional or anxiety driver rather than a scheduling failure.
