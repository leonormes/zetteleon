## Cooperation Runs on Feelings

Human cooperation is typically intuitive: people do not reason through the logic of cooperation in order to cooperate. Automatic feelings do that thinking for them.

### Scope & Conditions

Greene's dual-process framing of moral cognition. The feelings he lists include empathy, familial love, anger, gratitude, shame, guilt, loyalty and righteous indignation. He cites his own reanalysis, with Rand and Nowak, of published Prisoner's Dilemma and Public Goods Game data in support.

### Evidence

> "According to this view of human morality, cooperation is typically intuitive. We need not reason through the logic of cooperation in order to cooperate. Instead, we have feelings that do this thinking for us."
> (Joshua Greene, Moral Tribes, Chapter 2: Moral Machinery)

### Implications

- Evolution solves the cooperation problem with automatic emotional responses.
- Moral behaviour can be fast and unreasoned, because the feelings do the thinking.
