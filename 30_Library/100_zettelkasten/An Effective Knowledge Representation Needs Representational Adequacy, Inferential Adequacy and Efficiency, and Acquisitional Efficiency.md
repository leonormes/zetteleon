## An Effective Knowledge Representation Needs Representational Adequacy, Inferential Adequacy and Efficiency, and Acquisitional Efficiency

A knowledge representation is effective to the extent that it can represent all necessary kinds of knowledge accurately, manipulate that knowledge to produce new inferences efficiently, and take in new knowledge easily.

### Scope & Conditions

The source describes classical knowledge representation and reasoning (ontologies, semantic networks, rules), not how large language models store knowledge in their weights.

### Evidence

> "Representational Adequacy: The ability to represent all necessary types of knowledge accurately."

> "Inferential Adequacy and Efficiency: The capacity to manipulate knowledge structures to produce new insights efficiently."

> "Acquisitional Efficiency: The ability to easily acquire and integrate new knowledge into existing frameworks."

### Implications

- The three properties give a checklist for judging a graph or ontology design.
- A representation can fail on any one of them independently.
