## Archery Shot Process - Setup

Summary:
Setup involves setting the hands onto the bow, including hooking onto the string and establishing the pressure point in the bow grip.

Details:
- **Order of Execution**: Always **hook the string first**, then place the hand into the bow grip. Setting the bow hand first forces you to grip tightly; setting the hook first allows the bow hand to remain relaxed.
- **Shoulder Positioning**: Keep shoulders **open** relative to the target line. Avoid aligning the shoulders prematurely in this phase (premature shoulder alignment forces an excessively high raise and relies on gravity/momentum to drop into full draw, straining the shoulder joints).
- **Mandatory Protection**: Always use a finger tab or archery glove to protect against painful and permanent nerve damage.
- **The Hook (Draw Hand)**:
  - **Hook Consistency**: Executing the *exact same hook* on every single arrow is more critical than the specific depth you use.
  - **Visual Verification**: Visually inspect your finger placement on the string during setup until a clear, repeatable physical feel is established.
  - **Hook as One Unit**: The index, middle, and ring fingers must act together as a single, relaxed unit. Avoid bulging knuckles or an angled wrist joint.
  - **Avoid Over-Tensing**: Do not apply excess finger pressure (e.g. using 20 kg of muscle force to hold a 15 kg bow). Excess tension forces you to use extensor muscles to claw your hand open upon release, ruining release consistency.
  - **Hook Depth (First Joint Sweet Spot)**:
    - *Skeletal Alignment*: Setting a deep hook (close to the joint lines) places the string nearer to the skeletal line of force, allowing you to hold full draw weight with far less physical muscular exertion compared to fingertip hooks.
    - *Avoid Too Deep (Second Joint)*: Placing the string past the first knuckle (towards the second joint) makes a clean release impossible, forcing you to uncurl two entire finger digits and leading to wide, splayed arrow flights.
    - *Avoid Too Shallow (Fingertips)*: Hooking on the very fingertips introduces massive isometric tension on sensitive skin pads. Fingertip hooks deflect the string twice as much because tense muscles cannot relax quickly, leading to sideways plucking.
    - *The Sweet Spot*: Position the string directly in the first groove of your index finger, in or slightly behind the groove on your middle finger, and on the pad (slightly in front of the groove) of your ring finger.
  - **Straight vs. Slanted Hook**: Fingers do not need to sit perfectly perpendicular to the bowstring. A slanted/diagonal hook naturally crosses near the first joint of the index finger, deeper past the middle finger joint, and across the pad of the ring finger.
  - **Wrist Relaxation (Flat Back of Hand)**: Relax your wrist so the bowstring stretches your hand flat as you draw. A clenched hand or tensed wrist creates raised knuckles, which can flick the arrow off the rest.
  - **Parallel Fingers & Finger Spacers**: Keep fingers parallel on the string to avoid squeezing or pinching the arrow nock. Use a tab with a solid finger spacer to prevent string angles at full draw from compressing your fingers and pinching the nock.
  - **Weight Distribution Nuance**:
    - There is no single universal dogmatic ratio, but the top two fingers carry the main load (e.g. 40% index, 50% middle, with the ring finger acting as a stabilizing guide at ~10%).
    - Note that weight distribution can naturally shift between fingers as you move from the initial draw phase to full draw.
    - Shifting pressure between fingers dynamically (e.g. index more than ring) alters the tune of the bow, causing significant vertical misses downrange.
  - **Tactile Sensitivity**: Keeping a rigid, unchanged hook allows your nervous system to immediately detect micro-variations (2–5%) in pressure or alignment.
  - Align fingers starting with the first groove of the middle finger. Note that all four fingers (including the pinky) affect finger tension on the string.
  - Place one finger above the arrow and two below (split finger setup).
  - Keep the pinky relaxed and neutral. The thumb should rest just below the shelf.
  - Maintain a neutral wrist.
- **The Grip (Bow Hand)**:
  - Place the 'Y' of your hand into the grip so the pressure point sits directly on the thumb pad.
  - Push into the riser using the flat bone intersection between the index finger and thumb metacarpals (the knuckle bone joint), rather than the soft webbing of the thumb.
  - Drive tension into the pit/pressure point of the bow hand towards the target.
  - Keep the thumb alongside the bow and slightly retract your knuckles.
  - Keep fingers relaxed, curling them in without squeezing or touching the bow. **Avoid the "Death Grip"**: Gripping the bow tightly introduces torque, which will twist the bow upon release and throw your arrows off target. Hold the bow as gently as you would a "sick sparrow."
  - Relax the thumb downwards, maintaining slight tension into the riser.
