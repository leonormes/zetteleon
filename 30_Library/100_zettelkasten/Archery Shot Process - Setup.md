## Archery Shot Process - Setup

Setup involves setting the hands onto the bow, including hooking onto the string and establishing the pressure point in the bow grip.

### The Steps

1. Mandatory Protection: Always use a finger tab or archery glove.
2. Clear Gear: Ensure no bulky clothing can catch the bowstring.
3. Order of Execution: Always hook the string first, then place the hand into the bow grip.
4. Shoulder Positioning & The "Barrel of the Gun": Establish the initial angular geometry of the upper body. Raise the bow and drawing arm simultaneously in a coordinated hinging motion. The rear shoulder, front shoulder, front elbow, and front wrist must form a single, unbroken straight line. Keep the draw shoulder neutral (open, free).
   - _Respiratory Synchronization (Zen Breath):_ Inhale deeply for four seconds as the bow is raised, and exhale as the setup is finalized to oxygenate blood and lower heart rate prior to loading.
5. The Hook (Draw Hand):
   - Position the string directly in the first groove of your index finger, in or slightly behind the groove on your middle finger, and on the pad (slightly in front of the groove) of your ring finger.
   - For recurve: place one finger above the arrow and two below (split finger).
   - Keep fingers parallel on the string.
   - Relax your wrist so the bowstring stretches your hand flat as you draw.
   - Keep the pinky relaxed and neutral. The thumb should rest just below the shelf.
6. The Grip (Bow Hand):
   - Firmly establish your hand placement on the bow grip before drawing.
   - Place the 'Y' of your hand into the grip so the pressure point sits directly on the thumb pad.
   - Push into the riser using the flat bone intersection between the index finger and thumb metacarpals.
   - Form a relaxed "Y" shape with the thumb pointing forward toward the target and the index finger pointing down.
   - Keep the thumb alongside the bow and slightly retract your knuckles to a 45-degree angle.
   - Keep fingers completely relaxed ("jelly fingers"), curling them in without squeezing the bow.
   - Always use a finger sling or wrist sling.
7. Pre-Tension: Apply 1-2 inches of tension on the string, engage the serratus anterior to pull the shoulder blade down, and extend the pressure point toward the target.

### Why It Works

- Setting the bow hand first forces you to grip tightly; setting the hook first allows the bow hand to remain relaxed.
- The "Barrel of the Gun" locks the skeletal frame into its strongest possible configuration before the heaviest resistance of the draw cycle is encountered, ensuring subsequent drawing utilizes massive back muscles rather than fatigable arm muscles.
- Setting a deep hook (close to the joint lines) places the string nearer to the skeletal line of force, allowing you to hold full draw weight with far less muscular exertion compared to fingertip hooks.
- Relaxing your wrist ensures raised knuckles don't flick the arrow off the rest.
- Grip torque dominates horizontal drift; torquing or twisting the bow hand right or left heavily affects arrow impact.
- Placing the hand properly on the fleshy pad of the thumb connects pressure directly through the wrist into the bow shoulder and skeletal alignment.
- A completely passive, floppy bow hand slides around in the grip, loses direction, and disconnects the bow arm from the shoulder. Proper technique requires a distinction between passive fingers and an active hand structure.
- Creating distance between the draw hand and bow side before drawing prevents compressing the draw shoulder joint and blocking efficient elbow rotation.
- A fast, aggressive, highly directed release tightens groups and brings flyers closer to center.

### Faults & Diagnostics

- Premature shoulder alignment forces an excessively high raise and relies on gravity/momentum to drop into full draw, straining the shoulder joints.
- Applying excess finger pressure forces you to use extensor muscles to claw your hand open upon release, ruining release consistency.
- Placing the string past the first knuckle makes a clean release impossible. Hooking on the very fingertips introduces massive isometric tension on sensitive skin pads and leads to sideways plucking.
- A slipping hook causes sudden loss of tension, encouraging the draw elbow to collapse forward.
- Changing finger pressure distribution on the string impacts vertical height significantly.
- The "Death Grip": Gripping the bow tightly introduces torque, twisting the bow upon release.
- Actively dragging the shoulder artificially down out of its socket creates unnecessary tension.

### Elite Archer Variant—Florian Unruh's Draw Cycle

- Sub-Chin Draw: Drawing slightly below the chin before moving upward facilitates better rotation of the draw elbow and deeper engagement of the back (scapular) muscles.
- Bent-Arm Raise: Keeping the bow arm bent and the bow close to the body during the initial raise achieves a shorter pre-draw, keeping the shoulder joint open and neutral. The biomechanical cost is that the bow hand pressure point cannot be set statically at the start of the shot.

### Prerequisites Before Technique (Physical Foundations)

- Mobility: Structural flexibility and joint range of motion to get into position (e.g. broomstick/band dislocates).
- Motor Control: Neuromuscular coordination to control and isolate specific muscle groups (e.g. Thera-band/cable rows).
- Strength: Sufficient muscular strength to launch, hold, and control the load (e.g. full-depth chin-ups).

### Training Drills

#### Overhead Video Analysis

- Purpose: Verifies the precise linearity of the upper body during setup.
- How to do it:
  1. Position a camera directly above you, looking downward as you raise the bow into the setup position.
  2. The footage should reveal a perfectly straight line extending from the bow hand through both shoulders (Barrel of the Gun).

#### Pillar Resistance Test

- Purpose: Evaluates whether the front shoulder remains low and depressed under load.
- How to do it:
  1. Place your bow hand against a solid pillar in your archery stance.
  2. Mimic the setup motion against static resistance, ensuring the shoulder does not shrug upward.
