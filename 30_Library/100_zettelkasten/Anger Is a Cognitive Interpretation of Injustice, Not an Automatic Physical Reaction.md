## Anger Is a Cognitive Interpretation of Injustice, Not an Automatic Physical Reaction

Anger stems from the mind's interpretation of an event—specifically the belief that an undeserved wrong or injustice has occurred—rather than from the event itself; this cognitive-appraisal view is supported by Aristotle, Seneca, Epictetus, and modern psychological definitions (e.g. the American Psychological Association).

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to neurotypical emotional responses; does not fully account for anger driven primarily by biological or neurological pathology.

### Evidence

> "Anger stems from the mind's interpretation of an event—specifically the belief that an undeserved wrong or injustice has occurred—rather than the event itself."

### Implications

- Since the trigger is the interpretation, not the event, changing the interpretation (cognitive reframing) is a direct lever on the anger, not a workaround.
