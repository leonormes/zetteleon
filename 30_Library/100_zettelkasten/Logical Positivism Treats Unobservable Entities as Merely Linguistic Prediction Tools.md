## Logical Positivism Treats Unobservable Entities as Merely Linguistic Prediction Tools

Logical positivists were highly suspicious of theories proposing unobservable entities (like electrons or genes), arguing those terms were merely linguistic tools for predicting observable phenomena rather than descriptions of real hidden structure.

### Scope & Conditions

A corollary of logical positivism's aim-restriction; the root of the instrumentalism/realism dispute that Deutsch's explanatory-rationalism position later argues against directly.

### Evidence

> "if a theory proposed unobservable entities (like electrons or genes), they argued those terms were merely linguistic tools for predicting observable phenomena."

### Implications

- Directly opposes Deutsch's realist claim that explanation, not prediction, is science's purpose.

### Tensions

[contradicts:: [[Deutsch Rejects Instrumentalism Because Prediction Is a Means, Not the Purpose, of Science]], confidence=medium]

- [[Deutsch Rejects Instrumentalism Because Prediction Is a Means, Not the Purpose, of Science]]—that note holds prediction is a means to science's real purpose (explaining reality); this note holds unobservable-referring terms are only ever prediction tools, with no further explanatory purpose to serve. Not a hard contradiction: the assumption difference is whether theoretical terms about unobservables carry any explanatory weight beyond their predictive function—both positions are internally coherent once that assumption is named.
