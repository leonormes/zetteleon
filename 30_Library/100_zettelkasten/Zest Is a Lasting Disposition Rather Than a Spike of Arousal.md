## Zest Is a Lasting Disposition Rather Than a Spike of Arousal

Zest is a settled tendency to engage wholeheartedly, which differs from the passing spike of arousal that a novel or stimulating event produces. A person can feel highly aroused and be running on craving without showing zest. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- Applies to patterns over weeks and across activities, so one flat or excited day settles nothing.
- A person may act wholeheartedly on a flat day. Persistent flatness would count against calling them zestful.
- Mood and stimulants can change how energetic someone feels without changing the tendency.

### Evidence

> "You are a spirited person."
> (Seligman 2011, "Appendix: Signature Strengths Test", PDF p. 259) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/294/EPUB?open_at=toc:Appendix:%20Signature%20Strengths%20Test)

Seligman describes a kind of person, which suggests a trait. The contrast with arousal spikes is my own reasoning, drawing on the distinction between tonic and phasic experience in [[SoT - Values and Eudaimonia]].

### Implications

- The fading of a novelty spike is not a loss of zest or a failure of character.

### Falsifier

- Track engagement and arousal over several months. If ratings of zest rise and fall with momentary stimulation and show no stable individual pattern, the claim is wrong.
