## Executive Inhibition Deficits and Heightened Emotional Intensity in ADHD Impair the Reflective Pause Needed to Resist Emotional Reasoning

Executive inhibition deficits and heightened emotional intensity in ADHD impair the reflective pause needed to separate subjective affect from objective fact, making it harder to resist [[Emotional Reasoning]] before reacting. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- **Epistemic scope**: Applies to inferring external facts or global self-worth from transient feelings, not to the validity of the emotional experience itself ([[Your emotions are real but they are your responsibility]]).
- **Not unique to ADHD**: Emotional reasoning is transdiagnostic across anxiety, depression, trauma, and neurotypical stress; this claim narrows only how ADHD inhibition deficits and emotional lability compound the pause bottleneck.
- **When it weakens or fails**:
  - **Calibrated somatic markers**: Weakens when affect reflects accurate pre-verbal pattern recognition rather than distortion ([[Bodily Feelings Act as Pre-Verbal Cognition]]).
  - **Over-correction**: Discounting every intense feeling can breed chronic self-doubt ([[One Wrong Instinct Read as Proof That Instincts Cannot Be Trusted Feeds a Worry Loop]]).
  - **Acute flooding**: A cognitive pause often fails under acute dysregulation without prior body-first regulation ([[How to Use the Pause to Separate the Signal From the Emotional Noise]]).
