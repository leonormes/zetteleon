## A Note Should Be Written So It Can Be Understood Out of Context

A note should be written for a reader who does not have the surrounding thoughts, with full sentences and named sources, so that it is still understandable out of context. The sources are Sönke Ahrens, How to Take Smart Notes, and Dan Allosso, How to Make Notes and Write.

### Scope & Conditions

Ahrens gives the instruction for permanent notes; Allosso applies it to his Source Notes and Point Notes. The reader may be the same person years later.

### Evidence

> "write as if you were writing for someone else: Use full sentences, disclose your sources, make references and try to be as precise, clear and brief as possible."
> (Ahrens, How to Take Smart Notes, chapter "Everything You Need to Do")

> "Source Notes and especially Point Notes should be written in a way that you will understand them out of context, without having to reacquaint yourself with the entire train of thought; or another reader will understand them."
> (Allosso, How to Make Notes and Write, chapter on writing as thinking)

### Implications

- A note that depends on the note next to it is not yet self-contained.
- Naming the source in the note keeps it usable when the surrounding material is gone.
