## Digital Refactoring Proceeds at Software Speed While Physical Robotics Lags

Digital information processing undergoes rapid "unhobbling"—a refactoring of existing digital infrastructure at the speed of software iteration cycles. Physical robotics (atoms rather than bits) lags significantly due to capital requirements, safety constraints, regulatory overhead, and the irreducible messiness of operating in the physical world. The asymmetry arises because digital mistakes are cheap and reversible; physical mistakes are expensive and sometimes catastrophic.

### Scope & Conditions

Distinguishes between bit-based (digital/information) and atom-based (physical/robotic) domains. The timescale gap is structural, not a temporary lag—physical deployment will always be constrained by capital, safety testing, and the variance of the physical world. The principle is directional: expect large digital productivity gains before widespread physical robot deployment.

### Evidence

> "Digital information processing is undergoing 'unhobbling'—a rapid refactoring of existing digital infrastructure… conversely, physical robotics (atoms) will lag significantly due to capital requirements, safety risks, and the inherent messiness of the physical world."

### Implications

- Near-term AI productivity gains will be concentrated in knowledge work and digital domains; physical workflow automation timelines are longer.
- Investment in "bit-based" automation (software, data pipelines, agentic workflows) has a shorter feedback loop and lower deployment risk than physical robotics.
