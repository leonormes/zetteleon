## Claim—Treat The System as Iterative, not Perfectable

> [!claim] Statement
> Treating a PKM/productivity system as a permanent work-in-progress—small tweaks against lived feedback—outperforms pursuing a finished "perfect" setup, and defuses both perfectionism and system-hopping.

### Supporting Evidence

- Successful ADHDers in the report describe their systems as evolving, not solved.
- Embracing imperfection lowers the emotional cost of any single lapse, which keeps engagement going.

### Mechanism

- Reframes the goal from _arrive at perfect_ (unreachable → shame) to _improve marginally_ (reachable → reward), redirecting the novelty drive into tweaks rather than migrations.

### Counter-positions

- Perpetual tweaking can itself become the procrastination—fiddling with the system instead of using it. This is the live worry in [[Q - Iterating versus system-hopping]].
- Some stability is required; a system that changes weekly is as unusable as one that is "perfect".

### Crux

- Where iteration stops being improvement and becomes avoidance dressed as productivity.

### Falsifier

- If people who froze their system entirely (no tweaks) sustained it better than iterators, the "iterate" prescription would be wrong.
