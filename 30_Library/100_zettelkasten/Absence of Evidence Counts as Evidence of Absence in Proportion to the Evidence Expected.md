## Absence of Evidence Counts as Evidence of Absence in Proportion to the Evidence Expected

The test for a negative claim is to ask what you would expect to observe if the thing existed. Missing evidence then counts against it in proportion to how much evidence you would expect. This is why "you can't prove a negative" is not a barrier to knowing that something does not exist.

### Scope & Conditions

The test works only for claims that predict observations. It gives nothing for a hypothesis built so that no evidence could ever appear, see [[A Hypothesis Built to Be Undetectable Gives Nothing to Assess, so Belief Should Be Withheld]]. The source applies it to ghosts through inference to the best explanation, and says the case weakens for anyone who rejects that form of reasoning. The idea is stated in an AI chat with no named philosopher.

### Evidence

> "The test is: if X existed, what would I expect to observe? Absence of evidence counts as evidence of absence, in proportion to how much evidence you’d expect to find if X were real."
> (AI chat transcript pasted by Leon, 2026-10-23, section "Ghosts, fairies, and knowing that something doesn’t exist")

> "“You can’t prove a negative” is a myth. You know there’s no elephant in your fridge."
> (same section)

### Implications

- Disbelief can be justified by looking and finding nothing where something would have been found.
- Before applying it, write down the observations the claim predicts, which also shows whether the claim predicts any.
