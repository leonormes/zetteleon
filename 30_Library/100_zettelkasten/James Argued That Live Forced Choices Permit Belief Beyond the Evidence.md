## James Argued That Live Forced Choices Permit Belief Beyond the Evidence

James argued that when a choice is live and forced, belief beyond the evidence can be permitted. The qualifier matters: the permission covers a narrow class of choices, not belief in general. It is the main reply to a strict evidential rule.

### Scope & Conditions

This is a secondhand report from an AI chat that gives only the year, 1896. It does not name the work or define "live" and "forced", so those terms are used here as the chat gives them. The chat adds that neither side has decisively won.

### Evidence

> "James (1896) argued that some live, forced choices permit belief beyond the evidence. Neither side has decisively won."
> (AI chat transcript pasted by Leon, 2026-10-23, section "Is there knowledge about God, or only belief?")

### Implications

- Where a decision cannot be deferred, waiting for evidence is itself a choice, which is the situation the permission is for.
- The permission needs a test for which choices count as live and forced, otherwise it licenses any belief.
