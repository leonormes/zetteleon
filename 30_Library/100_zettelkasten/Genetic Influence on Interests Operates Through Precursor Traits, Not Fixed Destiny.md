### Genetic Influence on Interests Operates Through Precursor Traits, Not Fixed Destiny

Twin and adoption research indicates individual differences in interests are meaningfully influenced by genetics, but indirectly — through precursor traits such as aptitude and personality that shape which environments a person enters and how they respond to them, rather than by specifying a fixed destiny.

#### Scope & Conditions

Genetic influence is on precursor traits (aptitude, personality), not directly on named interests (e.g. "maths" or "music") themselves.

#### Evidence

> "Twin and adoption research suggests that individual differences in vocational and recreational interests are meaningfully influenced by genetics, partly through precursor traits such as aptitude and personality. But those traits influence which environments a person enters and how they respond to them; they do not specify a fixed destiny."

#### Implications

- "It's genetic" is not a reason to treat an interest, or its absence, as fixed or beyond the reach of environment/practice.
- Explains why interests can look heritable within a family while remaining highly responsive to exposure and encouragement.
