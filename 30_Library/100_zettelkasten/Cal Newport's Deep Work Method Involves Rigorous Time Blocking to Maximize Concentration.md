Summary: The Deep Work method, advocated by Cal Newport, is a productivity philosophy centered on maximizing "deep work"—cognitively demanding tasks performed in a state of distraction-free concentration.

Details: This approach fundamentally relies on rigorous time blocking, where every minute of the workday is scheduled to prioritize these deep work sessions and consciously minimize "shallow work" (less demanding, logistical tasks). The goal is to produce high-quality, high-value output and master complex information rapidly.

## Mechanics (from Newport's _Deep Work_, 2016; paraphrased, not quoted)

- **Definition:** deep work is professional activity done in distraction-free concentration that pushes cognitive capacity; shallow work is logistical, low-concentration work that is easy to replicate.
- **Time-block planning:** the day is divided into blocks and every block is given a job, including blocks for shallow work and for breaks. Nothing is left as unassigned time.
- **Revise, do not abandon:** when an interruption or overrun breaks the plan, the day is re-blocked rather than dropped. The schedule is a working plan, not a contract.
- **Four scheduling philosophies:** monastic, bimodal, rhythmic and journalistic. Time blocking is the mechanism that implements whichever is chosen.
- **Why it works:** the claim is that the ability to concentrate is scarce and trainable, and a pre-committed block removes the in-the-moment decision about whether to start.

## Limits and Open Questions

- The note rests on Newport's book alone; no independent evidence is linked yet (`evidence_links` is empty), hence `epistemic_status: medium`.
- A fully planned day is brittle for people whose work is interrupt-driven. The method assumes control over one's calendar.
- Whether the same rigour helps or hurts ADHD is untested; see the Deep Dive Sessions experiment below.
