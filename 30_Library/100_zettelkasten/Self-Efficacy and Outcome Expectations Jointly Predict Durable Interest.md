## Self-Efficacy and Outcome Expectations Jointly Predict Durable Interest

Social Cognitive Career Theory holds that durable interest is shaped by two linked expectations—self-efficacy ("can I become capable at this?") and outcome expectations ("what worthwhile outcome will this bring?")—continually updated by successes, failures, observing others, social encouragement, and emotional experience.

### Scope & Conditions

A career/vocational-psychology model, applied here to interests generally rather than only career choice.

### Evidence

> "A broad research model, Social Cognitive Career Theory, proposes that interests are heavily shaped by two linked expectations: Self-efficacy… Outcome expectations… People are more likely to develop durable interest where they expect both competence and valued outcomes."

### Implications

- An interest can be killed by low self-efficacy even where the outcome is genuinely valued, or vice versa.
- Both expectations are updateable, not fixed—interest can be deliberately rebuilt by changing either one.
