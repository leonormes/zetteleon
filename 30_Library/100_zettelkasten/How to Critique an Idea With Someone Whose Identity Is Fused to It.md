## How to Critique an Idea With Someone Whose Identity Is Fused to It

When a belief carries someone's identity, a critique registers as an attack, so push less on logic and manage the exchange through wording, questions, tolerance and exit. The four steps in the frontmatter are the author's own synthesis. They have not been tested, and the exact wording is unverified.

### Scope & Conditions

- Use it when the belief is identity-bearing, such as political, professional or intellectual views, and you want to keep the relationship or the conversation going.
- Do not use it for a calm disagreement about a belief held lightly, where a direct critique is fine.
- It aims to keep your own model, not to fix theirs, so it gives no guarantee that the other person changes their view.

### Evidence

The steps combine four claims, each with its own evidence and limits. Only two have any outside support: [[A Critique of a Belief That Is Fused to Identity Is Received as an Attack on the Person]] has an analogy from Dweck, and [[Asking What Led to a Conclusion Invites Self-Examination Where Correcting the Error Invites Defence]] has a related point from Schein. ### Implications

- The order matters: the wording comes first, the question second, and the warmth and the exit apply as the reaction develops.
- A step that does not fit the situation, such as an exit when nothing has escalated, is simply skipped.
