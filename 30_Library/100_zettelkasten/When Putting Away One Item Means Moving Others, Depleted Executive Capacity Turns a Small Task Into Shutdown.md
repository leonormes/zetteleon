## When Putting Away One Item Means Moving Others, Depleted Executive Capacity Turns a Small Task Into Shutdown

When returning one item means moving others or opening crowded storage, each obstacle adds a decision and a physical step to working memory. At low executive capacity the chain tends to end in abandonment and a messier room, so the fix is to cut it at its first link. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to put-away tasks in full or awkward storage, for people whose executive capacity is depleted. It says little about people with spare capacity, who can absorb a longer chain.

### Evidence

> "When executive functioning is depleted, the brain interprets moving obstacles and rearranging cupboards as an overwhelming chain of micro-decisions and motor commands."
> (AI chat summary pasted 2026-11-24, not checked against a primary source)

The chat cites professional organisers without naming them, so confidence is low. The mechanism fits [[ADHD Working Memory Deficits Create a Compulsive Re-Planning Loop]], which is the vault's own grounding.

### Implications

- A put-away rule needs an exit that never opens the cupboard: see [[How to Use a Holding Basket When an Item Has No Easy Home]].
- A family member abandoning a tidy mid-way may be hitting a cascade, not refusing the job.

### Falsifier

- If people with depleted capacity finished tidies in crowded storage as readily as in roomy storage, the claim would be wrong.
