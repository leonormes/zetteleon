## Minimum Viable Understanding (MVU)

The traditional analysis says a person knows a proposition when it is true, they believe it, and they are justified in believing it ([[The Traditional Definition of Knowledge is Justified True Belief]]). Almost every later position is an argument about one of those three legs. Gettier cases say the three conditions are not enough, pragmatism says truth should give way to utility, and internalists and externalists disagree about what justification is. Separately, the vault uses "knowledge" in an everyday sense that is looser than this analysis, as information tested against experience.

## 1. The Standard Claim and Its Challengers

| Question | Claim | Counter-claim | What moved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Are the three conditions enough? | [[The Traditional Definition of Knowledge is Justified True Belief]]: each condition is necessary and together they are sufficient. | [[Gettier Problems Challenge the Traditional Definition of Knowledge]]: a belief can be true and justified yet only luckily true, so not knowledge. | Justification has to connect the belief to the truth, not merely exist alongside it. |
| Is truth required? | [[Truth is a Necessary Condition for Knowledge]]: one cannot know something false, however well justified. | [[Pragmatic Truth Focuses on Utility Over Absolute Correctness]]: an idea is true enough if it reliably helps us predict and coordinate. | Not settled. Both notes sit in the vault and each records the other as a contradiction. |
| What must justification involve? | [[Internalism Requires Conscious First-Person Access to Justification]]: the knower must be able to reach the reasons that justify the belief. | [[Goldman Reliabilism Grounds Knowledge in a Reliable Belief-Forming Mechanism]] and [[Nozick's Tracking Theory Grounds Knowledge in Truth-Tracking Across Counterfactuals]]: no access is needed if the belief comes from a reliable process or tracks the truth across counterfactuals. | Knowledge becomes a relation between a person and a fact, so a non-expert can count as a knower. |
| Is certainty required? | [[Radical Scepticism Holds That Knowledge Requires Ruling Out Every Imaginable Possibility]]: knowledge needs every imaginable alternative excluded. | [[Fallibilism Holds That Knowledge Requires Good Justification, Not Certainty]]: good justification is enough and the knower may still be wrong. | The bar moved from certainty to good justification. The sceptical position stays consistent but costs nearly all empirical knowledge. |
| Can justification be settled apart from truth? | [[Plantinga Reformed Epistemology Holds That Belief in God Can Be Properly Basic]] and [[Plantinga Argues That Whether Theistic Belief Is Justified Cannot Be Settled Without Settling Whether It Is True]]: on externalist accounts justification depends on the reliability of the process, which depends on the facts. | [[Internalism Requires Conscious First-Person Access to Justification]]: the knower must be able to reach the reasons, so justification can be assessed from the inside. | Whether the objection bites depends on choosing internalism or externalism first. Both Plantinga notes are secondhand reports from an AI chat. |

## 2. Knowledge Versus Information

The vault's second thread is not about the definition at all. It asks what separates knowing from merely having information.

- [[Information Answers Who, What, When and Where Questions While Knowledge Answers Why and How]]—_the dividing line by question type._
- [[Knowledge Supports Prediction and Inference Where Information Alone Does Not]]—_knowledge as information combined with experience._
- [[Information vs Knowledge]]—_information is cheap and unlimited, knowledge is information tested against reality and integrated into a worldview._
- [[Individual Interpretation Creates Different Knowledge from the Same Information]]—_(inferred from title) the same input yields different knowledge per person._
- [[Tacit vs Explicit Knowledge]]—_(inferred from title) knowledge that cannot be fully stated._
- [[The Gap Between Knowing a Fact and Understanding a Concept]]—_knowing a fact and understanding a concept are different things._

## 3. Working Knowledge

- When someone says "I know", ask which leg is carrying the weight: the truth of it, the belief, or the justification. (inferred)
- Externalism explains why a non-expert can know something through a reliable process, and internalism explains why that still feels unsatisfying when they cannot say why. (inferred from the three notes in section 1)
- A claim can be useful without being known. Pragmatic truth and strict knowledge answer different questions. (inferred)

## 4. Tensions & Gaps

- The vault records Gettier's challenge but no note records a response to it. Neither the reliabilism note nor the tracking note says whether it answers Gettier cases, so the table above shows the challenge but not what it changed in the externalist accounts.
- The truth requirement and pragmatic truth remain a live contradiction with no resolution note and no record of your own position.
- The information and knowledge notes use knowledge in a looser sense, information combined with experience, than the justified true belief analysis. No note reconciles the two senses.
- The reliabilism note has no source URL recorded, so its evidence rests on a single captured quotation.
- [[Popper Replaced Justification With Conjecture That Has Survived Criticism]] drops the justification leg altogether, so the table above assumes a frame that critical rationalism rejects. [[The Traditional Definition of Knowledge is Justified True Belief]] records this only through the Deutsch notes. The vault now holds both and has not chosen which is its working definition.
- The notes added on 2026-10-23 (fallibilism, scepticism, Plantinga) come from an AI chat and report philosophers secondhand, so their attributions are unchecked against primary sources.
