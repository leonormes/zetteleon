## Asking Why a Song Exists Helps Determine Its Authenticity

Asking "Why does this song exist?" is a quick test of a song's authenticity, because the answer separates two origins. Either the artist had a need to express something, or a company identified a market need and had the song made to meet it.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies when judging the origin and intent of a piece of music, usually from the song and what is known about its maker. It treats the two origins as ends of a spectrum and not a strict binary, since a commissioned or commercially backed song can also carry real expressive intent. The answer is an inference about intent, not a measurement.

### Evidence

This is the author's own reasoning. It is not taken from a book, and no external source is cited. Two illustrations, both likely and unverified, show the contrast:

- An early Bob Dylan song probably exists because he felt a need to comment on social injustice.
- A modern pop song that sounds very like other hits may exist because a record label identified a profitable trend.

### Implications

- The question gives a cheap first pass for sorting music into expressive and commercial, before judging its quality.
- The two answers predict different downstream effects, set out in the linked notes: community or consumers, and rawness or polish.

### Falsifier

- A body of songs whose stated or inferred origin (expressive or market-driven) fails to match how listeners judge their authenticity, for example commissioned works widely heard as deeply expressive.
