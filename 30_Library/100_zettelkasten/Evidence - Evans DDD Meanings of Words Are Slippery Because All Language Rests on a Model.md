## Evidence - Evans DDD Meanings of Words Are Slippery Because All Language Rests on a Model

> all language is based on some model. The meanings of words are slippery things.

Source: Eric Evans, Domain-Driven Design: Tackling Complexity in the Heart of Software, chapter on the Ubiquitous Language (Calibre id 313; page not captured by the index)

### What it Supports

A shared word does not fix a shared meaning, because the meaning of a word comes from the model of the person using it. That is the mechanism behind [[The Illusion of Shared Understanding in Teams]]: the same terminology can sit on top of different models.

### What it Does not Show

It states the general principle, not a team study, and it does not address the crisis-surfacing part of the claim.
