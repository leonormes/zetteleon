## Executive Dysfunction Has Causes Beyond ADHD Including Autism and Degenerative Disease

Executive dysfunction is a cross-diagnostic symptom rather than a condition exclusive to ADHD, arising across neurodevelopmental conditions such as autism spectrum disorder alongside brain injury, brain tumours, and degenerative diseases including dementia and Alzheimer's disease. Because the frontal lobe is the primary seat of executive control, any pathology that impairs frontal-lobe networks can produce similar deficits in planning, problem-solving, and self-regulation regardless of diagnosis. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- **Clinical and neurological causes:** Covers neurodevelopmental conditions (ADHD, autism), acquired damage (brain injury, brain tumours), and neurodegenerative disorders (dementia, Alzheimer's disease).
- **Non-diagnostic stressors:** Distinct from everyday factors like poor sleep, stress, and loneliness that impair executive function without a diagnosis ([[Everyday Stressors Like Poor Sleep and Loneliness Can Impair Executive Function Without a Diagnosis]]).
- **Uneven profiles:** Conditions need not impair every executive domain equally or on one timeline ([[Executive Function Skills Develop and Decline on Different Timelines Across the Lifespan]]).

### Falsifier
