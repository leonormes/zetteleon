## Autonomy, Competence, and Relatedness Make an Interest Self-Sustaining

[[Self-Determination Theory needs can be met differently by neurotypical brains|Self-Determination Theory]] identifies three psychological needs—autonomy ("this is chosen and mine"), competence ("I can improve and become effective"), and relatedness ("I belong and connect")—and holds that an activity becomes more self-sustaining when it satisfies these through choice, visible progress, and connection, rather than through external reward alone.

### Scope & Conditions

The source explicitly notes these three needs are "a useful lens," not a complete account of all interests.

### Evidence

> "Autonomy | 'This is chosen and mine.' | Self-directed research, creative work, independent skill-building … Activities tend to become more self-sustaining when they offer choice, visible progress, meaningful challenge, and connection rather than only external rewards."

### Implications

- An externally-imposed but otherwise well-taught activity may still fail to become a lasting interest if it offers no autonomy.
- Useful checklist when redesigning a stalled interest or habit: which of the three needs is currently unmet?
