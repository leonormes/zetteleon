## Question—Where Is the line between Iterating and System-hopping?

Both look like restless dissatisfaction expressed as changes to the system. One is endorsed ([[Claim - Treat the system as iterative not perfectable]]); one is an anti-pattern ([[System-Hopping]]). What distinguishes them in practice?

### Candidate Distinctions to Test

- Target: iteration improves the _current_ system; hopping replaces it. But a big enough "tweak" is a migration in disguise.
- Trigger: iteration is prompted by a specific friction you hit while _using_ the system; hopping is prompted by seeing a shinier tool.
- Reversibility / cost: iteration is cheap and additive; hopping discards accumulated structure and incurs re-setup.
- Usage ratio: if time spent _changing_ the system exceeds time spent _using_ it, "iteration" has become avoidance.

### Why This Matters

This is the sharp edge for anyone whose energy runs towards building structure: "I'm only iterating" is the most comfortable cover story for starting over. Worth settling your own test before the next urge to rebuild strikes. _(Edit with your own read.)_
