## Conceptual Blending Integrates Two Domains Through Four Mental Spaces

Fauconnier and Turner's Conceptual Blending Theory models new-meaning creation through a conceptual integration network of four mental spaces: Input Space 1 and Input Space 2 (each holding one domain's specific elements and organising frame), a Generic Space (the abstract structure shared by both inputs), and a Blended Space (where selectively projected elements from the inputs combine into emergent structure not present in either input alone).

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to genuine cross-disciplinary synthesis, not simple analogy-spotting; the Blended Space's structure must be new, not merely a union of the two inputs.

### Evidence

> "The blended space develops its own emergent structure and meaning not found in any of the original spaces."

### Implications

- A structural gap between two unconnected note-clusters can be deliberately treated as Input Space 1 and Input Space 2 to force generation of a Generic Space and a new synthesis note.
- This gives a concrete procedure for turning "these two things seem unrelated" into an authored bridge note.
