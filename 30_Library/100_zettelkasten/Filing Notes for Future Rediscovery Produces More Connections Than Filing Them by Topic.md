## Filing Notes for Future Rediscovery Produces More Connections Than Filing Them by Topic

Filing a note by the situations in which you want to meet it again produces more useful connections than filing it by the topic it belongs to. The first is how a writer thinks, and the second is how an archivist thinks. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- Applies to notes meant to develop ideas and feed writing or decisions.
- A reference collection kept for known lookups may be better served by topic filing.
- It needs some idea of your current questions and interests, because they define the situations to file for.

### Evidence

> "In the old system, the question is: Under which topic do I store this note? In the new system, the question is: In which context will I want to stumble upon it again?"
> (Ahrens 2022, ch. 6, PDF p. 63) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/704/EPUB?open_at=toc:6%20Simplicity%20Is%20Paramount)

> "The archivist asks: Which keyword is the most fitting? A writer asks: In which circumstances will I want to stumble upon this note, even if I forget about it?"
> (Ahrens 2022, ch. 12, PDF p. 144) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/704/EPUB?open_at=toc:12%20Develop%20Ideas)

Ahrens sets out the contrast and argues for the second question. The comparative claim about the number of useful connections is my own reasoning from that argument, and these quotations do not measure it.

### Implications

- When saving a note, name a question or project in which meeting it would help.
- Choose links and keywords with that situation in mind, not by looking at the note alone.
- Ask where a note will be useful before asking where it belongs.

### Falsifier

- File matched sets of notes by topic and by situation of reuse. If after some months the second set has not produced more connections that get used, the claim is wrong.
