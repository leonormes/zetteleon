## Plantinga Argues That Whether Theistic Belief Is Justified Cannot Be Settled Without Settling Whether It Is True

On externalist accounts, whether a belief is justified depends on whether the process that formed it is reliable. For belief in God that depends on whether God exists, so Plantinga argues the justification question cannot be answered first. An argument that tries to dismiss theistic belief on justification alone then falls back into the original dispute about truth.

### Scope & Conditions

The result holds only on externalist accounts of justification. The chat says that for someone with an internalist view, where justification depends on reasons you can access, the repaired argument against theistic belief stays strong. This is a secondhand report of Plantinga's Warranted Christian Belief (2000), not read from the book.

### Evidence

> "Plantinga’s sharpest move (Warranted Christian Belief, 2000). He argues you can’t settle whether theistic belief is justified without first settling whether it is true. On externalist accounts, justification depends on whether the belief-forming process is reliable. That in turn depends on whether God exists."
> (AI chat transcript pasted by Leon, 2026-10-23, section "Is there knowledge about God, or only belief?")

> "If you hold an internalist view of justification (justification depends on reasons you can access), the repair is strong. If you hold an externalist one, Plantinga’s objection bites hard."
> (same section, Uncertainty)

### Implications

- Whether you accept the objection depends on whether you are an internalist or an externalist about justification, so that choice should be made explicit first.
- It shows a cost of externalism: justification can no longer be assessed independently of the facts it is meant to track.
