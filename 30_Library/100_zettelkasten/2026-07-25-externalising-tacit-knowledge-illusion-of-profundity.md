An idea sitting in your head is scaffolded by everything you're not saying: the emotional charge behind it, the intuitive leap that got you there, the private narrative that makes it feel connected to everything else you know. Speak or write the idea, and that scaffolding vanishes—what's left can look skeletal or flawed, even when the original insight was genuinely sound. This is IoED turned inward: the illusion applied to your own ideas rather than to an external system.

## Evidence

> "Internal thoughts can feel exceptionally deep because they are supported by 'tacit knowledge'—private emotional charge, intuitive leaps, and a rich subjective narrative… When externalized (spoken or written), this internal context vanishes, often leaving the idea looking 'skeletal' or flawed to others."—[[SoT - Illusion of Explanatory Depth (IoED)]]

## Steelman

An idea that looks "skeletal" once externalised may genuinely have been weak all along—the felt sense of profundity could itself be the illusion, not the externalisation process. The causal story here (support vanishes on externalisation) can't be cleanly distinguished from the alternative (the depth was never there) using introspection alone.
