## Gettier Problems Challenge the Traditional Definition of Knowledge

A person can hold a belief that is true and justified and still not know it, because the belief is true by luck. Gettier's 1963 counterexamples therefore show that justified true belief is not sufficient for knowledge. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- The attack is on sufficiency only. It leaves truth, belief and justification standing as necessary, which is why the standard response adds a condition and does not drop any.
- A worked case (Martin's version of one of Gettier's). Joan justifiably believes "Jack got the job" (false) and "if Jack or Jim got the job, there is a letter in her mailbox firing her". She infers that there is a letter. The letter is there, but because Jim got the job. Her belief is justified and true, and she does not know it. The case needs two assumptions: that justification carries over to what a justified belief entails, and that one can be justified in believing something false.
- Ayer replied by denying the first assumption. The reply does not reach cases where nothing is inferred ([[Gettier-Type Cases Arise Without Inference or False Belief, So Repairing the Inference Step Cannot Solve Them]]).
- The verdict that the person does not know rests on shared intuition, not on proof.

### Evidence

One book reports the effect of the paper on the field:

> "convincing just about everyone that knowledge could not be simply justified true belief"
> (Martin 2014, ch. 3, PDF p. 34) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/298/EPUB?open_at=toc:3%20Justification%20and%20Gettier%20problems)

A second book, Nagel (2014), adds that decades of later analyses have not secured broad support, and it notes earlier cases of the same shape by the Indian philosopher Dharmottara (around 770 CE). That second point is taken from the library's text and has not been separately quoted.

### Implications

- The failure of sufficiency is the main reason the analysis is treated as a starting point to improve and not as a finished definition.
- The responses divide into repairs that add a condition (no false belief, no defeaters, reliability, tracking, a causal link), and a rejection of the analysis itself (knowledge first).

### Falsifier

- A principled way of classing Gettier cases as knowledge, so that the intuition that the person does not know can be given up without cost, or a justification condition that excludes every such case and has no counterexamples of its own.
