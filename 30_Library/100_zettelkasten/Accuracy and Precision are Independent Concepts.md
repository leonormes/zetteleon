## Accuracy and Precision Are Independent Concepts

A measurement's accuracy is how close it is to the true value; its precision is how consistent repeated measurements are with each other. These are two different properties, and a measurement process can have either one without the other: repeated measurements can cluster tightly together (high precision) while being consistently off from the true value (low accuracy), or they can average out to the true value (high accuracy) while being scattered and inconsistent from one measurement to the next (low precision).

### Scope & Conditions

A claim about measurement processes specifically—repeatability (precision) versus correctness (accuracy)—not a general claim about vagueness or category boundaries. Despite the superficial word-association with "precisely defining" a boundary, this is a distinct, narrower technical concept from the vagueness cluster ([[Sorites Paradox]], [[Vague Boundaries]]) that link to it.

### Implications

- Improving one property does nothing for the other by default: a miscalibrated but very consistent instrument becomes more confidently wrong, not more correct, as you take more precise measurements.
- Diagnosing which property is failing changes the fix entirely: a precision problem calls for reducing measurement noise (better instrumentation, more careful technique); an accuracy problem calls for finding and removing systematic bias (recalibration), which no amount of repeated measurement or averaging alone will fix if the bias is constant across trials.
