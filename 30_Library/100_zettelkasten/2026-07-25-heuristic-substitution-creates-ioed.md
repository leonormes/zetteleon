When faced with a complex system, the brain swaps the hard question ("do I understand this?") for an easy one ("do I recognise this?"). Recognising a familiar label gets mistaken for auditing the causal logic, so the system gets tagged "known" and enquiry stops there. This substitution is the mechanism behind the Illusion of Explanatory Depth, not just a restatement of it.

## Evidence

> "The Query: 'Do I understand X?' The Substitution: Instead of auditing the causal logic, the brain substitutes an easier question: 'Do I recognise X? Do I know what X is for?' The False Positive: Because the label is familiar…, the brain tags the system as 'Known' and suppresses further enquiry."—[[SoT - Illusion of Explanatory Depth (IoED)]]

## Steelman

This could instead be plain confidence miscalibration rather than a specific substitution heuristic—the substitution framing may redescribe the phenomenon rather than explain it as an independent mechanism.
