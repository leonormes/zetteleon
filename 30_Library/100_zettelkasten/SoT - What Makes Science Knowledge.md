## Minimum Viable Understanding (MVU)

Science is the best-developed attempt to turn experience into knowledge, so the question is what makes it work. The vault records a chain of answers in which each fixes a problem in the last and creates a new one. Logical positivism tied science to observables, falsifiability replaced confirmation with refutation, and Duhem-Quine holism and the confirmation objection showed that falsification is not clean either. Kuhn moved the focus to history and paradigms, Lakatos and Laudan repaired his single-paradigm picture, and Feyerabend concluded that no fixed method survives.

## 1. The Chain of Positions

| Stage | Claim | What it fixed | Counter-claim or problem it created |
|---|---|---|---|
| Logical positivism | [[Logical Positivism Restricts Science's Aim to Predicting Sensory Patterns]]: a strictly logical theory of science built on observables alone. | Gave empiricism a precise criterion. | [[Observation Is Theory-Laden Rather Than Raw and Neutral]]: what counts as data is already shaped by instruments and assumptions. |
| Falsifiability | [[Falsifiability Distinguishes Science from Dogma]]: a theory states in advance what would prove it wrong. | Sidestepped the impossibility of confirming a generalisation, see [[The Problem of Induction Means Past Regularity Cannot Logically Guarantee Future Regularity]]. | [[Falsifiability Is Necessary But Not Sufficient for a Claim to Be Scientific]]: measurement, design and community scrutiny also matter. |
| Holism about testing | [[Duhem-Quine Holism Complicates Simple Falsification]]: an unexpected result can indict a background assumption or instrument instead of the theory. | Explained why refutation is rarely clean. | [[Underdetermination Means Multiple Theories Can Fit the Same Evidence]]: fit to data alone cannot choose between rivals. |
| Confirmation objection | [[Godfrey-Smith's Confirmation Objection to Strict Falsificationism]]: if theories can never be confirmed there is no basis for relying on well-tested ones. | Exposed the cost of pure falsificationism. | [[Theory Support Comes From the Reliability of the Testing Procedure]]: support comes from the reliability of the procedure that produced the observation. |
| Kuhn | [[Kuhn's Four-Stage Model of Paradigm Collapse]]: science cycles through normal science, drift, crisis and revolution. | Brought history into the account. | [[Kuhn's Incommensurability Thesis Denies Simple Cross-Paradigm Progress]]: rival paradigms use different standards, so progress is not a march to truth. |
| Repairs to Kuhn | [[Lakatos's Research Programmes Replace Kuhn's Single Paradigm With Competing Cores]], then [[Laudan Distinguishes Rationally Pursuing a Theory From Accepting It as True]]. | Allowed several competing frameworks at once. | [[Feyerabend's Epistemological Anarchism Denies Any Fixed Scientific Method]]: no fixed method exists, and strict rules hinder progress. |

## 2. What the Chain Leaves Standing

- [[A Scientific Theory Is a Well-Tested Explanatory Framework Not a Promoted Hypothesis]]—_a theory is a developed framework, not a guess awaiting promotion._
- [[Reproducibility and Replicability Are Distinct Standards of Scientific Reliability]]—_two separate reliability checks that are often conflated._
- [[A Falsifiable Engineering Claim Names Its Outcome Comparison Confounders and Uncertainty Bound]]—_what falsifiability looks like when applied to an everyday causal claim._
- [[Philosophy of Science Should Be Naturalistic, Continuous With Science Itself]]—_study how science works using the sciences themselves._
- [[Bayesian Updating Adjusts Beliefs as New Evidence Arrives]]—_a graded alternative to confirm-or-refute._

## 3. Working Knowledge

- Treat falsifiability as a screening question, not a full test: ask what result would count against the claim, then ask whether the measurement and design could actually deliver it. (inferred from the necessary-but-not-sufficient note)
- When a test fails, list the background assumptions and instruments before blaming the theory. (inferred from Duhem-Quine)
- Rely on a well-tested theory because of the reliability of the testing procedure, not because it has been proven. (from the testing-procedure note)

## 4. Tensions & Gaps

- Feyerabend's "no fixed method" conflicts directly with falsifiability as a rule of method. Both are held in the vault and the conflict is recorded as an edge, but no note says which is more defensible.
- The vault attributes falsificationism to Popper only through the Godfrey-Smith map, so there is no note on Popper's own argument.
- The final chapter of the book is missing, so the chain stops before the author's own resolution.
- The existing Godfrey-Smith map links a note on Merton's reward system that does not exist, so the sociological side of this chain has a broken link.
- The induction thread above is unresolved. The chat that supplied it says philosophers still disagree on whether Salmon wins, and gives a lead to a paper (name not recorded) arguing that Popper's and Musgrave's replies both fail. No note holds Musgrave's reply or that paper.
