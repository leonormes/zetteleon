## Card's Atrocity Paradigm Identifies Evil via Three Necessary Co-conditions

Claudia Card's "Atrocity Paradigm" identifies evil through three jointly necessary conditions: (1) culpable wrongdoing—the agent is responsible and could have acted otherwise; (2) reasonable foreseeability—the harm was predictable from the agent's position; (3) intolerable harm—the harm destroys the basic conditions for a decent or tolerable life. All three must be present. Satisfying one or two shifts the classification to ordinary wrongdoing, negligence, or tragedy.

### Scope & Conditions

A "combination approach" to moral classification drawn from Card's _The Atrocity Paradigm_. Functions as a tripartite filter for analytical evaluation rather than a metaphysical claim about the nature of evil. The paradigm simplifies a complex philosophical debate into an operationalisable heuristic—useful for analysis, not a complete theory.

### Evidence

> "Card's 'atrocity paradigm' combines three necessary elements: culpable wrongdoing, reasonable foreseeability, and intolerable harm."

### Implications

- All three conditions must be checked independently; missing one changes the moral category of the event, even if the harm is severe.
- The paradigm enables analytical precision by distinguishing evil from adjacent moral categories (accident, ordinary wrongdoing, natural disaster) that evoke different moral and legal responses.
