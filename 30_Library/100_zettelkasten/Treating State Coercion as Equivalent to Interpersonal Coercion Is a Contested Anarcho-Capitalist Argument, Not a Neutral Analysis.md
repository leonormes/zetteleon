## Treating State Coercion as Equivalent to Interpersonal Coercion Is a Contested Anarcho-Capitalist Argument, Not a Neutral Analysis

The argument that state actions should be judged by the same moral standards as individual actions—so that if it is wrong for a person to force a neighbour to labour or hand over money, it is in principle wrong for the state to enforce jury duty or taxation—relies entirely on an anarcho-capitalist framework, and its presentation typically omits standard counterarguments such as democratic consent, the social contract, and the necessity of solving collective-action problems.

### Scope & Conditions

A recognisable, coherent position within political philosophy, but one contested premise among several, not a neutral extension of common-sense ethics into politics.

### Evidence

> "If it is wrong for an individual to force a neighbour to perform labour or surrender money, it is in principle wrong for the state to enforce jury duty or taxation… This relies entirely on an anarcho-capitalist framework and dismisses concepts like the social contract or public goods."

### Implications

- When this "substitute an individual for the state" analogy is used to argue against a specific policy, check whether the argument has actually addressed the standard structural/legal differences political theorists cite to justify state authority, or merely asserted the analogy holds.
