## Communication Is Inherently Lossy Like Image Compression

Translating internal mental states into language and back involves unavoidable information loss, similar to compressing a high-resolution image into a lower-resolution format. Across four steps—internal neural pattern, linguistic encoding, listener decoding, and mental reconstruction—sensory richness, affective colouring, and tacit context are dropped.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to natural-language communication of complex, novel, or experiential concepts between human minds. Formal mathematical or machine protocols can achieve lossless symbol transmission, though they still presuppose shared grounding of the underlying definitions.

### Evidence

This claim is derived from information-theoretic and cognitive synthesis across the thought-language and experiential-filter notes in the vault rather than a single external quotation.

### Implications

- One-shot clarity is the exception; misunderstanding is the structural default when speakers assume listeners share their tacit context.
- Communicating nuanced ideas requires multiple phrasings, concrete examples, and feedback loops to restore lost resolution.
- Active listening and paraphrasing act as error-correction protocols on a noisy, lossy channel.
