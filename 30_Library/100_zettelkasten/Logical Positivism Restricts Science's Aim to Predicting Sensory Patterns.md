## Logical Positivism Restricts Science's Aim to Predicting Sensory Patterns

Logical positivists and logical empiricists (Carnap, Hempel, Schlick) held that the sole aim of science is to track and anticipate patterns in sensory experience, built on a strictly logical theory of science based purely on observables.

### Scope & Conditions

Early-20th-century movement; a stricter, more systematised descendant of classical empiricism.

### Evidence

> "They argued that the sole aim of science is to track and anticipate patterns in sensory experience."

### Implications

- Sets the target that later instrumentalist and constructive-empiricist positions inherit or soften.
