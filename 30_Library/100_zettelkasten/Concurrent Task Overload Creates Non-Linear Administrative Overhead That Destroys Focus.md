## Concurrent Task Overload Creates Non-Linear Administrative Overhead That Destroys Focus

Taking on too many concurrent tasks does not create a proportional increase in administrative overhead—it creates a non-linear flood. Each additional open commitment generates coordination, update, and context-switching overhead that compounds across all existing commitments. Beyond a threshold, this overhead consumes all remaining capacity for deep work, and no amount of efficiency optimisation can recover it. Workload limits are a prerequisite for focus, not a nice-to-have.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies at both individual and team levels. The non-linearity arises because each new commitment interacts with all existing ones (scheduling conflicts, dependencies, status updates). The threshold varies by role and task complexity but the structural principle is domain-agnostic.

### Evidence

> "taking on too many concurrent tasks creates a flood of uncompressible administrative overhead that destroys any chance of deep work." [35:54]

### Implications

- You cannot "efficiency-hack" your way out of excessive concurrent commitments; the only solution is reducing the count of open commitments.
- "Administrative slop" is a direct function of task volume—the correct diagnosis for lost focus capacity is workload, not time management technique.
