## Locating Truth-Status in Persons Is What Wounds, Not Believing in Truth

Summary: The damage done by a lifetime of right/wrong thinking comes not from caring about truth, but from assigning the predicate "right/wrong" to persons instead of propositions—and the judging machinery always has one permanent defendant: yourself.

Details: Commitment to truth is not the pathology; it is an asset. The pathology is a category error about _where truth-values live_. Propositions are truth-apt—they can be true or false. Persons are not truth-apt; they are the growers and carriers of propositions. When truth-status is misassigned to persons, three distortions follow. Disagreement becomes attack, because a challenge to a claim lands as a verdict on its holder. Error becomes defect, so beliefs are defended like body parts rather than examined like specimens. And changing one's mind becomes confession—updating requires first pleading guilty to having _been_ a wrong person, which makes learning prohibitively expensive.

The clause "including yourself" is the deepest cut. The same machinery that sorts others into right/wrong bins runs continuously on its owner. Every discovered mistake becomes self-indictment rather than information; the fear of _being_ wrong (an identity threat) crowds out curiosity about _what is_ wrong (a factual question). This is why the sting of disagreement can persist for decades: it was never about the claims. You cannot extend the gardener's compassion to others while treating your own errors as evidence of a defective plant.

Implications that grow from the correction:

1. Error becomes affordable. Once being mistaken is an event, not an identity, the cost of updating collapses. You can hold beliefs as hypotheses with confidence levels rather than as possessions.
2. Self-correction without self-attack. You can audit your own beliefs as specimens on the table—writing them down helps exteriorise them—and revise without a guilty verdict.
3. Criticism changes sign. Critique received stops being an attack; critique given stops being condemnation. Both become quality control on shared specimens.
4. Falsification becomes a service. Popper's insight—let theories die in your place—only works if the theory is _outside_ you. Disagreeing minds become your error-detectors instead of your enemies.
5. Conviction and humility become compatible. You can commit hard to claims while remaining relaxed about yourself, because the self was never what was at stake.
6. Keep the identity small. The fewer beliefs welded into the self, the smaller the attack surface—and the less of you must die for a bad idea to be let go.
