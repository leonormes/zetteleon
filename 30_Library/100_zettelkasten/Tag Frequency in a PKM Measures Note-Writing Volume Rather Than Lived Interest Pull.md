## Tag Frequency in a PKM Measures Note-Writing Volume Rather Than Lived Interest Pull

Tag frequency across a personal knowledge vault measures note-writing volume and topic atomisability rather than the actual strength of lived interest. Counting tags systematically overweights text-heavy technical subjects that atomise cleanly into notes while underweighting embodied or off-vault practices. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies when mining a PKM vault's tag counts or note totals to seed a personal interest model. Holds wherever a person's engagements span both text-mediated analytical domains and embodied, conversational, or live operational practices that leave sparse written traces.

### Evidence

> "Frequency is a proxy for engagement—note-writing volume—not engagement itself; it will overweight topics that are easy to atomise into notes (software, networking) and underweight embodied or non-written interests (archery, music) relative to their true pull."
> — [[A Portable Interest and PKM Knowledge Graph — Interest Seed and Candidate Drivers]], §Method Used here

In the vault's 14 September 2026 tag-count seed, `#SoftwareEngineering` (248 notes) dwarfed `#archery` (23 combined tag hits) and `#music` (9 notes), even though Leon's 15 September 2026 elicitation correction established years of full-time daily musicianship that went completely unrecorded in Obsidian.

### Implications

- Treat tag-count rankings strictly as observational starting seeds that must pass live elicitation and comparison before any interest strength or driver is inferred.
- Pair vault-volume audits with explicit prompts for embodied, historical, and off-vault practices that rarely produce notes.

### Falsifier

- Disconfirmed if independent prospective time-use and lived-pull ratings across all active interests correlate tightly with raw vault tag counts without systematic under-representation of embodied or off-vault domains.
