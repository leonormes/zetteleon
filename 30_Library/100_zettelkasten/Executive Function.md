## Minimum Viable Understanding (MVU)

**Executive Function (EF)** refers to the set of top-down cognitive control processes that organize behavior towards goals. Often described as the "CEO of the brain," it encompasses planning, prioritization, organization, task initiation, emotional regulation, task-switching, and working memory. 

## The Functional Domains

While definitions vary slightly, executive function generally breaks down into these operational capacities:
- **Working Memory**: Holding and manipulating information over short periods (see [[SoT - Working Memory & Schema Theory]]).
- **Inhibitory Control**: Suppressing impulsive responses and resisting distractions.
- **Cognitive Flexibility**: Shifting attention between tasks or adapting to changed rules.
- **Activation / Task Initiation**: Mustering the activation energy to begin a non-novel task (see [[MOC - Why Task Initiation is Difficult in ADHD]] for the root causes of failure here).

## Neurological Basis

The primary biological seat of executive function is the prefrontal cortex, which exerts top-down control over motor actions, emotional responses from the limbic system, and attention networks.

## Why it Matters in this Vault

- **The Processing Bottleneck**: Capturing a thought is a rapid associative reflex; _processing_ it into a system demands heavy executive function (evaluating priority, categorizing, scheduling). This explains the steep friction differential.
  - _See:_ [[Claim - Capture is easy but processing is hard]]
- **ADHD as an EF Disorder**: ADHD is fundamentally characterized by impairments across these precise control processes, rather than just an inability to "pay attention."
  - _See:_ [[Executive Function Challenges are Central to ADHD]]
- **The Need for Scaffolding**: When internal executive function is unreliable or easily depleted, it must be offloaded into the environment through [[SoT - Prosthetic Executive Function]] (e.g., routines, visual cues, and robust PKM systems).
  - _See:_ [[SoT - Behavioral Architecture]]
