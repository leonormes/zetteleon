## Executive Function

Executive function (EF) is the set of top-down cognitive control processes that organise behaviour towards goals, including planning, prioritisation, organisation, task initiation, task-switching and working memory. It is often called the CEO of the brain, and it is separate from the moment-to-moment load those processes have to manage. ^def

### Scope & Conditions

The domains this vault works with:

- Working memory: holding and manipulating information over short periods ([[SoT - Working Memory & Schema Theory]]).
- Inhibitory control: suppressing impulsive responses and resisting distraction ([[The Stroop Test Assesses Inhibitory Control via Interference Resistance]]).
- Cognitive flexibility: shifting attention between tasks or adapting to changed rules.
- Activation or task initiation: mustering the energy to begin a non-novel task ([[MOC - Why Task Initiation is Difficult in ADHD]], [[Task Initiation Activates Medial Prefrontal Regions for Energization]]).

Definitions vary between authors, and the four-way split is a working list and not a settled taxonomy. The primary biological seat is the prefrontal cortex ([[The Prefrontal Cortex Provides Executive Control Over Action]]). EF is not one unified trait: its domains develop and decline on different timelines ([[Executive Function Skills Develop and Decline on Different Timelines Across the Lifespan]]).

### Implications

- Capturing a thought is a light reflex, while processing it into a system is heavy EF work ([[Claim - Capture is easy but processing is hard]]).
- ADHD is characterised by impairment across these control processes and not only by trouble paying attention ([[Executive Function Challenges are Central to ADHD]]).
- When internal EF is unreliable or easily depleted, it is offloaded into the environment through routines, visual cues and PKM systems ([[SoT - Prosthetic Executive Function]], [[SoT - Behavioral Architecture]]).
