## Atomic Enough Means the Fewest Pieces Necessary to Be Useful for the Task

A note is atomic enough when it contains the fewest pieces necessary to be useful for its task, and the right level of atomicity is personal. The source is Bob Doto, A System for Writing.

### Scope & Conditions

Doto quotes a fellow note-maker for the "fewest pieces" wording and says you will develop a feel for when an idea becomes too simple or too complex.

### Evidence

> "What level of atomicity works best for you is personal. As a PKM comrade of mine has said, "make a note that contains the fewest number of pieces as necessary to be useful for its specified task.""
> (Doto, A System for Writing, section "Connecting Your Ideas")

> "In other words, shoot for "atomic enough.""
> (Doto, A System for Writing, section "Connecting Your Ideas")

### Implications

- The target is usefulness for a task, not the smallest possible note.
- A rule that fixes an exact size will sometimes contradict the level that works for the person using it.
