## Duhem-Quine Holism Complicates Simple Falsification

Godfrey-Smith points out that simple falsification rarely works in practice because of "holism about testing" (the Duhem-Quine thesis): an unexpected observation might mean the main theory is wrong, or it might just mean a background assumption or measuring instrument is faulty. A prediction only follows from a theory in conjunction with everything else assumed to make the test work, so a failed prediction never isolates a single culprit automatically.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies whenever a test result is used to falsify a theory; known as "holism about testing" or the Duhem-Quine thesis.

### Evidence

> "simple falsification rarely works in practice because of 'holism about testing'… an unexpected observation might mean the main theory is wrong, or it might just mean a background assumption or measuring instrument is faulty"

### Implications

- Names a specific mechanism by which theory-choice becomes underdetermined at the moment of testing, not only at the moment of comparing finished theories.
- Qualifies any account (Deutsch's included) that treats a single falsifying observation as automatically eliminating the theory under test.
