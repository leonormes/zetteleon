## Claim—Over-capture Plus Deferred Low-pressure Review is Sustainable

> [!claim] Statement
> Deliberately capturing more than you can immediately process—then handling it later in a low-stakes review—is more sustainable for ADHD than trying to file or decide at the moment of capture.

### Supporting Evidence

- Capturing freely (voice notes, quick entries) avoids losing ideas to the speed of thought.
- Separating capture from processing means the overwhelm of decision-making does not block the cheap, rewarding capture step. Builds on [[Claim - Capture is easy but processing is hard]].

### Mechanism

- Time-shifts the expensive processing to a moment you can choose for higher focus, and frames it as optional/low-pressure so RSD-driven avoidance is less likely to fire.

### Counter-positions

- Over-capture can create a backlog so large the review never happens—capture becomes hoarding.
- Without a trusted review cadence, "process later" is indistinguishable from "never".

### Crux

- Whether a reliable, genuinely low-friction review habit can be sustained; the strategy lives or dies on that one habit.

### Falsifier

- If over-capturers who never built a review habit did no worse than those who did, the deferred-review half would be carrying no weight.
