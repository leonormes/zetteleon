## Framework Synthesis Differs Fundamentally From Within-Paradigm Optimisation

High applicative competence—knowing all the facts and optimising within an existing rulebook—is structurally distinct from generative framework synthesis. Within-paradigm optimisation pursues efficiency and reliability through fast pattern matching over known knowns, whereas framework synthesis pursues breakthrough through slow pattern breaking in unknown unknowns, yielding new questions rather than faster answers.

### Scope & Conditions

Holds across scientific, engineering, and strategic domains whenever a problem resists incremental improvement. In stable domains with sound premises, within-paradigm optimisation remains the appropriate and productive mode.

### Evidence

This claim synthesises the Kuhnian normal-versus-revolutionary science distinction in [[SoT - Generative Intelligence]] (§§1–2) alongside [[A Paradigm Only Collapses When a Viable Replacement Exists]] and [[Flawed Mental Models Limit Mastery]].

### Implications

- Mistaking high applicative intelligence for generative intelligence hides the "Trap of Intelligence," where experts defend familiar dogma instead of testing core premises.
- Breakthroughs often look unproductive or heretical by within-paradigm metrics until a replacement framework proves its explanatory power.
