## Money Is Stored Capacity That Does No Work Until It Is Spent

Money held in an account is like a charged battery: it is a capacity to cause change and does no work by itself. Its power appears at the moment of spending, when that potential becomes labour, logistics or manufacturing. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

An analogy from physics, not a physical law. Money is also an institutional fact that serves as a tool for exchange and measurement.

### Evidence

Carried over from the earlier prose version of [[SoT - Financial Philosophy and Spendfulness]]; no outside source is cited.

### Implications

- Stored resources that are never deployed do no work.
- A purchase can be pictured as an instruction sent to society to carry out a task.
