## Executive Function Skills Develop and Decline on Different Timelines Across the Lifespan

Summary: The three core executive function skills do not develop or decline in lockstep across the human lifespan.

Details: Working memory develops through childhood and adolescence, peaks in the early 30s, and begins declining from around age 35 into middle and old age. Cognitive flexibility begins developing around age 3, with experts disagreeing on whether it completes by age 12 or continues improving until around age 29. Inhibitory control begins developing in infancy and starts declining once a person reaches their 60s. Because the three skills follow distinct curves, "executive function" as a single trait can decline unevenly—one component may weaken while another remains intact.
