## A Scientific Theory Is a Well-Tested Explanatory Framework Not a Promoted Hypothesis

A common misconception treats "hypothesis" and "theory" as stages on the same ladder, where enough proof promotes one into the other. In scientific usage they are different kinds of thing: a hypothesis is a specific, testable proposition, while a theory (such as evolutionary theory) is a broad, well-developed explanatory framework that has repeatedly survived testing. Evidence can strengthen a theory, but "proof" in the strict sense is generally a mathematical standard, not an empirical one—so theories remain provisional and gradable by evidential strength rather than ever becoming "proven" outright.

### Scope & Conditions

A terminology and category distinction, not a claim about how confident one should be in any specific theory.

### Implications

- "It's just a theory" and "it's proven" both misuse the term the same way—by treating theory status as a confidence threshold rather than as a category of explanatory framework.
- When a field's best explanation is called a "theory," that is usually a sign of maturity and explanatory scope, not tentativeness.
