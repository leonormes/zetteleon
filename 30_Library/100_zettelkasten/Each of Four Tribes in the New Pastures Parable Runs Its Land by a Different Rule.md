## Each of Four Tribes in the New Pastures Parable Runs Its Land by a Different Rule

In Greene's Parable of the New Pastures, four tribes around a forest each run their land by a different working rule. The Eastern tribe gives each family the same number of sheep, the Western tribe sets flock size by family size, the Northern tribe uses private fenced plots, and the Southern tribe shares both pasture and animals.

### Scope & Conditions

An illustration inside the parable, not a claim about real tribes.

### Evidence

> East: "Here the rule is simple: Each family gets the same number of sheep."
>
> West: "the size of a family's flock is determined by the family's size."
>
> North: "Here there is no common pasture. Each family has its own plot of land, surrounded by a fence."
>
> South: "They share not only their pasture but their animals, too."
> (Joshua Greene, Moral Tribes, Introduction: The Tragedy of Commonsense Morality)

### Implications

- Each rule is a workable internal answer to the tragedy of the commons.
- The clash on the new pastures comes from these answers being incompatible with one another, not from any one failing.
