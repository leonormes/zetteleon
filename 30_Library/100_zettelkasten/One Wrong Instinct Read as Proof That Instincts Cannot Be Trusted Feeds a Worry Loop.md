## One Wrong Instinct Read as Proof That Instincts Cannot Be Trusted Feeds a Worry Loop

Worry that one's instincts are wrong holds a person back. When an instinct does turn out wrong, the miss is counted as proof that instincts cannot be trusted, and that verdict keeps the worry alive. The loop is my reading of the two sentences below; the original states the worry and the proof step, not the feedback.

### Scope & Conditions

A personal reflection from one person, not a general finding. Split out of [[Emergent Mind]] on 2026-09-25; the wording below is unchanged from that note's first paragraph.

### Evidence

> "I am always worried that my instincts are wrong, and it holds me back. Because I am so worried about it, when I am wrong, I see it as proof to not trust my instincts."

### Implications

- One miss is being weighed as a verdict on the whole faculty, when it is a single outcome.
- The remedy sits in how a miss is read, not in having fewer misses (see the two notes linked under Related).
