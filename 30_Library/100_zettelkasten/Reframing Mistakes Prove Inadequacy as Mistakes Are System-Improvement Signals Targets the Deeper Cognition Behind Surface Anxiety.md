## Reframing "Mistakes Prove Inadequacy" as "Mistakes Are System-Improvement Signals" Targets the Deeper Cognition Behind Surface Anxiety

CBT treatment of anxious over-preparation works better when it challenges the deeper inference behind the surface prediction—not "I might be wrong" but "being wrong proves I am inadequate"—replacing it with a more accurate alternative such as "an error can be evidence that a model, checklist, or system needs improvement, not a verdict on me."

### Scope & Conditions

The surface prediction ("I might make a mistake") is often not the actual load-bearing belief; the deeper belief about what an error would mean about the person is what drives the safety behaviour and is the more productive target for reframing.

### Evidence

> "CBT works better when you challenge the deeper inference, not only the surface prediction… 'If I make an error, I have failed.' → 'An error can be evidence that a model, checklist, or system needs improvement—not a verdict on me.'"

### Implications

- Challenging only the surface prediction ("you probably won't make a mistake") leaves the deeper belief ("if I did, it would mean I'm inadequate") untouched and the safety behaviour intact.
