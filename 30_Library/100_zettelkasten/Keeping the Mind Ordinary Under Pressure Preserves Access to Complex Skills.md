## Keeping the Mind Ordinary Under Pressure Preserves Access to Complex Skills

The SoT claims that keeping the mind ordinary lets a person reach complex skills under pressure, in professional work such as coding and negotiation. Research on choking supports the direction of the claim, since skilled performance breaks down under pressure when conscious monitoring takes over from automatic execution. The mechanism the SoT names, an adrenaline surge that narrows attention, is not the one the choking research gives.

### Scope & Conditions

- The choking evidence comes from sport. Carrying it over to coding or negotiation is an assumption of this note.
- The source describes the failure as attention moving to the mechanics of a skill, not as arousal. It supports "do not change mode" only to the degree that holding the baseline keeps attention off the mechanics, and that link is inferred.
- It applies to practised, automatic skills. A new skill still needs conscious attention.

### Evidence

> "Choking, then, is a kind of neural glitch that occurs when the brain switches to a system of explicit monitoring in circumstances when it ought to stick to the implicit system. It is not something the performer does intentionally; it just happens."
> (Matthew Syed, *Bounce*, ch. 6 "The Curse of Choking and How to Avoid It")

> "they are able to direct their conscious attention to tactics and strategy even under severe pressure, leaving the complex motor skills to the implicit system."
> (same book, chapter notes)

> "Keeping the mind "ordinary" allows for access to complex skills under pressure."
> ([[SoT - Miyamoto Musashi's Philosophy on Mastering Emotions]], §3 Integration with ProdOS Domains)

The first two quotations are the research lead and the third is the vault's own claim.

### Implications

- The useful training target is where attention goes under stakes: toward tactics and the situation, and away from the mechanics of a practised skill.
- It explains why a crisis mode can cost performance even when it feels like extra effort, as set out in [[A Fighting Mind That Differs From the Daily Mind Adds Friction and Rigidity]].
- The working-memory limit in [[Cognitive Load]] is a second reason pressure hurts, because worry competes for the same capacity (inferred).
