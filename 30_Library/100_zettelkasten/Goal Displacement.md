## Definition

Goal displacement occurs when the original terminal goal of an activity is quietly replaced by conformity to the rules, procedures, or credentials that were originally only instrumental means toward it. The means become the end in themselves, and success gets measured by adherence to the proxy rather than by the underlying outcome it was meant to serve.

## Origin

Coined by sociologist Robert K. Merton (1940, "Bureaucratic Structure and Personality") to describe how bureaucratic rule-following—originally a means to organisational efficiency—can become an end in itself. Officials become so committed to procedure ("trained incapacity") that they lose sight of the goals the procedures were meant to serve; rigid adherence to the rule persists even when it actively defeats the purpose the rule was built for.

## In Productivity / ADHD Framing

In the personal-productivity context this vault mostly tracks, goal displacement shows up as credentials, certification, or task-completion counts becoming the measured "win," while the actual underlying goal—understanding, growth, mastery—is what compliance with the proxy was only ever supposed to serve. This is the specific failure mode a process-over-outcome orientation is designed to prevent: keep the reward attached to genuine engagement with the activity, not to the paperwork trail it leaves behind.
