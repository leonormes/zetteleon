## Information-Seeking Is Adaptive Only While It Remains Instrumental to an Actual Decision

Information-seeking is adaptive when it changes a real decision, prediction, or response capacity (instrumental, contingency, or recovery information); it becomes a safety behaviour rather than sound judgement when its actual function is reassurance or identity-protection—proving certainty is impossible, or proving one isn't inadequate—rather than improving the decision at hand.

### Scope & Conditions

Distinguishes five information types by the question each answers: instrumental ("will this change my next decision?"), contingency ("how will I know if my model is wrong?"), recovery ("what can I do if this fails?"), reassurance ("can I be certain nothing bad will happen?"), and identity reassurance ("can I prove I'm not inadequate?"). Research on anxiety and obsessive-compulsive traits finds a greater willingness to seek even non-instrumental information—information that cannot change the outcome—despite a real cost.

### Implications

- A practical test before continuing research: does this next piece of information change what I would do, or only how I feel about what I've already decided?
