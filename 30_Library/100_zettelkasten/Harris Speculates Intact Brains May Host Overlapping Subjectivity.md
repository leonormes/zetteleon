## Harris Speculates Intact Brains May Host Overlapping Subjectivity

Summary: Following Roland Puccetti, Harris tentatively suggests that even a healthy, intact brain—given each hemisphere's large processing load and the limited bandwidth of the corpus callosum—may exist in a fluid state of split and overlapping subjectivity.

Details: This is Harris's most speculative move, and he flags it as such ("we may currently live in…"). The reasoning is that if the callosum is a bandwidth bottleneck, full moment-to-moment integration of the two hemispheres might be impossible, so the ordinary brain could be a quietly divided one. Because Harris hedges rather than asserts, critiques that treat this as a firm claim and then dismiss it as a hasty generalisation attack a stronger position than he actually holds.

### Evidence

> "we may currently live in a 'fluid state of split and overlapping subjectivity'."

### Implications

- The claim is offered as a possibility, not a demonstrated fact.
- It relies on treating callosal bandwidth as the binding constraint on unified experience.
