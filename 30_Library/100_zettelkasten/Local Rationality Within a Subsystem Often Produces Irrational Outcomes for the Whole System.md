## Local Rationality Within a Subsystem Often Produces Irrational Outcomes for the Whole System

Rational decisions made by actors optimising within a local subsystem frequently produce irrational, counterproductive, or disastrous outcomes for the system as a whole (bounded rationality). ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Arises when actors have accurate information and sensible incentives only for their immediate boundary, without visibility into downstream couplings or shared stocks. Common in organisational silos, commons dilemmas, and multi-team software delivery.

### Evidence

Extracted from [[SoT - Systems Thinking]] (Meadows and Open University synthesis). No verbatim book quote has been verified for this seed note yet.

### Implications

- Blaming individual actors for systemic dysfunction misses the structural incentives driving their locally sensible choices.
- Fixing whole-system failure requires changing information flows and boundary incentives rather than demanding heroism from local participants.
