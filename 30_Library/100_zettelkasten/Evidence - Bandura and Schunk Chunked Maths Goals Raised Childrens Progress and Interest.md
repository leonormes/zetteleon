## Evidence - Bandura and Schunk Chunked Maths Goals Raised Childrens Progress and Interest

> the children advised to break their goal down into chunks progressed much more rapidly. Not only did they outscore the other groups, but they also became more interested in maths

Source: Owain Service, _Think Small_, describing a study by Albert Bandura and Dale Schunk in which children working through a 42-page subtraction booklet were split into groups; the chunking group was advised to tackle six pages per session.

### What it Supports

Breaking a large goal into manageable parts improved both progress and interest, which is the general mechanism behind [[Micro-Stepping Reduces Cognitive Load for Task Initiation|Breaking Projects Into Micro-Tasks Reduces ADHD Overwhelm]].

### What it Does not Show

The participants were children learning maths, not adults with ADHD, and this is a secondary account of the study, not the paper. It supports the decomposition mechanism, not the ADHD-specific claims about overwhelm, re-entry or dopamine.
