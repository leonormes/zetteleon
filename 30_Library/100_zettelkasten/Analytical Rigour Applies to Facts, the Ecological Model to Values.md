## Analytical Rigour Applies to Facts, the Ecological Model to Values

Analytical rigour fits factual and formal claims, where evidence and logic can settle the matter, while opinions, preferences and values are better understood with the ecological model, in which a person's conclusion is a grown product of their inputs. Applying either tool in the wrong domain gives false certainty about values or false relativism about facts. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies when deciding how to approach a disagreement or a claim. The line between the domains is a working distinction, since many claims mix a factual part with a values part. Values can still be chosen, argued over and acted on. The card leans on the fact-value distinction, which is contested.

### Evidence

This is the author's own reasoning. The note's origin was not recorded: its Provenance section was empty. Two sentences from Haidt support only the values side, that moral conclusions are not produced by theorem-style reasoning:

> "Western philosophy has been worshipping reason and distrusting the passions for thousands of years."
> (Haidt 2012, ch. 2, PDF p. 42) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/1639/EPUB?open_at=toc:2.%20The%20Intuitive%20Dog%20and%20Its%20Rational%20Tail)

> "Intuitions come first, strategic reasoning second."
> (Haidt 2012, "Introduction", PDF p. 12) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/1639/EPUB?open_at=toc:Introduction)

Haidt calls this over-trust of reason in moral matters the rationalist delusion.

### Implications

- Before arguing, sort the disagreement. Use evidence and logic for the factual or formal part, and trace the inputs behind the belief for the values part.
- A mixed claim splits into its factual and values parts, and each part gets its own tool.
- The two failure modes are mirror images: treating a value as a provable fact, and treating a fact as one opinion among many.

### Falsifier

- Evidence that disagreements over values are settled by evidence and logic as reliably as factual ones, or that factual disputes are resolved better by tracing the believer's inputs than by testing the claim.
