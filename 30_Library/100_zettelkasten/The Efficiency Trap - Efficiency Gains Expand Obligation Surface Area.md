## The Efficiency Trap - Efficiency Gains Expand Obligation Surface Area

Efficiency interventions are self-defeating because each efficiency gain expands the perceived surface area of obligation, which intensifies the very anxiety the system was built to soothe. This is structurally consistent with reward-prediction-error neuroscience, since a brain that treats completion as reward will continuously discount finished goals and reorient toward the next unpredicted gain.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies specifically to efficiency-maximisation as a strategy. Does not apply to bounded, values-aligned selection of a finite set of actions within accepted time constraints.

### Evidence

> "Productivity is a trap. Becoming more efficient just makes you more rushed, and trying to clear the decks simply makes them fill up again faster… Each efficiency gain expands the surface area of perceived obligation, which intensifies the very anxiety the system was constructed to soothe."

### Implications

- Clearing an inbox or automating a task frees capacity that is near-instantly refilled with new perceived obligations, so "getting more done" is not a stable end-state.
- System design goals should shift from throughput maximisation to deliberate scarcity: choosing what NOT to do.
