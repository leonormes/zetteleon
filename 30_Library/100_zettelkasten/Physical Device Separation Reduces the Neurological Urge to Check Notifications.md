## Physical Device Separation Reduces the Neurological Urge to Check Notifications

Leaving a mobile device plugged in a separate room (e.g., the kitchen) while at home—rather than keeping it nearby—weakens the neurological checking urge over time. The mechanism is not willpower suppression but circuit starvation: removing the physical proximity of the stimulus prevents the reward circuit from being triggered.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to non-work hours or designated focus periods at home. The benefit is neurological, not just behavioural—the goal is to let the checking-urge circuit weaken from disuse, which requires consistent application over days to weeks. Short-term application produces friction; sustained application produces rewiring.

### Evidence

> "Leave your phone plugged in a separate room (like the kitchen) whilst at home to weaken the neurological urge to check it." [16:33]

### Implications

- Increases the physical friction of distraction—the single most effective environmental lever for reducing impulsive behaviour.
- Consistent application allows dopamine circuits tied to notification-checking to attenuate, reducing the urge rather than merely delaying it.
