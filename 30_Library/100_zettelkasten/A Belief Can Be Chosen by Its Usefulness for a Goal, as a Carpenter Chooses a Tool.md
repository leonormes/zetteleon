## A Belief Can Be Chosen by Its Usefulness for a Goal, as a Carpenter Chooses a Tool

Instead of asking whether a belief is objectively true, ask whether it serves the goal in hand, as a carpenter picks a hammer or a saw for the job. The belief is treated as an instrument to be selected, not an identity to be defended.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to beliefs in the uncertain middle ground between fact and faith. It is bounded by the good-tool criteria in [[A Belief Is a Good Tool Only If It Holds Up to Feedback, Stays Revisable and Does Not Require Ignoring Evidence]], so it does not license beliefs that ignore evidence.

### Evidence

> "Rather than asking 'Is this belief objectively true?', the pragmatic question becomes 'Does this belief serve me?'."
> "Eyal compares choosing a belief to a carpenter selecting a tool."
> (Nir Eyal, Beyond Belief, via an LLM summary; chapter and page not captured)

### Implications

- Example from the source: "I can finish this marathon" is not a proven fact before the race, but holding it generates more effort than holding "I can't".
- Treats a belief as something to be selected for the job, not defended as part of one's identity.
