## External Supports for Discipline Divide Into Designed Systems and Embedded Social Structures That Fail in Different Ways

The external supports behind discipline divide into designed ones such as systems and environment, which you build, and social ones such as culture, incentives and accountability, which you are embedded in, and the two fail in different ways. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to any attempt to rely on structure for consistent action. The split is a working distinction and real supports often mix both.

### Evidence

This is my own reasoning, carried over from the earlier text of [[Discipline Is a Property of External Structure More Than of Will]]. No book quotation has been verified for this card. A designed support fails by being tinkered with instead of used, and its repair is to simplify and use the crudest version. A social support fails by disappearing when you leave the group, and its repair is to re-enter or rebuild the group.

### Implications

- Diagnose a failing routine by asking which family it belongs to before choosing the repair.
- Do not expect a designed system to supply the pressure that only other people supply.

### Falsifier

- If designed and social supports fail in the same ways and respond to the same repairs, the distinction adds nothing.
