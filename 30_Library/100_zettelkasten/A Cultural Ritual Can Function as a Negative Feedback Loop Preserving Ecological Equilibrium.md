## A Cultural Ritual Can Function as a Negative Feedback Loop Preserving Ecological Equilibrium

The Tsembaga people of New Guinea historically regulated pig populations through periodic ritual pig feasts, an embedded cultural practice that functioned as a negative feedback loop culling the population before it stripped the rainforest of resources—disrupting the ritual removes the feedback loop and exposes the ecosystem to overshoot.

### Scope & Conditions

Illustrates that negative feedback loops preserving a system's carrying capacity need not be biological or mechanical—cultural practices can occupy the same functional role.

### Evidence

> "The Tsembaga people of New Guinea historically maintained ecological equilibrium through ritualistic pig feasts. This cultural practice acts as an embedded negative feedback loop, periodically culling the pig population before it can strip the rainforest of its resources."

### Implications

- When assessing why a system stays stable, check for non-obvious regulating mechanisms (rituals, norms, informal practices) before assuming stability is automatic or requires no maintenance.
