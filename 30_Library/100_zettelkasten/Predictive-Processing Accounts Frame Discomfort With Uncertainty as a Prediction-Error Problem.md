## Predictive-Processing Accounts Frame Discomfort With Uncertainty as a Prediction-Error Problem

One influential account in cognitive science treats the brain as a system that continually predicts incoming sensory information and updates its models when predictions fail; on this view, uncertainty becomes uncomfortable specifically when the world's behaviour is difficult to predict and the cost of being wrong seems high, rather than uncertainty being aversive in itself.

### Scope & Conditions

The source explicitly notes one does not need to adopt the full predictive-processing framework to use this narrower insight.

### Evidence

> "One influential account in cognitive science treats brains as systems that continually predict incoming sensory information and update their models when predictions fail. You do not need to adopt the whole predictive-processing framework to use the insight: uncertainty becomes uncomfortable when the world's behaviour is difficult to predict and the cost of being wrong seems high."

### Implications

- Suggests two independent levers for reducing discomfort with uncertainty: improve predictability (build a better model) or lower the perceived cost of being wrong (reduce stakes/exposure)—either one weakens the discomfort even without resolving the other.
