## Collective Moral Licensing Leads Voters to Tolerate In-Group Corruption

Voters who identify as the moral resistance to a perceived extreme threat acquire collective moral credentials that license them to tolerate lies, manipulation, or corruption from their own representatives. Because their overarching political goal feels indisputably righteous, they relax the ethical scrutiny they would otherwise apply to misconduct. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- **Collective transfer:** Extends individual-level moral licensing—where a past good deed excuses a later ethical lapse—to a shared partisan identity, allowing voters to grant moral credit to in-group politicians.
- **Threat-driven asymmetry:** Operates most strongly under high polarisation when the opposing side is framed as an existential threat, and is documented as providing cover even for "apex corruption" at the highest levels of government.

### Evidence

> "Because the voters feel their overarching goal—defeating the extreme leader—is indisputably righteous, they extend moral licensing to their own politicians, tolerating lies, manipulation, clientelism, or hypocrisy that they would otherwise fiercely condemn."
> — _Political Contrast Effect Research_ (working synthesis; no external library source linked)

### Implications

- Moral self-certainty about the righteousness of a cause can itself become the mechanism that shields and enables corruption within that cause.
- Anti-corruption appeals aimed at partisan voters fail if they ignore how opposition-focused moral identity supplies pre-emptive absolution for in-group misconduct.
