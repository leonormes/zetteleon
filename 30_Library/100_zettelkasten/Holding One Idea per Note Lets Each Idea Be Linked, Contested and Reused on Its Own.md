## Holding One Idea per Note Lets Each Idea Be Linked, Contested and Reused on Its Own

A main note that holds exactly one idea lets that idea be linked, contested and reused without bringing unrelated ideas with it. This is the atomicity principle. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- Applies to main (permanent) notes. Fleeting and literature notes are exempt, because they are temporary or shaped by their source.
- One idea is a unit of meaning, not a length limit. The right level is a judgement about what is useful for the task.
- A claim that needs a but or a however is a prompt to check for a second idea.

### Evidence

> "Write exactly one note for each idea and write as if you were writing for someone else"
> (Ahrens 2022, ch. 2, PDF p. 37) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/704/EPUB?open_at=toc:2%20Everything%20You%20Need%20to%20Do)

> "The more "atomic" an idea, the more broadly you can employ it. The more complex an idea, the less surface area it has to be connected to others."
> (Doto 2024, "Part 2", PDF p. 78) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/1491/EPUB?open_at=toc:Part%202)

The first quotation states the practice and the second supports the linking and reuse benefit. The step to contesting an idea on its own is my own reasoning, since an objection to a bundled note does not say which idea it targets.

### Implications

- A note that answers two questions should be split, so each answer can be linked to and disputed separately.
- An existing note can be linked or embedded instead of restated.
- A long note is a reason to check for a second idea, and is not proof of one.

### Falsifier

- Take a set of ideas written both as single-idea notes and as bundled notes. If the single-idea versions are not linked, challenged or reused more precisely over time, the claim is wrong.
