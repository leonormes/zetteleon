## Truth is a Necessary Condition for Knowledge

A person cannot know something that is false, so truth is a necessary condition for knowledge. A belief that is strongly held and well justified but fails to correspond to reality does not qualify as knowledge in the strict philosophical sense. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- Applies to propositional knowledge in the strict sense. A false belief can still give a personal or subjective understanding, but that is distinct from knowledge.
- The condition says what knowledge is. It is not a test that can be run on a disputed claim. Using it as a filter ("God is not true, therefore belief in God is not knowledge") assumes the conclusion in dispute. In practice only justification can be assessed, because nobody has a view from outside their own evidence.
- The same definition makes "God does not exist" knowledge only if it is also justified, so an atheist holding it for bad reasons has at best a lucky true belief. This application came from an AI chat on knowledge of God (2026-10-23, the section "Is there knowledge about God, or only belief?"), and the chat is unverified.
- Pragmatist theories of truth dispute the correspondence picture of truth the condition assumes ([[Pragmatic Truth Focuses on Utility Over Absolute Correctness]]).

### Evidence

One book states the condition as the standard view:

> "Truth is a necessary condition for knowledge"
> (Martin 2014, ch. 1, PDF p. 17) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/298/EPUB?open_at=toc:1%20Defining%20%E2%80%98knowledge%E2%80%99)

The Evidence for the condition itself is conceptual. It rests on the way "know" is used, since "I knew it but it was false" is heard as a contradiction.

### Implications

- Truth is the only one of the three conditions that cannot be assessed from the knower's own position, which is why the practical weight falls on justification.
- It is the point on which the analysis and fallibilism meet: one can know without certainty, but not without truth ([[Fallibilism Holds That Knowledge Requires Good Justification, Not Certainty]]).

### Falsifier

- A clear case in which a person is said to know something that is false, where "know" is not just a loose way of saying "was sure".
