Knowledge serves as a source of power by enabling informed decision-making, problem-solving, and influence over outcomes.

Those who possess relevant knowledge can exert control over situations and guide actions effectively.

## Typed Relationships

[depends_on:: [[Knowledge Supports Prediction and Inference Where Information Alone Does Not]], confidence=medium]

- [[Knowledge Supports Prediction and Inference Where Information Alone Does Not]]—_the mechanism behind the claim: power via "informed decision-making" only follows if knowledge really supports prediction, inference and sound judgement._

## Related

- [[MOC - From Information to Knowledge]]—_home map: this note is the practical end of its information-to-knowledge arc, knowledge put to use._
- [[Information vs Knowledge]]—_"knowledge" here has to mean information tested against reality and integrated into a worldview; raw retrievable information would not confer the control the claim describes._
- [[Knowledge Emerges Through Application and Experience]]—_the route by which knowledge becomes usable in decisions and problem-solving._
- [[SoT - Authority-Competence Asymmetry]]—_counter-case: organisations where decision power follows title rather than knowledge, so knowing more does not by itself confer control._
- [[Latour Treats Scientific Facts as Socially Constructed Through Networks of Power]]—_reverse direction: on Latour's view power shapes what counts as fact, so the arrow between knowledge and power may run both ways._

## Tensions

- The claim reads as unconditional, but [[SoT - Authority-Competence Asymmetry]] documents knowledgeable people being overridden by positional authority. Both can hold if "power" is split into expert influence and positional control; the note does not say which it means.
