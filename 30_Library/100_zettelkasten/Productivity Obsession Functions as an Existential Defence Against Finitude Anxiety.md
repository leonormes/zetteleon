### Productivity Obsession Functions as an Existential Defence Against Finitude Anxiety
Oliver Burkeman's analysis in Four Thousand Weeks argues that compulsive productivity operates as an anxiety-management strategy against the groundlessness of finite lifespan, structurally parallel to Ernest Becker's "immortality projects" — symbolic systems that generate a felt sense of transcendence beyond biological limitation.

#### Scope & Conditions
Applies to the psychological function of productivity-system-building itself, distinct from the practical usefulness of any given system. The productivity system becomes the "immortality project" when its promise is escaping the anxiety of finitude rather than getting specific work done.

#### Evidence
> "The defence mechanism Burkeman identifies — compulsive productivity — operates through a logical structure parallel to what Ernest Becker described... as the construction of immortality projects... For the knowledge worker, the productivity system is the immortality project."

#### Implications
- Endless system-rebuilding (tool-hopping, re-architecting the vault, chasing the "perfect" setup) can itself be a symptom of finitude anxiety rather than a genuine productivity gain.
- Recognising this function allows a system to be evaluated on whether it enables bounded, values-aligned action rather than whether it feels like it is solving mortality.
