## Removing a Negative Feedback Loop Can Cause Ecological Overshoot (The Kaibab Deer Case)

When natural predators were removed from the Kaibab Plateau, a critical negative feedback loop on the deer population disappeared, allowing exponential population growth that overshot the habitat's carrying capacity, consumed vegetation down to the roots, and caused a population crash to a level far below the original carrying capacity.

### Scope & Conditions

A concrete instance of the general overshoot/collapse mechanism; real ecosystems usually have multiple overlapping feedback loops, so removing one does not guarantee total collapse.

### Evidence

> "Removing natural predators removes a critical negative feedback loop, allowing a deer population to grow exponentially. The population overshoots the habitat's carrying capacity, consuming vegetation down to the roots. The resulting permanent degradation of the food supply causes a massive population crash to a level far lower than the original carrying capacity."

### Implications

- Removing a regulating constraint (a predator, a review step, a rate limit) doesn't just remove friction—it can remove the very signal that would have prevented overshoot.
