This is the load-bearing consequence of the atelic claim, and the reason the argument is not merely academic. If the value of thinking is intrinsic, then it is _not revocable by anyone else_. No employer, institution or ranking can withdraw it, because none of them conferred it.

Hitz draws the examples deliberately from the margins: prisoners, factory workers, the economically disenfranchised. The point of choosing them is that the refuge is most visible where every other source of standing has been stripped away. What remains is not consolation for the absence of status—it is a good of a different type entirely, one that status was never a substitute for.

The practical corollary for anyone who thinks a great deal and produces intermittently: the hours of thinking were not a failure to produce. They were the activity.

## Steelman

"Dignity independent of output" is a claim that is easiest to make from a position where output is not in question. It is worth asking whether it functions as genuine philosophical consolation or as a rationalisation that happens to be most attractive to those who are already secure—and whether the historical figures cited would have described it that way themselves.
