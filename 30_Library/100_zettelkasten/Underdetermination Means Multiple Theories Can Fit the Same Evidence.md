## Underdetermination Means Multiple Theories Can Fit the Same Evidence

More than one theory can sometimes explain the same body of evidence equally well. When this happens, fit-to-data alone is not enough to choose between competing explanations—scientists also weigh explanatory power, coherence with other established knowledge, predictive success, and simplicity.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies whenever two or more incompatible theories both accommodate the current evidence. It is a standing limit on theory-choice, not a flaw specific to weak or immature fields.

### Implications

- A theory that merely fits the data is not automatically the best explanation—ask what else it would need to beat on explanatory power, simplicity, and predictive success.
- Underdetermination is a reason evidence alone rarely ends scientific debate outright; convergence from independent methods is what typically breaks the tie.
