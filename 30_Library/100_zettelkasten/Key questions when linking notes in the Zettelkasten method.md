## Key Questions when Linking Notes in the Zettelkasten Method

- What is this a part of? This question helps to identify broader concepts or themes that the note relates to. By asking this question, you can link your note to more general notes in your Zettelkasten.
- What is this similar to or different from? This encourages you to make connections between seemingly disparate ideas, fostering a more networked understanding of your knowledge base. This can lead to new insights and a deeper understanding of the relationships between different concepts.
- What compliments or competes with this? This question prompts you to consider different perspectives and arguments related to the note's content, enriching your understanding and potentially leading to new insights or syntheses.
- What larger pattern does this concept belong to? Similar to "What is this a part of?", this question prompts you to think about the broader context of your note. By identifying larger patterns, you can connect your note to other related ideas and create a more interconnected web of knowledge.
- What more basic components is this concept made of? This question encourages you to break down complex ideas into smaller, more manageable chunks, enhancing your understanding and making your notes more reusable in different contexts. By linking to these sub-components, you create a hierarchical structure that can aid both comprehension and recall.
- What does this idea enhance, make obsolete, retrieve, or reverse?
- This question, inspired by the work of Robert Boyle, encourages you to consider the note's implications and potential impact on existing knowledge or beliefs. This can help you to identify areas for further exploration and to develop more nuanced and insightful perspectives.
