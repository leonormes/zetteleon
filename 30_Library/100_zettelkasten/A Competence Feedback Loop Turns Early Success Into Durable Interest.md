## A Competence Feedback Loop Turns Early Success Into Durable Interest

A small early success in a domain creates a self-reinforcing loop—perceived capability increases willingness to engage, which increases practice and pattern recognition, which produces more success and strengthens identity and interest—so two people of similar underlying ability can end up with radically different relationships to the same subject depending on when and how that loop started.

### Scope & Conditions

Illustrated in the source with mathematics specifically; the loop is disrupted by anxiety and repeated failure, which push engagement the opposite way. Interest formed this way is also contingent on how the subject was taught—procedural, speedand evaluation-focused instruction can prevent a learner from ever meeting the parts of a subject (creativity, play, beauty, explanatory power) that build durable interest in someone else.

### Evidence

> "A small success → 'I can do this' → greater willingness to engage → more practice and attention → more success and pattern recognition → stronger identity and interest… This means two people of similar underlying ability can develop radically different relationships to maths depending on instruction, timing, feedback, peer environment, whether mistakes felt safe, and whether the subject was presented as meaningful rather than as a performance test."

### Implications

- Whether a subject "clicked" early is a stronger predictor of adult interest than raw aptitude.
- Interventions to build interest in a disliked subject should target early, safe wins over more exposure.
