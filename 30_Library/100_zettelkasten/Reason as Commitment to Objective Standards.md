## Reason as Commitment to Objective Standards

Anyone who insists that their answers are reasonable, justified or true, and that others ought to believe them, has committed to reason, meaning holding their beliefs accountable to objective standards. The source is Steven Pinker, Enlightenment Now.

### Scope & Conditions

Pinker's argument that reason is nonnegotiable in any discussion; it applies to whoever offers answers to any question, including what we should live for.

### Evidence

> "Reason is nonnegotiable. As soon as you show up to discuss the question of what we should live for (or any other question), as long as you insist that your answers, whatever they are, are reasonable or justified or true and that therefore other people ought to believe them too, then you have committed yourself to reason, and to holding your beliefs accountable to objective standards."
> (Enlightenment Now, Chapter 1: Dare to Understand!)

### Implications

- Rejecting reason while arguing for a position is self-undermining.
- The standard applies to every belief offered for others to accept.
