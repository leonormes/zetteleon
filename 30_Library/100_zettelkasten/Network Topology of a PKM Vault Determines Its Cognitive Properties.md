## Network Topology of a PKM Vault Determines Its Cognitive Properties

PKM vault architectures fall along a topology spectrum: centralised (single-hub index, efficient but a single point of failure), distributed (multiple hubs with MOCs, the most common mature-vault pattern), and decentralised or networked (dense bidirectional peer-to-peer links, maximum associative discovery but highest maintenance cost). The decentralised networked topology is the most cognitively faithful to human associative-memory architecture, where concepts activate adjacent concepts through spreading activation.

### Scope & Conditions

Dense bidirectional linking additionally exploits the spacing effect and retrieval-practice effect, since navigating a link constitutes a retrieval event and re-encountering a note through multiple MOC contexts mirrors interleaved, spaced-practice conditions that maximise long-term retention.

### Evidence

> "The decentralised networked topology is the most cognitively faithful to human memory architecture. Semantic memory is organised as a distributed associative network in which concepts activate adjacent concepts through spreading activation—a mechanism that bidirectional linking directly simulates."

### Implications

- MOCs should be treated as multi-dimensional access points—any atomic note can appear in several MOCs without duplication—not single-parent folders.
- Maintenance cost of dense linking is a deliberate trade-off for retrieval and consolidation benefits, not pure overhead.
