## Understanding Is Demonstrated by Creation Rather Than Passive Familiarity

Passive familiarity with a concept, tool, or mental model does not constitute understanding; genuine comprehension is demonstrated only when a person can build a working artefact with it or apply it to solve a real problem. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to operational, technical, and conceptual models where functional mastery—not mere recognition or verbal summary—is required. Falsified if learners who only read or recognise a model consistently perform as well on novel problem-solving and debugging tasks as those who build with it.

### Evidence

> "You do not understand a code library until you have built a small app with it. You do not understand a mental model until you have applied it to a real-life problem."
> ([[SoT - Illusion of Explanatory Depth (IoED)]])

### Implications

- Reading, highlighting, or nodding along to an explanation cannot verify comprehension; only generative output tests whether the underlying mechanism is actually grasped.
- Learning workflows should pair conceptual study with small, concrete build tests before treating a model or library as understood.

### Steelman

Requiring creation as the universal bar for understanding may be overly strict for purely interpretive or evaluative domains—a person can accurately critique, apply, or teach a model without having built a novel artefact with it, making creation sufficient rather than strictly necessary evidence of comprehension.
