## Experts Must De-Compile Abstractions Into Concrete Examples for Novices

Experts compress years of concrete experience into high-level abstractions, which makes those abstractions opaque to novices who lack the underlying examples. Bridging the expert-novice gap therefore requires two moves: concretisation—de-compiling abstract knowledge back into the concrete examples that formed it—and scaffolding—explicitly connecting new information to concepts the novice already possesses.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies whenever an expert teaches, mentors, or coordinates with someone outside their domain. Between peers who already share the same compressed abstractions, staying at the abstract level is faster and avoids unnecessary verbosity.

### Evidence

This claim synthesises the expert-novice communication pattern from [[SoT - Communication & Misunderstanding (The Experiential Filter)]] and [[Communicating Depends on Modelling What the Listener Already Knows]] rather than a single external quotation.

### Implications

- Explaining an abstraction with another abstraction compounds confusion for a novice; start with a concrete instance and name the pattern second.
- Asking what the listener already knows is a prerequisite for choosing effective scaffolds rather than an optional courtesy.
