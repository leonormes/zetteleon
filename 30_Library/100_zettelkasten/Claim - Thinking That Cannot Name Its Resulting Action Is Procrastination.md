## The Position

Extracted verbatim from [[SoT - Think Like a Man of Action, Act Like a Man of Thought]] §3A, "Shrink the Thought" (authored 2025-12-07):

> "Thinking must be decisive, not exploratory."
> "The Question: *What is the single, specific action this thinking is supposed to lead to?*"
> "The Effect: **If you can't answer, you are procrastinating.** Stop thinking and take any action."

This is the procedural form of [[Claim - A Note That Does Not Help You Act Is Noise]] — the same test, applied to a thinking session rather than a note, and stated more harshly: it does not call the output noise, it calls the thinker procrastinating.

## Why It Was Retired — 2026-08-29

**It misreads the maxim it sits under.** "Think like a man of action, act like a man of thought" is a chiasmus. Each half instructs you to import the *other* domain's virtue into your current one:

- *Think like a man of action* — while thinking, bring groundedness and contact with the real.
- *Act like a man of thought* — while acting, act on models already built rather than re-deriving at the point of performance.

The structure presupposes **two domains that remain distinct**. §3A collapses it into "only think about actions," which is a different and much stronger claim than the maxim makes.

## Additional Objection

The test is unfalsifiable in the direction that matters. "Name the action this will lead to" cannot be answered for model-building work at the time the work is done — the value shows up later, in the quality of action taken under a better model ([[Flawed Mental Models Limit Mastery]]). Requiring the answer up front does not filter procrastination; it filters slow-return thinking.
