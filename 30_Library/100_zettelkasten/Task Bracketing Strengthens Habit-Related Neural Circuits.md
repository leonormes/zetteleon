Task bracketing is a technique for strengthening habits by reinforcing the neural circuits at the beginning and end of a behavior. Neural circuits in the basal ganglia are most active at the start and finish of a habit. By creating clear triggers (start-cues) and ending rituals (end-cues), you can strengthen the entire habit loop.

[depends_on:: [[Neuroplasticity is the Foundation for Habit Change]], confidence=medium]

## What Is Claimed, and What Is Evidenced

The note makes two separate claims:

1. A neuroscience claim: basal ganglia circuits are most active at the start and the finish of a habit. The Power of Habit (in your library) cites Ann Graybiel's striatal recording studies for this line of work, but the passage that states the finding could not be retrieved, so it is unverified here.
2. A technique claim: creating clear start-cues and end-cues strengthens the whole habit loop. This is an inference from the first claim. The vault has no test of it, which is why the `epistemic_status` is `low`.

## Tensions

- [[The Hemingway Technique - End Work With Unfinished Problems]]—_Advises ending a session on an open problem, where bracketing asks for a clear ending. Both can hold: the Hemingway ending serves the next session's restart, while bracketing is about closing the habit loop. They apply to different ends (the session versus the habit)._
