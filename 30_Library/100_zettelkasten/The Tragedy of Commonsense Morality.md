## The Tragedy of Commonsense Morality

The Tragedy of Commonsense Morality is conflict between groups that are each moral internally. It is caused by incompatible visions of what a moral society should be, not by selfishness, and it is the second kind of moral problem Greene distinguishes (Us versus Them).

### Scope & Conditions

Illustrated by the Parable of the New Pastures, in which four tribes with different internal rules meet on shared land.

### Evidence

> "They fight not because they are fundamentally selfish but because they have incompatible visions of what a moral society should be."
>
> "Each tribe has its own version of moral common sense. The tribes of the new pastures fight not because they are immoral but because they view life on the new pastures from very different moral perspectives. I call this the Tragedy of Commonsense Morality."
> (Joshua Greene, Moral Tribes, Introduction: The Tragedy of Commonsense Morality)

### Implications

- Good will inside each group does not prevent conflict between groups.
- Moral disagreement between groups is not reducible to one side being selfish or immoral.
