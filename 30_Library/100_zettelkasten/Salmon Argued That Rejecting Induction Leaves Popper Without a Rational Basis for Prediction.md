## Salmon Argued That Rejecting Induction Leaves Popper Without a Rational Basis for Prediction

Salmon argued that Popper can say which theories have been refuted but cannot say why any survivor should be relied on to predict the future. Corroboration is a record of the past. If it says something about the future it is induction in disguise, and if it does not there is no rational basis for choosing the best-tested theory to act on.

### Scope & Conditions

This is a secondhand report from an AI chat, not read from Salmon's paper. The chat's illustration uses two theories that have passed every test so far, one of which says bridges built after 2027 collapse, and observes that every test ever run predates 2027. The chat says it is genuinely open whether Salmon wins, and that the Popperian replies may only relocate the problem.

### Evidence

> "Salmon’s 1981 paper “Rational Prediction” argues that Popper’s rejection of induction makes rational theory-based prediction impossible. Popper can tell you which theories have been refuted. He can’t tell you, without sneaking induction back in, why you should rely on any surviving theory to predict what happens next."
> (AI chat transcript pasted by Leon, 2026-10-23, section "Wesley Salmon’s criticism of Popper")

> "Either corroboration does tell you something about the future, in which case it’s induction in disguise. Or it doesn’t, in which case choosing the best-tested theory for prediction has no rational basis."
> (same section)

### Implications

- The same question applies to any belief held because it has survived criticism: why should surviving count in its favour?
- Every reply in the chat ends in a provisional decision and not in a bedrock, which the chat calls Popper's piles in the swamp.
