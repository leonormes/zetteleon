## The Void (Ku) Is Perceiving the Situation as It Is Without the Distortion of Fear or Desire

In the Book of Void, the last scroll of _The Book of Five Rings_, Musashi describes the ultimate state as one free from confusion. Emotions often act as a lens that distorts reality, as when "I feel this is impossible" is taken for a fact. In the Void the lens is removed and the situation is seen as it is and not as one fears it to be.

### Scope & Conditions

- This is a claim about perception under fear and desire. It does not say the person feels nothing.
- Perception is never wholly unmediated, because the brain reads the world through predictions ([[The Brain Uses Beliefs as Prediction Shortcuts Because Conscious Processing Is Limited]]). The realistic reading is that the Void reduces the distortion added by fear and desire, not that it removes all interpretation. This reading is the note's own.
- The SoT is the only source used here. The library holds no copy of the Book of Void.

### Evidence

> "Emotions often act as a lens that distorts reality (e.g., "I feel this is impossible")."
> ([[SoT - Miyamoto Musashi's Philosophy on Mastering Emotions]], §2C The Void)

> "By entering the Void, you see the situation _as it is_, not as you fear it to be."
> (same section)

### Implications

- It names the remedy for [[Emotional Reasoning]], where a feeling is taken as evidence about the world.
- It supports the claim that events carry no emotional charge of their own, which is the position of [[The External World is Neutral]].
- It gives a practical check before acting: ask whether the problem described is the situation or the fear of it.
