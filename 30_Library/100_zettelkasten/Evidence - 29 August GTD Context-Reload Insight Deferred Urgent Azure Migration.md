## Evidence - 29 August GTD Context-Reload Insight Deferred Urgent Azure Migration

> "If I were able to do the Allen thing and take time to plan the work I could unlock the GTD he talks about."
> — Observed session episode, 29 August 2026

Looking at a GTD next-action list, the author identified that they involuntarily reload full project context before acting, turning each small task into a heavy cognitive exercise. Across several exchanges the analysis deepened (disjointed closures feel unrewarding; horizontal execution across projects falters where vertical does not), yet it terminated in a prescription for more planning while FTFL-619—carrying a hard Azure auto-migration deadline of 1 September with only Monday 31 August remaining—was raised three times across five exchanges and never moved.

### What It Supports

- Supports [[Self-insights that prescribe more planning are the least trustworthy kind]] by demonstrating the pattern executing in real time: a genuinely sound self-observation still terminated in a planning prescription that displaced an urgent physical deadline.
- Illustrates that the subjective clarity and relief of theoretical elaboration can coexist with—and mask—task avoidance.

### What It Does Not Show

- Does not establish whether the underlying GTD bottleneck is context-loading (a cognitive tax removable by trusting prior planning) or context-reward (flat closure reward not under voluntary control); only a bounded horizontal execution session settles that distinction.
- Single observed episode; does not by itself measure base-rate frequency across all self-insights.
