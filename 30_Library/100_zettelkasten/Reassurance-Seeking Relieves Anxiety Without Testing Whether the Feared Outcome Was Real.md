## Reassurance-Seeking Relieves Anxiety Without Testing Whether the Feared Outcome Was Real

Checking or information-seeking used as a safety behaviour reduces distress immediately, but because relief arrives before the person can observe whether the feared outcome would have happened anyway, the nervous system credits the safety behaviour with preventing danger—which lowers tolerance for acting under ordinary uncertainty next time and makes the behaviour self-reinforcing.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to reassurance-seeking and safety-behaviour patterns generally (established in anxiety-disorder research); illustrated in the source with technical over-preparation (researching a system before making a change).

### Evidence

> "relief arrives immediately after seeking it, so the nervous system may credit the search with preventing an outcome that was never actually likely… checking or searching can reduce distress rapidly, but because the person does not get to discover that the feared outcome might not occur without the safety behaviour, the behaviour becomes self-reinforcing."

### Implications

- The fix is not "stop seeking information" but building in a corrective experience: acting under bounded uncertainty and observing that the feared outcome didn't occur.
- Explains why over-preparation can coexist with genuine expertise—the loop is about relief timing, not about whether the person's knowledge is objectively good.
