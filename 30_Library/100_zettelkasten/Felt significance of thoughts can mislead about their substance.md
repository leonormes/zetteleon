## Felt Significance of Thoughts Can Mislead about Their Substance

Summary: The phenomenological experience of a thought feeling important, profound, or insightful is not a reliable indicator of the thought's actual novelty, validity, or substance—these are separate dimensions that often dissociate.

Details:

There is a systematic gap between:

- Felt significance: The subjective sense that a thought is important, profound, or insightful
- Actual substance: The objective quality, novelty, validity, or practical value of the thought's content

This dissociation occurs because:

- Phenomenological qualities (emotional resonance, felt profundity) arise from the experience of thinking, not from the content itself
- The brain's bias toward its own thoughts amplifies felt significance
- Context, mood, and mental state influence how thoughts feel, independent of their merit
- Novel associations can feel profound even when the resulting idea is trivial

Common manifestations:

- "Shower thoughts" that feel brilliant but are obvious when written
- Late-night insights that seem trivial in the morning
- Ideas that feel groundbreaking but are already well-established
- Arguments that feel compelling internally but have clear flaws when externalized

The practical implication: Felt significance should be treated as a signal to examine a thought more carefully (through writing or articulation), not as evidence of the thought's actual value. The feeling itself is real and valid, but it's about the experience of thinking, not the quality of what is thought.

## Where the Causes Live in the Vault

The four causes listed above each have their own note.

- Qualities come from the experience of thinking, not the content. [[Thoughts are bundled with phenomenological qualities]]
- The brain favours its own thoughts. [[The brain is biased toward its own thoughts in the moment]]
- Mood and mental state colour how a thought feels. ## Tensions

- Personal meaning. The [[MOC - Why Thoughts Feel More Important When Thinking Them]] map notes that a thought which seems ordinary on the page can still carry real personal value (a realisation about your own situation). The claim above is about validity and novelty, not personal meaning, so both hold; the practical rule is to separate the three cases (feels important but empty, personally meaningful though ordinary, and both feeling and substance).
- [[Transition Times Are Valuable Windows for Creative Insight]]—_Argues that insights from the edge of sleep are valuable, while "late-night insights that seem trivial in the morning" is one of this note's manifestations. Compatible if the window is used to generate and the morning check is used to judge._
- Judging too early. In _A System for Writing_ (see below) Luhmann's practice is not to decide a note's usefulness at writing time, but later, by relating it to other notes. That suggests the check on substance can be deferred to the graph, not only done by rewriting on the spot.
