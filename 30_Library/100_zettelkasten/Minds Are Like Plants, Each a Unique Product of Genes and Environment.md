## Minds Are Like Plants, Each a Unique Product of Genes and Environment

Each human mind is a unique product of the genes and environment that shaped it, so sorting persons into right or wrong is mismatched to reality. A belief structure is better diagnosed by tracing its inputs, namely the soil (environmental stressors) and the fertiliser (absorbed narratives), than judged solely by its output. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to judging persons and their belief structures, especially in disagreement. It contrasts with an axiomatic framework, which assumes a single objective Truth built from core axioms, treats disagreement as an error and sorts people into right and wrong. Individual propositions can still be evaluated as true or false. The plant metaphor must not be read as saying that a mind cannot change.

### Evidence

This is a conceptual argument by analogy; no direct source or quotation is attached.

### Implications

- Disagreement becomes an occasion to collect data about inputs rather than a debate to win.
- The reflex to treat those who disagree as broken axioms loses its grounds.
