### Ranganathan's PMEST Facets Decompose a Subject Without Forcing a Single Parent Category

Ranganathan's faceted classification analyses any subject into five facets—Personality (the core defining entity), Matter (physical materials or properties involved), Energy (the actions, processes, or operations occurring), Space (the geographic or spatial environment), and Time (the chronological period)—so a subject need not be filed under a single hierarchical parent.

#### Scope & Conditions

Applies to classification of multidimensional or cross-disciplinary subjects that break a rigid enumerative (tree-shaped) hierarchy.

#### Evidence

> "Ranganathan posited that any complex subject could be systematically analyzed and broken down into five fundamental categories, or facets."

#### Implications

- A faceted tagging schema lets a query aggregate all notes sharing one facet value (e.g. all notes where Energy = Optimization) regardless of differing Personality/domain values.
- This is the mechanism by which cross-disciplinary interests become queryable without a shared folder.
