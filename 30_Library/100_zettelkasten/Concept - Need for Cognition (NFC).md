## Minimum Viable Understanding

Identified by Cacioppo & Petty (1982), Need for Cognition (NFC) measures how much a person actively seeks out and enjoys deliberation and complex thought. High-NFC individuals do not necessarily _choose_ to overthink; rather, they experience unresolved questions as mildly aversive (deprivation-type curiosity) and resolution as intrinsically rewarding.

## Mechanisms & Expressions

- Information Gap Theory: High NFC often presents as an "itch" that won't let go until a knowledge gap is closed (similar to Loewenstein's theory where curiosity behaves like hunger).
- Relocation of Bias: High-NFC people are not immune to cognitive biases like the Illusion of Explanatory Depth; they merely relocate these illusions to more flattering or complex domains (e.g., believing they understand _why_ they understand).
