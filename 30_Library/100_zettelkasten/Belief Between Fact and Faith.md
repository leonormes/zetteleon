## Belief Between Fact and Faith

A belief is a firmly held but provisional working mental model. It sits on a spectrum between fact, an objective and verifiable truth that holds whether or not anyone believes it, and faith, a deep conviction that needs no objective evidence or proof. What marks a belief out is that it stays open to revision when new evidence arrives.

### Scope & Conditions

A narrow, stipulative usage: belief is defined by its revisability, not by the strength of the conviction. It differs from belief as one component of knowledge in the justified-true-belief definition, so read the term in this sense only where the source is Eyal.

### Evidence

> "Belief: A firmly held opinion or working mental model that is provisional and open to revision when new evidence arrives."
> "Fact: An objective, measurable, and verifiable truth that exists independently of whether someone believes it."
> "Faith: A deep conviction that does not require objective evidence or empirical proof."
> (Nir Eyal, Beyond Belief, via an LLM summary; chapter and page not captured)

### Implications

- Gives a three-point spectrum for placing any conviction as fact, belief or faith.
- Makes revisability, not conviction strength, the test of whether something is a belief.
