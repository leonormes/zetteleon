Summary: Logic serves as the "grammar of reason" in mathematics, providing the rules for valid reasoning and the syntax for expressing mathematical structures precisely.

Details:

Just as natural language requires grammatical rules to form meaningful sentences, mathematics requires logical rules to construct valid arguments. Logic provides:

- Syntax: The formal language for expressing mathematical statements precisely
- Rules of inference: Valid patterns for deriving conclusions from premises
- Semantic framework: Methods for determining truth and validity

Role in Mathematics:

Logic is foundational to all mathematical activity. It enables mathematicians to:

1. Define concepts rigorously
2. State theorems precisely
3. Construct proofs that guarantee truth preservation
4. Communicate mathematical ideas unambiguously

Metaphor: Logic is to mathematical reasoning what grammar is to language—the structural framework that makes meaningful expression possible.
