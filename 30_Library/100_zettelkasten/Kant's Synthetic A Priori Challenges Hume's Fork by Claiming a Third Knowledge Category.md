## Kant's Synthetic A Priori Challenges Hume's Fork by Claiming a Third Knowledge Category

Immanuel Kant directly challenged Hume's Fork by arguing for the existence of synthetic a priori knowledge—claims that are both informative about the world (synthetic) and knowable independent of experience (a priori)—a category Hume's two-way division has no room for.

### Scope & Conditions

One of the deepest, most persistent disputes in epistemology; not resolved by either side's original formulation.

### Evidence

> "This binary remains one of the greatest debates in epistemology; Immanuel Kant famously dedicated his work to proving that synthetic a priori knowledge exists."

### Implications

- Citing Hume's Fork as a settled, closed dichotomy overstates its status—it's a strong historical position with a major unresolved rival, not a consensus result.
