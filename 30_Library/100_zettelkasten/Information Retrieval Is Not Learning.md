Retrieval and learning feel similar from the inside and are structurally different. Searching returns the answer without requiring you to build the structure that would have generated it, so the felt sense of competence arrives while the schema does not. This is the illusion of explanatory depth with a search engine attached — and now, with a language model attached.

The practical test is unchanged: can you produce the explanation without the source? If not, what you have is access, not understanding — which is fine until the moment access is unavailable or the retrieved answer is wrong, at which point there is no internal model to catch it.

## Steelman

Extended-mind arguments hold that externalised knowledge is genuine knowledge, and treating the internal copy as privileged is a bias rather than a fact about cognition. A surgeon who consults a checklist is not less competent for it. The claim is strongest for foundational material one must reason *with*, and weakest for reference material one merely reasons *about*.
