The neuroenergetic deficit theory of ADHD proposes that individuals with the condition can only access 75-85% of the neurocognitive energy of their neurotypical peers. This is due to catecholamine hypofunctionality leading to decreased neuronal energy through impaired astrocyte glucose and glycogen metabolism.

This chronic insufficiency of neural resources particularly affects task initiation, which requires maximal prefrontal cortex activation to overcome competing distractions, inhibit alternative behaviours, generate motor plans, and allocate attentional resources — all energetically expensive operations — and helps explain why all phases of task execution are impacted.

Sergeant's cognitive-energetic model adds a mechanism for *why*: ADHD reflects an inability to adjust energetic state (activation/arousal) to task demands. The "effort pool" responsible for modulating activation is dysfunctional, meaning the brain cannot adequately generate the task-directed energy needed to initiate action.

## Evidence

Brain state transition studies show ADHD brains struggle to shift from resting to active task states, with the Default Mode Network showing excess activity during tasks when it should be suppressed.
