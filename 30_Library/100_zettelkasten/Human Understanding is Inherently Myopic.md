A critical limitation of human cognition is the inherent myopia of our understanding. We often perceive only a small fraction of the complete picture, leading to an incomplete or biased view of reality. This natural constraint impacts our ability to grasp complex systems fully.

[[Information Overload]].

[[Myopic Understanding]]

[depends_on:: [[Limited Capacity Brain]], confidence=high]

## Why It Happens

Three mechanisms compound.

- Capacity. [[Limited Capacity Brain]] holds only three or four items in working memory at once, and [[SoT - Working Memory & Schema Theory]] explains how schemas stretch that limit without removing it. Levitin: "A critical point that bears repeating is that attention is a limited-capacity resource—there are definite limits to the number of things we can attend to at once." ([[Evidence - Levitin Says Attention Is a Limited-Capacity Resource With Definite Limits]])
- Completeness is assumed. We build the best story from what we have and treat it as all there is. Kahneman: "You cannot help dealing with the limited information you have as if it were all there is to know." ([[Evidence - Kahneman WYSIATI Says We Treat the Limited Information We Have as if It Were All There Is]]) A coherent story is easier to build from less, which is why the narrow view feels sufficient.
- Some of the picture is not ours. Much of what feels like our own understanding is held in the environment and in other people. Sloman and Fernbach: "We think the knowledge we have about how things work sits inside our skulls when in fact we're drawing a lot of it from the environment and from other people." ([[Evidence - Sloman and Fernbach Say We Mistake Knowledge in the Community for Knowledge in Our Heads]])

Three notes in the vault add the reinforcement loop: [[SoT - Illusion of Explanatory Depth (IoED)]] (felt understanding is not scope), [[Cognitive Biases Reinforce Mental Models]] (contradicting evidence is resisted) and [[The brain is biased toward its own thoughts in the moment|The Brain is Biased toward Its Own Thoughts in the Moment]].
