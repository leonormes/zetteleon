## Modernity Removed Inherited Social Structure So Personal Productivity Systems Must Be Built Deliberately

Modern conditions removed the informal social structure that once supplied routine by default, such as seasons, apprenticeships and fixed hours, so the same support now has to be rebuilt deliberately and individually. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to knowledge work in a distraction-saturated setting. It explains why personal productivity systems exist as a genre, and it does not say past people had more willpower.

### Evidence

This is my own reasoning, carried over from the earlier text of [[Discipline Is a Property of External Structure More Than of Will]]. No book quotation has been verified for this card. Three modern conditions drive it: digital distraction makes the environment adversarial and not neutral, the volume of input exceeds unaided working memory, and natural structure no longer supplies routine.

### Implications

- Treat attention protection as a precondition and not an optimisation.
- Expect to rebuild proto-systems by hand, not inherit them.

### Falsifier

- If people in modern settings keep disciplined routines without building any deliberate structure, the claim is wrong.
