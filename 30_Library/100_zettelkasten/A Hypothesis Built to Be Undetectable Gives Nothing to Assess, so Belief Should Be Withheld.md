## A Hypothesis Built to Be Undetectable Gives Nothing to Assess, so Belief Should Be Withheld

Where a claim rules out every test in advance there is no missing evidence to count against it, and the claim also says nothing that could differ if it were false. The honest response is to withhold belief, not to assert that it does not exist. The source uses Sagan's invisible dragon in the garage as the example.

### Scope & Conditions

This applies to a claim only as far as it is undetectable by design. The source adds that whether a God concept falls here depends on which concept is meant: a God who intervenes makes predictions, so the expected-evidence test applies, while a deist God who never acts sits much closer to the dragon. That God-concept reading is the chat's own reasoning and is not attributed to a philosopher.

### Evidence

> "Sagan’s example is a dragon in the garage that is invisible, incorporeal, gives off no heat and floats. Every possible test has been ruled out in advance."
> (AI chat transcript pasted by Leon, 2026-10-23, section "Ghosts, fairies, and knowing that something doesn’t exist")

> "But the claim also tells you nothing, because nothing would be different if it were false. The honest response isn’t “I believe it doesn’t exist”. It’s “there’s nothing here to assess”, so you withhold belief."
> (same section)

### Implications

- The useful sorting question is whether the claim predicts anything observable, not whether it sounds plausible.
- Withholding is a third stance alongside belief and disbelief, so "I do not believe it" need not become "I believe it is false".
