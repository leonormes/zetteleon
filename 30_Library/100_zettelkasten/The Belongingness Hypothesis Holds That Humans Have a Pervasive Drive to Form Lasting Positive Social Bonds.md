## The Belongingness Hypothesis Holds That Humans Have a Pervasive Drive to Form Lasting Positive Social Bonds

The belongingness hypothesis (Baumeister & Leary) proposes that people have a pervasive motivation to establish and maintain lasting, positive social bonds, and that threats of exclusion affect cognition, emotion, and behaviour.

### Scope & Conditions

A foundational social-psychology theory, cited here as the basis for why competence and social acceptance become linked.

### Evidence

> "The belongingness hypothesis proposes that people have a pervasive motivation to establish and maintain lasting, positive social bonds; threats of exclusion affect cognition, emotion, and behaviour." (Baumeister & Leary, 1995)

### Implications

- Explains why social rejection registers as more than an informational event—it engages a core motivational system, not just a preference.
