## Perfectionism's Harm Comes From Contingent Self-Worth and Fear of Failure, Not High Standards Alone

Research distinguishes adaptive personal standards from more harmful forms of perfectionism in which standards are socially prescribed, self-worth is made contingent on performance, or errors are treated as evidence of personal deficiency—with fear of failure, rather than high standards per se, being the significant driver of the distressing pattern.

### Scope & Conditions

Socially prescribed and self-oriented perfectionism specifically have been associated with fear of failure and test anxiety in the cited research.

### Evidence

> "Perfectionism is not simply high standards. Research distinguishes adaptive personal standards from more harmful forms in which standards are socially prescribed, self-worth is contingent on performance, or errors are treated as evidence of personal deficiency. Socially prescribed and self-oriented perfectionism have both been associated with fear of failure and test anxiety; fear of failure, rather than high standards alone, is an important part of the distressing pattern."

### Implications

- Treating "I have high standards" as the target for intervention misses the actual mechanism; the useful lever is the fear-of-failure/contingent-self-worth layer underneath.
