## A Fighting Mind That Differs From the Daily Mind Adds Friction and Rigidity

A mind that has to shift into a special fighting state when the stakes rise adds friction and rigidity at the moment flexibility is needed. Musashi therefore argues against amping up for battle and for holding the same baseline in a crisis as in daily life. The SoT's example is keeping the same mental baseline in a high-stakes meeting as while drinking tea.

### Scope & Conditions

- The SoT adds that holding the baseline prevents an "adrenaline dump" that leads to cognitive tunnelling. That mechanism is the SoT's own gloss, not Musashi's, and has not been checked against physiology. The pressure evidence is taken up in [[Keeping the Mind Ordinary Under Pressure Preserves Access to Complex Skills]].
- The SoT quotes "Do not let the enemy see your spirit" as a Musashi line. The wording is not sourced here.
- It claims that a separate crisis state costs something. It does not claim that urgency is never useful, and the Tensions section covers a case where it is.

### Evidence

> "Musashi argues against "amping up" for battle. If your "fighting mind" differs from your "daily mind," you introduce friction and rigidity."
> ([[SoT - Miyamoto Musashi's Philosophy on Mastering Emotions]], §2A Heijo-shin)

> "Maintain the same mental baseline in a high-stakes meeting as you do while drinking tea."
> (same section)

No primary-source quotation has been captured, and the library holds no copy of *The Book of Five Rings*.

### Implications

- If the baseline is the thing to protect, it is built in ordinary moments and not rehearsed only for crises (inferred).
- A ritual that produces a special performance state is a liability if the state cannot be reached on demand, because the person then depends on a switch that may not flip (inferred).
