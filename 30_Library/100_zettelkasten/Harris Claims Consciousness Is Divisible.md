## Harris Claims Consciousness Is Divisible

Summary: Harris argues that because a severed right hemisphere can process language and act independently, it is itself conscious—so consciousness can be split into separate centres of experience.

Details: The inference runs: the isolated right hemisphere understands words and executes deliberate behaviour, therefore "we have every reason to believe [it] is independently conscious," therefore consciousness itself is divisible. The move treats independent information-processing and behaviour as sufficient evidence of independent subjective experience. This is the load-bearing claim beneath Harris's attack on the unified self, and it is also the most contested step—the interpretation that severing the callosum yields _two subjects_ rather than _one subject with divided access_ is not settled science.

### Evidence

> "we have every reason to believe that the isolated right hemisphere is independently conscious."

### Implications

- If accepted, no single "seat" of consciousness survives the surgery.
- The claim's force depends entirely on equating processing with subjectivity (see tension below).

### Tensions

- [[Processing Is Not Proof of Phenomenal Consciousness]]—contradicts: behaviour and language-processing do not by themselves establish a separate felt experience.
