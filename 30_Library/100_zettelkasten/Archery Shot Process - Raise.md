## Archery Shot Process - Raise

Raise is the act of lifting the bow to eye level while seating the bow-side shoulder into its down-and-forward position and internally rotating the bow arm. One step in the overall shot process.

### The Steps

1. Start relaxed—no continuous tension in the front shoulder while nocking or setting up.
2. Weight forward—shift a small amount of weight onto the front foot so the bow shoulder drops and elongates.
3. Pre-tension the string—take up slight tension (approx. 1 inch).
4. Two-handed lift—raise both hands together to eye level. Shoulders open relative to the target line; both shoulders down and neutral.
5. Rotate the bow arm—internally rotate during the raise (elbow turned outward/vertical), before full draw weight loads onto the bow.
6. Reach—at the top of the raise, extend the base of the bow wrist/hand as far as possible directly toward the target. This one move seats the shoulder down-and-forward.
7. Hold the reach—maintain that reaching tension continuously while pulling the string back.

Verification—the acromion divot: when the reach is set correctly, the deltoid flares up, leaving a visible divot at the top of the shoulder (the acromion). If the shoulder rides up or alignment collapses, the divot disappears.

### Why It Works

- Two-handed lift: raising with the bow arm alone over-engages the deltoids and fatigues rapidly.
- Eye-level height: puts the line of draw force where it pushes the front shoulder down and back rather than letting it ride up. Raising excessively high and dropping into full draw on gravity/momentum strains the shoulder extensors.
- Down-and-forward, not just low: an actively forward front shoulder creates the mechanical space for the rear scapula to move freely and pull against the back wall. The front shoulder's position directly governs the rear shoulder's freedom of motion.
- Movement, not muscles: consciously flexing specific muscles (lats, serratus) to hold the shoulder low creates opposing tension. The reach is a pure movement cue that produces the same set as a side effect.
- Relaxed start + weight shift: from a relaxed, slightly dipped posture, the shoulder seats itself down and forward on the way up—no conscious forcing needed.

### Faults & Diagnostics

- High/creeping shoulder: felt immediately as tightness in the traps and neck, plus elevated pressure in the humerus—a reliable self-diagnostic.
- Over-pinned scapula: driving or over-pushing the bow forces the front scapula backward until it presses against the spine, locking the shoulder girdle.
- Why both are destructive: each restricts the rear shoulder blade's movement, preventing effective back tension and stopping the drawing elbow moving cleanly through the shot.

### Simplified Practice Cue

Stand facing forward with relaxed arms, look toward the target, and simply raise the bow arm. Raising naturally, without over-complicating the motion, keeps the shoulder set properly.

### Training Drills

#### Wall / Post Arm Rotation Drill

- Purpose: improves bow-arm internal rotation and flexibility, ensuring string clearance without raising the front shoulder.
- How:
  1. Stand next to a wall or doorframe and extend the bow arm straight against it.
  2. Depress the front shoulder (keep it low, set down with the lats).
  3. Internally rotate the forearm outward (elbow joint turning vertical/outward) against the post while applying forward pressure.
  4. Hold for 20 seconds. Repeat for 3–5 sets.
