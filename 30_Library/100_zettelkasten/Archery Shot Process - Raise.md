## Archery Shot Process - Raise

Summary:

Raise is the act of raising the bow while setting the bow-side shoulder into its optimal down-and-forward position and internally rotating the bow arm.

Details:

- Two-Handed Lift: Maintain slight tension (approx. 1 inch) on the string and lift both hands together to eye level. Raising only with the bow arm over-engages the deltoids and causes rapid fatigue.
- Shoulder Positioning — The Down-and-Forward Principle:
  - The front bow shoulder must sit **down and forward** — not just low, but also actively forward toward the target. This creates the mechanical space for the rear scapula to move freely and pull against the back wall.
  - Keep your shoulders open relative to the target line during the raise.
  - Movements vs Muscles: Instead of over-focusing on flexing specific muscles (like lats or serratus) to keep the front shoulder low—which can create unnecessary opposing tension—focus purely on the physical movement.
  - The "One Move" Fix (Reach): To lock the bow shoulder into its lowest, most stable position naturally, simply take the base of your bow wrist/hand and reach as far as you can directly toward the target.
  - Timing: Set this "reach" motion the moment you lift the bow to the top of your raise position. Continue this reaching tension continuously while you pull the string back.
  - The Diagnostic Check (The Acromion Divot): You can visually verify your shoulder is set correctly by looking for the "acromion divot." When reaching forward properly, the deltoid flares up, leaving a visible divot at the very top of the shoulder (the acromion bone). If the shoulder rides up or alignment collapses, this divot disappears.
  - Raising to eye-level height ensures the line of draw force helps push the front shoulder down and back rather than letting it ride up. Strictly keep both shoulders down in a neutral position—do not let your shoulders hike up.
  - Avoid raising the bow excessively high and relying on gravity/momentum to drop into full draw, as this strains the shoulder extensors.
- Common Shoulder Mistakes & Diagnostics:
  - **High/Creeping Shoulder:** When the shoulder rises or compresses, you will immediately feel tightness in your trap muscles and neck, along with elevated pressure in the humerus. This is a reliable self-diagnostic signal.
  - **Over-Pinned Scapula:** Driving or over-pushing the bow can force the front scapula backward until it presses tightly against your spine. This locks the shoulder girdle.
  - **Why Both Are Destructive:** Both a high shoulder and a pinned scapula restrict the movement of your rear shoulder blade, preventing effective back tension and stopping your drawing elbow from moving cleanly through the shot. The front shoulder's position directly governs the rear shoulder's freedom of motion.
- Relaxing & Setting the Shoulder Before the Draw:
  - Avoid keeping continuous tension in the front shoulder while nocking arrows or starting your setup. Stay relaxed between shots.
  - Shift a small amount of weight forward onto your front foot (a front-foot weight shift), which naturally allows your bow shoulder to drop, elongate, and stay relaxed.
  - From this relaxed, slightly dipped posture, raise the bow straight up to target height. The shoulder will naturally seat itself down and forward before you draw, without needing to consciously force it.
  - For an even simpler practice cue: stand facing forward with relaxed arms, look toward your target, and simply raise your bow arm up. Raising it naturally without over-complicating the motion keeps the shoulder set properly.
- Arm Rotation: Internally rotate the bow arm (elbow turned outward/vertical) _during_ the raise phase before loading the full draw weight onto the bow.

### Training Drills

#### Wall / Post Arm Rotation Drill

- Purpose: Improves bow-arm internal rotation and flexibility, ensuring string clearance without raising the front shoulder.
- How to do it:
  1. Stand next to a wall or doorframe and extend your bow arm straight against it.
  2. Depress your front shoulder (keep it low and set down with your lats).
  3. Internally rotate your forearm outward (elbow joint turning vertical/outward) against the post while applying forward pressure.
  4. Hold for 20 seconds. Repeat for 3–5 sets.
