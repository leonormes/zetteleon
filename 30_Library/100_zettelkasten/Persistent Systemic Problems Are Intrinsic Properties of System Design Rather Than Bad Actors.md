## Persistent Systemic Problems Are Intrinsic Properties of System Design Rather Than Bad Actors

Persistent systemic problems such as poverty, addiction, or chronic technical debt arise from the feedback loops, incentives, and structural interconnections of a system rather than from bad actors or bad luck. Because structure determines behavior, replacing the people inside an unchanged system reproduces the same dysfunctional outcomes. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to recurring patterns across human, organizational, and technical systems where outcomes persist across personnel changes or isolated interventions. Does not excuse individual agency or acute misconduct, but explains why individual blame fails to alter macro-level patterns.

### Evidence

Extracted from [[SoT - Systems Thinking]] (Current Understanding and MVU sections); no verbatim book quote is attached yet.

### Implications

- Diagnostic attention should focus on feedback loops, delays, and incentive structures rather than exclusively policing individual actors.
- Interventions that swap personnel without altering system structure will reliably regenerate the same problem.
