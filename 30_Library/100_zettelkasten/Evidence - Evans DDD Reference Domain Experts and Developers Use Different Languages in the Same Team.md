## Evidence - Evans DDD Reference Domain Experts and Developers Use Different Languages in the Same Team

> Domain experts use their jargon while technical team members have their own language tuned for discussing the domain in terms of design. The terminology of day-to-day discussions is disconnected from the terminology embedded in the code (ultimately the most important product of a software project).

Source: Eric Evans, Domain-Driven Design Reference: Definitions and Pattern Summaries, Ubiquitous Language section (Calibre id 396, PDF; page not captured by the index)

### What it Supports

Within one team, different roles run on different vocabularies, and the terminology of everyday talk drifts away from the terminology in the code. That is the divergence half of [[The Illusion of Shared Understanding in Teams]]: members hold different working languages, and so different models, under a common project.

### What it Does not Show

It does not show that members _believe_ they are aligned, nor that the mismatch only surfaces in a crisis. Those parts of the claim remain unsupported by this note.
