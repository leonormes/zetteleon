## Time Delays Between Action and Feedback Drive System Oscillation and Policy Failure

Time delays between an intervention and its observable feedback are a primary driver of system oscillation and policy failure, because decision-makers keep pushing after enough corrective force is already in the pipeline. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Occurs whenever a balancing feedback loop has perception, reporting, or response lags that are long relative to the rate at which the system changes. Applies to supply chains, monetary policy, ecosystems, and infrastructure autoscaling.

### Evidence

Extracted from [[SoT - Systems Thinking]] (Meadows and Open University synthesis). No verbatim book quote has been verified for this seed note yet.

### Implications

- Shortening feedback delays is often higher leverage than tuning the size of the intervention itself.
- Aggressive corrections applied to delayed signals amplify swings rather than damping them.
