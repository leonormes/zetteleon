## Requiring Knowledge to Rest on No False Belief Fails Because Trivial False Beliefs Do Not Destroy Knowledge

The no-falsehood repair adds a fourth condition to justified true belief: the belief must not be reached through any false premise. It fails as stated because ordinary knowledge often rests partly on trivial false beliefs, so the condition has to separate important false beliefs from unimportant ones, and no one has done that cleanly. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to the simple form of the repair. It does explain the original cases: Joan reasons from the false premise that Jack got the job. Three problems arise:

- Mildred knows that it sometimes snows in May where she lives. Her evidence includes a false detail, that she was returning from Aruba and not Antigua, and the detail does not matter.
- If only important false beliefs count, one must say how much weakening of the case makes a false belief important. A false belief can be a small part of the support or half of it, and the line is vague.
- Perceptual beliefs such as Max's rest on no conscious reasoning, so the repair has to treat them as resting on unconscious premises that the believer never considered ([[Gettier-Type Cases Arise Without Inference or False Belief, So Repairing the Inference Step Cannot Solve Them]]).

### Evidence

One book makes the objection directly:

> "appears to be much too strong a condition"
> (Martin 2014, "The no-falsehood reply", PDF p. 39) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/298/EPUB?open_at=toc:The%20no-falsehood%20reply)

The phrase is Martin's comment on requiring the truth of every belief that may have figured unconsciously in a person's belief. His Mildred and Albert cases show that a single false detail need not undermine knowledge.

### Implications

- A satisfactory repair has to weigh the false belief by its role in the justification, which brings the problem back to what justification is.
- The related repair that talks of true facts the believer does not know avoids false beliefs and meets a different problem ([[A No-Defeaters Condition Handles Many Gettier Cases but Cannot Say Which True Facts Count as Defeaters]]).

### Falsifier

- A precise, non-circular rule that separates important false beliefs from unimportant ones and classifies the standard cases correctly.
