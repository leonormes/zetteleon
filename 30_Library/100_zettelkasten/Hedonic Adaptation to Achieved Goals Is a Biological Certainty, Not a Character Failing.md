## Hedonic Adaptation to Achieved Goals Is a Biological Certainty, Not a Character Failing

Panel-study evidence for the hedonic treadmill shows individuals return to near-baseline subjective wellbeing after positive life events, because the once-novel circumstance becomes the new expected baseline. Durable wellbeing gains come from ongoing activities and practices, not from reaching outcome states.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to circumstantial or outcome-state changes, such as finishing a project or reaching a goal. Does not apply equally to sustained engagement in varied activity, which can produce lasting subjective-wellbeing change.

### Evidence

> "The hedonic treadmill effect… demonstrates that individuals return to near-baseline subjective well-being following positive life events… lasting changes in SWB are possible, but specifically through activities and practices rather than outcome-state changes."

### Implications

- Feeling flat after finishing a big project is an expected biological response, not evidence the project was the wrong one.
- Wellbeing and motivation should be engineered around recurring activities and practices, not a single terminal achievement.
