## If a Next Action Takes Less Than Two Minutes Do It Immediately Rather Than Track It

Once a next action has been defined and found to take less than two minutes to complete, it should be performed immediately rather than deferred to an organisational list. The management overhead of tracking a task—adding it to a list, reviewing it, re-reading it, deciding to act—typically exceeds the cost of simply completing it. The two-minute threshold is the efficiency cut-off below which the organisational apparatus becomes more expensive than the work itself.

### Scope & Conditions

Applied immediately after a next action has been defined during the Clarify stage and only if the item is actionable. The two-minute figure is a calibrated heuristic, not a rigid rule—the principle is that tracking overhead must not exceed task duration. The heuristic also prevents the organisational system from becoming cluttered with trivial micro-tasks.

### Evidence

> "Do it: If the action takes less than two minutes, do it immediately. This efficiency cutoff prevents you from spending more time tracking an item than it takes to simply complete it."

### Implications

- Reduces the overhead of managing micro-tasks by eliminating the tracking layer entirely for short actions.
- Prevents the organisational system from accumulating trivial entries that dilute its signal and add review overhead.
