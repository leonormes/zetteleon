## Knowledge Transmission Is Reconstruction, Not Copying

Transmitting knowledge through language means the receiver reconstructs meaning against a background of shared experience, rather than copying information from one mind to another.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies wherever knowledge passes through linguistic symbols; the source contrasts it with a view in which perfect transmission is possible.

### Evidence

> "knowledge transmission is not merely copying information from one mind to another but reconstructing meaning through active engagement with linguistic symbols against a background of shared experience and assumptions."

Source: [[beyond words]] (line 350).

### Implications

- The receiver reconstructs meaning from their own comparable experiences (source line 374).
- The source describes perfect transmission of knowledge via language as impossible (source line 350).
