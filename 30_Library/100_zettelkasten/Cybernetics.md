## Cybernetics

Cybernetics is the study of how mechanical, electrical, and biological systems regulate themselves and communicate information through feedback loops. Founded in Norbert Wiener's 1940s work on control and communication, it treats goal-directed behaviour as a continuous loop that senses output and adjusts action to close the gap with a target state. ^def

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to any self-regulating system—from servomechanisms and software controllers to cells and human habits—that feeds information about its own output back into its next action. It does not cover open-loop processes that fire without sensing outcomes, and contrasts with [[Emergence]] (how macro properties arise without a central controller) and [[Autopoiesis The Self-Referential System]] (self-producing systems in second-order cybernetics whose primary drive is their own continuation).

### Evidence

Grounded in [[Evidence - Gleick on Wiener Frames Cybernetics as Negative Feedback Control Where Information Is the Key]], which documents Wiener's framing of negative feedback and information as the steering mechanism in goal-directed machines. The biological half—cells using negative feedback to hold levels steady and positive feedback to make irreversible switches—is documented in Paul Nurse's *What Is Life?* (listed under Further Reading).

### Implications

- Stability depends on intact, timely feedback: delaying or cutting a negative feedback loop produces overshoot and collapse.
- Control is an information problem rather than a purely mechanical one, because a regulator acts on its model of the environment rather than the environment directly.
- Personal and operational workflows in ProdOS require closed sensing-and-adjustment loops rather than one-way execution lists.
