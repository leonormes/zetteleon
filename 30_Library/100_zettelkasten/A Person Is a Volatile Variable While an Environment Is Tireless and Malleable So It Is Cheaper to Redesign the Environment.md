## A Person Is a Volatile Variable While an Environment Is Tireless and Malleable So It Is Cheaper to Redesign the Environment

A person is a fatiguing, volatile variable while an environment is tireless and malleable, so it is cheaper to solve for the environment than to keep re-solving for the person. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Rests on Lewin's equation B = f(P, E). It applies where the environment can actually be changed, and it does not claim the person contributes nothing.

### Evidence

> "Behavior is a function of the Person in their Environment, or B = f (P,E)."
> (Clear 2018, "6: Motivation is Overrated; Environment Often Matters More", PDF p. 74) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/690/EPUB?open_at=toc:6:%20Motivation%20is%20Overrated%3B%20Environment%20Often%20Matters%20More)

> "In this way, the most common form of change is not internal, but external: we are changed by the world around us."
> (Clear 2018, "6: Motivation is Overrated; Environment Often Matters More", PDF p. 74) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/690/EPUB?open_at=toc:6:%20Motivation%20is%20Overrated%3B%20Environment%20Often%20Matters%20More)

### Implications

- Redesign cues, friction and defaults before trying harder.
- Treat the willpower each instance costs as a design variable and not a personal endowment.

### Falsifier

- If changing the environment while holding the person constant does not change behaviour more cheaply than changing the person, the asymmetry fails.
