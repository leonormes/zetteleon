## Core Actions of a GTD Weekly Review

What: The full set of actions David Allen prescribes for the Weekly Review, organised as a three-part drill: get clear, get current, get creative. The aim is to get your head empty again and to orient yourself for the next couple of weeks. It is done when you can honestly say, "I absolutely know right now everything I'm not doing but could be doing if I decided to."

How:

### 1. Get Clear

Gather the loose ends the week produced and process them until nothing is unprocessed.

1. Collect loose papers and materials: pull stray papers, business cards and receipts from desk, clothing and accessories into the in-tray.
2. Get "in" to empty: review meeting notes and scribbles, decide and list actions, projects, waiting-fors, calendar events and someday/maybes, file reference material, and take email, texts and voicemail inboxes to zero. Purge what is not needed.
3. Empty your head: write down any new project, action, waiting-for or someday/maybe you have not yet captured and clarified.

### 2. Get Current

Bring the orienting lists up to date by removing outdated reminders and completing the active ones.

1. Review Next Actions lists: mark off completed actions and record any further steps they suggest.
2. Review previous calendar data: go through the last two to three weeks of entries for remaining or emergent actions, and capture every "Oh! That reminds me" with its action.
3. Review upcoming calendar: look at shortand long-term entries and capture the projects and preparation they imply.
4. Review the Waiting For list: decide whether follow-up is needed, record next actions, and check off what has arrived.
5. Review Projects (and larger outcome) lists: evaluate each project and make sure at least one current kick-start action exists for it. This is where [[Weekly Review Verifies Project Actionability and Context]] applies.
6. Review any relevant checklists: is there anything else you need or want to do, given your engagements, interests and responsibilities?

### 3. Get Creative

Much of this happens by itself once you are clear and current; two triggers finish it off.

1. Review the Someday/Maybe list: promote anything that has become interesting or valuable to a project, delete what has stayed longer than it should, and add emerging possibilities.
2. Be creative and courageous: capture any new, risky or harebrained ideas in the system.

### Time and place

Allen suggests two hours early in the afternoon of the last working day of the week, when the week is still fresh, follow-up actions can still be acted on, and you can go into the weekend with nothing pulling at you. He is flexible about the slot as long as it is a regular one that gets you away from the daily grind.

Failure modes:

- Stopping at cleaning up and closure and skipping the creative part; Allen says the method is not simply about tidying.
- Doing it irregularly: Allen notes few people do it as consistently as they should, which is what keeps a clear mind and relaxed control out of reach.
- Not having a populated, reasonably current system to review, which makes the scope daunting; Allen names this himself.

## Why This Matters for ADHD

The full list is long, and for an ADHD brain that is a risk in itself: a ritual too big to start is a ritual that does not happen. The value of this note is as the reference for what the review is for, not as a script to run in full each week.
