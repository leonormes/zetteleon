## Household Scaffolds Should Match Each Users Access and Trust and Be Bridged Through One Master Record

In a household where people differ in what they will reach and trust, each person should use the scaffold that works for them, with one master record bridged to the shared surface, and not have the most experienced user impose theirs. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to shared routines where one person prefers digital reminders and others prefer a physical board in a shared room. Rests on one observer with a stake and an untested hypothesis about why an earlier whiteboard stalled.

### Evidence

> "Each person uses the surface they will actually reach and trust (a kitchen whiteboard for the family, digital tools for Dad), bridged through one master list at the Sunday review."
> ([[SoT - Family Household Chores]], §1.1)

This is my own reasoning applying [[An external structure counts as part of a cognitive process only if it is constantly available, easily accessible, and automatically trusted]].

### Implications

- Ask why the previous board stalled before judging the medium.
- Trial the bridged board for two weeks with an agreed Sunday review date.
- Keep one digital master list, copy the active week onto the board on Sunday, and photograph updates back.

### Falsifier

- If the board stays current for two weeks and bridging still loses state or costs more effort than it saves, one shared surface is cheaper.
