## Believing a Proposition Is a Necessary Condition for Knowing It

A person cannot know that a proposition is true unless they believe it, so belief is a necessary condition for knowledge. The condition is weak: it asks only that the person holds the proposition, not that they hold it strongly. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to propositional knowledge. The claim is contested at its edges, and the dispute is about what counts as believing:

- Some philosophers hold that confidence is also necessary. Others reply that a nervous person with a well-founded true belief still knows, even while doubting their own memory.
- Ordinary speech seems to deny the condition, as when someone says "I don't believe it, I know it". That is an idiom meaning the person is sure, not a literal denial of belief.
- The belief meant here is not the working model of [[Belief Between Fact and Faith]].

### Evidence

One book states the condition as the standard view and then tests it with a case of weak belief:

> "Belief is a necessary condition for knowledge"
> (Martin 2014, ch. 1, PDF p. 19) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/298/EPUB?open_at=toc:1%20Defining%20%E2%80%98knowledge%E2%80%99)

The book's test case is Howard, who has memorised the capitals of Europe, doubts himself in class and says Bratislava hesitantly, though he is right and his belief is well founded. The book reports that some philosophers say he does not know and others say he does, and the dispute is over strength of belief, not over whether belief is needed.

### Implications

- Knowledge is a kind of belief, so a theory of knowledge has to say what makes a belief better than other beliefs.
- The knowledge-first view disputes the direction of explanation, not the entailment: it treats knowing as basic and believing as derived ([[Knowledge-First Epistemology Treats Knowing as Basic and Believing as Derived]]).

### Falsifier

- A clear case of someone who knows a proposition and does not believe it in any sense, not merely someone who lacks confidence or who uses "know" to mean "am sure".
