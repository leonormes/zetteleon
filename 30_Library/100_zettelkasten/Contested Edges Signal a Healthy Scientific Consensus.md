## Contested Edges Signal a Healthy Scientific Consensus

A healthy consensus is not a monolith of total agreement. The core principles are settled, but the edges are a battleground—physicists universally agree on general relativity, but argue viciously over dark matter and quantum gravity. A field showing absolute uniformity, with no friction and no competing hypotheses, is more likely exhibiting institutional groupthink than genuine science, which always leaves unresolved anomalies researchers are aggressively trying to exploit.

### Scope & Conditions

One of five diagnostics (with Atom 006 and Atoms 008–010) for telling a legitimate scientific consensus apart from institutional groupthink.

### Evidence

> "If you look at a field and see absolute uniformity with no friction, no competing hypotheses, and no intense arguments over edge cases, you are likely looking at institutional groupthink."

### Implications

- Visible internal argument is evidence for a field's health, not against it.
