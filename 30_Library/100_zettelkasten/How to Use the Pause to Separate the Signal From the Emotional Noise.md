## How to Use the Pause to Separate the Signal From the Emotional Noise

The pause turns a reaction into a response by splitting the event, which is the signal, from the emotion it set off, which is the noise. The SoT gives the instruction in one line as a ProdOS tactic. The five steps in the frontmatter are this note's own elaboration and have not been tested.

### Scope & Conditions

- Use it when a feeling is strong enough to push toward an action that would be regretted, such as a reply to a message that stung or a snap decision after a setback.
- Do not use it when physical danger needs an immediate reflex, because a five-step routine is too slow for that.
- For an acute spike the gap may be too short to use. In that case a body-first step comes first, such as the technique in [[Emotional Regulation Techniques for ADHD Productivity]].

### Evidence

> "ProdOS Tactic: Use the "Pause" to distinguish between the Signal (The Event) and the Noise (The Emotion). See [[Responding vs. Reacting]]."
> ([[SoT - Miyamoto Musashi's Philosophy on Mastering Emotions]], §4 Practical Application)

> "We have found that labeling the affective aspect of a disturbing image reduces the distress a person feels while looking at the image."
> (Matthew D. Lieberman, *Social*, ch. 12 "Educating the Social Brain")

The steps are the note's own elaboration of the SoT line. The Lieberman finding is about labelling an emotion shown in an image, so it supports step 3 only by analogy. Naming the feeling also matches the vault's existing guidance to acknowledge emotions before managing them ([[Ownership of Emotions]]).

### Implications

- The pause only works if a gap exists to put it in. [[Responding vs. Reacting]] states that premise, and the claim in [[Humans Retain Freedom to Choose Their Response to Any Situation Regardless of Circumstances]] depends on it.
- Separating signal from noise is the practical form of the check in [[Emotions Are Not Reliable Indicators of Reality]], where the feeling is not a reading of the facts.
