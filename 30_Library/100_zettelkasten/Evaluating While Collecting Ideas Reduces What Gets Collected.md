## Evaluating While Collecting Ideas Reduces What Gets Collected

Evaluating ideas while still collecting them reduces how many ideas get collected. Keeping capture unjudged preserves coverage, while evaluation belongs in a separate subsequent pass. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- **Holds:** Where coverage is the goal and ideas are cheap to hold (inbox capture, brainstorming, first drafts).
- **Weakens or fails:** Where iteration cycles are short and cheap, or evaluation is fast and automatic—for example, tight-loop practices such as test-driven development (generating and evaluating interleaved by design) or recursive writing models (such as Flower and Hayes, unverified against source).
- **Falsification:** Evidence that interleaving evaluation with collection does not reduce collected output would weaken this claim.

### Evidence

- **Practitioner heuristic (mechanism untested):** The prior uncited quote (*"Attempting to merge these two distinct psychological states..."*) was removed because it was circular and lacked a verified source.
- **Candidate sources to verify:** Allen (*Getting Things Done*—including whether his remedy for new thoughts during clarify is a queue boundary rather than mental modes), Osborn (deferring judgement), Guilford (divergent vs convergent thinking), and Elbow (freewriting). Empirical findings on brainstorming deferral rules are mixed and not yet checked against primary sources. *(No Calibre book quotation attached yet.)*

### Implications

- **Queue boundary:** Keep capture unjudged; evaluate in a separate pass, and drop new thoughts that arise during evaluation back into the inbox.
