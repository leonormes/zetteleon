## Minimum Viable Understanding (MVU)

The ADHD brain operates on an Interest-Based Nervous System (IBNS), a term coined by Dr. William Dodson. Unlike the neurotypical "Importance-Based" system—which can initiate tasks based on abstract factors like priority, responsibility, or long-term rewards—the ADHD brain requires a specific chemical "ignition" to activate. Motivation in ADHD is not a choice of will, but a chemical reaction to specific environmental triggers.

---

## 1. The Importance Gap

The fundamental struggle of ADHD is the disconnect between knowing something is important and being able to do it.

- Neurotypical (Importance-Based): Motivation is driven by _Importance_, _Secondary Rewards_ (money/status), and _Consequences_. Completion provides a "shot of dopamine" (Satisfaction).
- ADHD (Interest-Based): Motivation is driven by the immediate qualities of the task itself. Completion often provides only "relief from anxiety" rather than a reward signal (see [[The 'Dopamine Shot' vs 'Relief from Anxiety' in Task Completion]]).

## 2. The INCUP Framework (The 5 Ignition Keys)

Dr. Dodson identifies five specific triggers that "boot up" the ADHD brain:

1. Interest: Fascination and genuine curiosity. The task is inherently engaging.
2. Novelty: New tools, new environments, or new ways of solving a problem. The ADHD brain releases dopamine on "Prediction Error" (Surprise).
3. Challenge: Competition, gamification, or the "Impossible Task." Proving someone wrong or beating a record.
4. Urgency: Imminent deadlines or crisis-mode. Urgency creates enough norepinephrine/dopamine to overcome baseline deficits (linked to "ADHD Paralysis").
5. Passion: Tasks that align deeply with personal values or "play."

## 3. The Biological Reality

This system is a result of Dopamine Hyposensitivity in the brain's reward centers (striatum). Because the brain doesn't "feel" the importance of a task at a chemical level, it remains in a low-power state until one of the INCUP keys is turned. This explains why ADHDers often become "crisis managers"—the brain only functions at full capacity when the environment provides the necessary stimulation.

## 4. Strategies for Management

- Externalise Urgency: Use tools like [[Visual Timers for ADHD Time Blindness]] or body doubling.
- Inject Novelty: Change your work environment (see [[Environmental and Physical Hacks for ADHD Focus]]).
- Gamify: Turn boring tasks into challenges or "Speed Trials."
