## Archery Shot Process - Finger Tab Selection & Maintenance

Summary:

Selecting, sizing, and maintaining a recurve finger tab—leather thickness for finger protection, base plate/shelf/spacer ergonomics, market tiers, and customization/maintenance routines that support a clean, passive release. Sourced from a recurve finger tab selection & optimisation guide.

Details:

### Finger Protection Sizing

- Standard Finger Safety Rule: minimum 1mm of leather thickness for every 10 lbs of draw weight (e.g. 3.6mm minimum for a 36 lb setup).
- Insufficient thickness risks a "nerve-masking" flinch: the nervous system instinctively softens back-tension engagement to avoid pain, degrading expansion (see [[Archery Shot Process - Expansion]]).
- Cordovan leather is recommended for snappier limb systems (e.g. Uukha VX1000) due to its low-friction, durable properties.

### Base Plate & Anatomy Fit

- The base plate must not exceed the width of the knuckles when hooked; plate orientation should align to roughly a 45-degree knuckle angle so it doesn't dig into the hand.
- An oversized plate interferes with anchor depth, preventing the hand sitting flush against the jaw—this degrades the string picture and facial reference consistency (see [[Archery Shot Process - Anchor]]).
- Shelves provide a flat surface for a repeatable under-jaw anchor.
- Finger spacers prevent "nock-pinching" (fingers squeezing the nock and lifting the arrow off the rest).
- Avoid unnecessary/heavy hardware—the fingers should act as passive hooks; hardware that encourages a clenched or active grip should be adjusted or removed (reinforces the passive-hook principle in [[Archery Shot Process - Release]]).

### Leather Trimming

- Trim leather to just past the fingertips. Overly long leather pinches between fingers and riser during arrow paradox, or catches the string and interferes with the string picture.

### Customization

- Paracord Strap Swap: replace stock finger straps with high-strength paracord and a sliding knot to lock the tab against rotation/slip through the draw cycle.
- Face leather thickness/hardness tunes perceived arrow spine via impulse duration: thicker/harder leather shortens contact time (stiffer reaction); thinner/softer leather lengthens it (softer reaction).

### Maintenance Checklist

- Clean the face leather regularly to remove salt/oil buildup.
- Replace grooved/worn leather immediately—a groove catches the string and causes plucking/horizontal deflection.
- Mixed hardware standards (e.g. metric limbs with imperial tab hardware) may need both Allen key sets on hand.

### Strategic Note—36 Lb Uukha Monolith/VX1000 Setup

- Requires 3.6–4mm minimum protection; Cordovan leather is non-negotiable for durability under the snappier VX1000 release.
- Elite tabs (Shibuya, Axcel Contour) suit this setup—palmography adjustment keeps the hand passive so the posterior deltoid can drive expansion without chassis interference (see [[Archery Shot Process - Expansion]]).
