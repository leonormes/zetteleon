## Trusting a Scientific Authority Feels Psychologically Identical to Trusting a False One

This is the "epistemology of testimony" problem: since no one can personally verify more than a fraction of human knowledge, belief is delegated to authorities. The subjective feeling of trusting a physicist because their explanation "makes sense" is functionally identical, from the inside, to a flat-earther trusting a video because it "makes sense" to them.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to any belief held on testimony rather than personal verification—which, for any one person, is nearly all specialist knowledge.

### Evidence

> "You are absolutely right that, on a purely psychological level, your decision to trust a physicist feels functionally identical to a flat-earther's decision to trust a YouTube video. Both of you are delegating your belief to an authority because it 'makes sense' to you."

### Implications

- The feeling of certainty cannot itself be used to distinguish justified from unjustified belief.
- Justification has to be located in properties of the trusted system (see [[Falsifiability Distinguishes Science from Dogma]]), not in how confident the feeling is.
