> [!claim] Statement
> For people with ADHD, externalising a thought (capture) makes almost no demand on executive function, whereas turning captured material into organised, actionable output (processing) makes a heavy one—so the two diverge sharply in difficulty.

## Supporting Evidence

- Capture (typing, dictating, jotting) is a single low-control action; processing needs sustained attention, sequencing and decision-making—the exact functions ADHD impairs. See [[Executive Function]].
- The report cites an NIH/PMC review arguing ADHD forces extra effort to link abstract information to concrete tasks, producing delay and inconsistent follow-up.

## Mechanism

- Reviewing and categorising leans on [[Cognitive Load|working memory]]; when capacity is low, the review step tips into overwhelm and is deferred or abandoned.

## Counter-positions

- The asymmetry may be motivational rather than capacity-based: processing is simply less novel/rewarding, so it loses the attention contest rather than exceeding ability.
- Neurotypical users also find processing harder than capture; the ADHD case may be a difference of degree, not kind.

## Crux

- Whether the gap is primarily a capacity limit (working memory) or a motivation/reward one. The two imply different fixes.

## Falsifier

- If, under matched interest/reward conditions, ADHD processing performance equalled neurotypical performance, the capacity framing would be undermined.
