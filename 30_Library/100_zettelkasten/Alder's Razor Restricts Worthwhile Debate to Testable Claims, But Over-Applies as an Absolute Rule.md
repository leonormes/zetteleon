## Alder's Razor Restricts Worthwhile Debate to Testable Claims, But Over-Applies as an Absolute Rule

Alder's razor (Newton's Flaming Laser Sword) holds that claims which cannot be settled through observation or experiment are not worth debating; taken as an absolute rule this is too restrictive, because historical, ethical, political, conceptual, and interpretive questions can be meaningful without being experimentally decidable.

### Scope & Conditions

Useful as a practical filter for deciding whether a specific factual disagreement is resolvable through evidence; overstated if used as a general rule for what is worth discussing at all.

### Evidence

> "propositions should have observable consequences or be connected to precise logic or mathematics… this approach 'cuts out the crap' but can also eliminate much of what people ordinarily consider meaningful discussion… Historical, ethical, political, conceptual, and interpretive questions may be meaningful without being experimentally decidable."

### Implications

- Before invoking Alder's razor to end a discussion, check whether the question is actually a factual dispute (where testability matters) or a values/interpretive question (where it doesn't apply).
