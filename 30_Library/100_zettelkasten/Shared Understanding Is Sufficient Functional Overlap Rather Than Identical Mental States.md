## Shared Understanding Is Sufficient Functional Overlap Rather Than Identical Mental States

Shared understanding is not the replication of identical mental states across minds, but sufficient functional overlap between two experiential worlds to enable cooperation. Effective communication is therefore the collaborative construction of a temporary shared language grounded in that intersection.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to practical coordination, teaching, and collaborative work where participants must act together without sharing identical backgrounds. Aesthetic or deeply personal experiences may still leave large unshared regions even when functional coordination succeeds.

### Evidence

This claim synthesises the pragmatic view of communication in the vault rather than quoting a single external source, grounded in [[Meaning emerges from language games]] and [[Shared biological architecture enables communication]].

### Implications

- Chasing "perfect" mutual comprehension before acting creates unnecessary paralysis; the target is enough overlap for the task at hand.
- Communicators can bridge wide gaps by deliberately expanding the intersection of their experiential worlds rather than trying to transmit their entire mental model.
