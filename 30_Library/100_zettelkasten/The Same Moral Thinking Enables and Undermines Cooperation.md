## The Same Moral Thinking Enables and Undermines Cooperation

The same moral thinking that enables cooperation within groups undermines cooperation between groups. Morality is therefore both part of the solution and part of the problem on the new pastures.

### Scope & Conditions

The core of the modern tragedy. Greene also lists tribalism, which he calls group-level selfishness, among the obstacles to cooperation between groups.

### Evidence

> "In the modern tragedy, the very same moral thinking that enables cooperation within groups undermines cooperation between groups."
> (Joshua Greene, Moral Tribes, Chapter 1: The Tragedy of the Commons)

### Implications

- Morality is "undoubtedly part of the solution, but it's also part of the problem" on the new pastures.
- Greene lists further obstacles to cooperation between groups: disagreement over the proper terms of cooperation, commitment to local proper nouns such as leaders, gods and holy books, a biased sense of fairness, and a biased perception of the facts.
