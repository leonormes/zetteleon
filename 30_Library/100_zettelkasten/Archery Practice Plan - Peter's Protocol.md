## Archery Practice Plan - Peter's Protocol

Summary:

A structured 3-phase archery practice and physical conditioning plan based on Peter's coaching advice. Designed to systematically refine form, build subconscious muscle memory via resistance bands, condition holding stamina with full equipment, and provide direct feedback during blank-bale sessions.

---

## 🏹 Structured Archery Practice Plan

### Phase 1: Home Practice & Subconscious Training

_Goal: Build muscle memory and refine your shot process without the pressure of a full line, target panic, or score-chasing._

#### Step 1: Your Shot Recipe (The 10-Step Process)

Break down your shot process into clear, step-by-step instructions (like a cooking recipe). When executing practice repetitions or resistance band drills, follow these core technical cues for each stage of the shot cycle:

1. [[Archery Shot Process - Nocking|Nocking]]
   - Action: Secure the arrow consistently onto the string nocking point and rest without forcing. Ensure proper vane cock-feather orientation.
2. [[Archery Shot Process - Stance|Stance]]
   - Action: Stand with a solid, shoulder-width base (square or slightly open). Maintain a 50/50 weight balance between left and right feet, settling center of gravity slightly forward over the balls of the feet.
3. [[Archery Shot Process - Posture|Posture]]
   - Action: Engage the core ("chest down / flat back") by exhaling completely to lower and lock the ribcage. Maintain absolute trunk stability along the shooting line with zero sway or leaning, ensuring clear kinesthetic awareness of upper-back muscle movement.
4. [[Archery Shot Process - Setup|Setup]]
   - Action: Hook first, then set grip. Establish a deep finger hook (string resting in the sweet spot of the first finger joint) while keeping wrist and back of hand completely flat and relaxed. Seat the bow-hand pressure point firmly onto the thumb pad with knuckles retracted at a 45-degree angle; keep fingers entirely passive ("jelly fingers") inside a finger sling.
5. [[Archery Shot Process - Raise|Raise]]
   - Action: Lift the bow straight up toward the target without arching the lower back. Keep the bow-side shoulder actively pushed down and low into its socket.
6. [[Archery Shot Process - Pre-draw|Pre-draw]]
   - Action: Perform a final core and structural check: verify tall spine alignment, low bow shoulder structure, and fixed head posture looking directly at target center before applying string load.
7. [[Archery Shot Process - Loading|Loading]]
   - Action: Draw the string straight back toward your chin using your back muscles (scapular retractors), leading with the draw elbow moving continuously "up and around" behind your back ("lawnmower pull"). Keep head motionless (bring string to face, never head to string) and actively maintain finger flexor tension to prevent draw-length creep.
8. [[Archery Shot Process - Anchor|Anchor]]
   - Action: Settle smoothly into solid bone-to-bone facial references in a single fluid motion: index knuckle seating under the jawline pocket, string lightly contacting nose tip and chin. Ensure exact full-draw clicker blade starting position consistency while sustaining continuous backward drawing pressure without hovering or "feathering."
9. [[Archery Shot Process - Expansion|Expansion]]
   - Action: Treat full draw ("holding") as a continuous transitionary phase ("California roll"), building dynamic two-way push-and-pull movement toward clicker activation. Maintain smooth, unbroken expansion through the final 1–4 millimeters without stalling, freezing, or collapsing forward.
10. [[Archery Shot Process - Release|Release]] & [[Archery Shot Process - Follow-through|Follow-through]]
    - Release Action: Treat release as an involuntary reaction to the clicker by passively relaxing finger flexor tension (dropping to ~40% hold) to let the string cut cleanly past relaxed fingertips.
    - Follow-through Action: Continue squeezing the draw elbow around the spine so the draw hand travels straight back along the neck/jawline ("scratch-neck / knock a parrot off shoulder"). Freeze like a statue with the bow arm raised, allowing the bow to drive forward and fall naturally onto the sling without torquing or grabbing the handle.

#### Step 2: Resistance Band Practice

Use a resistance band to execute your written shot recipe mechanically and deliberately.

- Volume: Shoot 3 dozen (36) "shots", divided into 6 ends (6 repetitions per end).
- Rest: Take a normal, structured break after each set of 6 reps to recover mental focus and prevent physical sloppiness.
- Optional Extension: If you aren't fatigued and form remains flawless, extend the session to 5 or 6 dozen repetitions to mirror a full club shooting session.
- Frequency: Perform 1 to 2 times a week at home.

---

### Phase 2: Strength & Hold Conditioning

_Goal: Build archery-specific muscular endurance and full-draw skeletal stability using your actual equipment setup._

- Equipment: Your full bow setup (including rods, dampers, and stabilizers).
- ⚠️ Safety Warning: DO NOT dry fire the bow! Never release the bowstring without an arrow safely loaded on an active shooting range. During at-home drawing routines, wrap your thumb around the string or use a dedicated release trainer/formaster device to prevent accidental release.
- Exercise Routine (Static Holds):
  - Draw & Hold: Draw smoothly to your regular anchor point using proper scapular loading and T-line posture.
  - Hold Duration: Start by holding full draw for 10 seconds per repetition.
  - Rest: Rest for exactly 15 seconds between repetitions.
  - Set Structure: Complete 6 reps per set, resting for 1+ minute between sets.
  - Progression: Gradually increase your static hold duration in 5-second increments over weeks until you can comfortably complete 6 sets of 6 reps without losing shoulder alignment or postural posture. _(Note: If form breaks down during a hold due to fatigue, let down immediately rather than muscling through bad posture)._

---

### Phase 3: Range & Blank Bale Training

_Goal: Focus purely on biomechanical form on the shooting range without visual aiming anxiety or score-chasing._

- Target Date: Wednesday the 5th.
- Activity: Dedicated blank-bale session with Peter at the range.
- Focus: Peter will observe your mechanical shooting process and provide tailored coaching feedback based on your live technique execution. Concentrate 100% of internal awareness on executing your 10-step Shot Recipe correctly rather than where arrows impact on the target.
