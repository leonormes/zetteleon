## Evil Structurally Requires a Radical Power Asymmetry Directed at the Defenceless

Evil characteristically manifests through a radical imbalance of power in which harmful agency is directed at those who are defenceless, helpless, or dependent. The vulnerability of the victim is not incidental to evil acts—it is structurally central. Perpetrators of evil systematically target those least able to resist, retaliate, or escape.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies across physical, developmental, situational, and institutional imbalances. The asymmetry is a diagnostic feature, not a sufficient condition—not all power imbalances constitute evil, but the absence of power asymmetry is a signal that what is occurring may be something other than evil (e.g. a fair fight or a mutual wrong).

### Evidence

> "The descriptive observation is that evil frequently occurs where there is a radical imbalance of power, and the perpetrator directs harmful agency toward those who are defenseless, helpless, or dependent."

### Implications

- Power asymmetry is a central tool for identifying evil phenomena analytically—look for it as a diagnostic marker before interrogating motivation.
- Structural vulnerability in victims is part of what makes evil acts particularly destructive and resistant to ordinary moral recourse.
