## Withdrawing Household Cooperation as Leverage Turns System Disagreements Into Conditional Penalties

Withdrawing cooperation on shared household tasks as leverage turns a disagreement about systems into a personal penalty and teaches children that cooperation is conditional rather than interdependent.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to shared household and parenting partnerships where one partner withholds routine labour to force alignment on a system.

### Evidence

> "Refusing to help tidy up was withdrawal used as leverage. It turned a disagreement about systems into a penalty. It has not changed anything, it shows the girls that cooperation is conditional, and it contradicts the interdependence I say I want."
> ([[Household Routines Proposal]], §1)

### Implications

- Resuming shared tasks unconditionally restores credibility before negotiating system changes.
- Conditional withdrawal undermines the interdependence parents want children to model.
