## Philosophy of Science Should Be Naturalistic, Continuous With Science Itself

To Godfrey-Smith, philosophy of science must be "naturalistic"—meaning it should be continuous with science itself, utilising fields like psychology to understand how observation works and sociology to understand how scientific communities function.

### Scope & Conditions

A methodological stance about how to study science, distinct from any specific claim about what makes a theory good.

### Evidence

> "philosophy of science must be 'naturalistic'—meaning it should be continuous with science itself, utilizing fields like psychology to understand how observation works and sociology to understand how scientific communities function"

### Implications

- Explains why Godfrey-Smith's other claims lean on empirical fields (psychology, sociology) rather than pure logical analysis, distinguishing his method from Deutsch's more purely philosophical Popperian approach.
