## Theory Support Comes From the Reliability of the Testing Procedure

Godfrey-Smith's view of how we evaluate theories focuses heavily on procedures: an observation provides support for a theory because of the reliability of the procedure (such as randomised sampling or experimental intervention) in which the observation was embedded.

### Scope & Conditions

Godfrey-Smith's naturalistic account of what confirmation actually consists of, offered as a replacement for naive induction (a bare tally of confirming instances).

### Evidence

> "an observation provides support for a theory because of the reliability of the _procedure_ (such as randomized sampling or experimental intervention) in which the observation was embedded"

### Implications

- A specific, testable alternative to both naive induction and pure falsificationism for explaining why testing yields rational confidence.
- Reframes "how many confirming instances" as the wrong question—the right question is how reliable the procedure that generated the instances is.
