## Quick Fixes That Target Symptoms Trigger Compensating Feedback That Restores the Old Equilibrium

Quick fixes that target surface symptoms leave the underlying system structure intact and trigger compensating feedback that pushes the system back to its old equilibrium. Over time, this dynamic—often called fixes that backfire—absorbs the intervention and leaves the original dysfunction unchanged or worse. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to complex systems governed by balancing feedback loops and policy resistance, where actors respond to local pressures without altering system goals or rules. Less applicable to simple mechanical faults where a single broken part can be swapped without downstream adaptation.

### Implications

- Policy and engineering interventions must trace how existing balancing loops will react to a proposed patch before deploying it.
- Short-term metric improvements after a superficial fix can mask delayed structural degradation.
