## Polarization Suppresses Negativity Bias Toward In-Group Lies

Ordinarily one lie damages a person's reputation out of proportion to its size, because bad information weighs more than good. Under high affective polarization that penalty is suppressed for politicians on the voter's own side, so their lies cost them little. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

A boundary condition on the general negativity bias: the normally strong asymmetry stops applying to a specific target (one's own side) under specific conditions (high affective polarization, with the other side seen as a serious threat). It describes the effect and not how it comes about. It does not say voters stop noticing the lie, only that the usual penalty for it is missing. The card is about politicians, and nothing here shows the same holds for other in-group members.

### Evidence

The suppression itself rests on an AI research summary (titled "Political Contrast Effect Research") whose wording could not be traced to a book, so it is unverified and the quotation that stood here has been removed. Two books support only the parts around it. Pinker describes the ordinary bias that is being suppressed:

> "they dwell on setbacks more than they savor good fortune, and that they are more stung by criticism than they are heartened by praise."
> (Pinker 2018, ch. 4, PDF p. 67) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/415/EPUB?open_at=toc:CHAPTER%204)

Pinker writes about negativity bias in public life in general and does not discuss its partisan suppression. Haidt supports the idea that group commitment makes members partial:

> "Morality binds and blinds."
> (Haidt 2012, "Introduction", PDF p. 13) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/1639/EPUB?open_at=toc:Introduction)

### Implications

- Ordinary accountability for dishonesty can be switched off selectively by group membership, not removed for everyone.
- Disclosing a politician's lies to their own supporters may do little under high polarization, so exposure alone is a weak check.

### Falsifier

- Evidence that voters punish in-group politicians for lies as heavily as out-group ones under high affective polarization, or that the usual penalty is missing for in-group politicians for reasons other than polarization.
