## Polarization Suppresses Negativity Bias Toward In-Group Lies

Under ordinary conditions a single immoral act (e.g. lying) severely and disproportionately damages a person's reputation—this is [[Negativity Bias]]. Under high affective polarization this bias is suppressed for in-group politicians, whose lies are reclassified from moral failings into instrumental competence needed to win an existential contest.

### Scope & Conditions

A boundary condition on general negativity bias: this atom describes when and why the normally powerful asymmetry (bad news outweighs good news) stops applying to a specific target (one's own side) under a specific condition (high polarization plus high out-group threat).

### Evidence

> "The negativity bias, which normally causes voters to aggressively punish lying, is suppressed. Because the extreme anchor is generating massive amounts of moral outrage, the in-group politician's routine lies are reclassified not as moral failings, but as instrumental competence."

### Implications

- Ordinary accountability mechanisms for dishonesty can be switched off selectively, by in-group membership, rather than uniformly.

### Related

- [[Negativity Bias]]—extends: this atom is a boundary condition on the general concept, describing the specific circumstance (partisan threat) under which the normally robust bias to punish bad behaviour is overridden.

### Library Sources

- _Enlightenment Now: The Case for Reason, Science, Humanism, and Progress_ (Steven Pinker), Ch. 4—discusses "the Negativity bias" explicitly, noting society's "vigilance for bad things around us" and how it shapes public perception of information—grounds the base mechanism this atom then describes being selectively suppressed under partisan conditions. Pinker's discussion is about negativity bias in general public discourse, not specifically its partisan suppression, so this is foundational rather than a direct match for the suppression claim.
