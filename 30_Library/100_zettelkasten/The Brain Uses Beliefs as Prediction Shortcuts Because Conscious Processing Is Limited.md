## The Brain Uses Beliefs as Prediction Shortcuts Because Conscious Processing Is Limited

Because conscious processing handles only about 50 bits of data per second out of roughly 11 million bits the senses collect, the brain works as a prediction engine. It uses beliefs as cognitive shortcuts to fill gaps, build a usable model of reality and decide without perfect information.

### Scope & Conditions

The bit figures are the source summary's own and are not verified here. The claim is that having beliefs is a structural necessity of cognition, not an optional extra.

### Evidence

> "Because the human conscious mind has a limited processing capacity—handling only about 50 bits of data per second out of the 11 million bits collected by sensory organs—the brain acts as a prediction engine. It relies on beliefs as essential cognitive shortcuts to fill in gaps, construct a usable model of reality, and make decisions without perfect information."
> (Nir Eyal, Beyond Belief, via an LLM summary; chapter and page not captured)

### Implications

- Having beliefs is unavoidable, so the live question is which beliefs to hold.
- Grounds the later claims that beliefs filter attention and set expectations.
