## Digital Distraction Is a Rational Response to an Unstructured Void

Digital distraction is not primarily a moral failing; it is a rational calculus in which the brain prefers a known pacifier—such as mindless scrolling—over the discomfort of an unstructured void of boredom or unclear purpose. Because willpower cannot sustainably win that fight, distraction only loses its grip when you construct a "Bigger Better Offer" of absorbing, intentional pursuits that make scrolling irrelevant by comparison.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to chronic compulsive checking and low-value screen time when free hours lack compelling structure or craft. Acute fatigue or physiological depletion can still trigger passive rest even when a strong deep-life plan exists.

### Evidence

This claim synthesises Cal Newport's "Bigger Better Offer" argument as recorded in [[SoT - Intentional Living]] rather than a controlled empirical study.

### Implications

- App blockers and guilt-driven willpower fail long-term if the underlying time remains empty and unappealing.
- Displacing distraction requires investing in high-engagement craft, community, and physical vitality so better alternatives already exist.
