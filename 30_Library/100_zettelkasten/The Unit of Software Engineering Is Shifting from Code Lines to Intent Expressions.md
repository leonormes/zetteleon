## The Unit of Software Engineering Is Shifting from Code Lines to Intent Expressions

The fundamental unit of software engineering is shifting from writing individual lines of syntax to expressing high-level intent to autonomous agent clusters. In this model, human engineering productivity is constrained by the precision and clarity of the intent expressed, not by typing speed or syntax knowledge. Engineering work is migrating up the abstraction stack: from implementation to orchestration.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies in environments where agents can manage multiple parallel repository-wide tasks simultaneously. The shift is not complete—low-level understanding remains essential for intent specification, debugging, and constraint design. Engineers who cannot reason about implementation will struggle to express effective intent even in this model.

### Evidence

> "Karpathy argues that the fundamental unit of work has shifted from writing lines of code to 'expressing will' to agent clusters."

### Implications

- Human productivity is constrained by clarity of high-level instructions rather than syntax proficiency; architectural thinking becomes the primary bottleneck.
- Engineers who invest in abstraction, systems thinking, and clear specification gain a compounding advantage as agent capability increases.
