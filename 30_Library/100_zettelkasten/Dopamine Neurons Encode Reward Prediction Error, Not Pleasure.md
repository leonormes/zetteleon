## Dopamine Neurons Encode Reward Prediction Error, Not Pleasure

Midbrain dopamine neurons in the VTA and substantia nigra encode reward prediction error (RPE)—the gap between received and predicted reward—not the hedonic experience of receiving a reward itself. A fully predicted reward produces no dopaminergic response.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to phasic dopamine signalling as characterised in Schultz's foundational RPE work. Does not describe tonic dopamine or the subjective experience of pleasure, which is handled by a separate "liking" system.

### Evidence

> "Midbrain dopamine neurons… do not encode the pleasure of receiving a reward. They encode reward prediction error (RPE): the difference between the reward actually received and the reward that was predicted."

### Implications

- Any system that ties psychological reward exclusively to achieving an outcome is neurologically self-limiting, since a fully expected outcome cannot generate a dopamine response.
- Reward-system design should target moments of positive surprise or novel confirmation, not just goal completion.
