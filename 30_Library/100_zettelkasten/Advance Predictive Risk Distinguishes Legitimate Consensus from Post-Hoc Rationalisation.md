## Advance Predictive Risk Distinguishes Legitimate Consensus from Post-Hoc Rationalisation

Legitimate models make dangerous, falsifiable predictions—stating exactly what should be found before anyone looks. The periodic table's consensus model predicted the existence and exact physical properties of elements like gallium years before their discovery. Groupthink systems rarely make risky, forward-looking predictions; instead they specialise in post-hoc rationalisation, retrofitting their theories to explain away unexpected data after the fact.

### Scope & Conditions

One of five diagnostics (with Atom 006, 007, 009, 010) for telling a legitimate scientific consensus apart from institutional groupthink.

### Evidence

> "The consensus model of the periodic table predicted the existence and exact physical properties of elements like Gallium years before they were discovered."

### Implications

- Ask whether a model has made a risky prediction that later came true, or only explains data it already had.
