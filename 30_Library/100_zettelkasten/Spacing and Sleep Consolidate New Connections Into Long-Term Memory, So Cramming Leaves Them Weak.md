## Spacing and Sleep Consolidate New Connections Into Long-Term Memory, So Cramming Leaves Them Weak

New connections formed while studying are consolidated into long-term memory over time and during sleep, so study spread over several days with sleep between sessions leaves stronger knowledge than the same hours crammed into one. Cramming forms the first connections and leaves them weak, so they are quickly forgotten. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- It applies to knowledge held through memory: facts, concepts and skills. It says nothing about whether the knowledge is true or understood.
- The mechanism is the one in [[Mental Activity Strengthens Connections Between Neurons That Fire Together, So Learning Physically Changes the Brain]]. This note adds the timing: the change is not finished when the session ends.
- The advice about intervals is a rule of thumb from one popular source, not a measured optimum. It is to return to a topic when it is almost forgotten, and to sleep after a day of learning. The claim about sleep rests on the book's account of dendritic spines, which has not been checked against the primary studies.
- A single reading followed by sleep is said to give the big picture, and a later reading to give the detail. That is the source's own suggestion.

### Evidence

One book states both halves. On cramming:

> "cramming forms initial connections but doesn’t allow for them to be moved to long-term memory, where they can be strengthened and consolidated"
> (Schewe and Oakley 2021, ch. 3, PDF p. 44) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/699/EPUB?open_at=toc:3.%20How%20to%20Learn%20Anything%20Deeply)

and on sleep:

> "that spine really connects with an axon when you’re sleeping"
> (Schewe and Oakley 2021, ch. 3, PDF p. 43) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/699/EPUB?open_at=toc:3.%20How%20to%20Learn%20Anything%20Deeply)

The same chapter gives the golden rule of spacing, which is to wait until you have almost forgotten something before you return to it, and compares ten hours crammed into a day with ten hours spread over ten days. Hanson (2015) also says that memories are reconsolidated in storage along with whatever else is in awareness. That point comes from the library's text and has not been separately quoted.

### Implications

- Schedule review across days, not in one block, and leave a night between a first study session and the next.
- A vault is a good place to do the returning: a note revisited when it has half faded does more than a note reread straight after writing. The techniques that put this into practice, such as retrieval practice and spaced repetition, are in [[SoT - Active Learning Techniques]].
- A note-making session that ends with a finished note has not finished learning the idea. The idea needs a later return.

### Falsifier

- Controlled evidence that material crammed in one session is retained as well as the same time spread over days with sleep between sessions, for the kind of material in question.
