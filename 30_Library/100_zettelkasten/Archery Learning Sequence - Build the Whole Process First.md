## Archery Learning Sequence - Build the Whole Process First

Hub: [[MOC - Archery Shot Process]]. Plan context: [[Archery Year-One Coaching Framework (Months 5-12)]].

Summary:

The source conversation mixed two different levels of practice. Build level (where Leon is now) is about getting one repeatable whole-shot process in place. Refine level (expert) is about smoothing a process that is already automatic. Use only the build-level checks now. The refine-level markers are parked in [[Archery Year-Two Inbox]].

---

### The Two Levels

| | Build level (now) | Refine level (later) |
|---|---|---|
| Aim | One repeatable 10-step routine, same every shot | A fluid, subconscious shot with no staccato pauses |
| Unit of practice | The whole routine at low fidelity, then one step at a time | The blended transitions between steps |
| Success signal | Each checkpoint is hit, shot after shot. The arrow position is ignored | Rhythm, timing spread and post-shot feel |
| Feedback | Checklist, mirror, video, A/B/C shot grade | Kinaesthetic feel in the 2-3 seconds after release |
| Attention | One focus per session | Almost none. The shot runs as a motor program |

Rule: do not judge a build-level session by refine-level markers. A clunky release while stance and posture are being fixed is expected, not a fault.

### Build Level: Bottom-Up, One Tier at a Time

World Archery coaching favours analytic learning for developing archers: assemble the shot from the foundation upward, then blend. Global learning (the whole movement from day one) risks hiding foundation errors that collapse later under bow weight.

The coach observation order is ground-up: stance, body and core, shoulders and head, arms, hands and release.

```
TIER 4  Reaction        Passive release and follow-through
TIER 3  Dynamic motion  Draw, anchor, expansion
TIER 2  Setup contacts  Bow hand grip, string hook, low bow shoulder
TIER 1  Foundation      Stance, pelvic tuck, core lock, T posture
```

| Tier | Step notes |
|---|---|
| 1 | [[Archery Shot Process - Stance]], [[Archery Shot Process - Posture]] |
| 2 | [[Archery Shot Process - Setup]], [[Archery Shot Process - Raise]], [[Archery Shot Process - Pre-draw]] |
| 3 | [[Archery Shot Process - Loading]], [[Archery Shot Process - Anchor]], [[Archery Shot Process - Expansion]] |
| 4 | [[Archery Shot Process - Release]], [[Archery Shot Process - Follow-through]] |

How to use it:

- One tier per session. Measure success only on that tier (for example, lower back stayed flat, bow shoulder stayed low).
- Give explicit permission to ignore the tiers above. A messy release or a 7-ring arrow while fixing stance is expected. Fixing both at once splits working memory.
- Release is a reaction, not an action. It follows from the back, shoulders and core, so fixing it first only treats a symptom. See [[Archery Shot Process - Faults and Diagnostics]].

### Build Level: The Standard Teaching Process (STP) Ladder

Move down this ladder for each tier. Low-load steps come first.

1. Mirror or mimic drills. Visual calibration without load. See [[Archery Practice Drills]] items 24 and 25.
2. Eyes-closed shooting. Pure proprioception.
3. Blank boss. Rhythm without target panic. See item 1 in [[Archery Practice Drills]].
4. Cut-out target faces. Scoring rings brought back in stages.
5. Full face and competition. Tests automaticity under pressure.

This overlaps the four stages of item 29 (Hierarchy of Training Complexity) in [[Archery Practice Drills]]. That item is organised by equipment (band, training bow, light bow, target). The ladder above is organised by feedback type. They can be combined.

### Build Level: Advance or Regress

Advance to the next tier when the current one holds at an 80-90% process hit rate across:

1. Natural on a stretch band or in front of a mirror.
2. The same feel with eyes closed.
3. Consistent on a blank boss for several ends without conscious thought.

Regress to the isolated step when:

- The draw hand jumps off the face, the bow shoulder rises, or the lower back arches. Stop chasing flow.
- Attention drifts from body feel to score. Drop down the ladder: blank boss or eyes closed.

The 80-90% figure is the source's number, not a measured one. Treat it as a rule of thumb until the coach confirms it. It fits the A-shot execution rate already used in [[Archery Year-One Coaching Framework (Months 5-12)]].

### Refine Level (Parked)

These are the markers of a finished, blended shot. They are not build-level goals. Captured in [[Archery Year-Two Inbox]]:

- Seamless rhythm: no abrupt pauses or surges in draw speed (Kisik Lee, _Total Archery_).
- Temporal consistency: raise to release in a tight window, full draw ideally under 4-5 seconds.
- Impassive demeanour: face, gaze and posture unchanged through release.
- Surprise release and follow-through along the line of draw without exaggeration.
- Hum test: a steady hum through release. A catch or gasp signals a tension spike. World Archery Level 1-2 drill.
- Group density over face value as proof of consistency.

One refine-level item is already usable now: a stall at full draw of more than 4-5 seconds is a cue to let down, which matches the let-down rule in the Year-One framework.

### Alternative Views / Corrections

- Source framing was inconsistent. The conversation first described practising the whole routine as the advanced stage, then agreed with Leon that bottom-up building is correct for a beginner. The second position is the one recorded here.
- Whole-process-first versus tier-by-tier. The source calls bottom-up tier work analytic learning. Leon's own framing is slightly different: build the whole repeatable routine first, then drill into specific problems. The Year-One framework sides with Leon ("build one repeatable shot process", one focus per block). Both can hold if the full 10-step routine is run at low fidelity every session and one tier gets the attention. Not yet checked with the coach.
