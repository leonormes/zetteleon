## The Words But and However Signal That What Follows May Deserve Its Own Note

When the words "but" or "however" appear while writing a note, what follows is usually a second idea that should be its own note. The source is Bob Doto, A System for Writing.

### Scope & Conditions

A rule of thumb from Doto, who says the answer to his own question is "(almost) always" yes; it is a prompt to ask, not an automatic split.

### Evidence

> "One rule of thumb is to keep an eye out for when "but" and "however" show up in your note-making. Whenever I write either of these two words while making a new note, I pause and ask myself, "Should what comes next be a new note?" The answer is (almost) always "Yes.""
> (Doto, A System for Writing, section "Connecting Your Ideas")

### Implications

- The heuristic can be applied while drafting, before a note is saved.
- The Atomic Signal Extractor prompt uses the same words as its conjunction test.
