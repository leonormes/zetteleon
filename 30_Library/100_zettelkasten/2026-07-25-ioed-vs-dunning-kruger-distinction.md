IoED and the Dunning-Kruger Effect (DKE) get conflated because both describe someone confidently overestimating themselves—but they diverge on three axes. Driver: DKE comes from a metacognitive deficit (lacking the skill needed to recognise you lack the skill); IoED comes from confabulation (mistaking familiarity for causal understanding). Calibration: DKE is about relative ranking ("I'm better than average"); IoED is about mechanistic knowledge ("I know how this works"). Correction: DKE improves by building competence; IoED improves by forcing a step-by-step explanation.

## Evidence

> "Primary Driver—Dunning-Kruger: Metacognitive Deficit… IoED: Confabulation… Calibration Type—DKE: Relative Ranking… IoED: Mechanistic Knowledge… Correction—DKE: Improving competence… IoED: Forcing detailed, step-by-step explanation."—[[SoT - Illusion of Explanatory Depth (IoED)]], §5 comparison table

## Steelman

Both biases could be instances of a single underlying phenomenon—a general failure of metacognitive calibration—in which case splitting them into two named effects with different "corrections" may overstate a distinction that doesn't cash out in different practical interventions.
