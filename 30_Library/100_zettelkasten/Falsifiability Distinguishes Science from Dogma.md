## Falsifiability Distinguishes Science from Dogma

A core tenet of the scientific method is that a theory must be falsifiable: a scientist must be able to say in advance, "if X happens during this experiment, my theory is wrong." Unscientific belief systems do the opposite—they are built to protect the core belief at all costs, inventing new excuses (the laser bent, the equipment was tampered with) rather than updating when disconfirmed.

### Scope & Conditions

The Popperian demarcation criterion. Applies to any claim presented as scientific; it classifies the structure of a claim, not the confidence with which it is held.

### Evidence

> "A core tenet of the scientific method is that a theory must be falsifiable… Science updates its models when the data demands it; dogma doubles down."

### Implications

- A flat-earther who reruns a falsifying laser-over-a-lake experiment and rationalises the result rather than updating is exhibiting the dogma pattern, not the science pattern—regardless of how sincerely they believe.
- Falsifiability is what makes the adversarial incentive structure in [[Science's Adversarial Incentive Structure Rewards Overturning Established Theories]] possible in the first place—you cannot professionally reward disproof of a claim that admits no disproof.
