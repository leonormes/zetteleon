Micro-stepping, or "task chunking," is a behavioral intervention for ADHD that involves breaking down large or overwhelming tasks into the smallest possible actionable steps. This reduces the cognitive load and lowers the internal "activation cost" required to get started, making it easier to initiate action.

Psychological Basis:

- Addresses "gnawing rats" of avoidance
- Lowers barrier to problem engagement
- Builds on [[Discomfort Signals Growth Potential]]

## The Rule of the First Step

Break a daunting task down until the first step is absurdly small: under two minutes and purely physical, such as "open the document and type the title" or "put on running shoes". A step that small lowers the activation energy below the threshold of [[SoT - ADHD Neurology & Core Concepts|The Wall of Awful]] and triggers immediate initiation. After the step you have permission to stop.

## At Project Level

Large projects overwhelm the ADHD brain because their scope obscures the next concrete action. By creating lists of specific, achievable steps with defined milestones, the pathway forward becomes visible. This approach enables picking up where you left off more easily, as each micro-task represents a clear re-entry point. Focusing on completing one small task at a time generates momentum and provides frequent dopamine rewards, sustaining engagement across work sessions.

## Evidence

- [[Evidence - Bandura and Schunk Chunked Maths Goals Raised Childrens Progress and Interest]]—_Children who were advised to chunk a large maths booklet into parts progressed faster and became more interested. It is a general study of chunking, not an ADHD study, so it supports the mechanism, not the ADHD-specific claims._

## Test Protocol (Untested)

A three-day experiment for the first-step rule, carried over from the note it was merged from:

1. Trigger: you are procrastinating on a big task, such as "Write Report".
2. Refuse to do the task.
3. Define one purely physical micro-action that takes under two minutes and commit only to that.
4. After the micro-action you may stop.

Expected outcome: about 80% of micro-actions convert into continued work (the momentum of inertia), and starting "felt easy". Results Log: not yet run.
