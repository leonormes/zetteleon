## Household Conflict Comes From Mismatched Process Values and Not From Opposing Goals

Household conflict usually comes from a mismatch in process values, long-term efficiency against immediate relief, and not from disagreement about the goal itself. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to partners who share a goal such as a tidy house but differ on how to get there. It does not cover conflicts where the goals themselves differ.

### Evidence

> "Conflict often arises not from disagreement on goals (e.g., "A tidy house"), but from a fundamental mismatch in Process Values."
> ([[SoT - Family Communication & Team Charter]], text before the 2026-11-24 refactor, §1)

This is my own reasoning, not an externally sourced finding. No outside source has been checked.

### Implications

- Stop arguing about the task and negotiate the system instead.
- The same word, such as help, means enforcing the system to one partner and doing the task now to the other.
- The efficiency partner tends to feel disrespected, and the immediacy partner tends to feel abandoned.

### Related

- [[SoT - Communication & Misunderstanding (The Experiential Filter)]]—shared mechanism: identical words land differently through different experiential filters.
- [[Fundamental Attribution Error]]—shared mechanism: reading the mismatch as a partner trait and not as a differing process value.

### Tensions

- [[Mind-Reading Fallacy and Projection]]—tension, not contradiction: the efficiency and immediacy labels are one partner's inference about the other.
