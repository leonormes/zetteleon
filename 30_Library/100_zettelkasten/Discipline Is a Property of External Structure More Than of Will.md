## Discipline Is a Property of External Structure More Than of Will

Discipline is largely a property of the structure a person is standing in, such as designed systems, environmental cues, culture, incentives and interpersonal accountability, and not mainly an internal quality. Willpower and motivation are volatile, fatiguing resources and poor foundations, so structure that makes the desired behaviour the path of least resistance reduces the willpower each instance costs. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

This is the stronger reading. When someone is reliably disciplined, the first hypothesis is that they are embedded in better structure, not that they have more will, and the will an action needs is a design variable and not a fixed endowment.

The claim is about acquisition and load-bearing. Its force decays as a habit becomes automatic and context-independent, it does not say where drive comes from, and it needs a neurodivergent qualifier because a cue that never varies stops being salient. It also assumes the structure is used and not endlessly rebuilt.

### Implications

- Treat a missed target as a cue, friction or trigger failure before treating it as weak will.
- Redesign the environment before trying harder, because the environment is the cheaper variable to change.
- For an ADHD person the structure stops being an optimisation and becomes a requirement.
- The first version of any support must be almost embarrassingly small, or it reintroduces the cost it exists to remove.

### Falsifier

- If redesigning cues, friction and defaults while holding the person constant produces no change in behaviour, the claim is wrong.
- If people placed in the same structure differ systematically in discipline in a way only will explains, the claim is wrong.
