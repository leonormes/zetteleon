## The Knowledge Illusion Mistakes Community Expertise for Individual Understanding

People systematically mistake knowledge stored in their community, tools, and environment for knowledge held inside their own heads. Relying on a community of knowledge—including experts, search engines, and AI—sustains an individual illusion of understanding because access to an explanation feels like possessing it. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies whenever individuals operate inside a division of cognitive labour where specialists, reference systems, or digital tools hold the causal detail behind everyday concepts. Relying on external expertise is often adaptive for collective action; the illusion becomes harmful when people mistake borrowed access for personal comprehension during decision-making, debugging, or debate.

### Evidence

Directly supported by [[Evidence - Sloman and Fernbach Say We Mistake Knowledge in the Community for Knowledge in Our Heads]]:

> We think the knowledge we have about how things work sits inside our skulls when in fact we're drawing a lot of it from the environment and from other people.

Quoted verbatim from the vault evidence note (Steven Sloman and Philip Fernbach, _The Knowledge Illusion_). That evidence note also records a boundary: the authors treat reliance on a community of knowledge as partly a strength, not solely a defect.

### Implications

Individual epistemic humility requires separating what one can reconstruct unaided from what one merely knows how to look up or delegate. In environments dense with search tools and AI assistants, testing personal understanding requires removing external scaffolds long enough to verify whether the causal model actually resides in one's own head.
