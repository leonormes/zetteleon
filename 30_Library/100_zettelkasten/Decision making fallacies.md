## Decision Making Fallacies

A hub for the fallacies and biases that distort how choices are made or judged. It began in February 2025 as a two-item list (Sunk Cost Fallacy and Appeal to Novelty, both written in a Definition / Software Context / Example template) and is meant to sit under [[MOC - Cognitive Biases]] section 3, Decision-Making Biases, and under section 5.2 of [[Thinking Patterns, Biases, and Heuristics in Development]].

### Commitment and Cost

- [[Sunk Cost Fallacy]]—_Continuing because of past investment rather than future prospects, with software examples such as keeping a failing architecture._
- [[Commitment, Consistency, and Sunk Cost Fallacy]]—_The social and ego side: once committed, people feel pressure to stay consistent and invent reasons to justify it._
- [[Goal Displacement]]—_The means quietly replace the original goal, so a rule or credential is followed after it has stopped serving the purpose._

### Novelty and Fixation

- [[Appeal to Novelty]]—_Assuming a newer tool or solution is better just because it is new, with no evidence of its merit._
- [[Novelty Effect]]—_The related engagement effect: interest in something new that fades over time. It explains the pull, where the fallacy is the error of inference._
- [[Claim - Novelty-craving drives self-defeating system-hopping]]—_The ADHD version: the burst of motivation from a new system decays before the system can mature._
- [[The Einstellung Effect Prevents Better Solutions]]—_Fixating on the first or familiar solution and missing better ones as the context changes._
- [[Satisficing Leads to Sub-optimal Solutions]]—_Taking the first option that clears a minimum bar and stopping the search there._

### Weighing Gains, Losses and time

- [[Loss Aversion Describes Asymmetric Pain of Loss vs Pleasure of Gain]]—_A loss hurts about twice as much as an equal gain pleases, which skews risky choices._
- [[Prospect Theory Models Decision-Making Relative to a Reference Point]]—_The descriptive model of choice under risk that loss aversion belongs to._
- [[The Emotional Cost of Being Wrong is Magnified by Loss Aversion]]—_Loss aversion applied to being wrong, which makes people avoid admitting error._
- [[Temporal Discounting is the Cognitive Bias Where People Value Immediate Rewards More]]—_Preferring smaller immediate rewards to larger delayed ones._
- [[Action Dominance is the Cognitive Bias Towards Action Over Inaction]]—_Attending more to actions than to inactions when weighing what to do._

### Judging Evidence and Outcomes

- [[Confirmation as a Perceived Shortcut to Truth]]—_External validation of a belief feels like a shortcut to truth and relieves uncertainty without showing the belief is correct._
- [[Goals Suffer from Survivorship Bias]]—_Crediting goals for success while ignoring that losers shared the same goals._
- [[Selective Citation of Successful Predictions Creates a Hindsight-Bias Illusion of Predictive Skill]]—_Quoting only the predictions that came true makes judgement look better than it was._

### Stalling instead of Deciding

- [[SoT - Perfectionism and Analysis Paralysis]]—_Fear of doing it wrong blocks the decision to start._
- [[Executive Dysfunction - The Root of Analysis Paralysis]]—_The executive-function account of the same stall._
- [[Information-Seeking Is Adaptive Only While It Remains Instrumental to an Actual Decision]]—_When gathering more information stops informing a decision and becomes a safety behaviour._

### Countermeasures

- [[Decoupling Ego from Outcomes to Improve Decisions]]—_Separate self-worth from a single result by thinking in bets._
- [[SoT - Commitment Devices (Ulysses Pacts)]]—_Bind a future choice in advance so a present bias cannot override it._
- [[Choice Architecture Designs the Environment to Make Desired Behaviors Easier]]—_Design the environment so the better choice is the easy one._
