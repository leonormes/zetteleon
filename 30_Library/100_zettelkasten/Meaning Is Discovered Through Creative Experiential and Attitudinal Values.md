## Meaning Is Discovered Through Creative Experiential and Attitudinal Values

Frankl identifies three distinct avenues through which individuals discover meaning. Creative values: what one gives to the world—contribution, work, deed. Experiential values: what one receives from the world—beauty, love, connection. Attitudinal values: how one stands toward unavoidable suffering—the capacity to find dignity and purpose within pain that cannot be removed. The third pathway is only valid for suffering that _is_ unavoidable; avoidable suffering should be eliminated rather than endured for meaning's sake.

### Scope & Conditions

The three-pathway structure is a practical taxonomy, not an abstract hierarchy. All three are available at any time, though access may be constrained by circumstance. The crucial boundary condition on attitudinal values: they do not justify suffering—they address what to do when suffering has already been imposed and cannot be changed.

### Implications

- Provides a structured framework for auditing personal sources of meaning—useful diagnostically when someone reports feeling empty despite apparent comfort.
- Validates suffering as a potential source of value when circumstances are unchangeable; removes the cultural pressure to "fix" or deny unavoidable pain.
