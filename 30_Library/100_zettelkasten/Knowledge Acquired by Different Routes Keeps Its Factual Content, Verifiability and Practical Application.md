## Knowledge Acquired by Different Routes Keeps Its Factual Content, Verifiability and Practical Application

When two people hold the same knowledge but gained and understood it in different ways, its factual content, practical application, communicability and verifiability are likely to stay the same.

### Scope & Conditions

The source hedges these as likely or true in many cases, and gives no evidence beyond general reasoning, so this is a low-confidence claim from an AI answer.

### Evidence

> "Factual Content: The core factual information or data points within the shared knowledge would likely be the same, regardless of how it was acquired or understood."

> "Verifiability: The knowledge should be equally verifiable or testable, regardless of how it was acquired or understood."

### Implications

- Knowledge can be shared and tested at the level of facts even when each person understands it differently.
- Both people should be able to communicate its essentials, possibly with different analogies.
