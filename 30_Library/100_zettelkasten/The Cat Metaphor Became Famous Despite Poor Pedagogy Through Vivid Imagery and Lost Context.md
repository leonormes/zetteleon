## The Cat Metaphor Became Famous Despite Poor Pedagogy Through Vivid Imagery and Lost Context

A cat trapped in a box with poison and radioactive material is a striking, dramatic image against the dry mathematics of physics, which made it irresistible to science fiction, television, and pop-science writing as a catchy stand-in for "an outcome is unknown." Over decades this "game of telephone" stripped away the original context—that Schrödinger meant it sarcastically, as a criticism—so science communicators began using his mocking critique as though it were a genuine illustrative tool. The metaphor's fame is a story about memorability and cultural transmission, not about its accuracy as an explanation.

### Scope & Conditions

Describes a mechanism of cultural transmission (why a bad explanatory device persists and spreads), not a claim about the underlying physics.

### Implications

- A metaphor's popularity is not evidence of its pedagogical accuracy—vividness and memorability can propagate a device independently of whether it actually teaches the concept.
- Recovering the original intent behind a famous analogy can reveal it was never meant to do the job it is now used for.
