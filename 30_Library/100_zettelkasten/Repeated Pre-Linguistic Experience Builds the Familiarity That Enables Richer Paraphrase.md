### Repeated Pre-Linguistic Experience Builds the Familiarity That Enables Richer Paraphrase

The more time spent directly experiencing a concept before putting it into words, the more familiar that concept becomes. That familiarity is what allows for more diverse and refined ways of expressing it — familiarity doesn't just follow experience, it feeds back into expression.

This describes a developmental loop, not a one-off translation: direct experience builds understanding → understanding builds familiarity with the concept → familiarity enables more varied paraphrase of it → the act of paraphrasing can itself refine the original understanding. [[Paraphrasing is a Complex Cognitive Skill|Paraphrasing]] is usually treated as evidence that meaning already exists independently of words; this claim adds an account of where the *capacity* to paraphrase well actually comes from.

#### Scope & Conditions

Applies to concept-level understanding built through direct engagement — experiential, embodied, or procedural — not to facts acquired secondhand from description alone. The broader claim that non-linguistic understanding isn't unique to humans is already established elsewhere in the vault (see [[SoT - The Primacy of Experience (Pre-Linguistic Understanding)]] §2.1 and [[Types of Non-Linguistic Knowledge]]); this note doesn't restate that evidence, it adds the developmental mechanism by which experience compounds into expressive range over time.

#### Evidence

> "the more time you spend experiencing this pre-linguistic experience, the more familiar a concept becomes"

Verbatim, from the now-superseded source note [[Developing the Idea of Pre-Linguistic Understanding]].

#### Implications

- Paraphrase range is a rough proxy for how much direct experience underlies a concept — a thin paraphrase range can signal thin experiential grounding, not just a vocabulary gap.
- Deliberately increasing direct engagement with a concept (not just reading descriptions of it) is a route to being able to paraphrase it better later, not a separate skill from paraphrasing itself.
