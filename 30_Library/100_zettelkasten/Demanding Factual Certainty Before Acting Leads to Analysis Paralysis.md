## Demanding Factual Certainty Before Acting Leads to Analysis Paralysis

Requiring absolute factual certainty before taking action produces analysis paralysis. Where certainty is unobtainable in advance, the demand for it stalls the decision.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies where certainty cannot be had before acting, as in the middle ground between fact and faith. The source presents it as one of two opposite failure modes, the other being acting on pure faith.

### Evidence

> "Demanding absolute factual certainty before taking action leads to analysis paralysis"
> (Nir Eyal, Beyond Belief, via an LLM summary; chapter and page not captured)

### Implications

- Corresponds to the limiting belief "I need to be certain before I act".
- The source offers choosing beliefs by usefulness as the alternative that allows action with imperfect information.
