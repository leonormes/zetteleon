## Hitchens Razor Places the Burden of Proof on the Claimant, Not the Sceptic

Hitchens's razor holds that whoever asserts a claim must supply evidence for it; the absence of a disproof does not count as evidence that the claim is true—"dismissed without evidence" should be read as "not accepted as established," not as "proven false."

### Scope & Conditions

A basic principle of rational argument, not a complete method for evaluating evidence on its own—it settles who must argue first, not how strong their evidence needs to be (that's closer to the Sagan standard) or whether it is conclusive.

### Evidence

> "someone asserting a supernatural explanation must provide evidence for it; the absence of a disproof does not count as evidence that the claim is true… 'Dismissed without evidence' is best understood as 'not accepted as established', rather than automatically proven false."

### Implications

- Rejecting a claim for lack of evidence is not the same speech act as proving the claim false; conflating the two overstates what scepticism has actually established.
