## Progress Comes From Testing Reforms

If we keep track of how our laws and manners are doing, think up improvements, try them out and keep those that make people better off, we can gradually make the world a better place. The source is Steven Pinker, Enlightenment Now.

### Scope & Conditions

Pinker's account of the Enlightenment belief in progress, which he contrasts with the Romantic belief in mystical forces that propel mankind toward utopia. The hope for progress was concentrated on human institutions such as governments, laws, schools, markets and international bodies.

### Evidence

> "If we keep track of how our laws and manners are doing, think up ways to improve them, try them out, and keep the ones that make people better off, we can gradually make the world a better place."
> (Enlightenment Now, Chapter 1: Dare to Understand!)

### Implications

- Progress is an experimental cycle of theory and trial, like science, not a destiny.
- Institutions, not human nature, are the natural target for improvement.
