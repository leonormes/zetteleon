## Applicative Intelligence

Applicative (or Reproductive) Intelligence is the capacity to master existing rules, recognise patterns rapidly, and optimise solutions within an established paradigm. Analogous to Thomas Kuhn's "Normal Science," it excels at efficiency, reliability, and social alignment within known territory.

### Scope & Conditions

Essential for engineering execution, institutional stability, and incremental progress whenever the underlying paradigm remains sound. It fails when anomalies require revising the foundational assumptions themselves.

### Evidence

Defined in [[SoT - Generative Intelligence]] and elaborated in [[SoT - Conventional Problem Solving]] as the normal-science counterpart to paradigm-creating thought.

### Implications

- Formal schooling and credentialing primarily reward applicative intelligence, creating the illusion that rule-mastery equals creative insight.
- When an underlying model is flawed, pure applicative effort only patches epicycles onto a failing structure ([[Ptolemaic System as a Patched Flawed Model]]).
