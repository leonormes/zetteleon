## Evil Harm Is Intolerable Harm That Destroys the Conditions for a Decent Life

In Claudia Card's philosophical framework, evil harm is "intolerable harm"—harm that deprives victims of the basic necessities required to make life possible, decent, or tolerable. This distinguishes evil from ordinary "badness" by a qualitative threshold: the harm is not merely unwanted but constitutively destructive of the conditions under which life can be lived with coherence, agency, or dignity.

### Scope & Conditions

Grounded in Card's _The Atrocity Paradigm_. Focuses on the objective impact on the victim's autonomy and psychological coherence rather than on the perpetrator's intentions. The threshold between "bad" and "intolerable" is not a bright line but a qualitative shift—harm that forecloses rather than merely impedes the basic conditions of human existence.

### Evidence

> "what philosopher Claudia Card characterizes as 'intolerable harm' that deprives victims of the basic necessities that make life possible, tolerable, or decent."

### Implications

- Evil is distinguished from ordinary wrongdoing by a quantitative difference that becomes qualitative—a threshold beyond which harm forecloses rather than merely impedes life.
- Measurement of evil should focus on the victim's remaining capacity for autonomous and coherent existence, not on the perpetrator's psychology.
