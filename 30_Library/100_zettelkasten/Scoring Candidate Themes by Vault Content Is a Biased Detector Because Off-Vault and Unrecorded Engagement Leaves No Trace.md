## Scoring Candidate Themes by Vault Content Is a Biased Detector Because Off-Vault and Unrecorded Engagement Leaves No Trace

If a candidate theme or domain is scored by how much vault content bridges it, two kinds of real engagement are invisible to the count and not merely under-weighted. Both look identical from inside the vault, as near-zero co-occurrence with other domains. Telling genuine thinness from hidden engagement means asking directly, not inferring from what is written.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to any theme-detection method that relies on vault content, such as tag co-occurrence, note volume or written reflection. The two invisible cases are:

- Off-vault outlets. A domain practised through paid work, such as DevOps, cloud and security, generates its reflection through the work itself and not through vault writing. It reads as low-engagement content to organise when it may be a live instance of the same theme or drive as the vault's richest domains.
- Pre-vault or unrecorded history. A domain with a real, intense past can be almost absent from the vault if that period predates it or was never written up, so frequency or density scoring misclassifies it as thin or consumption-only.

### Evidence

The music case is Leon's own correction, recorded in the interest seed note dated 2026-09-15: Leon was a musician who

> "spent all day everyday practicing and playing and listening"

This is a single worked case, not a measured bias rate.

### Implications

- Elicit before classifying: ask the person whether a thin domain is thin or merely unrecorded.
- This attaches a concrete failure case to the elicit-before-classify principle that the Portable Interest project already names as its Principle 2.
