## Dividing a Process Does Not Make the Intact Process Illusory

Summary: Showing that a biological process can be divided does not show that the process is an illusion; water can be split into hydrogen and oxygen, yet the wetness of intact water is real—likewise the unity of the self, while the brain is intact, need not be illusory just because the brain can be split.

Details: This objection defends a _process_ or _emergent_ account of the self against the charge of illusion. Its force is limited by what Harris actually targets: he denies a permanent, indivisible substance-self (a Cartesian soul), not the everyday phenomenal feeling of being one person. On the narrow target, the wetness analogy talks past him—Harris would agree the process-self is real while denying any indivisible core beneath it. The atom is therefore best read as clarifying _which_ self is at stake: real-as-process, not real-as-substance.

### Evidence

> "showing that water can be split into hydrogen and oxygen does not make the 'wetness' of water an illusion."

### Implications

- "The self is an illusion" and "the self is a real emergent process" can both be true of different senses of self.
- The disagreement with Harris is often terminological: substance-self versus process-self.
