## The Utilitarian Organ-Harvesting Thought Experiment Shows Maximising Aggregate Welfare Can Justify Intuitively Abhorrent Acts

Classic utilitarianism (maximising overall enjoyment or happiness) is criticised because it would logically require murdering one healthy patient to harvest their organs to save five dying patients, if the net welfare calculation favours it—a standard objection used to argue the theory's aggregate-maximising logic can license acts that are intuitively monstrous.

### Scope & Conditions

A well-known objection within moral philosophy, not a knockdown refutation—modern rule-utilitarians have developed defences against this specific case that this treatment doesn't address.

### Evidence

> "Utilitarianism… is flawed because it justifies intuitively abhorrent actions. For example, it would logically require murdering one healthy patient to harvest organs to save five dying patients."

### Implications

- Any aggregate-welfare argument should be checked against this kind of edge case before being accepted as a complete ethical framework.
