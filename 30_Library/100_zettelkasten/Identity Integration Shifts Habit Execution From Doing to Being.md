## Identity Integration Shifts Habit Execution From Doing to Being

Framing a repeated behaviour as part of who you are ("I am a runner"), rather than as a task you perform ("I am going for a run"), makes it easier to sustain. Each repetition then confirms a chosen self-concept instead of being negotiated from scratch. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- Applies to repeated behaviours that a person has chosen and values. A one-off action gives an identity nothing to build on.
- The identity needs to rest on observable actions. A label with no repetitions behind it is only an aspiration.
- It should be defined by showing up, not by flawless performance. Binding self-worth to an unbroken streak can backfire when normal capacity limits interrupt it.

### Evidence

> "Every action you take is a vote for the type of person you wish to become."
> (Clear 2018, ch. 2, PDF p. 41) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/690/EPUB?open_at=toc:2:%20How%20Your%20Habits%20Shape%20Your%20Identity%20%28and%20Vice%20Versa%29)

> "The ultimate form of intrinsic motivation is when a habit becomes part of your identity."
> (Clear 2018, ch. 2, PDF p. 38) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/690/EPUB?open_at=toc:2:%20How%20Your%20Habits%20Shape%20Your%20Identity%20%28and%20Vice%20Versa%29)

Clear states the mechanism in a practitioner book, and these quotations are assertions rather than a test of it. This claim is also point 8 of the 9-Point Consistency Framework in [[SoT - Intentional Living]].

### Implications

- Small repetitions count, because each one is evidence for the identity even when volume is minimal.
- Choose the identity by values first, then pick the smallest action that expresses it.
- Pair the framing with compassionate setback recovery, so a missed day does not read as a verdict on the self.

### Falsifier

- Compare people building the same habit with identity framing and with task or outcome framing. If adherence after several months is no better, or lapses lead to abandonment more often, the claim is wrong.

### Steelman

> "The tighter we cling to an identity, the harder it becomes to grow beyond it."
> (Clear 2018, ch. 20, PDF p. 203) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/690/EPUB?open_at=toc:20:%20The%20Downside%20of%20Creating%20Good%20Habits)

The strongest objection is that the same fusion which sustains a habit makes it costly to change or to miss. A person who cannot run may then lose a sense of self as well as an activity.
