## Archery Shot Process - Posture

Summary:
Posture focuses on engaging the core, positioning the hips and torso to transfer stability from the stance through the trunk.

Details:
- **Core Engagement (Chest-Down Position)**:
  - Tuck the pelvis slightly and engage the abs into a subtle "crunch" position to lock the core (archery posture differs from standard vertical "good posture"). Avoid arching the lower back.
  - Align and position the hips and torso to provide a stable, solid trunk.
- **Active Posture**: Posture must be actively engaged and maintained throughout the entire shot sequence—it is never "set and forgotten."
- **Spine & Alignment (Tug-of-War Principle)**:
  - Maintain a straight spine with no curving and no leaning towards or away from the target. Keep a straight vertical line between your shoulders, hips, and ankles.
  - The bow side (push) and draw side (pull) must balance equally. Favouring the draw side causes a backward postural tilt towards the rear foot.
- **The "T-Line" Alignment**: Ensure feet, hips, and shoulders form a clean, upright "T-Line". Proper T-line posture prevents unnecessary muscle strain and helps your sight pin float in a slow, controlled motion.
- **Head Alignment & Positioning**:
  - **Set Head Position Early**: Look directly at the target during the Setup phase. Keep your head completely stationary throughout the draw, anchor, and follow-through.
  - Avoid turning or adjusting your head mid-draw, as this creates postural shifts and shot inconsistency.
- **Posture Failure Points**:
  - *During Raise*: Arching the lower back to lift the bow.
  - *During Draw*: Pulling back with the spine/back instead of drawing with the scapula.
  - *During Follow-Through*: Leaning back or collapsing as the arrow leaves.

### Training Drills

#### **Upper Chest Balance Test**
* **Purpose:** Verifies that body weight is properly centred and core muscles are braced.
* **How to do it**:
  1. Assume your posture (pelvis slightly tucked, abs braced in a subtle crunch).
  2. Have a training partner apply light, steady pressure against your upper chest.
  3. If you tip backwards or lean into the push, adjust your weight until you remain completely rock-solid.

#### **Partner Head-Bracing Drill**
* **Purpose:** Eliminates mid-draw head movement and provides immediate kinesthetic feedback.
* **How to do it**:
  1. Set your head position during the Setup phase, looking directly at the target.
  2. Have a partner gently hold your head or cap in place without applying force.
  3. Execute your draw. If your head shifts or rotates, you will instantly feel resistance against your partner's hands.
