## Complexity Splitting

Complexity splitting is dividing a note that appears to hold one idea but hides two or more into separate single-idea notes. The label is this vault's name for the move that Bob Doto shows when he splits one note into two simpler ones.

### Scope & Conditions

The label is not Doto's. It names the move in his Figures 35 and 36, where a note that seems to hold one idea about apples turns out to be about a discrepancy in their nutritional value, and is split in two. It applies to notes that look atomic on the surface, and it is done when the note is made, not later.

### Evidence

> "Sometimes one idea is really two."
> (Doto, A System for Writing, caption of Figure 35, section "Connecting Your Ideas")

> "A complex idea has been split into two simpler ones."
> (Doto, A System for Writing, caption of Figure 36, section "Connecting Your Ideas")

> "If we take a step back, we see the idea isn't dealing with apples per se, but with a discrepancy regarding their nutritional value."
> (Doto, A System for Writing, section "Connecting Your Ideas")

> "Break them up now, so you don't have to later."
> (Doto, A System for Writing, section "More is Less")

### Implications

- The trigger is a note that seems to hold one idea but carries a second, often counterbalancing, thought; the words "but" and "however" are a common sign of it.
- The payoff is more places each idea can connect, and less sifting when a note is used in writing.
- Splitting at the moment of note-making costs less than untangling the note later.
