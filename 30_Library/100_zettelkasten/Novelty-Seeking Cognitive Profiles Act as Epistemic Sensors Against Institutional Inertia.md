## Novelty-Seeking Cognitive Profiles Act as Epistemic Sensors Against Institutional Inertia

Institutions optimise for exploitation: systemic stability, low variance and adherence to heuristics, which blinds them to paradigm-breaking anomalies. Individuals wired for high-variance exploration, such as mavericks and early adopters, serve as epistemic sensors. They bypass socialised rules to test edge cases directly, which keeps the wider system from becoming trapped in obsolete local maxima.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies where anomalies fall outside an organisation's established heuristics. The source gives no basis for its 8/10 confidence, so treat this as a working hypothesis.

The source's counterargument: unchecked exploration fragments the system, because most heuristics are Chesterton's Fences, load-bearing stabilisers learned from past failures.

The source's falsification criterion: the claim fails if organisations made up only of rule-adherent exploiters consistently identify, validate and integrate paradigm-shifting technologies faster than organisations that pair autonomous explorer vanguard units with institutional settler teams.

### Implications

- An institution that suppresses its outliers also removes its own anomaly detectors.
- The sensor role needs a route back into the institution, otherwise the fresh data is lost.
