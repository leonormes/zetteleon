## A Sense of Control Is Belief in an Available Response, Not a Guarantee Against Bad Outcomes

A healthy sense of control is not the belief that nothing bad can happen or that one knows everything; it is the belief that one has an available response that can influence or mitigate an adverse event, without that being a guarantee the event will never occur.

### Scope & Conditions

Distinguishes control from certainty specifically; certainty can support a sense of control but is not required for it.

### Evidence

> "A healthy sense of control is not 'nothing bad can happen' or 'I know everything.' It is closer to: 'I have enough orientation to identify the relevant variables, act safely, notice when the model is failing, and adapt or escalate.'… control is fundamentally the belief that you have an available response that can influence or mitigate an adverse event—not a guarantee that it will never occur."

### Implications

- Reframes "I need to understand everything before I act" as targeting the wrong variable (certainty) when the actual need is an available response (control).
- Gives a concrete operational translation for technical risk: not "understand the whole system" but "know the blast radius, rollback route, and escalation path."
