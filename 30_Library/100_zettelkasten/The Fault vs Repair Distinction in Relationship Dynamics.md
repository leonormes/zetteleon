A common relational impasse occurs when a partner conflates the **cause of a problem** with the **labour of solving it**.

## 📐 The Distinction

1. **Fault (Causality):** "Who caused the leak?" Often, both partners contribute to the existence of a problem (shared fault).
2. **Repair (Labour):** "Who is calling the plumber?" This is the active work of researching, initiating, and following up on solutions.

## 🧩 The "House Leak" Analogy

If a house has a leak, both partners might have forgotten to maintain the roof (Shared Fault). However, if only one partner is researching plumbers, calling for quotes, and checking the fix, while the other just says "Yeah, there's a leak," then the **Labour of Repair** is asymmetric.

## 🧠 Strategic Reframe

To break a cycle of "score-keeping," refocus the conversation from "Whose fault is this?" to **"Who is carrying the work of trying to solve it?"**

- **Goal:** Acknowledge shared contribution to problems while insisting on shared responsibility for the maintenance/repair process.
