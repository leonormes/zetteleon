## Evidence - Ultralearning Chess Experts Rely on Stored Patterns Where Beginners Remember Piece by Piece

> Give beginners and experts a randomized chess board (one that doesn't arise from normal play), and the experts no longer display the same marked advantage. Without the library of memorized patterns at their disposal, they have to resort to the beginner's method of remembering the board piece by piece.

Source: Scott Young, _Ultralearning_, on expert pattern memory in chess.

### What it Supports

The "compressed patterns" half of [[Comparison - Knowing vs Understanding]]: experts hold a board as familiar patterns, while beginners hold it as separate pieces. That is the difference between structured and raw information that the note's cognitive-load row relies on.

### What it Does not Show

The passage begins mid-argument and is a popular-science account of a chess result, not the original study. It concerns pattern memory in one domain, so it supports the mechanism, not the note's wider claim that understanding "integrates knowledge into actionable frameworks".
