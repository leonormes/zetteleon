## Internalism Requires Conscious First-Person Access to Justification

Internalism, a theory of epistemic justification, argues that for a belief to count as knowledge, the knower must have conscious, first-person access to the evidence or reasoning that justifies it.

### Scope & Conditions

One side of the internalism/externalism split over how individuals actually "know" scientific facts.

### Evidence

> "Internalism: Argues that for a belief to count as knowledge, the knower must have conscious, first-person access to the evidence or reasoning that justifies it."

### Implications

- Sets the demand that externalist accounts explicitly reject.
