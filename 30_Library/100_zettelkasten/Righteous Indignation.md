## Righteous Indignation

Righteous indignation is the distinctively moral kind of anger felt toward free riders. It drives pro-social punishment, and people also expect others to feel it toward noncooperators.

### Scope & Conditions

In Fehr and Gaechter's study, most participants said they would feel angry at a free rider and would expect others to feel anger toward them in the reverse role.

### Evidence

> "As you might expect, pro-social punishment is driven by emotions."
>
> "Most people indicated that they would feel angry, and that they would expect others to feel anger toward them if the roles were reversed. Call this distinctively moral kind of anger righteous indignation."
> (Joshua Greene, Moral Tribes, Chapter 2: Moral Machinery)

### Implications

- Moral anger is the emotional enforcement arm of cooperation.
- People also expect others to be indignant toward noncooperators.
