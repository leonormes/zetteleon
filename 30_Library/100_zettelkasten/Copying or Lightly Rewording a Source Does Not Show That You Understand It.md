## Copying or Lightly Rewording a Source Does Not Show That You Understand It

Copying or lightly rewording a source does not show that you understand it, because either can be done without reconstructing how the idea works. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- Applies when the output is offered as a sign of understanding. Copying still has uses, such as keeping an exact quotation.
- Careful paraphrase that rebuilds the reasons and relationships falls outside this claim.

### Evidence

> "Verbatim notes can be taken with almost no thinking, as if the words are taking a short cut from the ear to the hand, bypassing the brain."
> (Ahrens 2022, ch. 10, PDF p. 115) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/704/EPUB?open_at=toc:10%20Read%20for%20Understanding)

The quotation concerns verbatim notes. Extending it to light rewording, such as swapping synonyms, is my own reasoning.

### Implications

- Close the source before explaining, and say why and how as well as what.

### Falsifier

- If people who copy or swap synonyms answer new questions about an idea as well as people who reconstruct it, the claim is wrong.
