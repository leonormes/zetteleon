## Storage Should Be Designed for the Worst Day and Not for Peak Motivation

A storage system that demands precision only survives bursts of high capacity, and then collapses when energy dips. Designing for the worst day sets a low-effort floor that survives burnout and illness. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to any shared household system, most strongly where capacity swings. It does not rule out precise systems for people who can keep them up reliably.

### Evidence

> "Returning an item requires so little energy and deliberation that it remains sustainable even through burnout, illness, or acute executive dysfunction."
> (AI chat summary pasted 2026-11-24, not checked against Aarssen's own work)

### Implications

- Test a new system by asking whether the most tired person can use it unaided.
- Colour-coded or precision-folded systems belong in deep maintenance, not in the daily floor.
