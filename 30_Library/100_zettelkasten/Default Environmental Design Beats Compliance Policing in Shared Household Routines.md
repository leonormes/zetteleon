## Default Environmental Design Beats Compliance Policing in Shared Household Routines

Framing household routine breakdowns as environmental design problems—asking what makes the calm routine the default—is more effective than policing compliance or relying on willpower. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to recurring household transitions, especially where executive function or ADHD load makes memory-based adherence unreliable; must be balanced against over-engineering systems as avoidance.

### Evidence

> "I have ADHD; I rely on systems, not memory or willpower. ... Implication: the question is 'what makes the calm routine the default?', not 'who failed to comply?'"
> ([[Household Routines Proposal]], §3 Claim C)

### Implications

- Device-level or environmental defaults (such as agreed cut-off timers) remove the burden of remembering or nagging.
- Shifts partner discussions from blame to shared system design.
