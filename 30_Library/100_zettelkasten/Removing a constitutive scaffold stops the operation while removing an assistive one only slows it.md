## Removing a constitutive scaffold stops the operation while removing an assistive one only slows it

Removing a constitutive scaffold causes the offloaded operation to fail or not occur at all, whereas removing an assistive scaffold leaves the person able to complete the operation at a degraded baseline or slower pace. This counterfactual test separates a constitutive working-memory scaffold from a helpful aid. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- **Scoped to capacity-exceeding operations:** Applies to operations that exceed unaided internal working memory. Removing the scaffold does not abolish internal working memory itself; it returns the person to their unaided baseline, where those specific operations fail.
- **Empirical and testable:** Can be tested directly via withdrawal (e.g., [[How to Test an ADHD Scaffold Using ABAB Withdrawal]]).

### Evidence

- **Analytical and experiential reasoning (unsourced):** No cited empirical study is attached yet; proposed as a falsifiable diagnostic separating "is part of working memory for this operation" from "is a very good aid". *(No Calibre book quotation attached yet.)*

### Implications

- **System reliability:** If removing a scaffold halts operations rather than merely slowing them, outage recovery and low-friction backup are operational necessities, not polish.
