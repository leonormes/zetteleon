## Cognitive Defusion from Narrative

Learned assumptions and internal narratives about one's identity (e.g., "I am someone who quits") should be treated as habitual conceptualisations rather than irrefutable facts. Recognizing these narratives as "learned patterns" rather than essential traits reclaims agency and allows for the reframing of fixed traits into manageable habits.

### Scope & Conditions

Essential for dismantling learned helplessness and breaking fixed-mindset loops.

### Evidence

> "Ask yourself if this narrative is actually an irrefutable fact or just a habitual conceptualisation. Recognise that you have 'taught yourself' to accept these assumptions."

### Implications

- Shifts the locus of control from "nature" to "practice."
- Reduces the emotional impact of self-critical internal monologue by identifying it as a learned script.
