## Tying Discretionary Privileges to Completed Checklists Automates Accountability Without Nagging

Linking discretionary evening privileges such as screen time and Wi-Fi to completed daily checklists lets the household system enforce boundaries neutrally without parental nagging or emotional escalation. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies only when both parents have agreed the privilege boundary in advance and when check-ins pause immediately if a child is dysregulated. Does not apply to basic needs, safety, or connection.

### Evidence

> "Neutral Work–Privilege Alignment (`Work = Access`): Discretionary privileges (evening Wi-Fi, screen time, transport, discretionary spend) unlock cleanly when daily/weekly jobs pass check-in. Parents act as friendly, matter-of-fact checkers rather than nags."
> ([[SoT - Family Household Chores]], text before the 2026-11-24 compilation refactor, §1.2)

This is my own reasoning synthesised from household governance design, not an externally sourced empirical study.

### Implications

- Let automatic device or check-in locks carry the boundary so neither parent has to play the villain.
- Respond to pushback calmly without emotional escalation by stating the binary agreement and leaving the choice with the child.
- Avoid quietly rescuing unfinished child chores so the natural privilege pause teaches follow-through.
