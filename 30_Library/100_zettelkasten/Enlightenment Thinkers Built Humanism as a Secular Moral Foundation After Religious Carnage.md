## Enlightenment Thinkers Built Humanism as a Secular Moral Foundation After Religious Carnage

Enlightenment thinkers built humanism as a secular foundation for morality because they were haunted by the historical memory of centuries of religious carnage. The source is Steven Pinker, Enlightenment Now.

### Scope & Conditions

Pinker's historical account; the carnage he lists includes the Crusades, the Inquisition, witch hunts and the European wars of religion.

### Evidence

> "The thinkers of the Age of Reason and the Enlightenment saw an urgent need for a secular foundation for morality, because they were haunted by a historical memory of centuries of religious carnage: the Crusades, the Inquisition, witch hunts, the European wars of religion."
> (Enlightenment Now, Chapter 1: Dare to Understand!)

### Implications

- Humanism is presented as a response to group-based violence, which links it to tribalism as a problem.
- The moral foundation is chosen for its independence from any one religion.
