## Fallibilism Holds That Knowledge Requires Good Justification, Not Certainty

Fallibilism holds that a person can know something while accepting they might be wrong and would revise if new evidence turned up. What knowledge requires is justification, not proof. The source chat calls this the mainstream view.

### Scope & Conditions

The claim concerns empirical knowledge, where certainty is not available. The source says fallibilism is the majority view and not unanimous, and that an infallibilist reaches the sceptical conclusion for everything and not just for one case. No philosopher is named in the chat, and no primary source is held in the vault.

### Evidence

> "The mainstream view is fallibilism: you can know something while accepting you might be wrong and would revise if new evidence turned up."
> (AI chat transcript pasted by Leon, 2026-10-23, section "Ghosts, fairies, and knowing that something doesn’t exist")

> "The traditional definition needs justification, not proof."
> (same section)

### Implications

- Certainty is not the price of entry: knowing where your car is parked does not require ruling out a thief, a tow truck or a simulation glitch.
- A negative claim can be known on the same terms, see [[Absence of Evidence Counts as Evidence of Absence in Proportion to the Evidence Expected]].
