## Emotions Are Weak Evidence About External Reality Even When They Are Real Experiences

An emotion is a real experience, but it is weak evidence about the external situation that triggered it, because it follows the person's appraisal of that situation and the appraisal can be wrong. A feeling should therefore prompt a check of the facts and not stand in for one. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

The claim is about emotions as evidence of external facts, not about emotions as experiences: a feeling is real as a feeling, and it is weak evidence that a situation is as bad, or as good, as it feels. "Weak" does not mean worthless. Some emotions, such as fear at a sudden danger, are fast and often right, and any emotion is good evidence of what the person is appraising and cares about. Not trusting a feeling as evidence does not mean suppressing it: accepting the feeling and checking the facts go together.

### Evidence

The core is the author's own reasoning, recorded in 2025 without a source, and two books now support its premises. Kahneman describes how a feeling takes the place of a judgement:

> "The affect heuristic is an instance of substitution, in which the answer to an easy question (How do I feel about it?) serves as an answer to a much harder question (What do I think about it?)."
> (Kahneman 2012, ch. 13, PDF p. 167) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/53/EPUB?open_at=toc:13.%20Availability%2C%20Emotion%2C%20and%20Risk)

Ekman, who argues that emotions evolved to serve us, still says they follow what we sense and not what is true:

> "when we sense, rightly or wrongly, that something that seriously affects our welfare, for better or worse, is happening or about to happen."
> (Ekman 2012, ch. 2, PDF p. 31) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/301/EPUB?open_at=toc:2.%20When%20Do%20We%20Become%20Emotional%3F)

Neither book says emotions are unreliable overall. Together they support the narrower point that a feeling reports an appraisal, not the facts.

### Implications

- Treat a strong feeling as a prompt to ask what it is responding to and what would show it right or wrong, not as a result.
- Check in both directions: euphoria and dread are equally weak evidence.
- Keep the feeling as information about what the person appraises and cares about.

### Falsifier

- Evidence that people's emotional responses to a situation track its objective features at least as well as their considered judgement does, across everyday cases, so that a check adds nothing.
