### Visual Containment Enables Pre-Taxonomic Grouping Before Formal Categorisation
Spatial chunking via visual containment (grouping ideas within rectangles on a canvas) allows loose conceptual grouping to exist before formal taxonomy is established — the group exists before its name or definition. This matches how conceptual synthesis actually proceeds in working memory: associative proximity first, categorical formalisation later.

#### Scope & Conditions
Applies during early-stage synthesis of unstructured material, before category labels or hierarchy have been decided.

#### Evidence
> "Visual containment using rectangles (spatial chunking) further reduces the threshold by allowing loose conceptual grouping before formal taxonomy is established. The group exists before its name or definition."

#### Implications
- Grouping fragments spatially before naming the group reduces the pressure to prematurely categorise during capture.
- This sequencing — associative-first, categorical-later — should be supported explicitly rather than forced into folder-first organisation.
