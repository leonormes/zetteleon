## The Process vs. The Pattern

The process is the temporal, causal sequence that brings a pattern into being, such as a bee adding wax cell by cell. The pattern is the finished static structure, such as the honeycomb, whose geometry can be analysed without reference to its making.

### Scope & Conditions

Holds for patterns with a physical origin (comb, crystal, plant). It separates genesis from existence and says nothing about whether the origin was designed.

### Evidence

> "The act of chipping away at the marble is a temporal process involving a sequence of causes and effects. But the finished statue of David is a static, spatial object whose form can be appreciated and measured in a single moment."
> (Gemini session, section The Process vs. The Pattern)

### Implications

- A temporal origin does not negate the existence of a static, mathematically describable form.
