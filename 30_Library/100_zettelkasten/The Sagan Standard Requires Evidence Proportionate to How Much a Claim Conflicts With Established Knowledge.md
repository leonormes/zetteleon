### The Sagan Standard Requires Evidence Proportionate to How Much a Claim Conflicts With Established Knowledge

The Sagan standard ("extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence") raises the evidential burden for claims that conflict substantially with ordinary experience or established knowledge; "extraordinary evidence" refers to the strength and quality of the evidence required, not to evidence that merely appears dramatic or unusual.

#### Scope & Conditions

A widely used principle of scientific scepticism; popularised by Carl Sagan but the underlying reasoning predates him (echoed in earlier work by David Hume and Marcello Truzzi on evidence for extraordinary events).

#### Evidence

> "The principle raises the evidential burden for claims that conflict substantially with ordinary experience or established knowledge... 'Extraordinary evidence' is not a precise measurement. The strength and quality of evidence matter more than whether evidence appears dramatic or unusual."

#### Implications

- A claim shouldn't be granted a lower evidential bar just because the claimed evidence feels dramatic or compelling in the moment — drama is not the same axis as strength.
