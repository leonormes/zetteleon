Advice that closes with "what's the smallest action you could take in the next five minutes?" asks the reader to supply, at the moment of need, exactly the executive resource that is depleted or dysregulated — this is the same failure point [[SoT - Prosthetic Executive Function]] identifies as architectural negligence: relying on internal memory or will at the point of performance.

The stronger design does not remove structure; it moves the decision earlier, to a moment when executive function is available, and lets the environment carry it afterward — a launchpad, a visual timer, a pre-written checklist. This does not contradict the case for small, immediate first actions (see [[SoT - Think Like a Man of Action, Act Like a Man of Thought]]'s Minimum Viable Action); it adds the condition that the *trigger* for that action should already be decided and externalised before the moment it is needed, not invented from willpower in the moment.
