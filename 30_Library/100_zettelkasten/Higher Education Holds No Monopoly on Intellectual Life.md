The declinist argument runs: humanities enrolments are collapsing, therefore intellectual life is dying. It contains a suppressed premise—that the university _is_ intellectual life—and that premise is historically false.

The three requirements for the practice are curiosity, time and access to material. Universities supply concentration and structure, which are real advantages. They do not supply the requirements themselves, and never held exclusive rights to them. An institution can decline without the practice it housed declining with it.

The corollary is uncomfortable in both directions: it defends the amateur, but it also removes the excuse that one cannot think seriously without institutional permission.

## Steelman

Universities supply things the list omits and which are difficult to self-source: sustained expert correction, a peer group operating at your level, and the enforced encounter with material you would not have chosen. The autodidact's freedom is also freedom from anyone competent telling them they are wrong, and the historical examples are survivorship-biased—we do not have records of the mutual improvement societies that produced nothing.
