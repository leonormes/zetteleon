## Mental Representations Take Multiple Forms

Pre-linguistic thought manifests across multiple representational formats—visual-spatial imagery, logical relations, embodied motor skills, and distributed neural activation patterns—rather than a single verbal medium. These mental models and schemas represent meaning independently of spoken or written words.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to human conceptual, spatial, and procedural cognition across both pre-verbal development and adult skill mastery. Does not deny that inner speech recruits linguistic symbols for sequential reasoning; it denies that language is the sole or primary substrate of thought.

### Evidence

This claim is derived from cognitive-science synthesis across the pre-linguistic cognition, embodied knowledge, and mental-model notes in the vault rather than a single external quotation.

### Implications

- Visual diagrams, spatial metaphors, and physical gestures can preserve structural aspects of a mental model that linear prose flattens.
- Skill acquisition and intuitive problem-solving rely on embodied and spatial schemas that often run ahead of verbal explanation.
- Translating an idea into words is a format conversion between heterogeneous internal representations and discrete linguistic symbols.
