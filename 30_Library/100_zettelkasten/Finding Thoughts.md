## What the Vault Already Answers

Your question had three parts. Each has an answer elsewhere in the graph.

### How Do I Find Things?

- [[Hub Notes Provide Entry Points to Idea Clusters]]—_Hub notes are the "main index" you came back through: lists of links into a developed line of thought._
- [[Keyword Index Provides Sparse Entry Points]]—_Luhmann kept the index sparse on purpose, one to four entries per keyword, so you enter the network at a few points and follow links._
- [[Semantic Search via Embeddings]]—_Finding by meaning when you cannot remember the words you used._
- [[Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) Grounds LLM Outputs in External Knowledge]]—_The retrieval side of an LLM helper: pulling the relevant notes into context before answering._

### How Do I Continue from here without Rewriting the Same Thought?

- [[Linking as a Redundancy Reduction Strategy in Zettelkasten]]—_One canonical atomic note per idea, then link to it instead of restating it. This is the direct answer to rewriting._
- [[Key questions when linking notes in the Zettelkasten method]]—_What is this part of, and what is it similar to or different from: the questions to ask at the moment of linking._
- [[Creating Meaningful Links]]—_Contextual, and other, link types, and why the reason for a link matters._
- [[Practice - Flat linking and tagging]]—_Store flat and link, which removes the "where does this go?" decision at capture._
- [[SoT - HEAD Note Contract (The Workbench)]]—_A thought you want to return to is an open question. Park it as a HEAD note, phrased as a question, where you will see it._
- [[SoT - Evolutionary Note System]]—_The merge protocol: ephemeral thinking is squashed into durable notes, so a thought grows in place._
- [[Create a 'Research Hole' Parking Lot]]—_A place to park a thread so it does not derail you and can be picked up later._
