## Cognitive Offloading Frees Mental Resources for Decision-Making and Problem-Solving

Offloading thoughts onto an external medium—paper, screen, or a structured note system—frees limited short-term mental resources that would otherwise be spent on retention. The brain's short-term capacity is bounded (approximately seven items in a seven-second window), meaning any information held internally competes directly with the capacity needed for reasoning, deciding, and problem-solving. Once externalised, the mind no longer needs to hold the content—only the knowledge that it has been captured.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies whenever a thinking task demands more concurrent items than working memory can hold. Most effective when the external medium is trusted, accessible, and persistent (per the Otto case in Extended Mind theory). The gain is proportional to the cognitive complexity of the task: trivial tasks benefit little; deep analysis benefits most.

### Evidence

> "By offloading thoughts onto a page or a screen, you free up valuable mental resources. This offloading allows your mind to focus on making decisions and solving problems rather than struggling to remember information."

### Implications

- Externalisation is a prerequisite for complex thinking that exceeds the natural attention window, not merely a convenience.
- Productivity gains from note-taking come from shifting mental duty from retention to processing—the system stores; the mind generates.
