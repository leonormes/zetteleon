## Heijo-shin Is a Candidate Counter-Measure to Emotional Dysregulation and Rejection Sensitivity in ADHD

The SoT proposes Heijo-shin as a direct counter-measure to emotional dysregulation and rejection sensitivity, and places it in the Engine domain of the ProdOS model. The logic is that dysregulation is a large swing away from baseline with a slow return, and Heijo-shin is the practice of not swinging. This is a hypothesis of the vault and has no clinical evidence behind it.

### Scope & Conditions

- The research description of ADHD emotion regulation is about inhibiting a strong reaction and then calming down, not about the first flash of feeling. A baseline practice can only be expected to shorten the swing and its consequences. It cannot be expected to prevent the automatic emotion, and the vault's own principle already says to manage the behaviour and not the feeling ([[MOC - Emotional Dysregulation]]).
- It is not a replacement for treatment. The source below reports that emotion regulation is an executive function weakened by ADHD and improved by medication.
- "RSD" is a popular term and not a formal diagnosis ([[Rejection Sensitivity Is an Established Research Construct; RSD Is a Popular Term Without Formal Diagnostic Status]]), so this note uses "rejection sensitivity" for the research construct.

### Evidence

> "Have trouble calming myself down once I am emotionally upset"
> "Remain emotional or upset longer than others"
> (Russell A. Barkley, *Taking Charge of Adult ADHD*, "Problem Area 5: Poor Emotional Self-Regulation", in "The Five Areas of Difficulty in Managing Daily Activities")

> "Emotion regulation is one of the executive functions weakened by ADHD and improved by medication treatment."
> (same book, "What Side Effects Can I Expect?")

> "The concept of Heijo-shin is a direct counter-measure to Emotional Dysregulation and RSD."
> ([[SoT - Miyamoto Musashi's Philosophy on Mastering Emotions]], §3 Integration with ProdOS Domains, emphasis markers omitted)

The first two quotations describe the problem. Only the third states the hypothesis, and it is the vault's own claim.

### Implications

- It is testable on oneself. Note the time to return to baseline after an upset, practise holding the baseline in ordinary moments for several weeks, and drop the practice if recovery time does not fall. The routine in [[Self-monitoring reveals personal energy and trigger patterns]] gives a way to record it.
- If it helps, it helps most where the cost of a spike is behavioural, such as a hasty reply after a perceived slight. That is the use case for [[How to Use the Pause to Separate the Signal From the Emotional Noise]].
