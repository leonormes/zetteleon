## Information Gathering Serves as Active Procrastination That Masks Avoidance of Execution Friction

System tweaking, shiny-object switching, and endless research function as active procrastination that substitutes low-friction discovery for execution. Because these behaviours feel productive, they mask avoidance of starting friction and keep competence trapped at surface familiarity rather than mastery. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to knowledge work and self-directed learning, and intensifies under ADHD dopamine-seeking and novelty-seeking patterns where discovery is immediately rewarding while processing or execution feels aversive. Three recurring patterns mark the trap:

- **System tweaking**: reorganising tools, folders, or reading lists instead of engaging with the material.
- **Shiny object syndrome**: abandoning a demanding deep dive for a newer, more stimulating topic.
- **Research as a planning substitute**: gathering additional data to delay the friction and judgement risk of starting.

It does not condemn bounded upfront research or deliberate tool maintenance when time-boxed to a specific execution question.

Grounded in vault behavioural synthesis extending [[The Collector's Fallacy Conflates Gathering with Assimilation]] and [[Information Addiction in Overthinkers]]; no primary-source quotation is cited here. ### Implications

Treating preparation and organisation as progress conceals stagnation and places a ceiling on skill development ([[Flawed Mental Models Limit Mastery]]). Breaking the loop requires hard research boundaries—such as [[Time-Boxing Research Prevents Productive Procrastination]]—and forcing an early transition from gathering to building.
