## Archery Shot Process - Nocking

Nocking is the initial step of placing the arrow securely on the string and the arrow rest prior to setting your hands.

### The Steps

1. Safety & Setup: When standing on the shooting line, stay in your lane. Keep your bow completely vertical and rest the lower limb gently against your inner calf to safely and stably nock an arrow.
   - _Purpose:_ Keeps the arrow pointing safely away from other archers and leaves the bow steady and hands-free while you nock. Inferred; the note gives no stated reason.
2. Rest Placement & Nocking Under the Bead: Ensure the arrow shaft rests cleanly on top of the arrow rest flipper (above the shelf) and the arrow nock clicks securely directly *under* the brass nocking indicator bead on the bowstring.
   - _Purpose:_ Nocking at the same point under the bead every time fixes the vertical launch angle (nocking above it causes high/low grouping). Resting the shaft on top of the flipper keeps it from being wedged against the shelf, which launches erratic flyers.
3. Fletching Orientation (Index Fletching Facing Outward): Orient the fletchings correctly. For a right-handed archer, place two hen fletchings pointing inward towards the bow window, and the single odd-coloured index (cock) vane pointing directly away from the riser (toward the archer) so clearance is maintained past the shelf and rest flipper.
   - _Purpose:_ The fletchings must clear the riser, shelf and rest on the way past. An index vane facing the riser strikes it on release and gives wild, unpredictable flight.

### Faults & Diagnostics

- Nocking Above the Nock Indicator: Snapping the arrow nock above rather than below the brass nocking indicator alters the vertical launch angle, causing high/low grouping inconsistency. Consistently place the arrow nock directly beneath the bead.
- Cock Fletching Facing Riser: Orienting the odd-coloured index vane inward toward the bow causes the vane to strike the riser upon release, producing wild and unpredictable arrow flight. Ensure the index fletching faces directly outward away from the riser.
- Arrow Trapped Between Rest and Shelf: Placing the arrow shaft below the wire/plastic rest flipper wedges the arrow against the riser shelf, creating severe clearance blockage and launching erratic flyers. Always ensure the arrow shaft rests cleanly on top of the arrow rest flipper above the shelf before drawing.
