## Ethical Intuitionism Reasons From Uncontroversial Concrete Cases to Controversial Ones, Not From Abstract Theory Downward

Common-sense ethical intuitionism starts moral reasoning from concrete examples where the intuitive verdict is uncontroversial, then draws analogies to controversial cases—rather than starting from an abstract theory or generalisation and accepting whatever conclusions it logically forces, even when they contradict strong intuitions.

### Scope & Conditions

A stated methodological alternative to top-down ethical theorising (e.g. strict utilitarianism); intuitions can vary between cultures and individuals, which limits how universal the "uncontroversial" starting cases really are.

### Evidence

> "Moral reasoning should begin with concrete examples where intuitive verdicts are uncontroversial, and then draw analogies to controversial cases. Starting with abstract generalisations often forces people to embrace false or absurd consequences to remain intellectually consistent."

### Implications

- When a moral theory's logical conclusion feels absurd, that clash is itself evidence against the theory on this method, not something to be explained away in the theory's favour.
