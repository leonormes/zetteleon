## Ship of Theseus as Model for Constructed Self

The Ship of Theseus illustrates how identity persists through the continuous replacement of parts when structural pattern and continuity of function remain intact. Applied to the person, the self is an evolving configuration of beliefs, habits, and commitments rather than a static set of original materials. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to philosophical accounts of personal continuity across belief revision, habit change, and role transitions.

### Evidence

Classical philosophical thought experiment applied as a foundational premise (`axiom: true`) in the vault's constructed-self argument thread; no external quotation note is attached (`evidence_links: []`).

### Implications

- Dissolves anxiety about inauthentic change by treating ongoing renovation of beliefs and habits as the normal condition of a living system.
- Shifts self-consistency from preserving past opinions to maintaining coherent functional commitments.
