## Human Scientific Frameworks Fail to Model Systems That Lack a Human-Comprehensible Purpose

Human scientific frameworks are structurally constrained by anthropocentric teleology, so they fail when modelling systems or artifacts that lack a human-comprehensible purpose or causal lineage. The source calls the extreme case the Cosmic Litter hypothesis, from Roadside Picnic.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to systems with no human-legible purpose, such as the Zone's artefacts or an incomprehensibly advanced AI black box. The source rates its own confidence at 8/10 but gives no basis for the rating, and the case rests on fiction, so treat the claim as a working hypothesis.

The source's counterargument: physical effects, such as gravitational anomalies, can still be isolated, measured and folded into an expanded standard model, whatever the artefact's purpose.

The source's falsification criterion: the claim fails if a non-human artifact or AI black box is reverse-engineered, with its mechanisms and not just its outputs explained, using only pre-existing human scientific paradigms.

### Evidence

> "Human scientific frameworks are structurally constrained by anthropocentric teleology; they fail when attempting to model systems or artifacts that lack a human-comprehensible purpose or causal lineage"
> (Pasted Zone claim cards, Card 1; originating tool and date unknown)

### Implications

- Measuring an effect is not the same as explaining the mechanism behind it.
- Black-box AI systems are a live test case for the claim.
