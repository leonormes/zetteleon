## Decoupling the Person from the Proposition

By separating a claim from the person making it (exteriorizing the claim), you can rigorously critique and dismantle a false or harmful idea without classifying the person as an inherent error.

### Scope & Conditions

Useful when dealing with ego-fused individuals whose beliefs are hardcoded into their sense of self, causing them to view disagreement as a personal attack.

### Evidence

> "Because the claim is exterior to the person, you can subject that specimen to absolute, rigorous destruction… without having to classify the person as an inherent error in reality."

#### Implications

- Shift language to exclude "you" and "your" when discussing ideas (e.g., "This specific policy seems to miss the underlying data").
- Maintain distress tolerance when the other person becomes defensive, allowing them to experience the friction of a stress-tested idea without absorbing their emotional output.
