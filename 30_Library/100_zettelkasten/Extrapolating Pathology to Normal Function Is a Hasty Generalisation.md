## Extrapolating Pathology to Normal Function Is a Hasty Generalisation

Summary: Inferring that an intact brain is "silently split" because a surgically severed brain shows dual processing ignores that the intact corpus callosum dynamically integrates hemispheric states, producing a unified property the disconnected system lacks.

Details: A severed system and an integrated system are different systems, not the same system with the connection hidden. The intact callosum continuously binds the hemispheres, so emergence applies: the integration of parts can yield a unity that the isolated parts do not possess. Reasoning from the broken case to the working case treats the connection as inert, when it is doing exactly the binding work whose loss produces the split-brain effects in the first place. The objection is sound as a caution, though it is aimed at a speculation Harris himself hedges rather than asserts.

### Evidence

> "Assuming that an intact brain is just a split-brain in disguise ignores the concept of _emergence_."

### Implications

- Divided-access findings in split-brain patients do not licence claims about ordinary experience.
- Integration is a functional process, not a removable veneer over two fixed agents.
