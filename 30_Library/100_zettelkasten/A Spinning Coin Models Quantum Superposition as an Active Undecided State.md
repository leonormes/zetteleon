## A Spinning Coin Models Quantum Superposition as an Active Undecided State

A coin at rest on a table is definitively heads or tails—classical physics: binary and settled. Flicked into a rapid spin on its edge, the coin is in neither state, and it is not simply alternating too fast to see; the spin is a distinct, dynamic state in its own right, containing the mathematical potential to become heads or tails without having committed to either. Slapping a hand down to stop it (the "measurement") does not uncover a pre-existing hidden state—it forces the coin to make a choice, collapsing the spin into one classical outcome. The interaction itself changes the coin's reality from a blur of potential into a fixed result.

### Scope & Conditions

A teaching analogy for quantum superposition and measurement-induced collapse, aimed at laypeople; not a claim about the coin's own physics being quantum.

### Implications

- Unlike the cat, the spin itself is visible—there is no false ignorance, only a directly observable state of genuine overlap.
- Measurement is depicted as a physical interaction that destroys the superposition, not an act of "looking" that reveals a secret.
