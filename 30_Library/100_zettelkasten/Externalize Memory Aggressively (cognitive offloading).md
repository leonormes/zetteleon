## Externalize Memory Aggressively (cognitive offloading)

Your mind generates; your system stores. Externalising memory aggressively means moving recall, plans, and open questions out of working memory and into a single trusted external system—such as a graph-linked vault or repository README-trails—at the moment of thought.

### Scope & Conditions

Applies whenever holding items in the head ("to remember later") imposes a working-memory cost. It requires three conditions: capturing at the moment of thought, consolidating into one trusted place so retrieval does not become another cognitive load, and storing state (decisions made, open questions, next steps) rather than isolated facts.

Storage is not understanding: offloading removes recall load but does not perform the cognitive work of connecting and digesting ideas ([[Digital Tools Cannot Replace the Cognitive Processes of Understanding and Connecting Ideas]]), and a system captured into without review becomes a source of guilt ([[Task Management Systems Have Limited Efficacy for ADHD Productivity]]).

### Evidence

This concept is derived from practical PKM and software-engineering workflows—daily scratchpads, permanent notes in Obsidian/Logseq, and repository README-trails—rather than a single external quotation.

### Implications

- Capturing at the moment of thought into a single trusted system eliminates both working-memory retention cost and the meta-load of remembering where a note was placed.
- Storing state (decisions, open questions, and next steps) turns every project or thread into a resumable ticket that can be picked up cold.
