Your mind generates; your system stores. [supports:: [[Cognitive Offloading Frees Mental Resources for Decision-Making and Problem-Solving]]]

- Use a single second brain: Obsidian or Logseq for graph-linked notes. Create a "Daily Scratchpad" for raw thoughts and a "Permanent Notes" area for distilled concepts with links. Use templates like "Definition / Example / Why it matters / Connections."
- For code and research, keep "README-trails" in each repo/folder explaining decisions, open questions, and next steps. Treat every idea as a ticket you can pick up later.

## What "Aggressively" Means

- **Capture at the moment of thought:** anything held in the head "to remember later" is a working-memory cost until it is written down.
- **One trusted place:** several partial systems each need checking, so the load returns as "where did I put it?".
- **Store state, not just facts:** decisions made, questions left open and the next step, so work can be resumed cold.

## Limits

- Storage is not understanding. Offloading removes recall load but does not do the work of connecting and digesting ideas; see [[Digital Tools Cannot Replace the Cognitive Processes of Understanding and Connecting Ideas]].
- A system that is captured into but never reviewed becomes another source of guilt. See [[Task Management Systems Have Limited Efficacy for ADHD Productivity]].
