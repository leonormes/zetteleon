## The Professional Treatment of Dissent Signals Whether a Consensus Is Genuine

Pay attention to how a community treats anomalies and the people who point them out. In a functional scientific consensus, a researcher who uncovers a mathematically or empirically verified flaw is rewarded with publication, grants, and career advancement—the system is structurally incentivised to find its own errors. In institutional groupthink, questioning the core premise is treated as a moral failing, and the dissenter is socially or professionally excommunicated rather than empirically refuted.

### Scope & Conditions

One of five diagnostics (with Atom 006–008, 010) for telling a legitimate scientific consensus apart from institutional groupthink.

### Evidence

### Implications

- Watch how a field's community treats the person raising an anomaly, not just the anomaly itself.
