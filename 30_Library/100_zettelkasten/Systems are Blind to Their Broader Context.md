## Systems are Blind to Their Broader Context

Any defined system—whether a biological cell, an organisation, or a belief framework—operates within a boundary that filters what it can perceive and process. Because its operational reality is confined to internal signals and rules, it cannot perceive the broader meta-system, external purposes, or alternative logics that lie outside its boundary. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

Applies to bounded, self-maintaining systems whose sensors and criteria of relevance are defined internally.

### Evidence

Analogical support from the library, not direct statements of the claim.

> "we are shielded from the lower levels, the neural tangle. Our thoughts seem to run about in their own space"
> (Hofstadter 1979, "CHAPTER XX: Strange Loops, Or Tangled Hierarchies", p. 691) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/1132/PDF)

> "once you hit your head against the ceiling like this, where you are prevented from jumping out of the system to a yet higher authority"
> (Hofstadter 1979, "CHAPTER XX: Strange Loops, Or Tangled Hierarchies", p. 691) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/1132/PDF)

> "Systems blindness is the main thing we struggle with in our work"
> (Daniel 2013, ch. 13, PDF p. 115, quoting John Sterman) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/697/EPUB?open_at=toc:13%20System%20Blindness)

### Implications

- Bounded systems must build self-referential rules because they cannot import external context.
- Actors inside a subsystem optimise locally, even when that harms the wider system.
- Testing a system's assumptions needs a view from outside its boundary.
