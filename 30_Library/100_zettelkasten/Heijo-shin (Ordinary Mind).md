## Heijo-shin (Ordinary Mind)

Heijo-shin is a state in which the mind stays as constant in a crisis as it is in everyday life. Emotional mastery on this view is the cultivation of that steady baseline and not the suppression of feeling, so nothing in the inner state marks the moment as special.

### Scope & Conditions

- The term (平常心, "ordinary mind") is common in Japanese martial arts and Zen. The SoT attributes it to Musashi. Whether he used the word himself has not been checked, and the library holds no copy of *The Book of Five Rings*.
- It describes constancy of baseline, not absence of feeling. Feeling is expected to arise. What is refused is a separate "crisis mode".
- It is distinct from its neighbours. [[Fudo The Path of Immovability]] is about a mind that does not stick, and [[Mushin No Shin Mind Without Mind]] is about fluent action with the self-commentary silent. Heijo-shin is about continuity of state across contexts.

### Evidence

> "Musashi's Emotional Mastery is not the suppression of feeling, but the cultivation of Heijo-shin (Ordinary Mind)—a state where the mind remains constant during the chaos of battle (or crisis) as it is in everyday life."
> ([[SoT - Miyamoto Musashi's Philosophy on Mastering Emotions]], §1 Definitive Statement)

This is the vault's own formulation. A primary-source quotation has not yet been captured.

### Implications

- It gives a simple test of the state: whether a person's inner state at a tea break and in a crisis differ. The argument for why they should not is in [[A Fighting Mind That Differs From the Daily Mind Adds Friction and Rigidity]].
- It frames emotional regulation as a baseline practice and not a crisis technique, which is the premise of [[Heijo-shin Is a Candidate Counter-Measure to Emotional Dysregulation and Rejection Sensitivity in ADHD]].
