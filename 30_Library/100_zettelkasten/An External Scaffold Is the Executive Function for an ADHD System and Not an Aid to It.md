## An External Scaffold Is the Executive Function for an ADHD System and Not an Aid to It

For an ADHD cognitive system the SoT says the external task scaffold does not help with working memory but is the working memory for that operation. On this strong reading the vault is the externalised prefrontal cortex, and removing the scaffold removes the faculty and does not merely make things harder. The reading rests on extended mind theory.

### Scope & Conditions

- It is the strong, constitutive reading. The weaker instrumental reading, in which a scaffold supports a faculty that remains in the head, is the more common one and is what [[SoT - Prosthetic Executive Function]] works with.
- The extended mind conditions matter. In the SoT's account an external resource counts as part of the mind only when it is constant, accessible and trusted, like Otto's notebook ([[SoT - The Extended Mind]]).
- The claim is about one operation at a time ("the working memory for that operation"), not about the whole person.
- It has a different epistemic status from a proof, and [[Cognitive Scaffolds Are Constitutive but Lack Formal Closure]] says why.

### Evidence

> "The external task scaffold IS the executive function for an ADHD cognitive system. The vault IS the externalised prefrontal cortex. The scaffold does not help with working memory—it is the working memory for that operation. Removing it does not make things harder; it removes the faculty."
> ([[SoT - Structure is Truth is a Unifying Axiom Across Formal Systems]], §2, Cognitive Scaffolding row, emphasis markers omitted and the link markup removed)

> "Elements of the world outside may effectively act as mental "extensions," allowing us to think in ways our brains could not manage on their own."
> (Annie Murphy Paul, *The Extended Mind*, describing Clark and Chalmers)

The SoT dates the Clark and Chalmers paper to 1998 and Paul's book gives 1995. Which date refers to which stage of the paper has not been checked.

### Implications

- If the scaffold is part of the cognitive system, losing access to it is a loss of capacity and not an inconvenience. That makes backup, availability and a single point of failure real design concerns for the vault (inferred).
- It explains why [[External Structure and ADHD]] and [[Discipline Is a Property of External Structure More Than of Will]] treat structure as a property of the system and not of will.
