## Treating a Virtue as Something You Do Rather Than Something You Are Makes It Easier to Resume After a Lapse

Treating a virtue such as compassion or mindfulness as something you do in each action, rather than as something you are, makes it easier to resume after a lapse. A lapse is then one missed action and not proof that you lack the quality. ^claim

### Scope & Conditions

- Applies to qualities that can be acted out again soon, such as patience, attention or compassion.
- It does not excuse the lapse. Repair may still be owed to the people affected.
- Zen is the background. The old version of this note credited the teaching of enlightened activity over enlightened people to Suzuki Roshi, which I have not verified.

### Evidence

> "The skill to practice is to get back on track if you mess up, and to not let it derail you."
> (Babauta 2023, "What Happens When You Fail", PDF p. 72) · [open in Calibre](calibre://view-book/GCcalibreBooks/741/EPUB?open_at=toc:What%20Happens%20When%20You%20Fail)

Babauta treats recovery as a skill to practise. The quotation does not compare doing with being, so the claim that the framing makes recovery easier is my own reasoning and is a hypothesis.

### Implications

- After a lapse, name the next action that expresses the quality and do it.
- State household or work standards as actions that can be seen and repeated.
- Avoid asking whether you are a patient person when the useful question is what a patient act is now.

### Falsifier

- Compare how soon people resume after a lapse when the same standard is framed as an action and as an identity. If the action framing brings no quicker or more frequent return, the claim is wrong.
