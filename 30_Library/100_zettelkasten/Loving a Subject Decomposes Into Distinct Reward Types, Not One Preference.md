## Loving a Subject Decomposes Into Distinct Reward Types, Not One Preference

"Loving" a subject such as mathematics is not one uniform preference; different people who enjoy the same subject are often drawn to distinct reward types within it—for example mastery/challenge, pattern discovery, rigour/certainty, aesthetic compression, explanatory power, abstract play, relief from social ambiguity, or belonging within a shared practice. Conversely, disliking the subject does not imply a lack of intelligence or curiosity—it may reflect a preference for different feedback (sensory, social, practical), open rather than formal criteria, or a [[A Competence Feedback Loop Turns Early Success Into Durable Interest|competence loop]] that started later or worse.

### Scope & Conditions

Illustrated with mathematics in the source; framed as generalisable to any subject.

### Evidence

> "Someone who dislikes maths may not lack intelligence, discipline, or curiosity. They may prefer: meaning expressed through story, people, lived experience, language, visual form, or physical action… immediate sensory, social, or practical feedback."

### Implications

- Two people who both "love maths" may be pursuing incompatible experiences within it (e.g. rigour vs. play) and can talk past each other about what the subject is "really" about.
- Useful diagnostic when an interest feels stalled: which specific reward type is currently missing, not just "am I still interested."
