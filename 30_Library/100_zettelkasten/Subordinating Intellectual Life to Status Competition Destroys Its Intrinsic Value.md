The corruption is not that credentialism makes intellectual work harder. It is that credentialism *changes what the activity is*. An activity whose value is intrinsic cannot be simultaneously held as a means to an external end — the instrumental frame displaces the intrinsic one rather than sitting beside it.

The observable signature is the achiever who reaches the terminal state and finds it empty. The telic goal was reached; the atelic good it displaced was never collected. This is why professional burnout is common precisely among people who succeeded at the thing they optimised for.

## Steelman

Plenty of people appear to hold both frames without evident damage — the academic who loves the work *and* wants tenure, the engineer who enjoys the problem *and* the salary. If mixed motives were genuinely self-destroying, we would expect near-universal burnout among professionals in intellectual fields, which is not what we observe. The displacement may be a matter of degree and dominance rather than a categorical corruption.
