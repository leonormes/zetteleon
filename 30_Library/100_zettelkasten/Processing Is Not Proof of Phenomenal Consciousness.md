## Processing Is Not Proof of Phenomenal Consciousness

Summary: That a hemisphere can process a cue and drive a response (access consciousness) does not establish that there is something it is like to be that hemisphere (phenomenal consciousness); inferring the second from the first is an equivocation.

Details: This is the strongest objection to Harris's leap from "the right hemisphere understands words and acts" to "the right hemisphere is independently conscious." Ned Block's distinction is the sharp tool: _access_ consciousness is information being available for reasoning and behaviour; _phenomenal_ consciousness is subjective feel. A neural network can select an egg without our having shown it hosts a separate bubble of experience. Harris's argument only goes through if processing entails subjectivity, and that is exactly the premise in dispute—which is why the empirical debate over whether split-brain patients are one subject with divided access, or two subjects, remains open.

### Evidence

> "Just because a neural network can process a visual cue and trigger a motor response … does not definitively prove it generates a separate, isolated bubble of subjective experience."

### Implications

- Behavioural independence is necessary but not sufficient evidence for a separate subject.
- Naming the access/phenomenal split makes the objection precise where "agency vs subjectivity" is vague.
