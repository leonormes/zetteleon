### Suggested Reading Order

Foundational → Applied → Advanced. This order builds intuition before formalism, then shows real-world application.

1. Numbers: A Very Short Introduction—Gentle entry point. Builds intuition for what primes are, why they matter, and how they're distributed. (~1 hour read)
2. The Music of the Primes—The narrative backbone. Du Sautoy's account of the Riemann Hypothesis gives the _why_ behind prime number theory. Read this to fall in love with the subject. (~8 hours)
3. Understanding Cryptography—First rigorous treatment. Paar & Pelzl build the number-theoretic machinery (Fermat, Euler, primality testing) with academic precision. (~15 hours)
4. Serious Cryptography—Applied modern cryptography. Aumasson shows how primes are _used_ in RSA, DH, and elliptic curves, with security-conscious implementation detail. (~12 hours)
5. Real-World Cryptography—Deployment perspective. Wong covers how prime-based crypto works in TLS, Signal, and other production systems. (~10 hours)
6. Bulletproof TLS and PKI—Protocol deep-dive. Ristić covers the full TLS handshake, certificate validation, and cipher suite selection where primes are the invisible foundation. (~20 hours)
7. Algebra-Driven Design—Abstract structures. Maguire's algebraic approach (groups, rings, fields) provides the formal framework underlying modular arithmetic. (~15 hours)
8. Category Theory for Sciences—Highest abstraction. Spivak's category theory provides the universal language for the algebraic structures that number theory builds on. (~20 hours)

### Open Questions

Gaps in the current library that would strengthen this research track:

- Analytic Number Theory—No dedicated text on the analytic methods (complex analysis, L-functions) used in modern prime number theory. _Recommended:_ Apostol's "Introduction to Analytic Number Theory".
- Computational Number Theory—No book focused on algorithms for prime generation, factoring, and primality testing at scale. _Recommended:_ Crandall & Pomerance's "Prime Numbers: A Computational Perspective".
- Algebraic Number Theory—No text covering algebraic extensions, ideal theory, and class field theory. _Recommended:_ Neukirch's "Algebraic Number Theory".
- The Langlands Programme—The deepest current research connecting number theory to representation theory is absent. _Recommended:_ Gelbart's "An Elementary Introduction to the Langlands Program".
- Quantum Computing Impact—No coverage of Shor's algorithm and its implications for prime-based cryptography. _Recommended:_ Supplement with Nielsen & Chuang, Chapter 5.
