## 1. The Mastery Algorithm: Strategic Planning

Before starting, implement **Metalearning**—the process of learning *how* to learn the specific topic.

- **Why:** Understand motivation (Instrumental vs. Intrinsic).
- **What:** Identify the core facts, procedures, and concepts (The Map).
- **How:** Identify the resources, common pitfalls, and expert workflows.

---

## 2. Core Pillars of Execution

### A. Directness (Learning by Doing)

Learning should happen in the context where the skill will actually be used.

- **Action:** Favor project-based learning and engaging with primary source material over passive theory.
- **Principle:** The strongest neural links are forged when the brain perceives the information as immediately necessary for action.

### B. Drill (Isolated Refinement)

Identify weak points and practice them in isolation to improve specific sub-skills.

- **Direct-then-Drill:** Alternate between broad project work and targeted drilling.
- **Magnifying Glass Method:** Spend disproportionate time on the most difficult 20% of the material.

### C. Feedback (The Reality Check)

Seek immediate, aggressive feedback. Use errors as diagnostic data to refine the mental model.

---

## 3. Achieving Intuition

Intuition is the **Proceduralization** of knowledge. It is developed not by remembering facts, but by solving varied problems and applying techniques like the **Feynman Technique**.

- **Goal:** To see underlying patterns instantly without using working memory.
- **Transition:** Move from "Thinking about the tool" to "Looking through the tool."

## 4. Summary Matrix

| Phase | Goal | Action |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| **Setup** | Metalearning | Research the "Why, What, How" of the domain. |
| **Engage** | Directness | Build a real project immediately. |
| **Refine** | Drill | Break the project down into sub-skills and drill. |
| **Solidify** | Retrieval | Force active recall and spaced repetition. |
