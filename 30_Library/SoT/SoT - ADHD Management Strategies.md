## 1. Tactical Principle: Systemic Accommodation

We do not "fix" the brain; we build **External Scaffolding** that accounts for its failures. The goal is to offload the "Manager" (Executive Function) to the environment, allowing the brain to be the "Artisan" (Execution).

---

## 2. Environmental Design: Systematized Calm

The core principle: **"Make good choices easy and bad choices hard."**

### 2.1 Spatial Zoning

- **Active Zones (Hot):** Items used daily must be visible and at waist-height. If it’s in a drawer, it doesn't exist.
- **Passive Zones (Cold):** Low-frequency items go in opaque, high-shelf storage to reduce visual noise.
- **The Analog Table:** A dedicated space with **zero** digital devices for creation mode.

### 2.2 Visual Triage (The 3-Box Rule)

To prevent decision paralysis when decluttering:

1. **Exit:** Bin/Recycle.
2. **Buffer:** The "Maybe" box. Seal it. If unopened in 6 months, bin it.
3. **Retain:** Find a permanent home now.

## 3. Initiation Tactics: Breaking Inertia

### 3.1 The Starter Task (Micro-Victories)

A Starter Task is an action so small it requires **zero cognitive effort**.

- **The Rule:** Must be < 5 minutes and purely physical.
- **Examples:** "Open the document," "Put on running shoes," "Clear one corner of the desk."
- **The Goal:** Overcome inertia. Once the "motion state" is entered, momentum takes over.

### 3.2 The 120-Second Commitment

If a task feels daunting, commit to working for exactly **2 minutes**. You are authorized to stop after 120 seconds. (Often, the "Brain Pain" of initiation dissipates once you start).

### 3.3 Body Doubling

Leverage the "Observer Effect." Working in the presence of someone else (physical or virtual like Focusmate) triggers a "Hive Switch" that overrides internal resistance.

## 4. The Dopamine Menu (Fueling)

To prevent defaulting to "Junk Dopamine" (scrolling), select from a pre-compiled menu:

| Course | Duration | Example |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| **Appetizers** | < 5m | 20 Jumping Jacks, cold water splash, blast one "Hype Track." |
| **Mains** | 30m+ | Coding a prototype, intense workout, deep-dive learning. |
| **Sides** | Concurrent | Brown noise, Lo-fi music, fidget toys, "Study with Me" videos. |
| **Intermezzos** | 5-10m | Staring at the ceiling, breathing, walking **without** a phone. |
| **Desserts** | Post-work | Time-boxed gaming or social media. |

---

## 5. The Four-Step Organization Framework

1. **Brain Dump (Externalize):** Immediately move all mental load to a physical/digital capture tool.
2. **Hierarchical Planning:**
    - **Yearly/Monthly:** High-level milestones.
    - **Weekly:** Synthesis of the dump into priorities.
    - **Daily:** Limit to **3 Main Priorities** to prevent burnout.
3. **External Cues:** Use "Cognitive Prosthetics" (sticky notes, phone alerts) that physically interrupt your environment.
4. **Performance Tracking:** Use habit trackers to replace "Subjective Assumptions" with "Objective Data."

---

## 6. Strategic Interventions

- **Input Bundling:** If a task is boring, provide 80% of the stimulation via audio (music/brown noise) to prevent the brain from seeking distraction.
- **Spite-Driven Development:** If self-care fails, reframe the task as **Rebellion**. "I will finish this just to prove it’s not as hard as my brain says it is."
- **Dopamine Sequencing:** Hard things first. Starting with high-stimulation (scrolling) spikes the baseline, making later work physically painful.
