## Friction and Activation Energy

Behavior follows the path of least resistance. To control behavior, we must manipulate the physics of **Friction**.

### 1. Activation Energy ($E_a$)

In chemistry, $E_a$ is the energy required to _start_ a reaction.

- **The Threshold:** If the Activation Energy of a habit is higher than your current Motivation ($E_a > M$), action will not occur.
- **The Strategy:** Since Motivation ($M$) is volatile (see [[SoT - The Hydraulic Model of Willpower]]), we must aggressively lower $E_a$.

### 2. The Garden Hose Analogy

Imagine a bent garden hose with a weak trickle of water.

- **Willpower Approach:** Crank up the valve (Force the water through the kink). High tension, risk of bursting.
- **System Approach:** Unbend the hose (Remove the friction). The water flows naturally.

### 3. Designing Friction Coefficients

We must inverse the friction for Good vs. Bad habits.

| Goal | Strategy | Example |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| **Stop Bad Habit** | **Increase $E_a$** (Add Friction) | Unplug TV; put batteries in another room. |
| **Start Good Habit** | **Decrease $E_a$** (Remove Friction) | Pre-chop veg on Sunday; Gym clothes ready. |

### 4. The 2-Minute Rule

A hack to overcome **Static Friction**.

- **Physics:** Moving a stationary object (Static Friction) takes more energy than keeping it moving (Kinetic Friction).
- **Rule:** Scale any habit down to < 2 minutes.
    - "Run a marathon" $\to$ "Put on shoes."
- **Effect:** Once you start (Momentum), the friction drops. "Action leads to motivation."
