## Minimum Viable Understanding (MVU)

Understanding is fundamentally pre-linguistic: it is rooted in embodied interaction with the world and exists independently of language. Language is not the _source_ or _container_ of understanding but a specialised cognitive tool for articulating, refining, stabilising, and communicating an experiential foundation that comes first. Words are external stimuli that trigger active interpretation based on pre-existing, experientially derived cognitive structures—"we can know more than we can tell" (Polanyi). This has direct, practical consequences for how you teach (action before explanation), how you check communication actually landed (simulate, don't just ask "do you understand?"), and how you evaluate what an LLM can and can't do (it manipulates the map, never the territory).

## Working Knowledge

### Pre-Linguistic Evidence

- Infant Cognition: pre-verbal infants demonstrate object permanence, causal reasoning, and categorisation (sorting sounds/faces) long before producing words.
- Animal Cognition: non-linguistic species exhibit complex navigation and problem-solving, indicating abstract representation without syntax.
- Skill Acquisition: a mechanic understands an engine's torque through their hands—via direct, repeated feedback—long before they could explain the physics formula for it. Embodied competence routinely runs ahead of the verbal account of it, not just in infancy but throughout skill learning.
- Conclusion: the "Language of Thought" is likely not propositional (Mentalese) but Embodied Know-How—dynamic, sensorimotor schemas derived from interaction.

### Language as a Tool, Not a Source

Language functions as a scaffold for the mind:

- Articulation: translates fuzzy, tacit experiential knowledge into explicit, stable forms.
- Stabilisation: a single word ("photosynthesis") anchors a complex biological process into one handleable mental object.
- Coordination: language aligns the conceptual maps of individuals. It does not transfer thought directly; it _orchestrates_ the reconstruction of thought in the listener's mind.
- The Translation Gap: because Speaker A translates their rich, pre-linguistic model into necessarily limited words, and Listener B reconstructs a _different_ model from those words based on their own differing experience, some degree of miscommunication is structurally built in—not just a failure of care or vocabulary.

### Linguistic Relativity (The Weak Sapir-Whorf)

Linguistic Determinism (language determines the limits of thought) is rejected in favour of Linguistic Relativity (language influences thought patterns):

- The Lens Effect: language provides specific tools that highlight certain aspects of reality (e.g. colour terms, tense markers).
- The Mechanism: language habits shape _attention_, not _capacity_. It influences how the continuous flow of experience gets carved up, but does not imprison the thinker inside it.

### Embodied Cognition, Enactivism, and Tacit Knowledge

- Understanding emerges from the dynamic coupling of an organism and its environment—a potential for situated action, not a passive representation in the head.
- The Grounding Problem: if understanding requires a body to ground symbols in reality, disembodied LLMs may be structurally incapable of genuine understanding. They manipulate linguistic representations of understanding, not the understanding itself.
- Because understanding is experiential, much of it remains Tacit—know-how that cannot be fully verbalised. Educational and AI systems that rely solely on explicit language will always be incomplete.

### The Role of Metaphor

Metaphor is the bridge between experience and abstraction. Conceptual Metaphor Theory (Lakoff & Johnson): abstract concepts (Target) are understood by mapping them onto concrete, embodied experiences (Source)—_Argument is War_ (Defend, Attack), _Time is Motion_ (Approaching, Flying), _Theories are Buildings_ (Foundations, Support). Metaphor allows pre-linguistic sensorimotor logic to structure high-level abstract reasoning.

## Current Understanding

### Implications for Teaching

Action First, Words Second: you cannot productively "explain" a complex system to someone until they have a sensorimotor hook for it. Lecturing on torque for an hour is weaker than handing someone a wrench, letting them struggle to turn a bolt, _then_ explaining torque—the explanation now has a pre-linguistic slot to fill instead of nothing to attach to. Inquiry-based learning works on the same mechanism: a question forces the brain to build that slot before the answer arrives.

### Implications for Communication

Because of the Translation Gap above, "Do you understand?" is a weak check—the listener will nod regardless, because the check itself stays purely linguistic and proves nothing about whether the reconstructed model matches. "What does this look like in practice?" is a stronger check, because it forces the listener to simulate the pre-linguistic reality rather than echo the words back. Metaphor works as a bridge for the same reason pedagogy does: it maps an unfamiliar abstract concept (internet traffic) onto a pre-linguistic schema the listener already has (water flowing through a pipe).

### Implications for AI

The Chinese Room Problem, restated through the Grounding Problem above: an LLM operates entirely on the "Map" level (language) with zero access to the "Territory" (pre-linguistic reality)—it can generate "the stove is hot" without having the neural pathway for pain or heat. Under this view, AI hallucination is better modelled as an error of _grounding_ than an error of _fact_: the system does not "know" anything in the experiential sense; it predicts the token sequence that co-occurs with the input.

## Tensions & Gaps

- The Weak Sapir-Whorf claim (language shapes attention, not capacity) sits in some tension with the stronger Grounding Problem claim (a disembodied system manipulating only language may be incapable of genuine understanding at all). The first treats language as a lens on an existing capacity; the second treats the absence of embodiment as a hard ceiling on that capacity. Not yet reconciled explicitly in this note.
- No worked criterion yet for when a system's output crosses from "correct token prediction that happens to be true" into "grounded understanding"—the AI implications above assert the distinction but don't operationalise a test for it.
