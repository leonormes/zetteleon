## 1. Definitive Statement

> [!definition] Definition
> Push-up performance is optimized by transitioning from a "loose" state to a **high-tension, stable structure**. Success is measured by mechanical efficiency and controlled progression (ROM, TUT, Overload) rather than high-repetition "grinding."

## 2. Mechanical Framework (The Setup)

To maximize power and protect the joints, adhere to the three-step stability protocol:

1. **External Rotation Torque:** "Screw" your hands into the floor. This generates torque that stabilizes the shoulder and elbow joints.
2. **Scapular Positioning:** Maintain **Scapular Depression** (pulling blades toward back pockets) and **Protraction** (pushing the floor away). This creates a stable platform for the humerus.
3. **Core Rigidity:** Treat the movement as a **Moving Plank**. Eliminate energy leaks by maintaining a stiff trunk; hips and shoulders must move as a single unit.

## 3. Progression Variables (Phased Overload)

Instead of just doing "more reps," increase intensity via these three variables:

- **Extended Range of Motion (ROM):** Elevate hands on plates/books to allow the chest to descend below the hands, strengthening pectorals in a lengthened state.
- **Tempo & Isometrics (TUT):** Use a slow eccentric (3s) and a pause at the bottom (5s) to build connective tissue resilience and technical mastery.
- **Mechanical Overload:** Add weight (10–25 lbs) placed on the **mid/lower back** (not upper back) to avoid blocking scapular movement.

## 4. The 12-Week Phased Programme

| Phase | Duration | Focus | Protocol |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |:--- |
| **Phase 1** | Weeks 1–2 | Technique | 8–10 reps; hands on high box/bench. |
| **Phase 2** | Weeks 3–4 | Strength/TUT | 4–6 reps; 3s eccentric + 5–10s pause; lower box. |
| **Phase 3** | Weeks 5–8 | Floor Integration | 5–8 reps; floor-based with controlled tempo. |
| **Phase 4** | Weeks 9–12 | Overload | 5–8 reps; floor-based with weight or extended ROM. |

## 5. Minimum Viable Understanding (MVU)

> [!check] The Golden Rule
> **Stiffness is Strength.** Screw your hands in, protract your shoulders, and move your body as a single rigid unit. Increase difficulty through **Depth** and **Tempo** before adding weight or reps.
