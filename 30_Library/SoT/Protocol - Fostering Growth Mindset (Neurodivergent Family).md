## Logic Map

- Objective: Create a "Counter-Cultural Bubble" within the family where process is valued over outcome, neutralizing the shame of neurodivergent struggles.
- Diagnosis: Perfectionism in neurodiversity is not ego; it is Armor. "If I am perfect, they cannot criticise my difference."
- Strategy: Deconstruct the parent's mindset first (Model), then scaffold the child's mindset (Mirror).

---

## Part 1: The Parent's Work (Internal Deconstruction)

### 1. Externalise "The Judge"

Give the critical voice a name (e.g., "Mr. PerfectPants").

- Action: When self-criticism arises, narrate: _"Ah, The Judge is here. Thanks for your input, but I've got this."_
- Why: Creates cognitive distance between _You_ and _The Thought_.

### 2. The "And" Shift

Replace "But" (invalidation) with "And" (co-existence).

- _Old:_ "She is great, but I am bad."
- _New:_ "I admire her skill, and I am proud of my effort."

### 3. Redefine "Best"

- Old Metric: Rank vs. Others.
- New Metric: Rank vs. Yesterday.
- Refame: When seeing success, ask: _"What is one small step I can take to move towards that?"_

---

## Part 2: The Parenting Work (External Modelling)

### 1. External Monologue (Narrate the Struggle)

Let Bessie see the messy process.

- _Struggle:_ "Urgh, this is frustrating! My brain is finding this tricky. I need a break."
- _Envy:_ "Wow, their garden is amazing. I feel a bit jealous, but mostly inspired."
- _Failure:_ "Oops, I burnt dinner. Learned that recipe isn't for me!"

### 2. Family Culture of Process

Shift the post-school interrogation from _Outcome_ to _Process_.

- Don't Ask: "What mark did you get?"
- Ask:
    - "What was the hardest part, and how did you handle it?"
    - "What mistake did you make that you learned from?"
    - "What interesting question did you ask?"

### 3. Deconstruct Comparison

- Trigger: "Leo is better at maths than me."
- Response:
    1. Validate: "It sounds like you're feeling frustrated."
    2. Redirect: "Forget Leo. Look at _your_ maths from last month. Look at that growth."

---

## Part 3: Practical Exercises

### 1. The "Power of Yet" Game

- Rule: If anyone says "I can't do X," someone else must add "…yet."
- _Bessie:_ "I can't do algebra." -> _Dad:_ "You can't do it _yet_."

### 2. The "Failure Autopsy"

- Concept: Treat failure as cold data, not hot shame.
- The Lab: Sit down with a notebook after a mistake.
    1. Hypothesis: What did I think would happen?
    2. Data: What actually happened?
    3. Variable: What _one thing_ do I change next time?

### 3. The 30-Day "Beginner" Challenge

- Activity: Pick a skill you are both objectively bad at (e.g., Drawing, Juggling).
- Commitment: 10 mins/day.
- Goal: Laugh at the clumsiness. Prove that "Talent" is just "Practice in disguise."
