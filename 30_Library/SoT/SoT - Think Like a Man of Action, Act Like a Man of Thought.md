## 1. Minimum Viable Understanding (MVU)

Thinking and Action must exist in a state of mutual refinement, not mutual exclusion. To avoid the traps of Executive Dysfunction (Paralysis by Analysis or Impulsive Reaction), we must decouple _Generation_ from _Organization_ and use physical constraints to force cognitive closure.

The goal is to move from "Thinking about the Problem" to "Building the Solution" through iterative, intentional cycles.

---

## 2. The Core Conflict

- The "Thinker" Trap: Falling into "Encyclopedia Building"—collecting ideas with no endpoint. Thinking becomes a sophisticated form of procrastination.
- The "Actor" Trap: Reacting impulsively without a plan, leading to wasted effort or "motion" rather than progress.

---

## 3. The Four Protocols

### A. Shrink the Thought (The Forcing Function)

Thinking must be decisive, not exploratory.

- The Question: "What is the _single, specific action_ this thinking is supposed to lead to?"
- The Effect: If you can't answer, you are procrastinating. Stop thinking and take any action.

### B. Define the Action (The MVA)

"Thoughtful Action" does not mean "Big Action." It means "Intentional Action."

- The Method: Identify the Minimum Viable Action (MVA).
  - _Not_: "Learn about nutrition."
  - _Is_: "Write 'Buy one book' on the grocery list."
- The Why: Small, binary actions bypass the "Wall of Awful" (initiation paralysis).

### C. Time-Bound Containers (The Constraint)

Thinking expands to fill the time available (Parkinson's Law).

- The Rule: 15 minutes to Plan -> 5 minutes to Do.
- The Hard Stop: When the timer rings, thinking ends. You must execute the best _current_ hypothesis.

### D. Act First, Then Think (The Feedback Loop)

Real-world data beats theoretical models.

- The Cycle: Guess -> Do -> Reflect.
- The Shift: Instead of thinking to find the _perfect_ action, take a _good-enough_ action and then "think" about the result. This turns "failure" into "data."
