# History of Mathematical Logic

The relationship between logic and mathematics has evolved over millennia, with key figures and movements shaping our modern understanding.

## 1. Antiquity to Enlightenment

- **Ancient Greece:** Aristotle formalised logic through his system of syllogisms, providing the first rigorous framework for deductive reasoning. Simultaneously, Euclid's *Elements* presented geometry as an axiomatic system, the first grand application of logic to a mathematical field.
- **Leibniz (17th Century):** Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz dreamt of a *characteristica universalis* (universal formal language) and a *calculus ratiocinator* (calculus of reasoning) to solve problems via mechanical computation—a direct precursor to modern mathematical logic.

## 2. The Foundational Crisis (19th - Early 20th Century)

The 19th century saw a "foundational crisis" leading to deep investigation of logical underpinnings.

### Logicism
*Spearheaded by Gottlob Frege, Bertrand Russell, Alfred North Whitehead.*
The thesis that mathematics is reducible to logic. Their *Principia Mathematica* attempted to derive all mathematics from logical axioms, famously taking hundreds of pages to prove $1 + 1 = 2$.

### Formalism
*Led by David Hilbert.*
Viewed mathematics as the manipulation of symbols according to specified formal rules, independent of meaning. **Hilbert's Programme** aimed to place all mathematics on a firm, consistent axiomatic foundation.

### Intuitionism
*Championed by L.E.J. Brouwer.*
Posits that mathematics is a creation of the human mind. A statement is only true if it can be mentally constructed. Notably rejects the **Law of the Excluded Middle** ($P \lor \neg P$).

## 3. Gödel's Incompleteness
In 1931, Kurt Gödel permanently altered the landscape:
1.  **First Incompleteness Theorem:** Any consistent formal system powerful enough for arithmetic contains true statements unprovable within the system.
2.  **Second Incompleteness Theorem:** Such a system cannot prove its own consistency.

This demonstrated that the goals of Logicism and Hilbert's Programme were unattainable in their original forms: mathematics is inherently incomplete.
