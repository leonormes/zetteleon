## Current Understanding

**Brain Dumping** is a cognitive offloading technique designed to counteract the high cognitive load associated with working memory deficits, a common challenge in ADHD. It involves the systematic transfer of all thoughts, tasks, ideas, and worries from the mind to a trusted external system. The primary goal is not organization, but the immediate freeing up of mental bandwidth.

### The Two-Phase Process

The technique's effectiveness lies in its strict separation of two phases: **Capture** and **Clarify**.

**Phase 1: Capture (The Dump)**
This phase is about raw, unfiltered offloading.
1. **Select a Single, Trusted System:** Choose one reliable place for the dump (e.g., a physical notebook, a specific digital note, a whiteboard). Using multiple systems can create anxiety about losing information.
2. **Schedule a Session:** Dedicate a short, recurring, and non-negotiable time block (e.g., 15 minutes daily) for the brain dump.
3. **Write Everything Down:** During the session, write down *everything* occupying mental space. This includes tasks, worries, ideas, reminders, and partial thoughts. **Crucially, do not filter, judge, or organize during this phase.**

**Phase 2: Clarify (The Sort)**
Only *after* the capture phase is complete should sorting begin.
1. **Process the Items:** Go through the raw list and decide what each item is and where it belongs.
2. **Distribute to appropriate systems:** Move each item into its correct place (e.g., a task on a project board, an appointment on a calendar, an idea in a "someday/maybe" list, a worry in a journal).

### Common Failure Modes

- **Organizing during the dump:** This adds friction, re-engages the executive functions you are trying to rest, and defeats the purpose of offloading.
- **Using multiple, untrusted systems:** This fragments attention and creates a new cognitive load of having to remember where things were captured.

### Theoretical Framework

Brain dumping is a practical application of several key cognitive principles:

- **[[Writing Creates Cognitive Distancing|Cognitive Offloading]]:** It is the physical act of moving thoughts from internal working memory to an external medium. This externalization frees up the brain's limited processing power to focus on higher-order thinking (analysis, synthesis) rather than simple storage and recall.
- **[[SoT - The Extended Mind|The Extended Mind]]:** The trusted external system becomes an extension of the mind itself. By reliably storing and organizing thoughts, the system acts as a "prosthetic prefrontal cortex," compensating for executive function and working memory limitations.

## Minimum Viable Understanding (MVU)

A **Brain Dump** is the act of writing down everything on your mind into a single, trusted place *without* organizing it. The goal is to clear your head. Only after everything is written down do you sort the items into tasks, appointments, or ideas. Organizing *while* dumping defeats the purpose.

## Tensions & Gaps

- How to choose the "right" external system (analog vs. digital) and avoid the trap of constantly switching tools.
- The optimal frequency and duration of brain dumping sessions for different contexts (e.g., daily vs. weekly, project-specific vs. general).
- Strategies for processing the output of a brain dump when feeling overwhelmed by the list itself.

## Working Knowledge

- The barrier to entry must be near zero. A simple text file or a blank piece of paper is often more effective than a complex app.
- The "trust" in the external system is paramount. You must be confident that what you write down will be reviewed and will not get lost.
- A brain dump is not a to-do list, though it contains many to-do items. It is the raw material *from which* to-do lists and other organized structures are created.
