## Minimum Viable Understanding (MVU)

Temporal Management is the discipline of treating time as a physical resource. It moves from "Feral" working (reactive, algorithm-led) to Intentional living by merging the To-Do List (What) with the Calendar (When). Success is defined by Intentionality over Productivity: choosing what to do with time to align with values, rather than merely "cramming in" more output.

## Working Knowledge

### 1. Structural Architecture (The Three Pillars)

_Source: Sarah Hart-Unger_

- The Master Calendar (Single SoT): A single, functional calendar for ALL areas of life (Work, Family, Personal). Acts as a hard boundary for capacity.
- Airtight Task Management (Pipelines): A system for 100% capture from all inputs (Email, Text, Verbal). Must be processed daily into a trusted vessel.
- Nested Goals (Horizons): A multi-level planning system spanning Yearly, Seasonal, Monthly, Weekly, and Daily horizons.

### 2. Time Blocking (The Architect) vs. Time Boxing (The Builder)

- Time Blocking: Reserving a period for an activity (Macro). Focuses on _when_ (Reservation).
- Time Boxing: Allocating a fixed period for a specific outcome (Micro). Focuses on _how long_ (Limitation/Parkinson's Law).

### 3. Seasonality (Quintile Planning)

- Divide the year into five 10-week "Quintiles" rather than quarters.
- Designate specific "Reflection Seasons" (e.g., Nov/Dec) for system resets.

## Current Understanding

### The Human in the Machine

Current synthesis integrates the Hart-Unger "Pillars" with the Newport "Slow Productivity" philosophy. The system is tool-agnostic:

- Analog Advantage: Tools like the Hobonichi Cousin are valued for tactile memory and forced intentionality.
- AI Stance: AI should handle the _administrative burden_ of scheduling (e.g., Motion), but the human must remain the Problem Definer, deciding priorities based on values and energy.
