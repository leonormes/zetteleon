## Minimum Viable Understanding (MVU)

Life did not begin with a "spark" but through a continuous process of Chemical Evolution. Before biology existed, natural selection acted on self-replicating molecules (like RNA). The transition to life occurred when these molecules were enclosed in a membrane (The Protocell), allowing for resource capture and the eventual emergence of the Last Universal Common Ancestor (LUCA).

---

## 1. Phase 1: Chemical Evolution (The RNA World)

Before cells, the "Law of Increasing Functional Information" acted on chemistry.

- The Mechanism: In the primordial soup, molecules competed for stability and building blocks.
- The RNA World Hypothesis: RNA likely came first because it can both _store information_ (like DNA) and _catalyze reactions_ (like Proteins).
- Selection: Molecules that replicated faster or were more stable outcompeted others. This was "Survival of the Fittest" at the molecular level.

---

## 2. Phase 2: The Protocell Transition (The Container)

The critical leap from Chemistry to Biology was the Membrane.

- Physics: Lipids naturally form spheres (micelles) in water due to hydrophobic/hydrophilic forces.
- The Function: By enclosing self-replicating RNA within a lipid membrane, the system gained a "Body."
    - It could concentrate resources (Energy efficiency).
    - It could protect its genetic code from the environment.
- The Result: Evolution accelerated. Selection shifted from "Stable Molecules" to "Efficient Organisms."

---

## 3. Phase 3: LUCA (The Root)

Billions of years of protocell competition led to the Last Universal Common Ancestor (LUCA).

- Definition: The single-celled organism from which all life on Earth (Bacteria, Archaea, Eukaryotes) descends.
- Significance: The "Spark of Life" was not an event but a 500-million-year process of increasing complexity driven by thermodynamic selection.
