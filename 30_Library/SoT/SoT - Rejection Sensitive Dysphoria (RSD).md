## 1. Definitive Statement

> [!definition] Definition
> **Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD)** is a condition characterized by extreme, overwhelming emotional pain in response to perceived or actual rejection, criticism, or failure. It is not merely sadness; it is a neurological hypersensitivity where social disapproval is processed as a physical threat.

---

## 2. Core Mechanisms

- **Prevalence:** Linked strongly to ADHD; some experts estimate up to 99% of ADHD adults experience RSD symptoms.
- **The Emotional Switch:** The brain's emotional regulation filter fails to dampen signals of social disapproval, resulting in "fast-building, high-intensity" emotional floods.
- **Overinterpretation:** Neutural or ambiguous social cues are frequently misinterpreted as overt rejection.

---

## 3. Behavioral Manifestations

- **Avoidance:** Refusing to share ideas or take risks to avoid the possibility of triggering the "dysphoric" state.
- **People Pleasing:** Adapting one's output or opinions to ensure they are "acceptable" to others, at the cost of authentic expression.
- **Social Withdrawal:** Retreating from communities or roles where failure is possible.

---

## 4. The Internal World as a Safe Haven

For those with RSD, the **Internal World** (see [[SoT - The Internal World and the Need for Validation]]) serves as a critical psychological sanctuary. It is a space where ideas can be explored and cherished without the immediate threat of external judgment.

**The Peril of Sharing:** The act of sharing these internally nurtured ideas is exceptionally high-stakes. The creator perceives the potential for criticism as an imminent trigger for unbearable emotional pain, leading to a "protectionist" stance towards their own thoughts.

---

## 5. Summary

RSD transforms the intellectual process of "feedback" into an emotional "crucible." Recognizing the neurological basis of this pain is the first step towards implementing defensive protocols (like private externalization) that build resilience without trauma.
