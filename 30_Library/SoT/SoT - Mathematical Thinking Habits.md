# Mathematical Thinking Habits

Learning mathematics as an adult is not about memorisation but about cultivating specific "habits of mind". Mathematics is an art form involving creativity, visualisation, and pattern recognition.

## 1. The 7 Habits of Mathematical Thinking

1.  **Pattern Detective:** Humans are wired for apophenia (pattern finding). Consciously observe logical structures in architecture, music, and nature.
2.  **Probability & Experimentation:** Move beyond "luck". Analyse games of chance and counter-intuitive statistics (e.g., Birthday Paradox, Monty Hall) to train probabilistic reasoning.
3.  **Mathematical Language:** Practice "translating" real-world problems into formal constraints. (e.g., Expressing a discount as $y = 0.8x$).
4.  **Tinkering (Decomposition):** Break complex problems into atomic parts. Do not just memorise formulas; deconstruct them to understand the "why" (e.g., deriving the distance formula from Pythagoras).
5.  **Algorithmic Thinking:** Formalise efficient procedures for daily tasks. An algorithm is simply a reliable set of steps to achieve an outcome.
6.  **Visualisation:** Externalise the internal. Use diagrams, sketches, and mental models to manipulate spatial and logical relationships before calculating.
7.  **Estimation (Number Sense):** Practice educated guessing. Use "friendly numbers" (multiples of 10) to approximate costs and values, developing an intuitive sense of scale.

## 2. Advice for Adult Learners

- **The "Why" over the "What":** Traditional education often fails by focusing on drill-and-kill. Focus on the *ideas* and definitions.
- **Embrace Struggle:** Confusion is the state of learning. Mathematicians spend most of their time stuck; the value is in the perseverance.
- **Play:** Engage with puzzles (Chess, Go, logic puzzles) to train deductive reasoning without the baggage of academic math syntax.
- **Resource:** *The Magical Maze* (Ian Stewart) – structured around puzzles to guide mathematical thinking.
