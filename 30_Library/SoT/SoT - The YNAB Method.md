## 1. Definitive Statement

> [!definition] Rule One: Give Every Dollar a Job
> The YNAB Method is a **Zero-Based Budgeting (ZBB)** system that mandates the strategic allocation of all funds currently possessed until the **Ready to Assign (RTA)** balance reaches zero.

---

## 2. The Five Guiding Questions

The Method is executed by answering five hierarchical questions whenever money arrives:

### 1. Reality (Immediate Obligations)

*Question: What does this money need to do before I’m paid again?*
- Focus: Rent/Mortgage, groceries, and bills due first. This grounding in present cash-on-hand eliminates "forecasting" errors.

### 2. Stability (True Expenses)

*Question: What larger, less frequent spending do I need to prepare for?*
- **Rule Two: Embrace Your True Expenses.** Treat large, irregular bills (Insurance, car repairs, holidays) as monthly expenses.
- **The Mechanism:** Divide the total cost by the months until due (e.g., £600 car insurance / 12 = £50/month).
- **The Result:** You smooth out cash flow spikes. A "surprise" bill becomes a boring, pre-funded transaction, preventing the slide back into debt.

### 3. Resilience (The Buffer)

*Question: What can I set aside for next month’s spending?*
- **Rule Four: Age Your Money.** The goal is to spend money earned at least **30 days ago**.
- **Breaking the Cycle:** This creates a temporal buffer between income and outgo. You stop timing bills to paydays because next month is already funded.
- **The Outcome:** Rule 4 is not an action; it is the **result** of consistently applying Rules 1, 2, and 3. It is the metric of true financial resilience.

### 4. Creation (Aspirations)

*Question: What goals, large or small, do I want to prioritize?*
- Focus: Aspirations beyond bills (Vacations, hobbies, treating the family). This prevents the "deprivation trap."

### 5. Flexibility (Adaptation)

*Question: What changes do I need to make, if any?*
- **Rule Three: Roll With the Punches.** Acknowledge that no budget is perfect. Moving money between categories is a sign of **successful engagement**, not failure.
- **Conscious Trade-offs:** When overspending occurs, you must immediately move money from a lower-priority category to cover it. This makes the **true cost** of the overspending visible (e.g., "This meal out cost me £50 from my holiday fund").
- **Psychological Resilience:** Flexibility prevents the guilt and abandonment that usually follow rigid, unrealistic plans. The budget adapts to real life in real-time.

---

## 3. Key Mechanics

- **Ready to Assign (RTA):** The pool of unallocated cash. Must always be zero.
- **True Expenses:** Smoothing irregular costs to normalize cash flow.
- **The WAM (Whack-a-Mole):** Moving money from a low-priority category to cover an overspent high-priority one.
- **The Payday Reset:** Re-evaluating all jobs whenever new income hits the account.

## 5. Advanced Architecture & Modeling

For power users, the system evolves from simple tracking to **Financial Modeling**.

### 5.1 Asset & Liability Architecture

- **Tracking Accounts:** Integrate 401ks, IRAs, and HSAs as "Asset Tracking" accounts. This provides a "Net Worth" view without allowing market volatility to affect your liquid budget.
- **Liability Sync:** Connect Mortgages and Loans directly. This unlocks the **Debt Payment Target** type and **Payoff Simulator**, allowing you to model how extra payments impact interest savings and time.

### 5.2 Refill Behaviours

- **Refill Up To:** Caps a category (e.g., Groceries) at a specific amount. If you have £50 leftover, the system only asks for enough to reach the cap, optimizing cash flow efficiency.
- **Snoozing:** Temporarily deactivates a target's requirements for the current month (e.g., if a bill was paid mid-month during setup) without deleting the underlying logic.

### 5.3 High-Level Metrics

- **"Cost to be Me":** The aggregate sum of all monthly targets. It serves as a structural "gut check" against expected income.
- **The Margin:** The delta between "Cost to be Me" and Total Income. A healthy model requires a positive margin to absorb volatility.

### 5.4 ADHD Integration (Cognitive Support)

YNAB serves as a "Money Timer" for the neurodivergent brain, providing:

- **Concreteness:** Replaces "amorphous blob" forecasting with "Cash-on-Hand" reality.
- **The ADHD Tax Category:** Explicitly budgeting for inevitable slips (late fees, forgotten lunches) to remove shame and build resilience.
- **Visual Widgets:** Placing category balances on the home screen provides awareness without requiring the executive function of opening the app.

## 6. Case Study: Values-Based Taxonomy (Ashley L's Protocol)

*Source: Budget Nerds - Ashley L Case Study*

This framework shifts the budget from a **Functional List** (e.g., "Bills") to a **Values Hierarchy**, reframing spending as Identity Construction rather than Resource Depletion.

### 6.1 Intent-Based Categorization

- **Concept:** Group categories by *Why* you spend, not *Where* you spend.
    - **"Well-being":** Replaces "Groceries/Dining." Links food quality to health outcomes.
    - **"Adventure":** Replaces "Travel." Operationalizes specific experiences (e.g., "Tour de France").
    - **"Growth":** dedicated allocation for skills (e.g., French lessons).

### 6.2 The "Novelty Rule" (Time Perception)

- **The Theory:** Routine accelerates perceived time; novelty expands it.
- **The Category:** **"Side Quests."** A funded bucket for low-friction spontaneity (e.g., baking a complex cake, taking a random class).
- **Mechanism:** Removes the friction of "finding money" for new memories, effectively slowing down the perception of time passing.

### 6.3 Granularity as Control

- **Home Ecosystem:** Split into **Maintenance** (Repairs), **Improvement** (Capital Upgrades), and **Decor** (Vibes). This prevents aesthetic desires from cannibalizing maintenance funds.
- **Liability-to-Asset Conversion:** The "Car Payment to Self." Continuing the monthly outflow after the debt is cleared, diverting it into a "Next Car" bucket.
