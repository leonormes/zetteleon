## 1. Comparative Analysis

Different budgeting methods optimize for different needs (e.g., simplicity vs. control).

| Method | Primary Logic | Ideal For | Key Difference from ZBB |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |:--- |
| **Zero-Based (ZBB)** | Income - Outgo = 0. All dollars assigned before spending. | Maximum control; variable income. | Requires justification of *every* pound from scratch. |
| **50/30/20 Rule** | Fixed % split: 50% Needs, 30% Wants, 20% Savings. | Beginners; high-level guidance. | Coarse-grained; does not track individual pounds. |
| **Envelope System** | Physical/Digital cash containers for categories. | Curbing impulse spending. | More rigid; usually focused only on variable costs. |
| **Pay-Yourself-First** | Saving/Investing is automated first; spend the rest. | Passive savers with stable income. | Savings-first rather than holistic allocation. |
| **Incremental** | Tweak last month's numbers by a small %. | Minimal maintenance. | Assumes past spending was justified; prone to inefficiency. |

---

## 2. Methodology Summaries

### A. The Envelope System

Tangible and immediate. When the envelope is empty, spending stops. Digital versions (like YNAB) allow this logic to work with credit cards and bank transfers.

### B. The 50/30/20 Rule

A high-level guideline. It is simpler to maintain than ZBB but provides less visibility into *where* overspending is occurring within the broad "Wants" or "Needs" categories.

### C. Traditional (Static) Budgeting

Often based on **Forecasting** (predicting future income) rather than **Allocation** (assigning current cash). This frequently leads to "phantom money" errors where you budget money you don't actually have yet.

## 3. Selecting a Method

- **Choose ZBB (YNAB)** if you need to break poor habits or navigate a complex household with many moving parts.
- **Choose PYF** if you are already meeting your goals and want to reduce time spent on administrative tasks.
- **Choose 50/30/20** for a "Stress Test" to see if your fixed costs (Needs) are too high relative to your income.
