## 1. The Seven Pillars of Primal Movement

These seven foundational patterns engage every major joint and form the "alphabet" of human movement.

- [[SoT - Primal Movement - Squat|Squat]]: Bilateral knee dominance (e.g., Deep Squat).

## 2. Core Concept: Isometrics (\"Root Strength\")

Isometrics—creating tension without movement—are essential for joint stability and tendon health. This \"quiet\" strength is uncomfortable because it exposes weaknesses that momentum often hides.

### The Mechanism: Engine vs. Chassis

- **The Analogy:** Think of muscles as the **Engine** and tendons/joints as the **Chassis**. Dynamic exercise increases horsepower. However, a high-horsepower engine in a loose, rusty chassis will rattle the car apart. Isometrics act as the \"welding,\" tightening the bolts of the chassis to handle the force the engine produces.
- **End-Range Strengthening:** The body is weakest at the outer limits of mobility (e.g., the bottom of a squat or end-range of a rotation). Spending time under tension in these positions builds resilience where injury is most likely to occur.
- **Tendon Health & Rehab:** Tendons (collagen-based) resist compression and tension differently than muscle. Isometrics allow for safe, progressive loading of connective tissue without the aggravation of repetitive movement, making them the primary tool for treating tendonitis.

### Key Isometric Standards

- **The Hold:** Baseline of **20 seconds** for **3 sets**. Progress via duration (30-60s) rather than volume.
- **Breathing Mechanism:** Avoid the **Valsalva manoeuvre** (holding breath). Inhale before the hold; maintain controlled exhales during the contraction to manage blood pressure.
- **Alignment Logic:** Neutral spine is paramount. Tension must be generated without compromising structure (e.g., arching the back).

### Primary Exercises

- **The Dead Hang:** Decompresses the spine and builds grip endurance.
- **The Horse Stance:** Builds \"Root Strength\" in hips and knees without impact.
- **The L-Sit:** Develops \"Straight-Arm Strength,\" conditioning connective tissue.

## 3. Core Concept: Alignment & "Energy Leaks"

Propper alignment is not aesthetic; it is mechanical efficiency.

- **The Energy Leak:** Misalignment causes force to bypass muscles and dump into joints/tendons (e.g., knee valgus).
- **Dynamic Alignment:** The goal is maintaining structure *in motion*.
    - *Shoulders:* "Packed" (depressed and retracted) during crawling.
    - *Spine:* Thoracic extension to counter "Tech Neck."

## 4. Methodology: The Animal Flow System

A structured approach to ground-based movement, divided into six components.

- **Daily Implementation:** [[SoT - Protocol - 5-Minute Primal Activation]] (Full body activation).

### The "ABC" Forms

- **Ape:** Deep squat pattern (Lateral movement, Hip opening).
- **Beast:** Prone (face down) quadrupedal pattern (Anterior chain, Loaded shoulders).
- **Crab:** Supine (face up) quadrupedal pattern (Posterior chain, Chest opening). See **[[SoT - Primal Movement - Crab]]** for technical setup.

### The Six Components

1. **Mobilisations:** Wrist prep (Hardware check).
2. **Activations:** Static holds (Static Beast/Crab) to "bring the system online."
3. **Form Specific Stretches:** Dynamic mobility starting from base forms (Ape Reach, Scorpion Reach).
4. **Traveling Forms:** Locomotion patterns (Traveling Beast, Lateral Ape).
5. **Switches & Transitions:** The "Connectors" (Underswitch, Side Kickthrough) that link forms.
6. **Flow:** The fluid sequence of linking all components (Energy Transfer).

## 5. The Underswitch Algorithm

The fundamental transition unit of the system.

- **Function:** Transfers the body between Anterior (Beast) and Posterior (Crab) base structures.
- **Biomechanical Logic:**
    - **Axis:** Rotation occurs on the *ball* of the foot and opposing hand.
    - **Path:** The leg travels *under* the body (Low Centre of Mass).
    - **Safety:** Hips must stay low (below knee height) to protect the lumbar spine.
