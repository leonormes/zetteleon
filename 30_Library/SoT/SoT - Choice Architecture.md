# Choice Architecture

We are cognitive misers. We do not "choose" based on deep preference; we choose based on what is available and obvious. The architect of the choices controls the behavior.

## 1. The Massachusetts General Hospital Study
Anne Thorndike proved that changing the layout changes the consumption, without changing the "will" of the consumer.
- **Intervention:** She rearranged the cafeteria.
    - Added water bottles to soda fridges.
    - Placed water baskets at all food stations.
    - *Did not remove soda.*
- **Result:** Soda sales dropped 11.4%; Water sales increased 25.8%.
- **Insight:** People didn't "decide" to be healthy. They just grabbed what was closest.

## 2. Designing the Visual Field
The visual cortex dominates human processing. **"What you see is what you do."**

### A. Make It Obvious (The Open Valve)
To encourage a habit, place the cue in the direct line of sight.
- *Guitar:* On a stand in the living room (not in a case).
- *Vitamins:* Next to the coffee maker (Habit Stacking).

### B. Make It Invisible (The Closed Valve)
To break a habit, remove the cue from the visual field.
- *Phone:* Leave it in another room.
- *Junk Food:* Opaque jar on a high shelf.

**Rule:** If the "Childish Mind" has to search for the distraction, it will often give up. If the distraction is visible, it will consume it.
