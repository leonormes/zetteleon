# SoT - The Philosophy of Mathematics Education

> **The Core Thesis:** Mathematics is an art form—the art of explanation. The current education system often teaches the *tools* (notation, formulas) while withholding the *subject* (patterns, logic, beauty), leading to the "Maths Brain" myth.

## 1. The Two Types of Knowledge

### A. Applicative Knowledge (The "What")
*   **Definition:** Knowing how to apply an existing rule to a known problem.
*   **Example:** "Calculate the area of this triangle using $A=1/2bh$. "
*   **School Focus:** 90% of the curriculum. It treats students as computers executing code.

### B. Generative Knowledge (The "Why" & "How")
*   **Definition:** The ability to observe a pattern, abstract it, and *create* a new rule to describe it.
*   **Example:** "Why does a triangle take up half a rectangle? Can we prove it?"
*   **Real Maths:** This is the creative engine. It involves setting parameters, testing hypotheses, and crafting a logical narrative.

## 2. The "Maths Brain" Myth
*   **Origin:** A cultural belief that mathematical ability is innate and binary (you have it or you don't).
*   **Reality:** Mathematical thinking is a skill built through **Neuroplasticity**.
*   **The Damage:** The system rewards "fast calculators" and penalizes "deep thinkers," leading creative children to believe they are "bad at maths."

## 3. Key Mental Models for Teaching

### A. The "Toolbox" Analogy
*   **Concept:** Arithmetic skills are just tools (saws, hammers).
*   **Goal:** You don't learn to saw just to saw; you learn it to build furniture. We learn algebra to build models of the universe.

### B. The "Two Meanings of Equals"
*   **Operational (Primary School):** `=` means "The answer is..." (Left-to-Right command).
*   **Relational (Algebra):** `=` means "Is the same value as" (Balance).
*   **Fix:** Teach equivalence early. $4+4 = 5+3$.

### C. Scaling (The Unifying Theory of Multiplication)
*   **Old Model:** Repeated Addition (Breaks with fractions).
*   **New Model:** Scaling.
    *   $	imes 2$ = Zoom in 200%.
    *   $	imes 0.5$ = Zoom out 50%.
    *   This intuitively explains why multiplying by a fraction makes a number smaller.

## 4. Paul Lockhart's "Lament"
*   **The Analogy:** Imagine teaching music where children spend 10 years copying sheet music but never hear a song or touch an instrument. That is modern maths education.
*   **The Antidote:** Play. Let children create their own imaginary worlds (e.g., "What if lines could never cross?") and see what happens.
