## Commitment Devices (Ulysses Pacts)

A Commitment Device is a mechanism that locks in a future action while the user is in a state of rationality, preventing the future "Childish Mind" from sabotaging the plan.

### 1. The Myth of Ulysses

Ulysses wanted to hear the Sirens but knew they would lure him to his death.

- The Solution: He tied himself to the mast and plugged his crew's ears with wax.
- The Logic: He created a system where he _could not_ fail, regardless of how much he "wanted" to later.

### 2. Bridging the Reliability Gap

The "Adult Mind" (Rational) is often present _now_ but knows the "Childish Mind" (Irrational) will take over _later_ (when tired/stressed).

- Strategy: The Adult Mind makes a binding decision that the Childish Mind cannot undo.

### 3. Types of Pacts

1. Hard Constraint (The Wall): Impossible to bypass.
    - _Example:_ Internet blocker (Freedom.to) that cannot be disabled.
    - _Example:_ Automatic savings transfer (money is gone before you see it).
2. Price Constraint (The Sting): Failure is expensive.
    - _Example:_ Beeminder (charges your credit card if you miss a goal).
    - _Example:_ Giving a friend $100 to keep if you don't go to the gym.
3. Social Constraint (The Shame): Public declaration.
    - _Example:_ Posting a goal on social media.

Core Axiom: If you trust your future self to "do the right thing," you have already failed. Lock the door while you still have the key.
