## 1. Definitive Statement

> [!definition] Definition
> The **Bayesian Brain** hypothesis posits that the brain is essentially a **Prediction Machine**. It does not passively record sensory data; it actively generates a top-down "fantasy" of the world and uses bottom-up sensory input only to correct errors in that prediction.

---

## 2. The Prediction-Update Mechanism

The brain operates in a perpetual feedback loop:

1. **Prediction:** The brain generates an internal simulation of what it expects to see, hear, and feel based on its current mental model.
2. **Sensory Input:** The senses deliver actual information from the world.
3. **Error Signal (Prediction Error):** The brain compares its prediction to the reality. The difference is the "Prediction Error."
4. **Model Update:** If the error is large (surprise), the brain is forced to update its internal model to account for the new data.

---

## 3. Dopamine: The "Update Your Model" Signal

Dopamine is the primary biochemical messenger for **Salience** and **Prediction Error**.

- **Positive Prediction Error:** When something unexpected and potentially good or interesting happens, midbrain dopamine neurons fire.
- **The Reward:** This dopamine surge feels good, reinforcing the behavior that led to the discovery.
- **Enhanced Learning:** The surge flags the event as significant, promoting synaptic plasticity and "stamping in" the new information.

---

## 4. The ADHD Connection: Novelty as Self-Medication

The leading theory of ADHD involves **Dopamine Dysregulation**, particularly in the prefrontal cortex.

- **Under-arousal:** Routine, predictable tasks provide zero prediction error and thus zero dopamine, causing the brain to "shut down" or seek distraction.
- **Novelty Seeking:** The ADHD brain's love for the "quirky" and "unexpected" is a neurobiological strategy. **High Prediction Error = High Dopamine.**
- **The Goal:** Seeking novelty is an attempt to generate the dopamine necessary to bring the brain to an optimal state of arousal and focus.

---

## 5. Summary

We are only consciously alerted when reality *doesn't* match our model. Understanding this allows us to leverage high-prediction-error environments (Novelty, Challenge) to regulate focus and drive learning.
