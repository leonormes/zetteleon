## Minimum Viable Understanding (MVU)

The earlier notes ask what knowledge is. This one asks what to do with the answer: how to build an argument for a belief, how strongly to hold it, and how to disagree without it becoming personal. The vault has a set of rules of thumb for this, called razors, and a set of notes on holding beliefs as revisable hypotheses. Several of them have recorded limits or rivals, so they are best used as prompts rather than rules.

## 1. A Working Sequence for Building an Argument

This sequence is a synthesis of the linked notes, and the order is inferred.

1. State the claim as a proposition, because rightness and wrongness belong to propositions and not persons ([[Truth-Status Belongs to Propositions, Not Persons]]).
2. Decide whether it is worth arguing at all, and in which sense ([[Alder's Razor Restricts Worthwhile Debate to Testable Claims, But Over-Applies as an Absolute Rule]], [[Worth Debating Splits Into Factual Resolution and Relational or Philosophical Value, and Conflating Them Causes Confusion]]).
3. Say what would count against it ([[Falsifiability Distinguishes Science from Dogma]]).
4. Supply the evidence yourself, because the claimant carries the burden ([[Hitchens Razor Places the Burden of Proof on the Claimant, Not the Sceptic]]).
5. Scale the evidence to how far the claim departs from what is already established ([[The Sagan Standard Requires Evidence Proportionate to How Much a Claim Conflicts With Established Knowledge]]).
6. List the rivals that fit the same evidence, then prefer the one with fewer assumptions ([[Underdetermination Means Multiple Theories Can Fit the Same Evidence]], [[Occam's Razor Favours Fewer Assumptions Among Equally Explanatory Theories, Not Simplicity Itself]]).
7. Hold it at a graded confidence and update as evidence arrives ([[Probabilistic Thinking Treats Beliefs as Hypotheses With Confidence Levels]], [[Bayesian Updating Adjusts Beliefs as New Evidence Arrives]]).

## 2. The Razors and Their Limits

| Rule | Claim | Recorded limit or rival |
|---|---|---|
| Burden of proof | [[Hitchens Razor Places the Burden of Proof on the Claimant, Not the Sceptic]]: dismissing a claim made without evidence means not accepted, not proven false. | The burden rule does not say how strong the evidence must be. [[The Sagan Standard Requires Evidence Proportionate to How Much a Claim Conflicts With Established Knowledge]] supplies that. |
| Parsimony | [[Occam's Razor Favours Fewer Assumptions Among Equally Explanatory Theories, Not Simplicity Itself]]: a tie-breaker among theories that explain equally well. | It does not say the simplest explanation is true. |
| Testability | [[Alder's Razor Restricts Worthwhile Debate to Testable Claims, But Over-Applies as an Absolute Rule]]: untestable claims are not worth debating. | Too restrictive as an absolute rule, and [[Worth Debating Splits Into Factual Resolution and Relational or Philosophical Value, and Conflating Them Causes Confusion]] separates the two senses of worth. |

### Negatives, Undetectable Claims and Exposure (added 2026-10-23)

- [[Absence of Evidence Counts as Evidence of Absence in Proportion to the Evidence Expected]]—_you can justify disbelief where evidence was expected and absent._
- [[A Hypothesis Built to Be Undetectable Gives Nothing to Assess, so Belief Should Be Withheld]]—_where nothing could appear, withhold instead of disbelieving._
- [[A Test Counts Only If It Could Have Failed, so Survival of Criticism Must Be Checkable by Others]]—_"it survived criticism" needs a test that could have failed._
- [[Fallibilism Holds That Knowledge Requires Good Justification, Not Certainty]]—_the standard all of the above are held to._

## 3. Holding Beliefs

- [[Belief Between Fact and Faith]]—_a belief is a provisional working model, between verifiable fact and faith that needs no evidence._
- [[Probabilistic Thinking Treats Beliefs as Hypotheses With Confidence Levels]]—_action proportional to confidence and stakes._
- [[Strong Opinions, Loosely Held Balances Conviction and Humility]]—_conviction with a willingness to revise._
- [[Preferring Science Is Itself a Falsifiable, Evidence-Based Belief, Not a Dogmatic One]]—_even the preference for evidence is held as revisable._
- [[Locating Truth-Status in Persons Is What Wounds, Not Believing in Truth]]—_the damage comes from judging persons, not from caring about truth._
- [[Multiplicity Is a Stage to Pass Through, Not a Destination]]—_from "one truth", through "no right or wrong", to commitment held fallibly._
- [[The Realization that Knowledge is Contingent Can Lead to a Crisis of Certainty]] and [[The World's Ambiguity Drives the Need for Certainty]]—_why the process feels unsettling, and why people grab for certainty anyway._

## 4. Counter-claims Within This Set

| Claim | Counter-claim |
|---|---|
| [[Reason as Commitment to Objective Standards]]: anyone who says their answers are justified has committed to objective standards. | [[Beliefs as Defining Spaces]]: the useful question is not whether a belief is objectively true but whether it expands or contracts the life it creates. |
| [[Confirmation as a Perceived Shortcut to Truth]]: validation by others feels like truth and may not be. | [[Reality Testing]]: the external world, including other people, is how we check our thoughts. |
| [[Clifford Held That It Is Always Wrong to Believe on Insufficient Evidence]]: no exceptions. | [[James Argued That Live Forced Choices Permit Belief Beyond the Evidence]]: some live, forced choices permit belief beyond it. |

The first pair is the same disagreement as truth against utility in [[SoT - What Knowledge Is]]. (inferred) The Clifford and James pair is secondhand, from an AI chat, with only the years 1877 and 1896 recorded.
