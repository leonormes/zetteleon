## Minimum Viable Understanding (MVU)

Mathematical thinking is a methodology for problem-solving that extends beyond computation. It involves adopting a specific mindset: identifying underlying patterns, stripping problems to their structural essence, applying logic and abstraction, and embracing failure as an inherent part of the discovery process.

## Working Knowledge

- The Three Stages of Mathematical Rigour (Terence Tao's framework):
  1. _Pre-rigorous_: Intuitive, informal, pattern-based (early education).
  2. _Rigorous_: Strict rules, formal proofs, learning the mechanics (undergraduate level).
  3. _Post-rigorous_: Professional level where fluid intuition is restored, but backed by the ability to seamlessly translate ideas into rigorous proofs at will.
- Core Cognitive Strategies:
  - _Pattern Detection_: Finding structural geometries or sequences in nature and daily life.
  - _Deconstruction & Tinkering_: Breaking down complex problems, asking "what if?", and randomly combining concepts until illumination occurs.
  - _Visualisation & Abstraction_: Translating messy real-world scenarios into abstract algorithms, algebraic formulas, or spatial models.
  - _Embracing Failure_: Mistakes are cheap in mathematics; systematically eliminating incorrect paths is a necessary process to earn the solution.
- Probabilistic vs. Deterministic Reasoning: Understanding theoretical vs. experimental probability is crucial for data-driven, logical decision-making in real-world scenarios (as opposed to relying on cognitive biases).

## Current Understanding

True mathematical fluency requires transcending the "rigorous" stage of formal rules to reach a "post-rigorous" state where intuition leads the way, supported by rigorous verification. This mindset can be universally applied to real-world problems by treating them as structural puzzles: identifying the core essence, deconstructing the parts, and applying algorithms. Asking "dumb" or foundational questions—like attempting to disprove an assumed truth—often unlocks the necessary perspective for a breakthrough.
