## 1. Goal: Leverage Structure Over Willpower

Traditional habit advice fails ADHD users by relying on internal motivation. This protocol offloads the "effort" of habit change into an analytical and environmental process.

---

## 2. Step 1: Map (Architecture Disclosure)

Before attempting change, you must make the unconscious pattern visible. Record the following components of your current loop:

- **Cue:** The trigger (a specific feeling, situation, or time).
- **Automatic Thoughts:** The internal monologue that follows the cue.
- **Feelings:** The emotional state (anxiety, boredom, excitement).
- **Routine:** The actual behavioral response (e.g., doomscrolling).
- **Result:** The immediate reward or consequence.

**Test:** If you cannot identify the *feeling* that precedes the routine, your map is incomplete.

---

## 3. Step 2: Build (ADHD-Friendly Design)

Design new routines for your **Real Self**, not your idealized self.

- **Micro-Steps:** Break the habit into a chain of tiny, sequential actions (< 30 seconds each).
- **If-Then Rules:** Automate the start. *"If it's 9:00 AM, then I open my daily note."*
- **Habit Stacking:** Anchor the new habit to a high-reliability existing one.
- **Gamified Feedback:** Use visual streak counters to provide the dopamine hit of progress.

---

## 4. Step 3: Dismantle (Replacement Logic)

Bad habits are not broken through resistance; they are broken through **Routine Swapping**.

- **The Constraint:** You must keep the **Cue** and the **Reward** identical.
- **The Action:** Swap only the **Routine** for a constructive behavior that satisfies the same need.
- **Example:**
    - *Cue:* Feeling overwhelmed (Anxiety).
    - *Old Routine:* Doomscrolling (Escapism).
    - *New Routine:* 2-minute brain dump (Control).
    - *Reward:* Feeling of calm/clarity (Achievement).

---

## 5. Summary

Clarity removes the ambiguity that triggers ADHD avoidance. By mapping the loop, building with micro-steps, and dismantling via replacement, you transform habit change into an engineering task.
