## 1. Functional Anatomy & Benefits

Hanging is a foundational human movement that serves as a corrective for the sedentary "flexed" posture.

- **Spinal Decompression:** Utilises lower body weight to create traction, increasing intervertebral space and blood flow.
- **Shoulder Remodelling:** Restores overhead extension and can increase subacromial space to reduce impingement.
- **Grip Strength:** A primary biomarker for longevity; engages all muscles of the hand and forearm. **Note:** Grip endurance is often the \"number one limiter\" in upper body progress—if the grip fails, the exercise fails before the target muscles fatigue.

## 5. Progression Path

1. **Assisted Hang:** Tops of feet on ground to modulate percentage of body weight.
2. **Timed Intervals:** 5–10s holds to allow connective tissue adaptation.
3. **The 30-Second Baseline:** The first major milestone. Achieve a stable 30s hang before progressing to advanced variations.
4. **The 7-Minute Challenge:** Accumulate 7 minutes of total hanging time throughout a single day.
5. **Unilateral (Single-Arm):** Progress from weight shifting -> finger regression -> full release.

## 6. Force Mechanics Warning

- **Static Load:** 1x body weight.
- **Dynamic Load (Swings):** 2x to 3x body weight. **Warning:** Master static holds before introducing dynamic swings to avoid connective tissue failure.
