## Subjects

## Bessie's KS4 Curriculum

### Compulsory Subjects

	- English Language

\- Core Skill:: Reading

\- What kind of texts are studied?:: High-quality fiction and non-fiction from the 19th, 20th, and 21st centuries.

\- Key Reading Skill 1:: Identify and interpret explicit and implicit information and ideas.

\- Key Reading Skill 2:: Analyse how writers use language and structure to create effects.

\- Key Reading Skill 3:: Compare writers' ideas and perspectives across different texts.

\- Core Skill:: Writing

\- Writing Type 1:: Creative Writing (e.g., descriptions, narratives).

\- Writing Type 2:: Transactional Writing (e.g., writing to argue, persuade, or advise).

\- Key Writing Skill:: Plan, draft, edit, and proof-read work, focusing on grammar, punctuation, and spelling.

\- Core Skill:: Spoken Language

\- What is the purpose of the Spoken Language component?:: To present information and ideas clearly and respond to questions in a formal setting.

\- How is Spoken Language assessed?:: As a separate endorsement (Pass, Merit, Distinction) that does not affect the final GCSE grade.

\- English Literature

\- Component 1:: Shakespeare

\- What is studied?:: One complete play by William Shakespeare.

\- Examples of Shakespeare plays studied:: Macbeth, Romeo and Juliet.

\- Component 2:: 19th-Century Novel

\- What is studied?:: One classic novel from the 19th Century.

\- Examples of 19th-Century novels studied:: A Christmas Carol, The Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde.

\- Component 3:: Modern Prose or Drama

\- What is studied?:: One post-1914 fiction or drama text.

\- Example of modern text studied:: An Inspector Calls.

\- Component 4:: Poetry

\- What is studied in the poetry component?:: A collection of poems from an anthology, often linked by a theme.

\- Common Poetry Themes:: Power and Conflict, Love and Relationships.

\- What key poetry skill is assessed?:: Comparing poems from the anthology and analysing unseen poetry.

\- Mathematics

\- Key Area:: Number

\- Includes:: Rational numbers (fractions, decimals, percentages), standard form, estimation.

\- Key Area:: Algebra

\- Includes:: Simplifying expressions, solving linear and quadratic equations, working with graphs and sequences.

\- Key Area:: Ratio, Proportion, and Rates of Change

\- Includes:: Using ratio notation, solving problems with direct and inverse proportion.

\- Key Area:: Geometry and Measures

\- Includes:: Applying formulae for area and volume, Pythagoras' theorem, trigonometry.

\- Key Area:: Statistics & Probability

\- Includes:: Constructing and interpreting charts, calculating probabilities, using measures of central tendency.

\- Combined Science (leads to two GCSEs)

\- Discipline:: Biology

\- Topics:: Cell biology, Organisation, Infection and response, Bioenergetics (photosynthesis/respiration), Homeostasis, Inheritance, Variation and Evolution, Ecology.

\- Discipline:: Chemistry

\- Topics:: Atomic structure, The periodic table, Bonding, Quantitative chemistry, Chemical and energy changes, Rates of reaction, Organic chemistry, Chemical analysis, Earth and atmosphere.

\- Discipline:: Physics

\- Topics:: Energy, Electricity, Particle model of matter, Atomic structure, Forces, Waves, Magnetism and electromagnetism.

### Bessie's Optional Subjects

	- Film Studies

\- Key Concept:: Mise-en-scène

\- Mise-en-scène definition:: Everything that appears in the frame, including setting, props, costume, lighting, and figure expression.

\- Key Concept:: Cinematography

\- Cinematography definition:: The art of motion-picture photography, including camera angles, shot types (e.g., close-up), and camera movement.

\- Component 1:: US Film

\- Focus:: Comparative study of two US films from different periods, exploring genre and context.

\- Component 2:: Global Cinema

\- Focus:: Analysis of one global English-language film and one non-English-language film.

\- Component 3:: Production (Practical)

\- What is the production component?:: A practical coursework element.

\- Production options:: Create a screenplay for a short film, a shooting script, or a film sequence, with an evaluative analysis.

\- History

\- Core Skill:: How do historians work?:: By analysing and evaluating historical sources, understanding different interpretations, and constructing evidence-based arguments.

\- Thematic Study Topic (example):: Medicine in Britain, c1250–present.

\- Period Study Topic (example):: Conflict and Tension, 1918-1939.

\- British Depth Study Topic (example):: Elizabethan England, c1568–1603.

\- Modern Depth Study Topic (example):: Germany, 1890-1945: Democracy and Dictatorship.

\- Art

\- Component 1:: Portfolio

\- What is the Portfolio component?:: A coursework component where students develop projects based on a theme.

\- What does the portfolio demonstrate?:: Skills in various media (drawing, painting, etc.), research into other artists, and the development of ideas.

\- Component 2:: Externally Set Assignment (Exam)

\- What is the Externally Set Assignment?:: Students respond to a theme set by the exam board, creating preparatory work and a final piece under timed conditions.

\- How long is the timed final piece created over?:: Typically 10 hours.

\- Philosophy and Ethics (Religious Studies - AQA Specification)

\- Component 1:: The Study of Religions

\- Focus:: In-depth study of the beliefs, teachings, and practices of two major world religions.

\- Example Religions:: Christianity and Islam.

\- Component 2:: Thematic Studies

\- Theme A:: Relationships and Families

\- Key Questions:: What are religious teachings on marriage, divorce, and sexuality?

\- Theme B:: Religion and Life

\- Key Questions:: What are religious perspectives on the origins of the universe, abortion, and euthanasia?

\- Theme C:: The Existence of God and Revelation

\- Key Questions:: What are the philosophical arguments for and against the existence of God?

\- Theme D:: Religion, Peace and Conflict

\- Key Questions:: What are religious views on war, violence, and pacifism?

\- Photography

\- Key Technical Skill:: Exposure Triangle

\- What are the three elements of the Exposure Triangle?:: Aperture, Shutter Speed, and ISO.

\- Component 1:: Portfolio

\- What is the Photography Portfolio?:: A coursework component where students develop projects based on themes.

\- Example Themes:: Portraiture, Landscape, Still Life.

\- What technical skills are developed?:: Using a DSLR camera, composition techniques, lighting, and digital editing (e.g., Photoshop).

\- Component 2:: Externally Set Assignment (Exam)

\- What is the Photography ESA?:: Students respond to a theme, creating a portfolio of developmental work and a final piece under timed conditions.

\- How long is the supervised time for the final piece?:: Typically 10 hours.
