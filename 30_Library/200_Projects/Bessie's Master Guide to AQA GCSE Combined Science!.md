Hello Bessie! Welcome to your friendly, easy-to-read outline of the AQA GCSE Combined Science (Trilogy) curriculum. Science can seem like a huge mountain to climb, but once we break it down into smaller topics, it's totally manageable. You're going to do brilliantly! 💪

---

## 📝 How Does the Course Work?

Combined Science is a "double award". That means you'll study all three sciences—Biology, Chemistry, and Physics—but you'll end up with two GCSE grades at the end of it all.

The Exams:

- You will take 6 papers in total (2 for Biology, 2 for Chemistry, and 2 for Physics).
- Each paper is 1 hour and 15 minutes long.
- Each paper is worth 70 marks (making up 16.7% of your final grade).
- Papers include a mix of multiple-choice, structured, short answer, and open response questions.

---

## 🧬 Biology Topics

Biology is all about living things—from tiny microscopic cells to giant ecosystems!

### Paper 1

1. Cell Biology: The building blocks of life (animal, plant, and bacterial cells) and how they divide (mitosis).
2. Organisation: How cells form tissues, organs, and systems (like the human digestive system and plant transport systems).
3. Infection and Response: How diseases spread and how our amazing immune system fights them off.
4. Bioenergetics: How plants make food (photosynthesis) and how we get energy (respiration).

### Paper 2

1. Homeostasis and Response: How your body keeps everything balanced (like temperature and blood sugar) and how your nervous system works.
2. Inheritance, Variation, and Evolution: DNA, genetics, how traits are passed down, and how species evolve over time.
3. Ecology: How living things interact with their environment and each other.

---

## 🧪 Chemistry Topics

Chemistry is the study of matter, what stuff is made of, and how it changes when mixed together!

### Paper 1

1. Atomic Structure and the Periodic Table: What atoms look like inside and how the famous Periodic Table works.
2. Bonding, Structure, and the Properties of Matter: How atoms stick together and why materials behave the way they do.
3. Quantitative Chemistry: The maths of chemistry! Calculating masses and concentrations.
4. Chemical Changes: Reactions involving metals, acids, and alkalis.
5. Energy Changes: Reactions that get hot (exothermic) and reactions that get cold (endothermic).

### Paper 2

1. The Rate and Extent of Chemical Change: How fast reactions happen and how we can change the speed.
2. Organic Chemistry: The wonderful world of carbon compounds (like crude oil and fuels).
3. Chemical Analysis: Testing substances to find out exactly what's in them.
4. Chemistry of the Atmosphere: How the Earth's atmosphere evolved and climate change.
5. Using Resources: Sustainability, recycling, and getting clean water.

---

## ⚡ Physics Topics

Physics is all about energy, forces, and how the universe works!

### Paper 1

1. Energy: Different types of energy stores, efficiency, and renewable/non-renewable resources.
2. Electricity: Circuits, current, voltage, resistance, and the national grid.
3. Particle Model of Matter: How solids, liquids, and gases behave, and density.
4. Atomic Structure: Radioactivity, isotopes, and nuclear energy.

### Paper 2

1. Forces: Gravity, friction, speed, acceleration, and Newton's laws of motion.
2. Waves: Electromagnetic waves (like light and X-rays) and how they travel.
3. Magnetism and Electromagnetism: Magnets, magnetic fields, and how they interact with electricity.

---

## 🔬 Required Practicals

Science isn't just about reading textbooks; it's about doing experiments! Throughout the course, you will complete 21 Required Practicals (7 in Biology, 6 in Chemistry, and 8 in Physics).

- You won't be tested on these in a lab during the exams.
- Instead, the exam papers will have questions asking you _how_ you did them, what equipment you used, and how to make the experiments fair and safe.

---

## 💡 Top Tips for Bessie's Revision

1. Mix it up: Since you have 6 exams close together, make a revision timetable that rotates between Biology, Chemistry, and Physics.
2. Learn your formulas: Physics has equations you need to remember. Practice rearranging them!
3. Active Recall: Flashcards and past papers are your best friends. Don't just re-read your notes.
4. Know your practicals: Be ready to describe methods and identify variables (independent, dependent, and control) for the 21 required practicals.
