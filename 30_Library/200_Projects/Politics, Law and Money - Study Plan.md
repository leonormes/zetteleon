### Module 1: The Human Basis: Morality, Reason and Co-operation

Core idea: before constitutions or legal codes exist, people rely on social mechanisms to coexist. Morality evolved as a psychological adaptation that lets otherwise selfish individuals gain the mutual benefits of cooperation.

Sources: Greene, _Moral Tribes_; Pinker, _Enlightenment Now_.

Already in the vault:

- [[Technological Progress Accumulates Reliably While Moral and Political Progress Is Fragile and Reversible]]—_a counterweight to the Pinker thesis: measurable gains are real, but rights and institutions can erode within generations._
- [[Evil Attacks the Shared Moral Community Itself Not Merely a Norm Within It]]—_the shared moral framework that this module asks where cooperation comes from._

Atomic notes from this module (from Greene's Shared Pasture Parable, checked against _Moral Tribes_):

- [[Self-Interested Herders Erode a Shared Pasture Because Its Cost Is Shared]]—_Hardin's parable: each herder gains, the cost is shared, the commons is eroded._
- [[Morality as Adaptations for Cooperation]]—_Greene's definition of morality._
- [[Me Versus Us]]—_the basic moral problem, selfishness against concern for others._
- [[The Tragedy of Commonsense Morality]]—_Us versus Them: tribes that are each moral internally, in conflict._
- [[Each of Four Tribes in the New Pastures Parable Runs Its Land by a Different Rule]]—_the Parable of the New Pastures: four working rules for shared land._
- [[Cooperation Runs on Feelings]]—_cooperation is typically intuitive, driven by automatic feelings._
- [[Pro-Social Punishment Restores Cooperation]]—_the Public Goods Game: paying to punish free riders raises contributions._
- [[Righteous Indignation]]—_the distinctively moral anger toward free riders._
- [[Morality Evolved Through Competition Between Groups]]—_cooperation evolves only when cooperators outcompete non-cooperators._
- [[The Same Moral Thinking Enables and Undermines Cooperation]]—_the modern tragedy: within-group cooperation undermines between-group cooperation._

Atomic notes from this module (from Pinker's four Enlightenment ideals, checked against _Enlightenment Now_):

- [[The Four Themes of the Enlightenment]]—_reason, science, humanism and progress tie the Enlightenment's ideas together._
- [[Reason as Commitment to Objective Standards]]—_anyone who argues for an answer has committed to reason._
- [[Enlightenment Thinkers Rejected Faith and Gut Feelings as Generators of Delusion]]—_what the Enlightenment thinkers refused to fall back on._
- [[Science as Refined Reason]]—_science is reason refined to understand the world._
- [[The Methods of Science Are a Paradigm of How to Achieve Reliable Knowledge]]—_skepticism, fallibilism, open debate and empirical testing._
- [[Humanism Privileges the Individual Over the Group]]—_individual well-being over the glory of tribe, nation or religion._
- [[Enlightenment Thinkers Built Humanism as a Secular Moral Foundation After Religious Carnage]]—_why the thinkers built it._
- [[Progress Comes From Testing Reforms]]—_keep the reforms that make people better off._
- [[Reason and Sympathy Can Be Applied to Enhance Human Flourishing]]—_the Enlightenment principle in one sentence._

The ten Greene notes cover the parable and its argument, which bears on the first question above (intuition versus reason). The nine Pinker notes cover only the book's opening statement of the four themes (its Introduction and Chapter 1). They bear on the second question (how reason, science and humanitarian values protect progress against tribalism and dogma) but not on the rest of the book's evidence, so the second question and both source boxes stay open. Two tensions to think about while reading: Pinker's "reason, not gut feelings" against Greene's "cooperation runs on feelings", and Pinker's steady progress against the Gray note's fragile, reversible gains.

Module done when: I can explain, without notes, why moral intuitions and reasoning pull apart and what Pinker says holds the line against tribalism.
