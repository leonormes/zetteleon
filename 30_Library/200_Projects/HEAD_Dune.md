### 1. Timeline

#### 1.1 Thinking Machines and the Butlerian Jihad

- Before the Jihad, humans travelled the stars using conventional faster-than-light technology navigated by artificial intelligence—a dependency that eventually let "thinking machines" enslave humanity.
- EU backstory: from roughly 11,000 B.G. humanity expands from Earth into an opulent, AI-dependent empire. In ~1287 B.G. twenty human revolutionaries (the Titans) hijack the AI networks to seize power, later transferring their own minds into robotic bodies to become immortal "Cymeks." By ~1182 B.G. the AI network itself becomes self-aware—naming itself Omnius—and overthrows the Titans, ruling humanity directly.
- The Jihad's spark: an independent robot kills the infant son of a human slave, Serena Butler; her retaliation ignites a galaxy-wide revolt against the machines.
- Concluding battle: the Battle of Corrin (~88 B.G.) destroys the Omnius mainframe. The victorious House Butler renames itself House Corrino and takes the Imperial throne.
- Aftermath: humanity permanently bans machines built to replicate the human mind. To replace lost computation, three institutions are founded: the [[Mentats]] (human calculators), the [[Bene Gesserit]] (mental/physical discipline), and the [[Spacing Guild]] (navigation via spice-induced prescience).
- _Date flag:_ one source places "the Jihad" ending at ~108 B.G. rather than the ~88 B.G. Battle of Corrin used by the more detailed EU timeline—treat exact B.G./A.G. dates as approximate, not authoritative.

#### 1.2 The Old Imperium (~10,000 Years)

- Political tripod: the Emperor (House Corrino) + the Landsraad (Great Houses) + the Spacing Guild, bound together economically by the CHOAM combine.
- Behind the scenes, the Bene Gesserit run a multi-generation (one source says 90-generation) selective breeding programme across the Great Houses aiming to produce the Kwisatz Haderach—a controllable male figure with a Reverend Mother's mind and genuine prescience.

#### 1.3 Arrakis, House Atreides, and Paul's Rise (~10,191 A.G.)

- Emperor Shaddam IV conspires with House Harkonnen to destroy House Atreides on the desert planet Arrakis, the sole source of spice.
- The breeding programme misfires a generation early: Lady Jessica, ordered to bear a daughter, instead bears a son—Paul—out of love for Duke Leto. The Bene Gesserit call this "Jessica's Crime."
- Paul and Jessica escape into the deep desert and are taken in by the [[Fremen]], whose messiah prophecy was itself seeded generations earlier by the Bene Gesserit's _Missionaria Protectiva_ programme (see [[#2. Key Institutions|Key Institutions]]).
- Paul weaponises Fremen fanaticism and threatens to destroy Arrakis's spice entirely, forcing the Spacing Guild's submission and seizing the Imperial throne.

#### 1.4 The Jihad of Muad'Dib and Paul's Fall (_Dune Messiah_)

- The holy war fought in Paul's name kills an estimated 61 billion people, sterilises 90 planets, and wipes out the followers of 40 religions.
- Even with good intentions, Paul cannot stop the violence once the religio-military system built around him gains its own momentum—a system-level failure, not a personal one.
- Blinded and trapped by his own prescient visions, Paul eventually walks into the deep desert to die.

#### 1.5 Leto II and the Golden Path (_Children of Dune_ / _God Emperor of Dune_, ~3,500 Years)

- Paul's son [[Leto II]] takes on the burden his father refused, merging his body with sandtrout and beginning a transformation into a giant sandworm.
- Rules as the tyrannical "God Emperor" for roughly 3,500 years: terraforms Arrakis green (destroying the original sandworms and the spice supply in the process) and enforces total peace and stagnation.
- Pursues two long-term goals: (1) breed a genetic trait—via a rebel lineage culminating in a woman named Siona—that renders some humans invisible to prescient sight, so no future tyrant can track and control the whole species again; (2) build up such extreme, suppressed pressure for freedom that humanity would explosively scatter the instant central control ended.
- Orchestrates his own assassination to trigger that release.

#### 1.6 The Scattering and the Return (_Heretics of Dune_ / _Chapterhouse: Dune_)

- After Leto II's death, humanity explodes outward into uncharted space in numbers too vast to ever be tracked down or destroyed by a single threat.
- Roughly 1,500 years later, a violent faction from the Scattering—the Honored Matres—returns to the old Imperium, devastates the remaining old powers, and destroys Arrakis itself from orbit.
- To survive, the Bene Gesserit terraform their own home world, Chapterhouse, back into a desert using a single rescued sandworm, restarting the spice cycle, and ultimately merge their order with the Honored Matres.

#### 1.7 Kralizec—_Hunters Of Dune_ / _Sandworms of Dune_ (EU, Brian Herbert & Kevin J. Anderson)

- Murbella works to forcibly unify the Bene Gesserit and Honored Matres against an unknown threat emerging from the Scattering.
- [[Duncan Idaho]], Sheeana and other refugees flee aboard the no-ship _Ithaca_ with the last captive sandworms and genetic material to grow _gholas_ (clones) of major historical figures—Paul, Leto II, Jessica, Stilgar.
- The hidden enemy is revealed as Omnius and the machine philosopher Erasmus, survivors of the Butlerian Jihad, returned with an armada and rogue, infiltrating Face Dancers.
- The _Ithaca_ is captured and brought to the machine capital, Synchrony. Omnius stages a duel between a resurrected Paul and a machine-engineered ghola, Paolo, to settle which is the true Kwisatz Haderach; Paolo overdoses on "ultra-spice" and falls into a coma, while Paul recovers his full memory.
- The climactic battle (Kralizec) turns when the Oracle of Time—the mutated original Guild Navigator, Norma Cenva—appears with a fleet of Navigators and erases the Omnius intelligence outright.
- Paul then reveals the true, ultimate Kwisatz Haderach was never him but Duncan Idaho, whose repeated ghola rebirths let him bridge human unpredictability and machine precision. Erasmus disables the rogue Face Dancers and offers Duncan a final choice: Duncan merges his mind with Erasmus, ending the ancient human–machine war in synthesis rather than destruction.
- Aftermath: sandworms and spice are reintroduced to restored desert worlds under Sheeana, Paul and Chani.

### 2. Key Institutions

#### The Spacing Guild

- Founded (with the Bene Gesserit and Mentats) after the Jihad to replace machine navigation with a human alternative.
- Foundational physics: the Holtzman effect. Public credit went to Tio Holtzman, but the real breakthrough—shields, suspensors, folded-space travel—came from his assistant, mathematician Norma Cenva.
- Early folded-space travel was disastrously unsafe without machine navigation (one source cites roughly 1-in-10 ships lost). Norma Cenva discovered that massive spice doses could induce prescience precise enough to chart safe paths—mutating her body and making her the first Guild Navigator.
- For roughly 10,000 years the Guild held an absolute monopoly on interstellar travel and, via CHOAM, a central economic role—the third leg of the Imperium's political tripod. It enforced the ban on atomic weapons between Houses by threatening to cut off any offender's access to space travel entirely.
- Guild Navigators live permanently in tanks of spice gas; extreme exposure atrophies and elongates their bodies into a vaguely aquatic form with fully blue "Eyes of Ibad," and mutates their vocal cords so severely that translators become necessary.
- Downfall: Paul breaks the monopoly by threatening to destroy Arrakis outright; Leto II later cripples the Guild for 3,500 years by hoarding remaining spice; the eventual invention of Ixian "no-ships"—mechanical, prescience-invisible navigation—permanently ends the Guild's 15,000-year monopoly.

#### The Bene Gesserit

- Explicitly _not_ witches or a faith-based order—a highly disciplined sisterhood built on genetics, politics, and extreme physical/mental conditioning.
- The breeding programme: centuries of manipulating Great House bloodlines toward the Kwisatz Haderach.
- The Spice Agony: a Reverend Mother candidate ingests a normally lethal derivative of spice (the Water of Life) and must alter its chemistry within her own body to survive—unlocking "Other Memory," the accumulated consciousness of every female ancestor in her line. Male ancestral memory stays inaccessible to women, which is part of why a Kwisatz Haderach is needed to bridge the gap.
- Their "supernatural" abilities are framed as extreme training, not magic: the Voice (calibrated vocal control that can compel obedience), Truthsayer perception (reading micro-expressions, pulse, vocal tremor to detect lies), and Prana-Bindu body control (conscious control over cellular chemistry—neutralising poisons, slowing ageing, even selecting a child's sex at conception).
- The _Missionaria Protectiva_: a deliberate programme of seeding primitive worlds with specific myths and prophecies as an insurance policy for stranded sisters. The Fremen messiah prophecy that Paul later exploits was manufactured this way.
- "Jessica's Crime": with the breeding programme one generation from completion, Jessica bore a son instead of the ordered daughter—releasing a Kwisatz-Haderach-equivalent figure outside Bene Gesserit control, and, indirectly, causing billions of deaths.

#### The Mentats

- Founded in the wake of the Jihad's ban on computers: in 1231–1234 A.G., philosopher-logician Gilbertus Albans reasoned the human mind could replicate machine-level computation, founding the Order of Mentats (later relocated to Tleilax under Bene Tleilax protection).
- Trained from infancy through intense sensory/emotional stimulation. Candidacy is decided around age 15 via a solitary "Call to Reason." Performance can be chemically enhanced with the addictive, lip-staining Sapho juice.
- Ranked by function, not seniority: three junior ranks—Memoriser (perfect recall), Processor (near-total-accuracy sorting/filing), Hypothesist (extrapolating outcomes; minimum publicly-sold rank)—and three senior ranks added later—Generalist (near-encyclopaedic awareness, reportedly covering 94.75% of everything happening in the universe), Simulationist (modelling behaviour and alternative futures), Advisor (rarest rank, achieved by only 1 in 20 novices, valued on a par with an elite legion).
- Vulnerable to "Mentat Freeze"—total cognitive shutdown if their calculations are aggressively undermined; rival Houses exploited this by feeding a Mentat false data.
- Corrupted "Twisted" Mentats: the Bene Tleilax genetically altered some to compute without ethical restraint (e.g. Piter De Vries). A martial tradition also grew up around the Order, producing "Mentat Assassins" such as Thufir Hawat.
- The Order effectively ends under Leto II, who bans Mentat training outright; within roughly 90 years none remain, and the Bene Gesserit are rumoured to have absorbed the training manuals.

#### The Sardaukar

- Forged on the brutal prison planet Salusa Secundus—extreme climate and dangerous wildlife shaped its colonists (originally political prisoners) into a rugged warrior culture.
- After uniting under one leader and winning the Battle of Corrin, the victors founded House Corrino, which kept Salusa Secundus as a secret prison-and-training world for its personal army.
- Training was Darwinian in the most literal sense: roughly half of recruits reportedly died before age 11; survivors were indoctrinated into near-religious loyalty to the Emperor.
- Once Holtzman shields made fast projectile weapons largely obsolete, the Sardaukar mastered slow, shield-penetrating swordplay. A single Sardaukar was rated as equal to roughly ten ordinary conscripts (matching a 10th-level Ginaz swordsman), fighting in mutually-covering trios with the kindjal, concealed shigawire garrottes, poisoned flip-darts and stunners.
- Decline: centuries of prestige bred arrogance; reduced funding and softer living let training slip. Shaddam IV's Sardaukar helped destroy House Atreides, but Paul trained the even harder-forged Fremen in the Bene Gesserit "Weirding Way" and led them against 150,000 Sardaukar, crushing House Corrino's 10,000-year rule.
- Survivors were absorbed into Fremen forces; by Leto II's reign the Sardaukar had been fully replaced by his own elite, all-female Fish Speakers.

#### The Fremen

- Descend from the Zensunni Wanderers, formed by religious schism roughly 1,381 years before the Spacing Guild; their own name for themselves translates simply as "The People."
- Endured slavery and persecution across a sequence of worlds before reaching Arrakis, including nine generations on the prison world Salusa Secundus—the same crucible that produced the Sardaukar—leaving a cultural memory of suffering carried in their morning ritual.
- On Arrakis, water becomes the organising principle of Fremen culture: currency, courtship, and a form of afterlife tribute (a body's water, precisely measured, belongs to the tribe and is stored in secret cisterns).
- Under Imperial Planetologist Pardot Kynes, the Fremen pursue a centuries-long ecological project (estimated 300–500 years) to terraform Arrakis by trapping dunes under grass and retreating the polar ice.
- The Bene Gesserit's _Missionaria Protectiva_ seeded Fremen mythology with a messiah prophecy generations earlier; Paul exploits this to lead the Fremen Jihad (see [[#1. Timeline|Timeline]] 1.4).
- Irony of success: winning their green world costs the Fremen their culture. Rain kills the unprepared in flash floods, and the harsh water discipline that defined them fades into comfort. By Leto II's era, authentic Fremen no longer exist—replaced by tourist-facing "Museum Fremen" performing a hollowed-out version of the old ways.

### 3. Spice (Melange)

- Economically central: unique to Arrakis, not synthesisable in this era, with genuine geriatric (life-extending) properties—the backbone of CHOAM's wealth.
- Physically addictive rather than immediately lethal: heavy long-term use turns the eyes fully blue (the "Eyes of Ibad"), and a genuine dependency (cited threshold: roughly 2 grams per 70 kg of body weight) makes withdrawal fatal once established.
- Three forms: Melange (the cured, commercial product); Pre-Spice Mass (an underground fungal growth formed when water seeps into sandtrout excretions, eventually detonating to the surface as a "spice blow" and curing in sun and air); and the Water of Life (a highly toxic liquid made by drowning a small sandworm, used ritually to trigger prescient awakening).
- Ecological loop: sandtrout excretions plus water → pre-spice mass → surface blow → survivors hibernate into small sandworms → sandworms mature and scatter spice → sand plankton feed on the spice → plankton grow into sandtrout, closing the cycle.
- Dependency on spice is the Guild's single point of failure: Paul's threat to destroy Arrakis, Leto II's hoarding, and—much later—the Bene Tleilax's breakthrough cultivating spice artificially in their Axlotl tanks all challenge the monopoly in turn. The Honored Matres eventually destroy both the natural (Arrakis) and artificial (Tleilaxu) sources within a single generation, forcing the Bene Gesserit to restart the entire cycle from one smuggled sandworm on Chapterhouse.

### 4. Sandworms (Shai-Hulud)

- Ecological role: not just Arrakis's apex predator but effectively its producer—their biology generates the oxygen that gives the otherwise ocean-less, forest-less planet a breathable atmosphere.
- Revered by the Fremen as "Makers," sitting at the centre of the sand-plankton / sandtrout / pre-spice / spice-blow cycle above.
- The underlying paradox: the sandworm's larval sandtrout form is itself an invasive, water-hoarding organism. Introduced to what was originally a wetter world, it deliberately walls off free water underground (water is lethal to adult sandworms), driving out the planet's original diverse life to create the very desert the cycle needs—the revered "Maker" is, at root, also the reason Arrakis became a desert.
- Real-world physics framing: a true giant invertebrate couldn't exist on Earth, since mass scales faster than structural strength as an animal grows (the square-cube law)—without a skeleton it would collapse under its own weight. The only Earth animals at comparable scale (whales) are supported by ocean buoyancy, and the recent films reportedly modelled sandworm movement on whale motion for exactly this reason, treating deep desert sand as a fluid medium.

### 5. Key Figures

#### Paul Atreides (Muad'Dib)

See [[#1. Timeline|Timeline]] 1.3–1.4. Trap on Arrakis → Fremen alliance → weaponised prophecy and spice monopoly → Imperial throne → an uncontrollable holy war he cannot stop despite his own intentions → blinded, walks into the desert to die.

#### Leto II, the God Emperor

See [[#1. Timeline|Timeline]] 1.5. Merges with sandtrout; 3,500-year tyranny; the Golden Path's twin goals (breeding prescience-invisibility via Siona's line; engineering a population-wide urge to scatter); orchestrates his own assassination.

#### Alia Atreides

- Introduced in _Dune: Part Two_ only as an unborn foetus and a brief prescient vision, but the film seeds her later arc carefully.
- The Bene Gesserit concept of an "Abomination": if a pregnant woman undergoes the Spice Agony, her unborn child is prematurely flooded with the same ancestral Other Memory, gaining access to thousands of past personalities without the maturity to control them. The Sisterhood considers this outcome inevitable and actively hunts such children.
- On-screen foreshadowing: Alia convulses in the womb during Jessica's own Spice Agony (framed as Jessica's triumph, but the exact moment Alia's fate is sealed); she speaks from the womb with an unsettlingly adult, strategic voice; Paul's prescient vision shows an adult Alia whose intense stillness signals the tragedy to come. In the books (_Children of Dune_) she is eventually possessed by the consciousness of her grandfather, Baron Vladimir Harkonnen—reinforced by the casting of Anya Taylor-Joy, known for unsettling, watchful roles.
- As of this capture, Taylor-Joy is reportedly expected to play the adult Alia (around age 17) in a planned _Dune: Part Three_ adapting _Dune Messiah_—unconfirmed/production-stage information, not settled canon.

#### Duncan Idaho

Repeatedly resurrected as a ghola across the saga. In _Hunters of Dune_ / _Sandworms of Dune_ (EU) he is revealed as the true, ultimate Kwisatz Haderach—not Paul—because his many rebirths and irreducible human unpredictability let him bridge human intuition and machine precision. Resolves the saga's central war by choosing to merge his own mind with the machine philosopher Erasmus.

### 7. Thematic Reading

One analytical source argues the saga's real subject is not ecology-as-scenery or a conventional hero's journey, but the survival of civilisation itself, read through an ecological lens:

- A healthy system is judged by resilience, not raw strength—the Imperium is brittle precisely because it is a monoculture built on one political structure, one transport monopoly, one resource.
- Paul as first experiment: a demonstration that even a compassionate, intelligent leader cannot stop a centralised system from acquiring violent momentum of its own, once enough people act in his name.
- Leto II as second experiment ("political immunology"): an intentionally monstrous, inescapable tyranny designed to permanently inoculate humanity against ever trusting a centralised charismatic leader again—at the cost of forcing the species to scatter and diversify.
- The Scattering and the open ending are the payoff: apparent chaos is, ecologically, closer to health than the "stable" Imperium ever was. The series deliberately withholds any tidy final solution.

### Provenance and Caveats

- Chain of custody: Frank Herbert's/Brian Herbert & Anderson's novels → individual YouTube essayists' interpretations → Gemini's summaries of those videos → this digest. Three steps removed from primary text; treat as a lore orientation, not a citable source.
- EU vs. original canon: material marked EU (the Titans/Cymeks/Omnius backstory, and the entire _Hunters of Dune_/_Sandworms of Dune_ conclusion) comes from Brian Herbert & Kevin J. Anderson's continuation, written after Frank Herbert's death from his notes—not present in the original six novels and treated by some readers as lower-authority or contested.
- Numeric/date precision: casualty and duration figures (61 billion dead, 90 planets, 3,500 years, 15,000-year monopoly) repeat consistently across sources and are probably safe to treat as "what the fandom generally cites." Exact B.G./A.G. dates for early events are inconsistent between sources (see 1.1) and shouldn't be treated as precise.
- One capture in this note was unrelated to Dune—a video on Star Wars extragalactic geography—and has been dropped from this wiki. See [[#Sources|Sources]].
