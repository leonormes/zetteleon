## GCSE (9–1) Mathematics—Curriculum Tracker

> A tier-split checklist of the GCSE (9–1) Maths specification, organised by content domain and DfE reference code (N, A, R, G, P, S). Tick items off with `- [x]` as you cover them.

---

### 📘 Foundation Tier

#### 1. Number (22–28% of Assessment)

##### Structure and Calculation

- [ ] N1: Order positive/negative integers, decimals, and fractions; use symbols $=, \neq, <, >, \leq, \geq$.
- [ ] N2: Apply the four operations ($+, -, \times, \div$) to integers, decimals, simple fractions, and mixed numbers. Understand place value.
- [ ] N3: Use inverse operations and conventional notation for priority of operations (BIDMAS/brackets, powers, roots, reciprocals).
- [ ] N4: Prime numbers, factors, multiples, HCF, LCM, and prime factorisation (product notation).
- [ ] N5: Systematic listing strategies.
- [ ] N6: Positive integer powers and associated real roots (square, cube, higher); recognise powers of 2, 3, 4, 5.
- [ ] N7: Calculate with roots and integer indices.
- [ ] N8: Calculate exactly with fractions and multiples of $\pi$.
- [ ] N9: Calculate with and interpret standard form $A \times 10^n$ ($1 \leq A < 10$).

##### Fractions, Decimals and Percentages

- [ ] N10: Work interchangeably with terminating decimals and their corresponding fractions.
- [ ] N11: Identify and work with fractions in ratio problems.
- [ ] N12: Interpret fractions and percentages as operators.

##### Measures and Accuracy

- [ ] N13: Use standard units of mass, length, time, money, and compound measures.
- [ ] N14: Estimate answers; check calculations using approximation/estimation.
- [ ] N15: Round numbers to decimal places or significant figures; use inequality notation for simple error intervals (truncation/rounding).
- [ ] N16: Apply and interpret limits of accuracy.

#### 2. Algebra (17–23% of Assessment)

##### Notation, Vocabulary and Manipulation

- [ ] A1: Use and interpret algebraic notation ($ab$, $3y$, $a^2$, $a^3$, $\frac{a}{b}$, brackets).
- [ ] A2: Substitute numerical values into formulae and expressions.
- [ ] A3: Understand terms: expression, equation, formula, identity, inequality, term, factor.
- [ ] A4: Simplify/manipulate expressions: collect like terms, multiply single term over bracket, take out common factors, expand two binomials, factorise quadratics of the form $x^2 + bx + c$.
- [ ] A5: Rearrange formulae to change the subject.
- [ ] A6: Know the difference between an equation and an identity; argue mathematically to show equivalence.
- [ ] A7: Interpret simple expressions as functions with inputs and outputs.

##### Graphs

- [ ] A8: Work with coordinates in all four quadrants.
- [ ] A9: Plot straight-line graphs; use $y = mx + c$ to identify parallel lines; find the equation of a line through points.
- [ ] A10: Identify and interpret gradients and intercepts of linear functions.
- [ ] A11: Identify roots, intercepts, and turning points of quadratics graphically; deduce roots algebraically.
- [ ] A12: Recognise, sketch, and interpret graphs of linear, quadratic, simple cubic, and reciprocal functions ($\frac{1}{x}$).
- [ ] A14: Plot and interpret non-standard graphs in real contexts (distance, speed, acceleration).

##### Solving Equations and Inequalities

- [ ] A17: Solve linear equations in one unknown algebraically (including unknowns on both sides) or graphically.
- [ ] A18: Solve quadratic equations algebraically by factorising or graphically.
- [ ] A19: Solve two simultaneous linear equations algebraically or graphically.
- [ ] A21: Translate real situations into equations/formulae and solve them.
- [ ] A22: Solve linear inequalities in one variable; represent solutions on a number line.

##### Sequences

- [ ] A23: Generate terms of a sequence from term-to-term or position-to-term rules.
- [ ] A24: Recognise triangular, square, cube numbers, arithmetic progressions, Fibonacci sequences, quadratic sequences, and geometric progressions.
- [ ] A25: Deduce expressions for the $n$th term of linear sequences.

#### 3. Ratio, Proportion and Rates of Change (22–28% of Assessment)

- [ ] R1: Change freely between standard units and compound units.
- [ ] R2: Use scale factors, scale diagrams, and maps.
- [ ] R3: Express one quantity as a fraction of another.
- [ ] R4: Use ratio notation, including reduction to simplest form.
- [ ] R5: Divide a quantity into a given ratio; apply ratio to real contexts (mixing, scaling, concentrations).
- [ ] R6–R8: Express multiplicative relationships; understand proportion as equality of ratios; link ratios to fractions.
- [ ] R9: Define percentage as 'parts per hundred'; interpret percentage changes multiplicatively; solve percentage problems (increase/decrease, original value, simple interest).
- [ ] R10 & R11: Solve direct and inverse proportion problems; use compound units (speed, density, pressure).
- [ ] R12: Compare lengths, areas, and volumes using ratio notation; link to similarity.
- [ ] R13 & R14: Understand and interpret inverse proportion ($X \propto \frac{1}{Y}$); interpret the gradient of a straight line as a rate of change.
- [ ] R16: Set up and solve growth and decay problems, including compound interest.

#### 4. Geometry and Measures (12–18% of Assessment)

##### Properties and Constructions

- [ ] G1: Use conventional geometric terms, notation, and labelling conventions; draw diagrams from written text.
- [ ] G2: Ruler and compass constructions (perpendicular bisectors, angle bisectors); solve loci problems.
- [ ] G3: Apply angle facts (angles at a point, on a straight line, vertically opposite, alternate, and corresponding lines); find polygon angle sums.
- [ ] G4: Properties of special quadrilaterals and triangles.
- [ ] G5 & G6: Geometric congruence criteria for triangles (SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS); simple geometric proofs including Pythagoras' theorem.
- [ ] G7: Identify, describe, and construct transformations: rotation, reflection, translation (using vectors), and enlargement (including fractions).
- [ ] G9: Circle definitions: centre, radius, chord, diameter, circumference, tangent, arc, sector, segment.
- [ ] G12 & G13: 3D shape properties; construct plans and elevations.

##### Mensuration and Calculation

- [ ] G14 & G15: Use standard units; measure lines/angles; interpret maps, scale drawings, and bearings.
- [ ] G16: Calculate areas of triangles, parallelograms, trapezia; volumes of cuboids and right prisms.
- [ ] G17 & G18: Formulas for circle circumference and area; calculate perimeters/areas of composite shapes; surface area and volume of spheres, pyramids, cones, and cylinders.
- [ ] G20 & G21: Apply Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometric ratios ($\sin, \cos, \tan$) in 2D right-angled triangles; know exact values for $0^\circ, 30^\circ, 45^\circ, 60^\circ, 90^\circ$.

##### Vectors

- [ ] G24 & G25: Describe translations as 2D column vectors; apply addition, subtraction, and scalar multiplication.

#### 5. Probability (Probability + Statistics: 12–18% of aSsessment)

- [ ] P1–P3: Record and analyse frequencies using tables and frequency trees; understand randomness, fairness, and the 0–1 probability scale.
- [ ] P4: Apply the property that exhaustive sets of mutually exclusive outcomes sum to 1.
- [ ] P5–P7: Venn diagrams, tree diagrams, grids, and sample space diagrams to calculate theoretical probabilities.
- [ ] P8: Calculate probabilities of independent and dependent combined events.

#### 6. Statistics (Probability + Statistics: 12–18% of aSsessment)

- [ ] S1 & S2: Sampling limitations; interpret/construct charts (bar charts, pie charts, pictograms, vertical line charts, time series tables/graphs).
- [ ] S4: Calculate/compare measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode, modal class) and spread (range, identifying outliers).
- [ ] S6: Construct and interpret scatter graphs; understand correlation vs. causation; draw lines of best fit; interpolation and extrapolation.

### 🔺 Higher Tier—Extension Content

> Higher students are assessed on all Foundation topics plus the advanced subtopics below.

#### 1. Number (12–18% of Assessment)

- [ ] N5: Apply the product rule for counting.
- [ ] N6: Estimate powers and roots of any given positive number.
- [ ] N7: Calculate with fractional indices.
- [ ] N8: Simplify surds (e.g., $\sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3}$) and rationalise denominators.
- [ ] N10: Convert recurring decimals into fractions and vice versa.
- [ ] N16: Apply limits of accuracy using upper and lower bounds.

#### 2. Algebra (27–33% of Assessment)

##### Manipulation and Functions

- [ ] A4: Factorise quadratic expressions of the form $ax^2 + bx + c$; manipulate algebraic fractions.
- [ ] A7: Formal function notation; find composite functions and inverse functions.

##### Graphs and Circular Equations

- [ ] A9: Identify perpendicular lines using gradients ($m_1 \times m_2 = -1$).
- [ ] A11: Deduce turning points of quadratic functions by completing the square.
- [ ] A12: Sketch exponential graphs ($y = k^x$) and trigonometric graphs ($y = \sin x, y = \cos x, y = \tan x$) for any angle.
- [ ] A13: Sketch translations and reflections of a given function, $f(x)$.
- [ ] A15: Estimate gradients of curves (by drawing tangents) and areas under graphs (distance-time, velocity-time, financial contexts).
- [ ] A16: Recognise the equation of a circle centred at the origin ($x^2 + y^2 = r^2$); find the equation of a tangent to a circle at a given point.

##### Equations and Inequalities

- [ ] A18: Solve quadratics by completing the square and using the quadratic formula.
- [ ] A19: Solve simultaneous equations where one is linear and one is quadratic.
- [ ] A20: Find approximate solutions to equations numerically using iteration.
- [ ] A22: Solve quadratic inequalities in one variable; solve linear inequalities in two variables and represent using set notation or shaded regions on a graph.

##### Sequences

- [ ] A25: Deduce expressions for the $n$th term of quadratic sequences.

#### 3. Ratio, Proportion and Rates of Change (17–23% of Assessment)

- [ ] R14 & R15: Interpret the gradient at a point on a curve as an instantaneous rate of change (using chords and tangents).
- [ ] R16: Set up, solve, and work with general iterative processes for exponential growth and decay.

#### 4. Geometry and Measures (17–23% of Assessment)

##### Advanced Shapes and Proofs

- [ ] G7: Use negative scale factors for enlargements.
- [ ] G8: Describe the invariance achieved by combinations of transformations.
- [ ] G10: Apply and prove the standard circle theorems (angles at centre/circumference, cyclic quadrilaterals, alternate segment theorem, tangents, chords).

##### Advanced Trigonometry and Vectors

- [ ] G19: Apply rules of similarity to link lengths, areas, and volumes in similar figures.
- [ ] G20: Apply trigonometry to 3D shapes.
- [ ] G22 & G23: Know and apply the Sine Rule ($\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B}$), Cosine Rule ($a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$), and Area formula ($\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$) for any triangle.
- [ ] G25: Use vectors to construct geometric arguments and formal proofs.

#### 5. Probability (Probability + Statistics: 12–18% of aSsessment)

- [ ] P9: Calculate and interpret conditional probabilities using expected frequencies, two-way tables, tree diagrams, and Venn diagrams.

#### 6. Statistics (Probability + Statistics: 12–18% of aSsessment)

- [ ] S3: Construct and interpret histograms with unequal class intervals.
- [ ] S4: Construct and interpret cumulative frequency graphs and box plots (including quartiles and inter-quartile range).
