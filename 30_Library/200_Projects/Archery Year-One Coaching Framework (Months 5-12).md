### Coaching Framework Months 5-12

### Verdict

- Your year-one job is to build one repeatable shot process and make it hold under fatigue and pressure. Refining it can wait. Everything below serves that.
- Your own data already says this. Tail leakage (15–20 arrows ≤3 per session) is a consistency problem, not a capability problem. Form work and score are the same project. Only the per-session total makes them look opposed.
- Change what you judge, not what you record. Keep plotting arrows, but judge sessions on lead metrics: execution rate, outliers measured from your group's centre, and shot-timing spread. Review score monthly on a rolling basis.
- Make most sessions form sessions. Designate a scored session roughly once a week.
- The indoor season fits this well. October to May is months 5–12, and 20 yards gives fast feedback.

---

### Pressure-test First: Three Things in Your Current Setup

1. Your anchor changed in the first week of your form block. You moved from a tab with a shelf to one without on 4 October, and the anchor felt wrong. The anchor is your main reference point.
    - If the switch wasn't forced (by wear or fit), going back to a shelved tab is the cheapest fix.
    - If you keep the new tab, make "new anchor" the _only_ focus of block 1 and label every score confounded until it settles.
    - Either option is fine. Switching between tabs is not.
2. You stepped up from 30–32 lb to about 36 lb. Check two things before blaming form for tail arrows:
    - Holding capacity. Can you hold full draw in good form for about 20 seconds _late_ in a session? If not, some tails are fatigue.
    - Arrow spine. Were your arrows re-selected for 36 lb? If not, do a bare-shaft check. Underspined arrows throw erratic fliers that look exactly like form faults.
3. Single-session data is too noisy to judge changes by. Your 50-yard hypothesis was built, then falsified, within a few sessions. Judge only on rolling windows of at least three sessions.

### 1. First-year Focus

#### Block Plan (Default Order)

Each block ends on its date, not when the skill is perfect. The previous focus then becomes one warm-up check.

|Block|Approx. dates|Single focus|Exit signal (no score involved)|
|---|---|---|---|
|1|Oct–mid Nov|Anchor and string picture|Video shows the same anchor contact on ≥9/10 shots; you can name your 2–3 reference points|
|2|mid Nov–Dec|Relaxed bow hand and held follow-through|Bow rocks forward into the sling with no sideways kick; finish held for 2 seconds on A shots|
|3|Jan–Feb|Continuous draw-through the release, plus let-down discipline|Shot-timing spread narrows; you let down instead of forcing bad shots|
|4|Mar–Apr|Routine under pressure|Your A-rate in scored sessions roughly matches your A-rate in form sessions|
|5|May|Outdoor transition: sight marks, distances, wind|Nothing new technically|

If video shows a worse fault, that fault jumps the queue.

### 2. Measuring Form without Score

#### Metrics

|Metric|What it measures|How|When|
|---|---|---|---|
|Execution rate (% A)|Whether you executed the process|Grade every arrow A/B/C on feel _before_ checking where it landed|Every form session|
|Called-tail ratio|Self-awareness, and whether tails come from form or equipment|Of arrows outside your group, what % did you grade B or C?|Weekly|
|Group-relative outliers|Variance, independent of sight marks|Count arrows beyond a fixed distance from the _group_ centre, not the target centre|Every session|
|Group size vs group offset|Precision (form) vs accuracy (sight setting)|From arrow plots: spread around the group's centre. An offset group is a sight-mark issue, not a form issue|Weekly|
|Shot timing (anchor to release)|Repeatability of tempo|From video timestamps: median and spread, comparing A shots with tail shots|Fortnightly|
|Let-down count|Discipline (a higher count early on is good)|Tally|Every session|
|Effort and shoulder|Load and fatigue|Rate effort 1–10 at the end; note any shoulder niggles|Every session|

Set the outlier threshold once, from your current data, so that roughly your worst 10–15% of arrows count as outliers. Then freeze it.

#### Rules for Getting Signal rather than Noise

- Compare like with like: same distance, same face, same arrow count.
- Judge on at least three sessions. Never on one.
- Make one change at a time and label it in the log. You already do this; keep doing it.
- Use a fixed video protocol:
    - Film from two angles: from behind, and side-on from the shooting line. Never film from in front of the line, and only with the club's agreement.
    - Mark the tripod positions with tape so every recording is comparable.
    - Review only against the current block's checklist, with three items at most.
    - Timebox reviews to 20 minutes. Open-ended review is a perfectionism trap: you'll find 15 faults and try to fix all of them.

### 3. Self-coaching Feedback Loops

#### Shot Calling

Grade each arrow, then cross-check the grade against where it landed:

| Good result          | Bad result               |                                          |
| -------------------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- |
| Good execution (A)   | The process works        | Look at equipment, aim or wind, not form |
| Poor execution (B/C) | Luck; don't reinforce it | Expected; note which checkpoint broke    |

The key signal is un-called tails: arrows you honestly graded A that still left the group. They point to a tune or spine problem, or to a fault you can't feel yet. Those are the arrows to look at on video.

#### Checkpoints

|Checkpoint|Good execution|Poor execution|
|---|---|---|
|Draw hand at release|Moves back along the jaw and neck as the fingers relax; finishes in the same place|Flies out sideways (pluck), creeps forward, or stops dead|
|Bow arm|Holds its line, with slight movement towards the target|Drops, swings, or collapses|
|Bow after release|Rocks forward into the sling, centred|Kicks sideways (torque), or you catch it (grip)|
|Feel|The shot happens _during_ continuous effort|The shot happens from a static hold, or you deliberately "let go"|
|Anchor|Same contact points, same string picture|Hunting for the anchor; string picture shifts|
|Aim|Pin floats around the centre and you release while it floats|Chasing the gold, snapping as the pin crosses it, long holds|
|Timing|Inside your usual window|Long hold; that should have been a let-down|

#### Rough Directional Tendencies

These assume a right-handed archer; mirror them if you shoot left-handed. They are tendencies, not rules, and equipment can produce all of them.

- Low: creep, or the bow arm dropping.
- High: heel pressure on the grip.
- Left: a plucked release, or the bow arm collapsing.

#### Tail Review on Video

- Film the whole session from a tripod.
- Afterwards, watch only the outlier arrows alongside two A arrows from the same angle.
- Spend 10 minutes, then stop.
- This is "rescue the bad arrows" turned into a feedback loop.

### 4. Practice Structure and Mindset

#### Weekly Shape

- Form sessions most of the time.
- One score session a week, used for badges and the Portsmouth 450 attempt.
- Two or three 10–15 minute band or SPT sessions at home.

#### Form Session Template (About 90–120 Minutes, 60–75 Arrows, 3-arrow Ends)

|Phase|Time|Arrows|Content|Rule|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|1. Physical prep|10 min|0|Band pull-aparts, shoulder rotation, 3–5 band draws to anchor|Identical every time|
|2. Blank boss|10–15 min|12–15|3–5 m, no face, block cue only; a few eyes-closed if club rules allow|No aiming|
|3. Bridge|10 min|6–9|Full distance, blank paper or reversed face; call every shot|No scoring|
|4. Process block|30–40 min|24–30|Normal face; grade A/B/C and plot each arrow|One cue; let down whenever a checkpoint fails|
|5. Transfer|10–15 min|9–12|Scored ends with your full routine|Process cue only; no technical changes|
|6. Close|5 min|3|Blank boss; finish on an A shot|Then stop|
|7. Log|2 min|n/a|% A, outliers, let-downs, effort, one sentence|Three lines at most|

That is roughly 80% unscored and 20% scored.

#### Score Session Template

- Warm-up: the same prep, plus 6 blank-boss arrows, plus sighters.
    - This doubles as a test of your first-dozen hypothesis: does a structured warm-up close the gap?
- During the round: one cue, "routine". No technique work.
- If shots start falling apart: switch to a pre-chosen "safe mode" cue. Choose it yourself in advance; "soft bow hand, hold the finish" is one example.
- Between ends: only the A/B/C grade. All analysis happens afterwards.

#### Heuristics for an Analytical Perfectionist

1. Separate analysing from shooting. Off the line you analyse; on the line you shoot. Between ends you get one word and one grade, and everything else waits for the log.
2. Write every change down before you make it. Keep a change card:

    - Change:
    - Why (what you observed):
    - Lead metric expected to improve:
    - Dip you'll tolerate (for example, up to X handicap points):
    - Review date (at least 3–4 weeks, or about 8 sessions):
    - Decision rule:
        - Lead metric up: keep the change, whatever the score.
        - Lead metric flat and score down: revert, or get a coach to watch.

    This gives your analytical side a legitimate job, which is designing the experiment, and keeps it away from in-the-moment verdicts.

3. Score is a lagging indicator. Inside a trial window it is not decision data.
4. Grade the shot, not the arrow.
5. Blocks end on dates, not on perfection. Good enough moves on.
6. Limit your inputs. Use one coaching vocabulary. New YouTube ideas go into a "Year 2 inbox" note; capturing an idea there gives you permission not to act on it.
7. Put the dip in proportion. You reached A2, Portsmouth 425 and an A1-level 70 m score within five months, so the base is sound. Badges already in hand stay in hand, and a few weeks of dip costs nothing permanent.
