> [!info] Tier 1—Parent-only (Leon)
> Preparation note for bringing Zofja on board as co-System Administrator. Do not show this note to Zofja or the children. The note she sees is [[Family Chores - Everyone's Guide]].

## Outcome—Definition Of Done

Zofja has read the [[Family Chores - Everyone's Guide]], agreed the audit times and Tag-Team protocol, and completed one full week as co-Inspector without rescuing a failed task. She experiences the system as a relief, not a criticism.

---

## 1. Why the Usual Pitch Fails

- Her background (hoarder household next door, mess still physically present) means "standards" language lands as criticism of her and her family of origin.
- Per [[SoT - Family Communication & Team Charter]], she runs on the Immediacy Value: caving to the children's resistance is her fastest route to stress relief. Lecturing her about long-term systems reads as "I'm drowning and he's lecturing."
- Therefore: never sell the system as raising standards. Sell it as reducing her load and her conflict exposure.

## 2. The Framing (What to Say)

1. Sanctuary, not standards. Use the vault's own language: a calm, restful sanctuary; fewer arguments; more free time ([[SoT - Family Chores Purpose and Values]] §2). Avoid the words "standards", "mess", "discipline".
2. The system is the bad guy. Wi-Fi and screens are locked by the Audit Gate, not by her. "Work = Access" means she never has to argue, negotiate, or be the villain ([[SoT - Family Household Governance]]).
3. Scripts replace improvisation. The Grace Period gives her one fixed sentence ("Just a reminder—we are checking chores in 30 minutes"); the Failure Autopsy gives her three fixed questions. She never has to invent a response mid-conflict ([[Family Chores Operational Plan]]).
4. The Tap-Out is her safety valve. If the children's pushback saturates her, she taps out and I take full authority over the process—validated by the Parental SLA ([[SoT - Family Communication & Team Charter]] Protocol A).
5. Her invisible work is on the map. The [[Family Job Categories - Research]] Ownership Map shows the children that Mum's quieter jobs count as jobs. This defuses "why do I have to do it?" and makes her labour visible instead of assumed.

## 3. Conversation Architecture

Follow the four-step sequence from [[SoT - Family Communication & Team Charter]] §4:

1. Validate identity: "We're both good parents; this isn't about either of us failing."
2. State the meta-problem: "We have different definitions of what helping looks like—you fix the stress now, I build systems. Both are right; they clash."
3. Use the Invalidation-Stalemate breaker if she minimises: she doesn't have to agree the mess is a problem, only that the stress is real and we help each other when stressed.
4. Invite collaboration: her voice shapes the audit times, the privilege list, and the guide itself ("this guide can change" is already in the family note).

Timing: private, calm, away from the children and away from any live mess. Not after a school-day meltdown.

## 4. What She Sees and Agrees

- Show her only [[Family Chores - Everyone's Guide]]—one page, plain English, already includes her.
- Ask her to co-decide the open loops from [[Family Chores Operational Plan]] §4: audit times (18:00 daily / Sunday 17:00), the exact privilege list, and access-control mechanics. Deciding these with her makes it our system, not my system imposed on her.
- Agree the two non-negotiables for her role: No Rescue (she does not perform a child's failed task) and Grey Rock (complaints get the neutral script, not debate). Frame both as her protection: they end the arguments, she doesn't fight them.

## 5. Onboarding Sequence (Micro-Steps)

1. Print one copy of [[Family Chores - Everyone's Guide]].
2. Book 30 undisturbed minutes with Zofja (no kids, no mess in view).
3. Open with the Section 3 script; hand her the printed guide.
4. Ask her to pick the daily and Sunday audit times.
5. Agree the privilege list together; write it down.
6. Rehearse the two scripts aloud once each (reminder + Grey Rock line).
7. Agree the Tap-Out signal (a word or gesture) and what I do when she uses it.
8. Run one practice week with me as lead Inspector and her observing.
9. Week two: she leads one daily check-in with me present.
10. Friday of week two: 10-minute private retro—what felt hard, what to change.

## 6. Failure Modes to Watch

- She caves at check-in → do not criticise in front of the children (Parental SLA: back-end only). Raise it at the retro; treat it as a Failure Autopsy on the system, not on her.
- She experiences the audit as her being audited → re-anchor: parents are Inspectors of the children's checklists; her own work is on the Ownership Map as already done.
- Pushback spike from the kids in week one → expected entropy per the No Rescue protocol; pre-warn her so it doesn't feel like the system failing.
