## Purpose

Supporting research for [[Family Chores Operational Plan]]. Household chores are only one slice of the total work a family does. This note maps the whole set of "jobs" a family runs—paid work, logistics, education, admin, and the invisible mental/emotional labor—so the operational plan can eventually account for the full system, not just physical chores.

---

## 1. Income Generation

- Primary employment/salary (Dad and/or Mum).
- Side income, freelance, or contract work.
- Managing investments, pensions, and long-term savings.
- Kids' pocket money/allowance systems tied to contribution (already partially defined in the operational plan).

---

## 2. Household Chores & Home Maintenance

Standard physical upkeep—the layer already covered in the operational plan and Master Household Chores Inventory:

- Cleaning (kitchen, bathrooms, bedrooms, shared zones).
- Laundry (washing, drying, folding, putting away).
- Bins, recycling, and outdoor/garden work.
- Home repairs, appliance servicing, gutters/HVAC, light bulbs, smoke alarm checks.
- Vehicle maintenance: fuel, servicing, insurance/tax renewal, cleaning.

---

## 3. Meal Planning & Food Logistics

- Weekly menu planning and grocery list building.
- Grocery shopping (in-store or online).
- Cooking, packing school lunches, replenishing canteen accounts.
- Pantry/fridge stock management.

---

## 4. Education & School Logistics

- School drop-off and pick-up.
- Homework support and tracking assignments/deadlines.
- Attending parents' evenings, IEP/SEN meetings.
- Managing school communications, permission slips, and admin forms.
- Researching, enrolling in, and paying for extracurricular clubs, tutoring, and summer camps.

---

## 5. Transport & Scheduling ("The Family Taxi")

- Driving kids to clubs, activities, and playdates.
- Coordinating whose turn it is to drive (car-share/rota logistics).
- Keeping the shared family calendar up to date and resolving clashes between siblings' activities.

---

## 6. Health & Medical Management

- Booking and attending GP, dentist, and optician appointments.
- Managing prescriptions and over-the-counter medication.
- Handling sick days, medical emergencies, and school medical notifications.
- Tracking vaccination/certificate renewals (sports medicals, insurance).

---

## 7. Finance & Household Administration

- Paying bills and budgeting.
- Managing bank accounts, direct debits, subscriptions, and renewals.
- Filing tax returns and dealing with official correspondence/mail.
- Insurance management (home, car, life, health).

---

## 8. Social, Emotional & Relationship Labor

This is the least visible category and the one most often skewed toward one parent—worth naming explicitly so it can be shared rather than assumed (research on "mental load"/cognitive labor consistently finds this):

- Emotional regulation and support during meltdowns, transitions, and conflicts.
- Maintaining family relationships (grandparents, extended family, friend groups).
- Planning family time: date nights, quiet evenings, outings.
- Discipline and behavioural consistency across parents.
- "Meta-parenting"—holding the long-term view of each child's development and adjusting the parenting approach over time.

---

## 9. Special Occasions & Seasonal Planning

- Birthdays, holidays, and gift-giving (buying, wrapping, cards) for immediate and extended family.
- Planning and booking family holidays/travel.
- Organising children's birthday parties.
- Preparing for the start of the school year (uniforms, supplies, enrolments).

---

## 10. Pet Care

Not hypothetical for us: one dog with Addison's disease, one cat, and two snakes. Splits into two tiers (per [[For pet care we have a dog who has addisons diseas]]):

- Parent-only medical tier (non-delegable):
    - Dog's daily steroid tablets, given every morning with food.
    - Monthly vet-administered hormone injection (recurring calendar appointment).
    - Stress management and pre-emptive vet consultation before known stressors (fireworks, boarding).
    - Red-flag monitoring: lethargy, vomiting, diarrhoea, collapse → same-day vet contact.
    - Medication safety: no NSAIDs (Rimadyl, Metacam, Meloxicam); vet-approved pain relief only.
    - Regular electrolyte blood tests.
- Shared routine tier (children can own or rotate):
    - Cat: feeding morning/evening, litter tray, grooming.
    - Snakes: daily temperature/humidity checks, feeding every 1–2 weeks (calendar-tracked), water and substrate cleaning, shed monitoring.
- Boarding/cover during travel: parent-arranged — the Addison's medication handover makes this higher-stakes than typical boarding.

Family-facing version of this split lives in [[Family Chores - Everyone's Guide]].

## Frequency View

Categorizing by how often tasks recur helps decide what belongs on a daily chore chart versus a monthly/seasonal planning cadence:

| Frequency | Examples |
|:--------- |:-------- |
| Constant/daily | School run, meal prep, emotional check-ins, tidying |
| Weekly | Grocery shop, laundry, homework review, activity transport |
| Monthly | Budget review, medical/dental bookings, decluttering |
| Seasonal/yearly | School enrolments, holiday planning, tax returns, birthday/gift planning |

## Whole-Family Ownership Map

The chore chart only shows the children's slice of the system. The rest of the family's work is invisible to them, which is precisely what fuels "why do I have to do it?" arguments — the children can only see their own contribution, so any demand looks arbitrary. The fix is transparency: publish the full map so every family member's load is visible.

Ownership below reflects what is currently documented in the vault. Items marked _TBC_ need to be agreed (Mum/Dad split) at a family or parental meeting — do not let them silently default to one parent.

| # | Job category | Who carries it today | Visible to the kids? |
|:- |:------------ |:-------------------- |:-------------------- |
| 1 | Income generation | Mum & Dad | Should be — name it in the guide |
| 2 | Household chores | Whole family: children rotate the three weekly nodes; parents own repairs, appliances, vehicle, monthly infrastructure | Kids' slice yes; parents' slice now added to the guide |
| 3 | Meal planning & food | Mum & Dad (split TBC) | Now in guide |
| 4 | Education & school logistics | Mum & Dad (split TBC) | Partially |
| 5 | Transport & scheduling | Mum & Dad (split TBC) | Now in guide |
| 6 | Health & medical | Mum & Dad; dog's Addison's care strictly parent-only | Yes (guide) |
| 7 | Finance & admin | Mum & Dad (split TBC) | Now in guide |
| 8 | Social & emotional labour | Mum & Dad (split TBC — highest risk of silent single-parent default) | Hard to show, but name it |
| 9 | Occasions & seasonal | Mum & Dad (split TBC) | Now in guide |
| 10 | Pet care | Two tiers: parents (dog medical), children shared (cat, snakes) | Yes (guide) |

Principle: **every category has a named owner, and the whole map is public within the family.** Fairness challenges are then answered by pointing at the map, not by relitigating each task.

## Why This Matters for the Operational Plan

The current [[Family Chores Operational Plan]] focuses on physical household chores distributed among the children (kitchen, bathrooms, bins/car). The categories above show that:

1. Parents carry several additional "invisible" job categories (mental load, finance, transport, medical, emotional labor) that don't appear on a chore chart but still need explicit ownership or a Tag-Team Handover protocol, consistent with [[SoT - Family Communication & Team Charter]].
2. Some categories (education support, transport, special occasions) could be partially delegated to older children as they mature—extending the "Earned Access" model beyond physical chores.
3. Naming the mental/emotional labor category explicitly reduces the risk it silently defaults to one parent, which research shows is the most common failure mode in dual-earner households.
4. Making the parents' job categories visible to the children directly defuses the recurring "why do I have to do it?" argument: the answer stops being an assertion of authority and becomes an observable fact — everyone in the house has jobs, every day, and the children's rotation is a small, age-sized share of the total. This is the transparency principle now reflected in [[Family Chores - Everyone's Guide]].
