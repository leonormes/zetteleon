## Social Judgment Theory's Latitude Recalibration

Social Judgment Theory holds that people sort incoming political information into a latitude of acceptance, a latitude of noncommitment, and a latitude of rejection relative to their existing attitudes. An extreme anchor figure stretches the whole scale, broadening the latitude of acceptance to absorb behaviour that would otherwise fall into noncommitment or rejection.

### Scope & Conditions

Pioneered by Muzafer Sherif. Applies when the extreme figure is judged to fall deep within the evaluator's latitude of rejection, which is what stretches the rest of the scale. The latitude of noncommitment shrinks correspondingly, forcing more binary in-group/out-group categorisation.

### Evidence

> "When an individual encounters an extreme political figure whose actions fall deep within their latitude of rejection, it triggers an intense contrast effect… the voter's internal latitude of acceptance undergoes a structural broadening."

### Implications

- Neutrality becomes psychologically harder to sustain as the latitude of noncommitment shrinks.
- Routine manipulation by an in-group politician can migrate from "unacceptable" to "acceptable" purely via this recalibration, without the politician's behaviour changing.
