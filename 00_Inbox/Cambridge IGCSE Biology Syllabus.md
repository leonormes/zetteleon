Here is a detailed outline of the **21 topics** covered in the Cambridge IGCSE Biology syllabus. To make this easier to digest, I have grouped them into five logical themes.

# 1. The Building Blocks of Life (Topics 1–5)

This section establishes the foundation for understanding biology.

- **1. Characteristics and classification:** Defining what makes something "alive" (e.g., respiration, reproduction) and how we name and group species.
- **2. Organisation of the organism:** Understanding cell structures (like the nucleus and mitochondria) and different levels of organisation (tissues, organs, systems).
- **3. Movement in and out of cells:** How substances move via diffusion, osmosis, and active transport.
- **4. Biological molecules:** The chemistry of life, including carbohydrates, fats, proteins, DNA, and how to test for them.
- **5. Enzymes:** How biological catalysts work, and how temperature and pH affect their activity.

# 2. Energy and Transport (Topics 6–9)

This section covers how organisms get energy and move materials around.

- **6. Plant nutrition:** Photosynthesis, leaf structure, and the mineral requirements of plants.
- **7. Human nutrition:** Diet, the digestive system, and how we break down and absorb food.
- **8. Transport in plants:** How xylem and phloem transport water, minerals, and sugars (transpiration and translocation).
- **9. Transport in animals:** The heart, blood vessels, and blood components (red/white cells, platelets).

# 3. Body Systems and Health (Topics 10–15)

This section focuses on human physiology and maintenance.

- **10. Diseases and immunity:** Pathogens, transmission, body defences, and vaccination.
- **11. Gas exchange in humans:** The structure of the lungs and the mechanics of breathing.
- **12. Respiration:** How cells break down nutrients to release energy (aerobic vs. anaerobic respiration).
- **13. Excretion in humans:** How the body removes toxic waste, focusing on the kidneys and liver.
- **14. Coordination and response:** The nervous system (reflexes, the eye) and the endocrine system (hormones like adrenaline and insulin), plus plant tropisms.
- **15. Drugs:** The effects of medicinal drugs (antibiotics) and misused drugs on the body.

# 4. Genetics and Continuity (Topics 16–18)

This section explores how life is passed down and changes over time.

- **16. Reproduction:** Asexual vs. sexual reproduction in plants and humans, including detailed anatomy and sex hormones.
- **17. Inheritance:** Chromosomes, genes, protein synthesis, and how traits are passed on (Monohybrid crosses, Mitosis/Meiosis).
- **18. Variation and selection:** Natural selection, evolution, and selective breeding.

# 5. Ecology and Technology (Topics 19–21)

This section looks at the wider world and human intervention.

- **19. Organisms and their environment:** Energy flow, food chains/webs, and nutrient cycles (Carbon and Nitrogen).
- **20. Human influences on ecosystems:** Food production, habitat destruction, pollution, and conservation.
- **21. Biotechnology and genetic modification:** Using bacteria for industry (biofuels, bread) and genetic engineering (modifying crops, producing insulin).

**Note on Depth:** Remember that **Core** candidates cover the fundamentals of these topics, while **Extended** candidates must learn additional details (Supplement material), such as specific chemical equations for respiration or the detailed function of the kidney nephron.
