While there is no single document that covers every aspect of networking, the technical requirements for using NHS patient data for research are detailed across several key regulatory and infrastructure documents.

## Key Technical and Infrastructure Documents

- HSCN Connection Agreement: This is the mandatory legal and technical document that sets out the terms and conditions for any organization connecting to the Health and Social Care Network (HSCN). It establishes the framework for security, integrity, and performance, including acceptable use and responsibilities for personal data transmitted over the network.
- HSCN Technical Security Standards (TSS): These standards provide the granular networking requirements for organizations handling patient data. Key standards include:
    - TSS 201: Network security architecture, including segmentation and monitoring.
    - TSS 202: Secure remote access capabilities for staff.
    - TSS 203: Data encryption requirements both in transit and at rest.
    - TSS 205: Protective monitoring and automated incident response.
- Secure Data Environment (SDE) Policy Guidelines and Technical Specification: Informed by the "Data Saves Lives" strategy, these documents define the technical rules for platforms providing research access to data. The "full technical specification" covers requirements for interoperability, cyber security, and the use of privacy-enhancing technologies within research workspaces.

## Compliance and Regulatory Frameworks

- Data Security and Protection Toolkit (DSPT): This is the primary self-assessment tool used to measure an organization's performance against the National Data Guardian's 10 Data Security Standards. Achieving a "Standards Met" or "Standards Exceeded" status is a mandatory requirement for connecting to NHS systems and accessing patient data.
- "Data Saves Lives" Strategy (2022): This is the overarching policy document that mandates the shift from "data sharing" (physical transfer) to "data access" via Secure Data Environments. It sets the 11 draft guidelines that serve as the technical foundation for all future research networking.
- Data (Use and Access) Act 2025: This legislation introduced mandatory information standards for IT suppliers to ensure data can flow safely and seamlessly through the healthcare system using standardized structures.

For research involving international networking across GDPR boundaries, the ICO's 2026 Updated Guidance on International Transfers provides a "three-step test" and a "data protection test" (formerly Transfer Risk Assessment) to ensure the level of protection in the destination jurisdiction is "not materially lower" than in the UK.
