This is a systemic issue, not a moral failure. You are operating on a "High-Frequency Trading" algorithm (seeking immediate liquidity/dopamine) while trying to build a long-term "Asset Portfolio" (life satisfaction/duty). The two systems use different currencies and operate on different time horizons.

Here is the architectural breakdown of why you feel this way, the difference in "feeling" you are asking about, and how to bridge the gap.

1. The Core Conflict: Hedonia vs. Eudaimonia
In psychological frameworks, there are two distinct types of well-being. Your ADHD brain is hardwired for the first, but you are craving the second.
 - Hedonia (The Sugar Rush):
   - Goal: Maximise pleasure, minimise pain now.
   - Mechanism: Driven by Phasic Dopamine. This is a sharp spike in neurotransmitters caused by novel stimuli, sugar, gaming, or risk.
   - System Behaviour: High amplitude, short duration. It crashes quickly, leading to the "emptiness" you feel. It requires constantly increasing inputs to maintain the same output (tolerance).
   - ADHD Vulnerability: The ADHD brain has a baseline deficit in tonic dopamine, making it hungry for these phasic spikes just to feel "normal."
 - Eudaimonia (The Slow Burn):
   - Goal: Meaning, self-realisation, and acting in accordance with deeply held values (duty).
   - Mechanism: Driven by Serotonin and Tonic Dopamine. This is a background hum of satisfaction rather than a sharp spike.
   - System Behaviour: Low amplitude, long duration. It is stable and compounding. It does not crash; it supports.
2. Does Duty "Feel" the Same?
No. The neurochemistry is different.
You are asking if a life of responsibility gives you the same "buzz" as your current dopamine sources. It does not. If you expect duty to feel like a video game win or a sugar rush, you will always be disappointed.
 - The Hedonic Feeling: Thrill, agitation, hyper-focus, relief. It is "loud."
 - The Eudaimonic (Duty) Feeling: Quietness, resonance, alignment, solidity. It is the absence of the background noise of guilt and anxiety.
The "Software Architect" Analogy:
 - Hedonic life feels like hot-fixing a crashing server. It’s high-adrenaline, frantic, and you feel like a hero for a split second when the green light comes back on. But the code is spaghetti, and you know it will crash again in 10 minutes.
 - Duty/Values-based life feels like running a system with clean, well-documented code. There is no adrenaline. There is just the quiet satisfaction of watching the system handle the load without buckling. It is boring in the micro, but deeply satisfying in the macro.
3. The "Interest-Based" Nervous System
Neurotypical brains have an Importance-Based nervous system. They can see a task is "important" (duty) and that creates enough neurochemical motivation to do it.
You have an Interest-Based nervous system. Importance does not trigger your dopamine receptors. Only four things typically do:
 - Interest (Fascination)
 - Novelty (Newness)
 - Challenge (Difficulty)
 - Urgency (Deadline/Panic)
"Duty" and "Responsibility" are rarely interesting, novel, or urgent until it is too late. This is why you feel you have "reduced life to just feeling good"—your hardware requires high-octane fuel that "duty" rarely provides.
4. How to Bridge the Gap (The Patch)
You cannot simply "decide" to feel good about duty. You must re-engineer the inputs so your system recognises duty as a valid dopamine source.
A. Values as Constraints, Not Goals
Don't view duty as a "goal" to achieve. View it as an architectural constraint.
 - Current State: "I need to feel good, so I will do X."
 - Target State: "I will do X, provided it fits within the constraints of my values."
B. The "Eudaimonic Dopamine" Hack
Since your brain demands dopamine, you must artificially attach dopamine to duty.
 - Visualise the Micro-Win: Do not focus on "being a responsible person" (too abstract). Focus on "clearing this specific error log" (concrete).
 - The "Done" List: ADHD brains often forget what they achieved 5 minutes ago. Keep a visible log of "Duty Tasks" completed. Reviewing this list provides a small serotonin hit of "I am competent."
C. Reframing "Boring" as "Safe"
When you feel the itch for a dopamine spike (boredom), reframe it. That boredom is not a bug; it is a feature of a stable system. Learn to sit with the "quiet" of duty and label it as Safety rather than Stagnation.
