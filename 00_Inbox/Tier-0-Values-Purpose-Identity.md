## Tier 0: Values, Purpose & Identity

### Why "Better" Needs a Target

The productivity literature (GTD, timeboxing, habit loops) optimises for _execution_. None of it defines the aim. Optimising with no answer to "towards what" is the trap itself.

This tier answers the antecedent question that Tiers 1–4 depend on but never address:

- Why do you strive?
- Why the guilt when you don't?
- Why does success stop feeling good?
- What is better, and why is it better?

---

### The Mechanics of Wanting

#### Why You Strive at All

Three real levels. They're not competing—they're concurrent.

Evolutionary: Status-tracking was substrate for resource and mate access. Not a choice you made; it's substrate.

Psychological (Self-Determination Theory): Deci & Ryan identify three intrinsic needs:

- _Competence_—mastery is intrinsically rewarding, not just instrumentally useful
- _Autonomy_—agency in choices
- _Relatedness_—connection to others

You can't ignore these by willpower. They're wired.

Existential (Terror Management Theory): Greenberg, Pyszczynski, Solomon built on Becker: achievement functions as a symbolic-immortality buffer against death-anxiety. (Recent large replications find this effect smaller than early studies claimed, but it's real.)

#### Why the Guilt

Higgins' Self-Discrepancy Theory: Guilt specifically arises from the gap between your _actual self_ (what you are) and your _ought self_ (what you believe you should be).

The real question: whose "ought"?

- Secular inheritance: Weber traced how Protestant anxiety about salvation got secularised into "idleness is moral failure." People with no religious belief still inherit this.
- ADHD specifically: Barkley reframes ADHD as a _performance_ disorder, not a knowledge disorder. You know what to do and still don't—and that visible gap reads as character failure to you and everyone watching, when it's a _production_ problem, not a moral one. That's exactly the distinction between capability and execution.

#### Why the Good Feeling Fades

Hedonic Adaptation (Brickman & Campbell): Affect doesn't track absolute state—it tracks _change against your adapting baseline_. You're always recalibrating. Later work (Diener, Lucas & Scollon) shows the treadmill isn't perfectly flat, but adaptation is real.

Dopamine and Prediction Error: Dopamine fires on the _gap_ between expected and actual outcome, not the outcome itself. Once you expect success, achieving it stops registering as reward. So the target has to keep moving, or the reward machinery goes silent.

> This is why finishing a task you assumed you'd finish feels like nothing. The dopamine already spent itself predicting it.

### What You're Actually Optimising Towards

#### Purpose

No single answer. Three real positions:

Sartre (Existentialism): No built-in purpose. It's authored through your choices. Anxiety comes from the freedom of authorship.

Frankl (Logo therapy): Meaning isn't abstract—it's found through work, love, or the _stance you take towards unavoidable suffering_. Meaning is always available; purpose is often smaller and more everyday than career frameworks make it sound.

Camus (Absurdism): No cosmic purpose exists. The right response isn't despair or false hope—it's engaging anyway.

_Note on Ikigai:_ The Western "love/good-at/paid-for/world-needs" Venn diagram is a commercial flattening. The original concept is smaller and more everyday than a career framework.

#### Values

Schwartz's Theory of Basic Human Values is empirically validated across dozens of countries. Four higher-order groups sit in genuine, measured tension:

- _Achievement & Power_ ↔ _Benevolence & Universalism_
- _Self-Direction_ ↔ _Conformity & Security_

Over-indexing on achievement structurally crowds out connection, not just as a feeling—it's a tension in the design itself.

ACT (Acceptance & Commitment Therapy) framing: Values are _directions_, never destinations. A value is a verb ("being present"), not a noun ("success"). This is exactly why finishing tasks can never satisfy a values-question—wrong category of thing entirely.

#### Identity

McAdams: Identity is the evolving internal story integrating your past and imagined future. It's narrative, not fixed.

Self-Complexity Theory (more practical): The more independent self-aspects you hold, the more a hit to one is buffered by the others. You have several: engineer, archer, teacher, father. This isn't overhead—it's protective architecture.

---

### Your Signal (Right Now)

Connection—care, contribution pulls hardest in you.

In Schwartz's structure, that sits directly opposite the achievement/power quadrant. This isn't a minor preference—it's what the whole striving apparatus needs pointing at.

When you strive, it feels like yours. Not an inherited obligation. That rules out the Weber-style mechanism. Combined with the ADHD performance-gap: if the desire is genuinely yours and the friction is still there, the friction is _production_, not motivation.

> The machinery in Tiers 1–4 is contribution-agnostic. It optimises for "more done," not "more done that reaches someone." Same tools. Different aim required.

---

### How This Reframes Tier 1–4

Tiers 1–4 aren't made obsolete by having a target. They're given one.

- Externalising executive function (3.6) stops being a personal productivity hack and becomes a _commons practice_—freeing your attention so you can actually pay it forward.
- Granular action translation (4.8) isn't an optimisation trick. It's what lets you do work that connects cleanly to the people it reaches.
- Iterative reflection (4.11) isn't ego-driven review. It's adjustment to the gap between what you intended to contribute and what actually landed.

The loop closes: Tier 0 gives Tiers 1–4 a direction. Tiers 1–4 give Tier 0 a route.

---

### Immediate Action

Pick one thing on your plate right now—something at FITFILE, an exam script, something for one of your daughters.

Finish this sentence:

> _"This matters because it [helps/protects/reaches] ___."_

Name the person, not the deliverable. That's the whole action. Just that.
