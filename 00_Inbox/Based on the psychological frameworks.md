## Primary Psychological Drivers

To understand the underlying logic of these interruptions, we can categorise the behaviour into three distinct models:

- **Dominance-Driven Control:** In this framework, interrupting is a calculated or semi-conscious power move. By cutting you off, the manager asserts higher social status and "grabs control of the emotional temperature in the room". This is particularly relevant in your weekly whole-company meetings, where the presence of an audience might trigger a need to establish authority.
- **Anxiety-Driven "Queue Jumping":** The manager may be experiencing "conversational impatience". This occurs when a person fears they will lose their train of thought or be judged as "slow" if they do not speak immediately. Their nervous system perceives a potential silence or a missed point as a threat, leading them to "yank the curtain shut" on your contribution to stay safe.
- **Neurological Timing Blindness:** This is a "glitch in social timing" rather than a malicious act. Some individuals struggle to read micro-pauses or the subtle inhales that indicate a speaker is not yet finished. In this case, the manager is misreading "conversational traffic lights" and genuinely believes it is their turn to speak.

## Strategic Response Framework

To decode the specific driver, the text suggests observing the pattern rather than the single event:

1. **Map the Pattern:** Does the manager interrupt everyone, or only those junior to them? If they fall silent when their own boss speaks, the driver is **dominance**. If they interrupt everyone regardless of rank, it is likely **anxiety or timing blindness**.
2. **Boundary Re-establishment:** Use a neutral, non-sarcastic phrase such as, **"Hang on, I’d like to finish my thought"**. This provides real-time feedback and signals that a boundary exists.
3. **Strategic Pausing:** Do not try to "outrun" the interruption by speaking faster. Instead, slow down and allow a brief silence after asserting your boundary to reset the conversational rhythm.
4. **Identify Trigger Topics:** Note if the interruptions spike during specific subjects, such as feedback or performance. Managers often interrupt to avoid letting "someone else’s reality land," especially if that reality is confronting.

## Real-Time Tactical Scripts

When an interruption occurs in the moment, your response should be immediate, calm, and neutral. This provides the "flash of feedback" required to reset their nervous system's habit.

- **The Assertive Reclaim:** "Hang on, I’d like to finish my thought." Use a steady, level tone to signal a boundary exists.
- **The Collaborative Bridge:** "I have two more points to complete the logic on this; let me finish those, then I’d really like to hear your view."
- **The Strategic Pause:** If they interrupt, stop speaking immediately. Once they finish, wait two seconds of silence before saying, "Coming back to the point I was finishing..." This demonstrates you are not participating in their "unconscious race".

## Environmental Controls for 1-to-1s

If the manager’s driver is **anxiety** (fear of losing the thread), you can use external structures to reduce their cognitive load:

- **Pre-meeting Agendas:** Send a high-level framework of your points via email before the 1-to-1. If they have the data beforehand, their "nervous system" may feel safer waiting for you to finish.
- **Visual Aids:** Use a shared document or a notepad. If they see you have a list of three points, they are more likely to wait until point three is checked off before intervening.
- **The 'Two-Second' Rule:** Suggest a pilot where you both agree to wait two seconds after the other finishes before responding. This gives their brain space to choose their words more sharply.
