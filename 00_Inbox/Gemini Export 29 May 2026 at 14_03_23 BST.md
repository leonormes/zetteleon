## 1. Equation Manipulation: Key Concepts and Practice

In GCSE mathematics, moving symbols around an equation to isolate a specific variable (like solving for $x$) is formally called rearranging formulae or changing the subject. For Bessie, this is a core skill within the Algebra section of the Edexcel Foundation specification, which typically accounts for 17–23% of the total marks.

The "golden rule" she should keep in mind is to always perform the same operation to both sides of the equation to keep it balanced.

### Core Skills for Rearranging Formulae

- Inverse Operations: To "move" a symbol, you do the opposite of what is currently happening to it:
  - To move a $+$, you $-$.
  - To move a $-$, you $+$.
  - To move a $\times$, you $\div$.
  - To move a $\div$, you $\times$.
- Order of Operations (BODMAS): When rearranging, you often work in the reverse order of BODMAS to "unpeel" the layers around the variable you want to isolate.


2. Bessie's "Equation Challenge" Sheet (Foundation Level)

These problems are designed to practice the _skill_ of manipulation without being as mathematically complex as the videos you watched.

### Level 1: One-Step Manipulation (Inverse Operations)

- Goal: Move one number to leave $x$ on its own.
1. $x + 7 = 15$ _(Hint: The opposite of adding 7 is…)_
2. $x - 4 = 10$ _(Hint: The opposite of subtracting 4 is…)_
3. $3x = 12$ _(Hint: $3x$ means $3 \\times x$. The opposite of multiplying by 3 is…)_
4. $\frac{x}{2} = 5$ _(Hint: This means $x \\div 2$. The opposite of dividing by 2 is…)_

### Level 2: Two-Step Manipulation

- Goal: Move the "loose" number first, then move the number attached to $x$.
1. $2x + 3 = 11$
   - Step 1: Subtract 3 from both sides.
   - Step 2: Divide both sides by 2.
1. $5x - 10 = 20$

### Level 3: Changing the Subject (No Numbers)

- Goal: Rearrange the symbols exactly like Andy Maths, but with simpler formulas.
1. Make $a$ the subject of: $a + b = c$
2. Make $y$ the subject of: $x = \frac{y}{z}$


3. Support Strategies for Bessie

- The "Detective" Approach: If she gets stuck, ask her to "investigate" what is currently happening to $x$. Is it being multiplied? Added to? Identifying the operation first makes choosing the inverse operation much easier.
- Verbalizing Logic: Have her explain her steps out loud. Instead of just writing "$-3$", she could say, "I am subtracting 3 from both sides to get rid of the $+3$ next to $x$.".
- Check by Substitution: Once she has a value for $x$, she can "plug" it back into the original equation to see if it works, which builds immediate confidence.
