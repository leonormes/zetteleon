## Inclusion-Exclusion Model of Political Benchmarking

Per Schwarz and Bless's Inclusion/Exclusion Model, when a political figure dominates accessible memory through extreme, norm-breaking behaviour, evaluators exclude that figure from the general category "politicians" and instead treat them as a separate, isolated comparison benchmark—so other politicians are judged relative to that one extreme case rather than as members of the same class.

### Scope & Conditions

Requires the extreme figure to be highly cognitively accessible (e.g. dominating the news cycle). Distinguishes this from a "contagion" reading where one bad actor taints trust in the whole category—see [[Scandal Spillover Contagion Versus Contrast]].

### Evidence

> "They exclude the extreme leader, treating them as a distinct, isolated, and overwhelmingly negative benchmark… other politicians who lie, manipulate the media, or engage in standard political maneuvering are viewed not as corrupt actors degrading the system, but as the comparatively benign, safe alternative."

### Implications

- Explains why ordinary political dishonesty stops being classified as "what politicians do" and starts being read as "not as bad as the real threat."
