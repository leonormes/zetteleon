The video you linked, titled **"Blindsided at Work? Use This One Sentence to Take Your Power Back"** from the channel **The Wizard of Words**, outlines a structured, three-part verbal strategy for responding to public blame or professional ambushes to maintain your status and shift the power dynamic.

The strategy focuses on moving the interaction from an emotional defence to a strategic negotiation.

### The Three-Line Power Framework

The video’s central concept is that your immediate, composed response dictates how the room judges you. By avoiding immediate defence, explaining, or reacting, you force the focus back onto the accuser's intent.

Here is the structured approach:

**1\. Stop the Momentum & Demand Clarity**

- **Line:** "Hang on, exactly what are you saying I did?" \[[01:21](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FFIwuEJC3mg&t=81)\]
- **Function:** This stops the accuser's momentum cold and forces them to repeat their accusation. According to the video, most attackers cannot repeat their accusation without revealing their true motive, allowing the room to see the dynamic in real-time.

**2\. Shift into the Evaluator Seat**

- **Transition:** "I want to be sure I'm understanding your concern correctly." \[[02:09](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FFIwuEJC3mg&t=129)\]
- **Function:** By stating this, you are no longer the accused ('the prey'); you become the composed evaluator, viewing the situation from a position of control and status.

**3\. Reframing: The Core Power Sentence**

- **The Line:** "So tell me specifically what outcome are you actually looking for?" \[[02:30](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FFIwuEJC3mg&t=150)\]
- **Function:** This is the ultimate reframing technique, shifting the entire situation from one of **blame** to one of **strategy**.
	- **"So tell me"** signals leadership and direction. \[[02:49](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FFIwuEJC3mg&t=169)\]
	- **"Specifically"** forces clarity, as passive-aggressive communicators "thrive in fog." \[[02:56](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FFIwuEJC3mg&t=176)\]
	- **"What outcome are you actually looking for?"** forces the accuser to reveal their motive (or lack thereof). Many accusers, the video notes, do not want a solution but rather chaos, attention, or control; cornering them on the desired outcome exposes this to everyone present. \[[03:03](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FFIwuEJC3mg&t=183)\]

The presenter, Dan O'Connor, stresses that these lines should be prepared and rehearsed through the **SPAR method** (Study, Practice, Automate, Respond) so that they can be delivered automatically with composure and zero emotion, preventing your natural instincts from leading to a defensive response.
