## Reality Check & Context

The content provides an accurate, accessible summary of Xenophanes' surviving fragments. The speaker correctly identifies that Xenophanes was not an atheist, but rather an early proponent of monotheism or pantheism who critiqued the human-like gods of Homer and Hesiod.

The video's core practical argument—using ancient scepticism to navigate modern media—is a useful synthesis of established ideas. It effectively updates Pyrrhonian suspension of judgement (_epoché_) as a defence mechanism against the modern attention economy, confirmation bias, and algorithmic rage-baiting.

However, the epistemological claim that 'we can never know anything for sure' is presented somewhat uncritically. While useful as a psychological buffer, global scepticism is a contested philosophical position. The video acknowledges that we can 'know things better' through evidence, but it glosses over how one should evaluate that evidence if all knowledge is strictly relegated to opinion.

## Missing Context

- The paradox of scepticism: The assertion that 'no one can know the truth' is itself presented as a factual claim, a classic philosophical paradox that the video does not address.
