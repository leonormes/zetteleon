### Atoms

#### Atom 1: Clarify Stage Definition

- kind: definition
- statement: The Clarify stage is the executive decision-making bridge that transforms raw "stuff" into actionable projects and usable information.
- scope_and_conditions: Applies to the second stage of the GTD five-stage workflow (Capture, Clarify, Organise, Reflect, Engage).
- evidence: "the 'Clarify' (or processing) stage functions as the critical executive decision-making bridge… where you transform that vague inventory into actionable projects and usable information." (no timestamp)
- implications:
  - Clarifying is about deciding meaning, not immediately executing the work.
  - It serves as a mandatory filter between raw input and the organisational system.
- confidence: high
- tags: [gtd, productivity, decision-making, workflow]

#### Atom 2: Sequential Processing Rule

- kind: procedure
- statement: Process the top item of the "in" tray first and one at a time to prevent "emergency scanning" and distracting context-switching.
- scope_and_conditions: Applied during the clarifying session for all items in a capture container.
- evidence: "Process the top item first… Process one item at a time: This prevents distracting context-switching and forces the requisite attention to make a firm decision." (no timestamp)
- implications:
  - Treating all items as equal prevents urgent/attractive items from causing stagnation of others.
  - Focuses cognitive resources on a single discrete decision.
- confidence: high
- tags: [focus, gtd, processing, attention-management]

#### Atom 3: Decision Finality Constraint

- kind: constraint
- statement: Never return an item to the "in" container once it has been picked up for clarification.
- scope_and_conditions: Applies once a decision-making process for an item has started.
- evidence: "Never put anything back into 'in': Returning an item to your 'in' tray without deciding its fate drains psychological fuel and creates 'decision fatigue'." (no timestamp)
- implications:
  - Eliminates the loop of re-processing the same ambiguous items.
  - Protects psychological energy by forcing a binary decision.
- confidence: high
- tags: [decision-fatigue, gtd, efficiency, discipline]

#### Atom 4: Actionability Filter

- kind: procedure
- statement: Every input must be subjected to a binary "Is it actionable?" test to determine its categorical flow.
- scope_and_conditions: The first question asked for every item during the Clarify stage.
- evidence: "For every single item you clarify, you must apply a rigorous, binary thought process by asking: Is it actionable?" (no timestamp)
- implications:
  - Non-actionable items are diverted to Trash, Incubate (Someday/Maybe), or Reference.
  - Actionable items require further outcome and next action definition.
- confidence: high
- tags: [filtering, logic, gtd, triage]

#### Atom 5: Next Action Specification

- kind: definition
- statement: A "Next Action" is the absolute next physical, visible activity required to move a situation forward.
- scope_and_conditions: Applies to any item determined to be actionable.
- evidence: "You must define the absolute next physical, visible activity required to move the situation forward… must be translated into the physical reality of 'draft email to Bob'." (no timestamp)
- implications:
  - Vague directives (e.g., "set meeting") are rejected as incomplete.
  - Reduces the friction of starting a task by defining the physical entry point.
- confidence: high
- tags: [action-definition, clarity, gtd, execution]

#### Atom 6: Multi-Step Project Rule

- kind: definition
- statement: Any desired outcome that requires more than one step to complete is classified as a "Project" and must be tracked on a master Projects list.
- scope_and_conditions: Applied when defining the desired outcome of an actionable item.
- evidence: "If the commitment takes more than one step to complete, it becomes a 'Project' and must be tracked on a master Projects list." (no timestamp)
- implications:
  - Prevents complex commitments from being lost as single tasks.
  - Ensures a high-level review of all open loops.
- confidence: high
- tags: [gtd, project-management, planning, organisation]

#### Atom 7: Two-Minute Efficiency Heuristic

- kind: heuristic
- statement: If an identified next action takes less than two minutes to complete, perform it immediately instead of deferring or tracking it.
- scope_and_conditions: Applied once the next action has been defined.
- evidence: "Do it: If the action takes less than two minutes, do it immediately. This efficiency cutoff prevents you from spending more time tracking an item than it takes to simply complete it." (no timestamp)
- implications:
  - Reduces the overhead of managing micro-tasks.
  - Prevents the organisational system from becoming cluttered with trivial actions.
- confidence: high
- tags: [productivity, time-management, gtd, efficiency]

#### Atom 8: Separation of Vision and Execution

- kind: distinction
- statement: The psychological states of gathering ideas (Visionary/Capture) and judging ideas (Executive/Clarify) must be kept distinct to prevent system failure.
- scope_and_conditions: Fundamental requirement for avoiding traditional to-do list failure.
- evidence: "Attempting to merge these two distinct psychological states—gathering ideas while simultaneously trying to organize them—is why traditional to-do lists fail." (no timestamp)
- implications:
  - Do not try to organise or clarify while in a "Capture" mode.
  - Executive scrutiny (Clarify) must precede the physical sorting (Organise).
- confidence: high
- tags: [psychology, workflow, gtd, cognition]
