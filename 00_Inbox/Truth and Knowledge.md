## Truth and Knowledge

The question of whether something needs to be true to be considered knowledge is a complex philosophical issue:

**Traditional View**

- The classical definition of knowledge, dating back to Plato, is "justified true belief"

- This suggests that for something to be knowledge, it must be true, believed, and justified.

**Gettier Problems**

- Edmund Gettier challenged this traditional view in 1963 by presenting cases where justified true beliefs did not seem to count as knowledge

- This led to ongoing debates about the nature of knowledge and its relationship to truth.

**Truth as a Condition**

- Most epistemologists maintain that truth is a necessary condition for knowledge

- The idea is that one cannot know something that is false, even if they believe it strongly and have justification for that belief.

**Subjective Knowledge**

- Some argue for a more subjective view of knowledge, where personal understanding and belief play a significant role.

- In this view, what constitutes 'knowledge' for an individual might not necessarily be objectively true.
