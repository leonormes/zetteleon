## For Pet Care We Have a Dog Who Has Addisons Disease, a Cat, and 2 Snakes

Good context—here's how your Pet Care category breaks down with your actual animals in mind.

### Dog (Addison's dIsease)

This is the most medically demanding of the four and needs its own sub-routine, not just generic "pet care":

- Daily medication—steroid tablets (prednisone/prednisolone) given every morning with food to replace cortisol; missing doses risks a relapse of symptoms.[^1][^2]
- Monthly injection—a vet-administered mineralocorticoid (e.g., Zycortal/fludrocortisone) to replace aldosterone; needs to be booked into the calendar as a recurring appointment.[^2]
- Stress management—Addison's dogs are vulnerable to "Addisonian crises" triggered by stress (fireworks, house moves, boarding), so routine and calm handling matter more than with a typical dog; the vet may need to be consulted about a temporary dose increase before known stressful events.[^3][^2]
- Watch-list symptoms—lethargy, vomiting, diarrhoea, loss of appetite, or collapse are red flags requiring same-day vet contact, not a "wait and see" approach.[^3][^2]
- Regular blood tests—electrolyte (sodium/potassium) monitoring at least monthly initially, then every 2-3 months once stable.[^4][^2]
- Medication safety—NSAIDs (Rimadyl, Metacam, Meloxicam) are unsafe for this dog; only vet-approved pain relief (e.g., Tramadol, Gabapentin) should ever be given.[^4]

### Cat

Standard care: feeding, litter tray maintenance, grooming, and annual vet/vaccination check-ups—no special medical routine unless a condition emerges.

### Snakes (2)

Reptile husbandry is a distinct category from mammal pet care and worth separating on any chore chart:

- Enclosure environment checks—daily verification of temperature gradient and humidity, since snakes rely entirely on external heat sources for digestion and health.
- Feeding schedule—typically every 1-2 weeks (varies by species/size), not daily, which makes it easy to lose track of without a calendar reminder.
- Water and substrate—fresh water and periodic substrate/enclosure cleaning.
- Shedding checks—monitoring for incomplete sheds, which can cause circulation problems if not addressed.

Given the dog's condition, I'd suggest the "Pet Care" line in your operational plan explicitly splits into two tiers: a parent-owned medical tier (dog's meds, injections, vet monitoring—too high-stakes to delegate to kids) and a shared routine tier (cat litter/feeding, snake feeding/enclosure checks) that could rotate through the kids' chore system like the other categories.
