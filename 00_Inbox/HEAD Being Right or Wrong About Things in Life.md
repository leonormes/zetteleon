Here are the structural adjustments to make when engaging with an ego-fused individual:

### 1. Linguistic Decoupling

You have to structurally enforce the distance between the person and the idea using your language. Remove "you" and "your" entirely when discussing the proposition.

- The Error: "Your reasoning on this policy doesn't make sense." (Fuses the person to the flaw).
- The Correction: "This specific policy seems to miss the underlying economic data." (Points directly at the specimen on the table).

By continuously using third-party phrasing ("this idea," "that narrative," "the argument"), you refuse to participate in their ego-fusion. You are relentlessly placing the object back on the table, even as they try to pull it back into their chest.

### 2. Shift from Error-Correction to Trace Logging

When someone is fused to an idea, acting as an arbiter who rejects their output will immediately trigger their defences. Instead of dismantling the final plant, ask them to show you their telemetry. Shift from making statements to querying their inputs.

Ask: _"What was the primary factor that led to that conclusion?"_ or _"How does this framework solve [Specific Problem] in practice?"_

This forces them to take a step back and look at the components of their own belief. They have to pick up the specimen and examine it to answer you, which subtly initiates the decoupling process for them.

### 3. Apply Deliberate Distress Tolerance

When their system registers a threat, they will likely become defensive, agitated, or upset. This is where you must exercise high distress tolerance.

Their emotional dysregulation is simply their system's response to perceived danger. You do not need to validate their false belief to soothe their anxiety, nor do you need to rescue them from the discomfort of having their ideas challenged. Let them experience the friction. You can remain entirely warm and present as a human being while allowing them to sit with the discomfort of a stress-tested idea. You hold the boundary without absorbing their emotional output.

Some beliefs are so heavily load-bearing for a person's identity that pulling on them threatens total structural collapse. If they escalate to hostility or shut down completely, the diagnostic is complete: the environment is not stable enough for this specific stress test.

Your goal is not to fix their model; it is to maintain yours. You can simply say, _"I look at the data differently, but I understand why you value that approach,"_ and walk away. You leave their humanity intact, you leave the bad idea on the table, and you exit the loop.
