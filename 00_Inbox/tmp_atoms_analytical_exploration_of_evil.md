### Atom 4: Distinction between Malice and Banality
- kind: distinction
- statement: Evil can arise from either deliberate malicious intent (sadism/perverse will) or from "thoughtlessness" and bureaucratic banality where motivation is disconnected from the magnitude of harm.
- scope_and_conditions: References the Arendtian framework (Eichmann) vs. the Kantian framework (perverse will).
- evidence: "Hannah Arendt's analysis of Adolf Eichmann revealed that profound evil can arise from 'thoughtlessness' and bureaucratic banality rather than monstrous motivation." (no timestamp)
- implications:
  - Malicious intent is a sufficient but not a necessary condition for evil.
  - Foreseeable harm magnitude matters more than the specific psychological state of the perpetrator.
- confidence: high
- tags: [hannah-arendt, motivation, psychology, ethics]

- scope_and_conditions: Proposed by Eve Garrard; manifests as apathy, suppression of empathy, or ideological restructuring.
- evidence: "the perpetrator psychologically silences considerations that should function as absolute barriers to action... a systematic suppression or elimination of moral responsiveness." (no timestamp)
- implications:

- tags: [eve-garrard, moral-psychology, empathy, ideology]

### Atom 6: Constraint of Culpability and Foreseeability
- kind: constraint
- statement: For an act to be classified as evil, the harm must be culpably inflicted by an agent with the capacity for moral judgment and the ability to reasonably foresee the consequences.
- scope_and_conditions: Distinguishes evil from accidental harm, natural disasters, or actions by those lacking moral capacity.
- evidence: "the perpetrator must have the capacity to act otherwise and the ability to reasonably foresee the consequences." (no timestamp)
- implications:
  - Evil requires agency and awareness; without them, the event is a tragedy or accident, not evil.
  - Culpability is tied to the possession of information and the capacity to recognize likely outcomes.
- confidence: high
- tags: [culpability, agency, law, responsibility]

- kind: procedure
- statement: The "Atrocity Paradigm" identifies evil through three necessary elements: culpable wrongdoing, reasonable foreseeability, and intolerable harm.
