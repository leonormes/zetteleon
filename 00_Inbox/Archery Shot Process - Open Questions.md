## Archery Shot Process - Open Questions

Four places where the shot-process notes disagree with themselves or with each other, found while adding a Purpose line to every numbered step. None is resolved here. Each needs a coach opinion or a primary source before a note is changed.

### 1. Should the knees be locked?

- **Says never lock:** Stance step 5. Locked knees switch off the leg muscles and reduce balance. See [[Archery Shot Process - Stance]].
- **Says lock:** Jake Kaminski, under Faults & Diagnostics in the same note. Pull the kneecaps up and engage the quads and glutes to lock the hip girdle.
- **Why it matters:** Both aim at a stable hip girdle but give opposite instructions. The cue may differ in practice: engaging the quads and glutes is not the same as hyperextending the joint.
- **To settle:** Ask the coach what the knees should feel like at full draw. Check whether Kaminski means a muscular lock rather than a joint lock.

### 2. How deep should the string hook sit?

- **Says deep:** Why It Works in [[Archery Shot Process - Setup]]. A hook close to the joint lines puts the string nearer the skeletal line of force, so you hold full weight with less muscular effort.
- **Says not deep:** Setup step 5, Rolling In. Do not hook deep into the knuckle groove, because it pinches nerves and causes numbness. Start towards the fingertips and roll in to gather a pad.
- **Why it matters:** The note never says what "deep" means. The two statements may describe different depths, or one may be a mistake.
- **To settle:** Ask the coach to check the actual hook on your hand. Decide a single target position, for example string on the pad just short of the joint, and rewrite both statements to match.

### 3. Who drives the lift: both hands or the bow arm?

- **Both hands:** Raise steps and Why It Works. Lifting with the bow arm alone over-engages the deltoids, which fatigue quickly. See [[Archery Shot Process - Raise]].
- **Bow arm leads:** Kaminski, under Alternative Views. The bow arm is the main driving force and the draw hand follows through the string.
- **Why it matters:** This decides what you feel in the shoulder during the raise, and it changes the cue you rehearse.
- **To settle:** Try both on a blank bale and note which shoulder stays lower and calmer. Ask the coach which suits your draw weight.

### 4. Should you actively pull the bow shoulder down?

- **Pulls down:** Raise step 5 and the reach cue. Seat the shoulder down and forward and lock the internal rotation.
- **Does not pull down:** Coach Ash Morgan, under Alternative Views. Forcing the shoulder down breaks the structural connection and pushes it out of the socket. Start neutral with only a tiny downward direction and raise naturally. Stability comes from conditioning.
- **Why it matters:** Raise also says the reach produces the low shoulder as a side effect, so the notes partly agree. The open point is how much deliberate depression to add.
- **To settle:** Film the raise from behind (see the Behind-the-Line Video Check in [[Archery Shot Process - Posture]]) and ask the coach whether your shoulder needs more downward direction or less.
