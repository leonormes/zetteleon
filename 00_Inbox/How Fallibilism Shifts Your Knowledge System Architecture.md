### The Core Philosophical Inversion

Justificationism treats knowledge as something you _secure_—the epistemic game is accumulating positive evidence to raise a claim's credibility until it becomes bedrock. Fallibilism, as Deutsch frames it in _The Beginning of Infinity_, inverts the game entirely:

> _"The opposing position—namely the recognition that there are no authoritative sources of knowledge, nor any reliable means of justifying ideas as being true or probable—is called fallibilism. Fallibilists expect even their best and most fundamental explanations to contain misconceptions in addition to truth."_

> _"The logic of fallibilism is that one not only seeks to correct the misconceptions of the past, but hopes in the future to find and change mistaken ideas that no one today questions or finds problematic."_

This isn't merely a philosophical preference—it rewires every structural decision in a PKM.

### Clash-by-clash: where Deutsch Collides with Your Existing System

Clash 1—The word `supports`

> _"A `supports` link presupposes that the epistemic game is accumulating positive evidence to raise the credibility of a claim. Deutsch's view: that game is unwinnable and miscast. Evidence cannot lift a conjecture's status; only failed attempts at refutation tell you anything, and even then they tell you the conjecture has survived, not that it's 'supported.'"_

Clash 2—`Claim` as the primary unit

Your seven note types include `Claim` but not `Explanation`. For Deutsch, a claim like "the Earth orbits the Sun" is epistemically thin without the gravitational-mechanical explanation that makes it work. The unit of knowledge is the good explanation—and "good" means hard to vary while still accounting for the phenomenon.

### Three Architectural Shifts Fallibilism Demands

#### 1. The Hard-to-Vary Filter (promotion criterion)

> _"Your existing three-layer granularity gives you Thesis-level claims. Deutsch gives you a promotion criterion. A claim should only ascend to thesis status if its explanation is hard to vary—i.e., if changing the explanation breaks its predictive grip on the phenomena. This converts your hierarchy from a foundationalist pyramid to a promotion ladder filtered by explanatory tightness. Same structure, fallibilist soul."_

The change: `Domain → Thesis → Support` stays structurally, but the criterion for promotion changes from "is this well-evidenced?" to "is the explanation behind this hard to vary?"

#### 2. `Explanation` As a First-class Note Type

High-leverage move from the "Fallibilist Heptabase" proposal:

> _"Promote Explanation to a first-class note type (or merge it into an upgraded Claim). Mandatory field: Why is this explanation hard to vary? If you can't answer, the card is a placeholder, not knowledge."_

#### 3. Replace `supports` with Typed Conjecture Links

The proposed typed link vocabulary:

- `predicts`—the explanation forbids certain observations
- `explains`—the explanation accounts for phenomenon X
- `is-challenged-by`—a refutation attempt (replaces the justificationist `refutes`)
- `survived`—the challenge failed; the conjecture still stands
- `superseded-by`—a better explanation has replaced this one

The `supports` link is abolished because it presupposes the wrong game.

This costs almost nothing structurally—it's a different question asked during your existing maintenance windows."_
