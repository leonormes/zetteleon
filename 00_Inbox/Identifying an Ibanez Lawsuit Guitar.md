Identify the guitar

Based on the visual evidence, this is an **Ibanez Model 2355**, manufactured during the "Lawsuit Era" (specifically mid-1970s, pre-1977).

It is a high-fidelity copy of the **Gibson ES-175**.

### Identification Logic

- **Framework (Lawsuit Era):** The "Open Book" headstock shape is the primary identifier. This specific contour was the subject of the legal dispute between Gibson and Ibanez. Post-1977 models (like the FA-100) switched to a Guild-style headstock to avoid litigation.
- **Logo Topology:** The cursive "Script" Ibanez logo (without the underline swoosh seen in later years) dates this instrument to the early-to-mid 1970s (approx. 1971–1976).
- **Design Language:**
	- **Inlays:** Split-parallelogram fretboard markers (distinctive to the ES-175 architecture).
	- **Headstock Inlay:** The "Crown" or stylized flower motif is a direct reference to Gibson's trade dress.
	- **Tailpiece:** The wire trapeze tailpiece with the geometric cross-bar is standard hardware for the 2355 model.
	- **Body:** 16-inch hollow body with a sharp Florentine cutaway.

### Deviations from Stock (Modifications)

- **Pickups:** The pickup covers bear the **Bartolini** logo. These are aftermarket replacements (the original units would likely have been Ibanez Super 70s). Bartolini pickups are a common upgrade for jazz musicians seeking a warmer, more articulate response than the stock ceramic magnets provided.
- **Bridge:** The bridge appears to be a rosewood floating base, consistent with the original specifications, though the saddle may have been swapped or adjusted over time.
