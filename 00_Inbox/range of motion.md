This video by Adam Frater provides a foundational framework for beginning calisthenics at home without specialised equipment. It prioritises structural integrity and muscle engagement over simple repetition counts.

## Core Principles of the Framework

 - Prioritise Range of Motion (ROM) over Repetitions:
     - Reps are secondary to the quality of movement. Full ROM ensures the entire muscle spectrum is trained [01:06].
   - Muscle growth and strength are most effectively built when a muscle is at its most elongated state (e.g., the bottom of a push-up) [02:13].
   - Time Under Tension (TUT): Focus on slow, controlled movements to maximise muscle strain rather than rapid, "bad" reps [03:41].
 - Integrated Core Strength:
   - The core is a 360-degree system surrounding the spine, not just the visible abdominal muscles [04:12].
   - The Hollow Body Position: This is the essential structural foundation for calisthenics. It involves tucking the ribs, removing the lower back arch, and engaging the glutes [06:18].
   - Effective calisthenics movements (push-ups, handstands, levers) should be treated as active core exercises [07:44].
 - Active Mobility vs. Passive Stretching:
   - Avoid passive stretching, which only temporarily elongates tendons [11:42].
   - Utilise Active Mobility: Engaging the muscles through a new range of motion to build the "brain-muscle connection," teaching the nervous system that these positions are safe and controllable [11:51].
 - Training Muscles, Not Just Skills:
   - Skills (like handstands) require specific foundational strength in the forearms, shoulders, and core [13:23].
   - Supplement skill practice with strength-building variations, such as pike push-ups or elevated pike push-ups, to prepare the physical structure for the skill [13:52].

## Practical Implementation at Home

The video suggests using household items to replicate gym equipment:

 - Pulling Movements: Use a sturdy kitchen table for bodyweight rows [15:15].
 - Dips/L-Sits: Use chairs or bar stools as makeshift parallettes [16:07].
 - Environmental Integration: Spend time on the floor (e.g., while watching TV) to naturally work on hip and hamstring flexibility through active positions like the straddle sit [09:04].
