You've done a very interesting thing here. It's a blend of paraphrasing and extending the original idea into new territory. Here's a breakdown:
Paraphrasing Aspects:

- Focus on Meaning Preceding Language: You've successfully captured the core idea that meaning (or understanding, as you put it) isn't _dependent_ on language. The original paragraph argued this through the existence of paraphrasing; you've argued it through examples of non-linguistic understanding.
- Independence of Concept and Expression: You maintain the idea that the _concept_ (the football player's understanding of the ball's trajectory) exists separately from any _linguistic description_ of that concept.
- Paraphrasing as a Tool for Understanding: You retain the idea that working with different linguistic expressions (paraphrasing someone else's words) can aid in comprehension. This connects to the original's "moving the light" metaphor.

Extension and New Ideas:

- "Pre-linguistic Process of the Mind": This is a key phrase that moves beyond simple paraphrasing. The original paragraph implied this, but you've made it explicit and central. You're framing understanding as fundamentally a cognitive process that _precedes_ and is _independent_ of language.
- Examples of Non-Human Understanding: The examples of the football player and animals are _new_ and very effective. They strengthen the argument for pre-linguistic understanding by showing that it's not unique to humans with language. The original paragraph only focused on the _human_ ability to paraphrase. Your examples broaden the scope considerably.
- Emphasis on Experience: You introduce the idea that "the more time you spend experiencing this pre-linguistic experience, the more familiar a concept becomes." This is a significant addition. It suggests a _developmental_ aspect to understanding:
  - Understanding starts with direct, non-linguistic experience.
  - Repeated experience builds familiarity with the concept.
  - This familiarity then allows for more diverse linguistic expression (more ways to paraphrase).
  - This creates a feedback loop: experience leads to understanding, which leads to better linguistic expression, which can further refine understanding.
  - The original paragraph focused on the ability to express an existing meaning, this is adding the idea of the origin of the meaning.

Is it a New Idea?
It's more accurate to say you've _developed_ the original idea rather than presenting a completely _new_ one. You've taken the core concept (separation of meaning and language) and:

1. Shifted the Focus: From the _evidence_ for that separation (paraphrasing) to the _nature_ of understanding itself (pre-linguistic).
2. Broadened the Scope: From human linguistic ability to include non-human and experiential understanding.
3. Added a Developmental Dimension: Suggesting how understanding develops from experience to linguistic expression.

So, while you're still engaging with the original idea, you've significantly expanded and reframed it. You've gone from a philosophical argument about the nature of language to a more cognitive and developmental argument about the nature of understanding. It's a very natural and insightful progression of the thought. It is best described as a sophisticated paraphrase.
