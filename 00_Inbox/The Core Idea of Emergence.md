Yes, your description is a very good way of thinking about it. Emergence is precisely the phenomenon where complex, large-scale patterns arise from the collective actions of many small, simple interactions.  
The secondary effects (the emergent properties) absolutely depend on the primary effects (the local interactions), but they also exhibit novel properties that are not present in the individual components themselves.

### **The Core Idea of Emergence**

The central concept of emergence is that the **whole is more than the sum of its parts**. You can't understand the complex, high-level behaviour just by studying the individual components in isolation. You have to study their interactions and the system as a whole.  
An emergent system is one where the global behaviour is:

1. **Dependent on** the lower-level components and their interactions.  
2. **Not reducible to** the properties of those components. The emergent pattern is a new *kind* of thing.

### **Your "Primary vs. Secondary" Effects**

This is an excellent way to frame it.

* **Primary Effects:** These are the simple, local rules that individual agents or components follow. For example, an individual bird in a flock follows rules like "don't crash into your neighbours", "fly in the same average direction as your neighbours", and "steer towards the average position of your neighbours".  
* **Secondary Effects:** This is the global, emergent behaviour that results from all those primary interactions happening at once. In the bird example, the secondary effect is the mesmerising, coordinated movement of the entire flock, which can swerve and dive as if it were a single organism.

Crucially, you cannot predict the complex, fluid motion of the flock (secondary effect) by just looking at one bird's simple rules (primary effects). The complexity arises *from the interactions*.

### **Classic Examples**

* **Ant Colonies** 🐜: Individual ants follow simple pheromone trails. The colony as a whole solves complex problems like finding the shortest path to a food source. The "intelligence" is in the colony, not the individual ant.  
* **Consciousness** 🧠: Individual neurons fire based on simple electrochemical signals. The interaction of billions of neurons gives rise to the emergent property of consciousness, thought, and self-awareness. A single neuron is not "conscious".  
* **Temperature and Pressure** 🔥: For a gas in a box, temperature is an emergent property. It's the average kinetic energy of all the gas molecules. A single molecule doesn't have a temperature; the property only exists at the macroscopic level of the collective.  
* **Traffic Jams** 🚗: A traffic jam can move backwards along a motorway, even while every car in it is moving forwards. The jam is an emergent phenomenon created by the interactions (slowing down, speeding up, changing lanes) of individual drivers.
