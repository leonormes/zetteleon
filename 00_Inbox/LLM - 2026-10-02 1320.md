Your instinct is right, and the data backs it: the winter should be about making the whole group smaller, not rescuing bad arrows. That lever is used up. The remaining gap is shot-to-shot consistency, which is a form problem, and indoor is the best setting to work on it: one distance, no wind, the same round every week.

The main risk is your own habit of treating every session as a scored test. Form changes usually cost points before they pay off. If every arrow counts towards your trend, you'll be tempted to abandon a good change in week two. The plan below is built to stop that happening.

## Pressure-testing the Plan

1. "Form and focus" isn't a target yet. Scores can tell you _whether_ the group is shrinking, but not _why_ it isn't. You need one named fault from someone watching you shoot, either your coach or video. I can't see you shoot, so any specific form fix from me would be a guess dressed up as advice.
2. Separate training from testing. Wednesday sessions are for training: tag them in the tracker and leave them out of the trend. Sunday Portsmouths are the control measurement. Decide in advance to give any change 4 weeks before you judge it.
3. You shoot too few arrows to change a habit. Two sessions a week is roughly 100–120 arrows, which is not much for retraining movement. Short home drills fill the gap without needing a boss.
4. Expect a flat or worse November. A dip while you rework form is normal and doesn't mean the plan has failed. Note it in the tracker now, so future-you doesn't read it as regression.
5. Change one thing at a time. You already apply this principle to analysis, so apply it to practice too.
6. Possible kit gap: a clicker. Your kit notes list riser, limbs, stabilisers, plunger and sight, but no clicker. If you don't have one fitted, it's the biggest form-related kit decision available to you this winter, and you should make it with your coach. It would reset your scores for several weeks, so it's a Block 1–2 change or nothing this season. You may already have one and it just isn't in my notes.

## Progressive Plan

The principle is Wednesdays train, Sundays test. Dates come from my reading of the programme grid, and the PDF text came out jumbled, so check them against the printed sheet.

### Block 1—Lock down and Diagnose (4 Oct–1 Nov)

- Kit freeze:
    - Use one tab, for good.
    - Do any tuning now (plunger, nocking point, bare shaft at 20 yd) with the coach. Tuning in the middle of winter would muddy all your later data.
- Diagnosis: get the coach to watch two ends and name the one fault costing you most.
- Warm-up routine: fix a short routine to do before sighters: band reps plus the same pre-shot sequence every time. Track the gap between your first dozen and the rest of the round to see whether it works. It can be measured separately from later form work, so it's safe to run alongside.
- Fixtures:
    - Sundays: Portsmouth on 4 Oct and 18 Oct as your baseline.
    - Sunday 11 Oct is Worcester, a different face and scoring, so treat it as variety only.
    - 25 Oct is the Fun Shoot. Clocks go back that day.
- Exit criterion: the fault is named, the kit is frozen, and you have two baseline Portsmouths.

### Block 2—Build the Routine (2 Nov–19 Dec)

- Wednesdays: choose Bray I over Portsmouth. You shoot 30 arrows instead of 60, which means less fatigue at 36 lb and more attention per arrow.
- Execution scoring. This is how "focus" becomes something you can measure. After each arrow, mark ✓ or ✗ for whether you carried out your one cue, regardless of where the arrow landed. Track two things:
    - your execution percentage per session
    - your average points on ✓ arrows versus ✗ arrows

    This test can prove you wrong, which fits how you work. If ✓ arrows don't score better than ✗ arrows after about three weeks, you've picked the wrong cue.

- The cue card: you already have a documented shot process in the vault. Boil it down to one cue per block and write it on a card on your quiver. It puts the structure outside your head, so it doesn't use up working memory.
- Home drills: 5 minutes, three times a week. Do band shot-process reps in front of a mirror, tied to something you already do every day. Use the band, not the bow: drawing the bow at home is a dry-fire risk.
- Sundays:
    - Portsmouth on 1 Nov, 15 Nov and 13 Dec feed the trend.
    - Worcester, WA18 (22 Nov) and the Club Round (29 Nov) are variety.

### Block 3—Consolidate (20 Dec–2 Jan)

- Shoot fewer sessions and make no changes: Christmas Fun Shoot on 20 Dec, Clout at Barling on 27 Dec, then a break.
- Spend 20 minutes reviewing Block 2: execution trend, ✓ versus ✗ arrows, and the first-dozen gap.

### Block 4—Test: January Challenge (3–31 Jan)

- Four Sunday Portsmouths in a row (3, 10, 17 and 24 Jan) make the cleanest dataset of your winter.
- Make no technique changes during January. Just carry out the routine.
- Compare these four cards against your October baseline on handicap, first-dozen gap, E2 vs E1 and odd/even ends.
- If Jane confirms indoor qualifying rules, this is also your qualifying block.
- Frostbite on 31 Jan (tbc) is outdoors at 30 m. It's optional and can't count for classification.

### Block 5—Refine and Add Pressure (Feb–Mar)

- If the first fault is stable, add a second cue. If not, stay on the first.
- Smaller faces make you more disciplined with your aim and make group size more obvious:
    - WA18 on 21 Feb and 7 Mar
    - Vegas at 18 m on 14 Feb
    - Vegas 300 on 17 Mar
    - Bray II at 25 yd on 31 Mar
- Pressure drills:
    - Call each shot before you look at where it landed.
    - Shoot a "beat your average" end once a session.

### Block 6—Peak (April)

- Freeze everything by early April.
- Rehearse your competition routine on the last few Wednesdays.
- Club Championships look like Sunday 25 Apr.
- Recheck sight marks before the outdoor season, which may start early.
