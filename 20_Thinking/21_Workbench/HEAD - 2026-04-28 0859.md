CONFIDENTIAL–FOR THE ATTENTION OF THE OFSTED INSPECTION TEAM

To: Liz Smith, HMI (Lead Inspector)

Re: Ofsted Inspection of Belfairs Academy (28 April 2026)–SEND Provision Concerns

From: Leon Ormes

Date: 28 April 2026

Dear Ms Smith,

I am writing to you directly regarding your current inspection of Belfairs Academy to highlight severe, ongoing concerns regarding the school's provision for students with Special Educational Needs and Disabilities (SEND). I am submitting this as evidence of systemic failures in leadership, teaching quality, and the implementation of support, using the experiences of my daughter, Bessie (Year 9), as a primary example.

Despite Bessie having a clear Individual Support Plan (ISP) designed to support her neurodevelopmental needs (including ADHD and dyslexia), the day-to-day implementation of this plan across her timetable is completely inconsistent and routinely ignored. The core issue at Belfairs Academy is a profound gap between the support agreed upon in paperwork and the reality of the classroom experience.

Specifically, the school consistently fails to provide fundamental, agreed-upon accommodations:

- Accessible Resources: Bessie requires printed resources on blue paper to negate the need for extensive copying from the board, which she finds exceptionally difficult. Despite this being a formal requirement, teachers frequently fail to provide this.
- Appropriate Adaptations: The agreed use of a laptop for longer writing tasks is inconsistently permitted, and tasks are rarely broken down into manageable steps to support her focus.

Most alarmingly, there is a pervasive culture where unmet learning needs are treated as behavioural issues. When Bessie struggles to access the curriculum due to the school's failure to adapt their teaching, her resulting anxiety and disengagement are met with punitive measures. She reports that rather than offering pedagogical support, some teachers simply raise their voices or tell her to leave the classroom. This lack of a supportive, neurodiversity-informed approach is actively damaging her mental well-being and destroying her love of learning.

We have held numerous meetings with school leadership and the SENCO, and drafted multiple formal plans to address these issues. However, the leadership demonstrates a persistent inability to effectively communicate these strategies to classroom teachers or hold staff accountable for implementing them. Promises are made in meetings, but the required support never materialises consistently in the classroom.

I urge the inspection team to scrutinise the reality of SEND provision at Belfairs Academy. Please look beyond the SEN register and the written ISPs, and investigate whether these vital support strategies are actually being delivered consistently in lessons.

I would welcome the opportunity to speak with a member of the inspection team confidentially during your visit to provide further context.

Yours sincerely,
