## The Question

[[Discipline Is a Property of External Structure More Than of Will]] holds that reliability comes from environment rather than will. [[Strong Habits are Context-Independent]] says a mature habit runs regardless of location, mood or disruption — in which case external support is not the backbone once the habit is strong. Is the structure claim scoped to *acquisition*, with its force decaying as the habit matures?

## Why It Matters

It decides whether I keep building scaffolding indefinitely or expect to dismantle it. Given ADHD, I have assumed scaffolding is permanent — but if that assumption is wrong for mature habits, I am maintaining infrastructure that no longer earns its keep, and the maintenance itself is a monotony risk.

## What I Currently Think

The acquisition reading is almost certainly right as a matter of logic. What I do not know is whether context-independence is achievable *for me* — the habit literature's 'strong habit' may assume a neurotypical consolidation curve, and nothing in the vault tests it against ADHD.

## What Would Settle It

Identify one habit I have held for over a year and deliberately remove its scaffolding for two weeks. If it survives, the claim is acquisition-scoped and I can start retiring supports. If it collapses, that is a genuinely important finding about my own consolidation curve and it belongs in [[SoT - Behavioral Architecture]].
