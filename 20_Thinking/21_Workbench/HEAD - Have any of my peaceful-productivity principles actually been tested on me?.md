## The Question

Every principle in [[SoT - Principles for Peaceful Productivity]] is grounded in a mechanism claim, and not one has an experiment or evidence note recording personal adherence or outcome. Principle 3 extends [[The Five-Item To-Do List Reduces Overwhelm]], which is itself a `hypothesis` with `status: pending` — a three-day experiment never run. Principle 8's energy filter presumes reliable interoception and [[Self-monitoring reveals personal energy and trigger patterns]] supplies the method, but nothing records that I ever did the logging.

## Why It Matters

This is well-justified, well-linked, and entirely unmeasured — which is the exact shape of the meta-work trap the Trap-of-the-Architect thread describes. A beautifully grounded principle set I have never tested is knowledge work standing in for the actual work.

## What I Currently Think

The uncomfortable answer is probably 'no, none of them'. That does not make the principles wrong — the mechanism claims behind them are solid — but it means I hold them as beliefs rather than findings, and I should stop presenting the SoT as though the grounding implies the practice.

## What Would Settle It

Pick the three principles with the lowest activation cost — 3 (one needle-mover), 6 (ten-minute walk), 9 (ten-minute reset) — and log adherence for fourteen days. One line per day. Then write one `evidence` note with the result, whatever it is. Fourteen data points beats another round of grounding.
