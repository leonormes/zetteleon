Who are the 4 horseman of the apocalypse? Where are they from?

---

The Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse are symbolic figures representing different calamities that will occur during the end times.

They originate from the Book of Revelation (specifically Chapter 6), which is the final book of the New Testament in the Christian Bible. The text was written by a Christian visionary known as John of Patmos around the end of the first century AD, whilst he was exiled on the Greek island of Patmos.

According to the text, the horsemen are summoned when the "Lamb of God" (representing Jesus Christ) opens the first four of the seven seals on a prophetic scroll, unleashing these forces upon the earth to enact divine judgement.

Here is a breakdown of the four figures:

### 1\. The White Horse (Conquest or Pestilence)

- Description: The rider carries a bow and is given a crown. He rides out "conquering and to conquer".
- Meaning: Historically and biblically, this rider is most commonly interpreted as representing Conquest or militarism. However, in modern popular culture, this horseman is frequently reinterpreted as Pestilence or infectious disease.

### 2\. The Red Horse (War)

- Description: The rider is given a great sword and the power to take peace from the earth, causing people to slay one another.
- Meaning: The fiery red colour of the horse and the sword clearly symbolise War, bloodshed, and violence.

### 3\. The Black Horse (Famine)

- Description: The rider carries a pair of measuring scales in his hand. A voice is heard declaring high prices for basic commodities like wheat and barley, while commanding not to harm the oil and the wine.
- Meaning: This horseman represents Famine and economic oppression. The scales signify the careful rationing of scarce food supplies, where basic necessities become incredibly expensive, yet luxury items (oil and wine) remain available to the wealthy.

### 4\. The Pale Horse (Death)

- Description: This is the only horseman explicitly named in the scripture. The rider is named Death, and Hades (the realm of the dead) follows closely behind him.
- Meaning: The "pale" colour of the horse is often described as the sickly, greenish-grey pallor of a corpse. Together, Death and Hades are given authority over a quarter of the earth to kill by sword, famine, plague, and wild beasts.
