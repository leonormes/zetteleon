## 14a-appeal_to_authority

## Appeal to Authority Fallacy

The "Appeal to Authority" fallacy, also called "Argumentum ad Verecundiam" in Latin.

This fallacy occurs when someone argues that a claim must be true simply because an authority figure or expert said it was, without providing additional evidence or reasoning.

### Understanding the Appeal to Authority Fallacy

An appeal to authority is considered fallacious when:

1. The cited authority is not actually an expert in the relevant field.
2. The expert's opinion is controversial or not widely accepted among other experts in the field.
3. The claim falls outside the expert's area of expertise.
4. The argument relies solely on the expert's opinion without presenting supporting evidence

It's important to note that not all references to expert opinions are fallacious. In fact, citing credible experts can strengthen an argument when used properly. The fallacy occurs when the expert's opinion is treated as infallible or used as the sole basis for an argument

### Proper Use of Expert Opinion

While appealing to authority can be fallacious, expert opinions are still valuable in many contexts. Here's how to use expert opinions effectively:

1. Cite experts within their field of expertise.
2. Present the expert's reasoning and evidence, not just their conclusion.
3. Acknowledge any controversies or disagreements among experts on the topic.
4. Use expert opinions as supporting evidence, not as the entire argument.

### Recognizing and Avoiding the Fallacy

To avoid falling into this fallacy:

1. Question the credibility and relevance of the cited authority.
2. Look for supporting evidence beyond the expert's opinion.
3. Consider alternative viewpoints and potential biases.
4. Evaluate the argument based on its merits, not just on who is making it

Remember, even genuine experts can be wrong, and their opinions should be subject to scrutiny and verification. Critical thinking involves evaluating arguments based on evidence and reasoning, not just on the status of the person making the claim.
