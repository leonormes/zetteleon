## The Question

[[Outsourcing Writing to AI Bypasses the Cognitive Strain That Builds Professional Competence]] argues the strain of writing *is* the mechanism of professional competence and must not be optimised away. [[Agent-First Implementation Cycle]] treats cognitive friction as a cost to eliminate. Both cannot be unconditionally true.

## Why It Matters

This is the single most consequential open question in the vault for me personally, because I am a Staff Architect who delegates increasing amounts of thinking to agents — including this note. If strain is the mechanism, then every agent-first workflow I build is quietly deskilling me. If it is merely cost, I am leaving productivity on the table by hand-writing things.

## What I Currently Think

The resolution is probably a scope split: strain is the mechanism when the goal is *my* competence, and a cost when the goal is *output* in a domain where I am already competent and do not intend to deepen. The uncomfortable part is that this makes it a decision I have to make deliberately per task, and I currently make it by default.

## What Would Settle It

Name three concrete work activities and classify each as skill-building or output-only, then check whether my actual tooling matches the classification. If I cannot classify one of them, that is the real finding. Where they mismatch, that is a change to make, not a note to write.
