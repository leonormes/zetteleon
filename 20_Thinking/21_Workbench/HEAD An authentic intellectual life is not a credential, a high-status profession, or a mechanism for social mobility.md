An analytical comparison between Zena Hitz's atelic model of intellectual life and utilitarian educational frameworks—specifically Human Capital Theory (pioneered by economists such as Gary Becker and Theodore Schultz)—reveals a fundamental conflict regarding the purpose of thought, the nature of knowledge, and the definition of human value.

### Core Philosophical Conflicts

#### 1\. Teleology: Intrinsic Worth vs. Instrumental Utility

- The Atelic Position: Drawing on Aristotelian virtue ethics (specifically _otium_, or contemplative leisure), Hitz posits that intellectual life belongs to the class of human activities that are inherently worth doing for their own sake. Like love, art, or play, reading and reflection do not require an external product or financial yield to justify their existence.
- The Utilitarian Position: Rooted in neoclassical economics, Human Capital Theory models education as a capital expenditure. Time, effort, and money spent on learning are evaluated based on their marginal product—specifically increased labour productivity, higher personal earnings, and macroeconomic growth.
- The Conflict: Under Human Capital Theory, an intellectual pursuit that yields no market value or positional advantage is classified as an inefficient allocation of resources. Conversely, Hitz argues that subjecting thought to economic optimization corrupts the practice, transforming learning from a source of liberation into a hyper-competitive engine of status anxiety.

#### 2\. Epistemology: Contemplative Inquiry vs. Commodified Skill Acquisition

- The Atelic Position: Defines genuine learning as a process characterised by disorientation, cognitive friction, and productive failure. Knowledge is acquired by confronting reality directly, forcing the individual to abandon faulty internal assumptions when they fail to withstand scrutiny.
- The Utilitarian Position: Operationalises knowledge as discrete, transferrable skills and information packages. Learning is treated as an input-output transaction, often reduced to information retrieval, standardised testing, and measurable competency metrics.
- The Conflict: Utilitarian models demand predictable, quantifiable, and time-efficient educational outcomes. This directly opposes the open-ended uncertainty and prolonged confusion that Hitz identifies as essential for deep intellectual transformation.

#### 3\. Philosophical Anthropology: Human Flourishing vs. Homo Economicus

- The Atelic Position: Views the human being as an entity whose ultimate aim is _eudaimonia_ (holistic flourishing). Intellectual engagement offers an unassailable "inner refuge"—a source of personal dignity that protects individuals (such as prisoners, manual workers, or the economically disenfranchised) from societal contempt.
- The Utilitarian Position: Reduces the individual to a unit of economic potential whose capabilities are valued according to market demand. Human worth becomes tightly bound to productivity metrics and occupational status.
- The Conflict: Hitz's model maintains that a person's intellectual life retains supreme value regardless of their economic output or social utility. Human Capital Theory implicitly diminishes the value of non-monetised intellectual activity, treating non-productive contemplation as economically irrelevant.

#### 4\. Societal Architecture: Non-Competitive Sanctuary vs. Positional Sorting

- The Atelic Position: Conceptualises intellectual life as a democratic, non-zero-sum sanctuary. Because inner cultivation is not a finite resource, one person's intellectual growth does not diminish another's, allowing study to serve as a genuine equalizer across social classes.
- The Utilitarian Position: Functions as a mechanism for credentialism and positional sorting. Educational institutions serve as gatekeepers, issuing signals (degrees, class ranks) that allow labour markets to rank candidates hierarchically.
- The Conflict: Utilitarian frameworks turn education into a zero-sum competition for scarce positional goods (e.g., admission to elite universities, high-status corporate roles). This incentivises game-playing, short-term memorisation, and compliance over critical enquiry, ultimately alienating students from the intrinsic joy of learning.

### Analytical Synthesis

The divergence between these two frameworks represents a trade-off between economic productivity and existential depth:

- Human Capital Theory offers a powerful explanatory model for macroeconomic planning, workforce development, and institutional resource allocation. However, when adopted as an overarching philosophy of education, it fails to explain why individuals pursue knowledge outside market incentives and exacerbates burnout by evaluating human worth strictly through productivity metrics.
- Hitz's Atelic Model provides a robust philosophical defense of human dignity, character formation, and intellectual freedom. While it offers little guidance for structural labour market planning, it exposes the psychological costs of treating education solely as an economic transaction, demonstrating that an intellectual life disconnected from careerism remains essential for individual flourishing.
