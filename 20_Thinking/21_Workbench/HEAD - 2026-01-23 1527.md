Here is a glossary of key terms related to estimating, points, and velocity found in your sources:

- **Story Points:** A unitless measure used to express the relative size, complexity, and effort of a feature. Unlike time estimates, story points do not "decay" (become inaccurate) as a team improves their skills or tools,.
- **Ideal Days:** An estimate of how long a task would take with absolutely no interruptions (no email, meetings, or breaks). This is distinct from "elapsed time," which accounts for real-world delays,.
- **Velocity:** A measure of a team's rate of progress, calculated by summing the number of story points (or ideal days) for work items that are fully "Done" at the end of a Sprint. It is used to forecast future delivery, not as a performance target,.
- **Yesterday's Weather:** A technique where the team uses the velocity achieved in the _most recent_ Sprint to forecast the capacity for the _upcoming_ Sprint, rather than using a long-term average,.
- **Planning Poker:** A collaborative technique where the team discusses a feature and simultaneously reveals their individual estimates (often using cards with numbers like 1, 2, 3, 5, 8). This forces the team to discuss discrepancies and reach a consensus,.
- **Cone of Uncertainty:** A concept illustrating that estimates given early in a project are highly inaccurate (up to 160% off), but become increasingly accurate as the project progresses and details become known,.
- **Triangulation:** An estimation method where a team compares a new story against multiple previously estimated stories (e.g., "Is this bigger than that 3-point story but smaller than that 8-point story?") to increase accuracy.
- **Disaggregation:** The process of splitting a large, difficult-to-estimate story into smaller, easier-to-estimate pieces to reduce uncertainty.
- **Pigs Estimate:** A rule derived from the "Chicken and Pig" fable, stating that only those committed to doing the actual work (the "Pigs") are allowed to vote on the estimates. Product Owners and stakeholders ("Chickens") do not estimate.
