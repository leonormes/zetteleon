Based on _Agile Estimating and Planning_, estimating size is a distinct activity from estimating schedule. Cohn argues that you must separate the estimate of **size** (how big is it?) from the estimate of **duration** (when will it be done?).

Here are the core concepts regarding Estimating Size:

- **Estimate Size, Derive Duration:** You cannot accurately predict a date immediately. Instead, the team estimates the size of the work, measures their rate of progress (velocity), and then _derives_ the schedule by dividing the total size by the velocity,.
- **Story Points:** These are the preferred unit of measure. They are a relative measure of effort, complexity, and risk. A "5-point" story should be roughly half the size of a "10-point" story. Because they are abstract, they don't "decay" (become inaccurate) even if the team gets faster or uses different technologies.
- **Ideal Days:** An alternative unit is "Ideal Days," which represents how long a task would take if you had absolutely no interruptions (no email, meetings, or phone calls),. While easier to explain to outsiders, they can be confused with calendar days.
- **The Power of Analogy:** Humans are better at estimating relative size ("This rock is twice as big as that rock") than absolute size ("This rock weighs 4.2 kg"). Agile estimation relies on comparing new user stories to previously completed ones.

**Planning Poker** is the primary technique recommended for teams to reach a consensus on these estimates collaboratively.
