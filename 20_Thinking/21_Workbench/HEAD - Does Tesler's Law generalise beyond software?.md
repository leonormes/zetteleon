## The Question

[[SoT - Conservation of Complexity]] states Tesler's Law: complexity cannot be destroyed, only relocated between control flow and representation. Principle 10 of [[SoT - Principles for Peaceful Productivity]] says to aggressively reject complexity in schedules, goals, tools and social circles. If Tesler's Law generalises, Principle 10 is describing relocation and calling it removal. Does it generalise?

## Why It Matters

Principle 10 is the one I lean on hardest when a system starts feeling heavy, and it is the justification for every simplification I have made to this vault. If simplifying merely moves the load into my judgement or onto other people, then 'radical simplicity' has a hidden cost I am not accounting for — and given an executive function deficit, judgement is the most expensive place to put it.

## What I Currently Think

I suspect it does generalise, and that the honest version of Principle 10 is 'relocate complexity to where it costs least' rather than 'reject it'. For me that means pushing it into *structure* — checklists, defaults, automation — and out of *in-the-moment decisions*, which is exactly what [[SoT - Behavioral Architecture]] already argues. If that is right, Principle 10 and Tesler's Law agree and only the wording conflicts.

## What Would Settle It

Take one simplification I actually made and trace where the complexity went. If I can name the new location, the law generalises and Principle 10 needs rewording. If the complexity genuinely disappeared, the law is software-specific and the `contradicts` edge should be dropped.
