## The Question

[[SoT - ADHD Management Protocols]] §7.2 prescribes deliberate boredom to reset the dopamine baseline. [[ADHD Systems Fail When They Become Monotonous]] argues routine maintenance stops delivering dopamine and the system gets abandoned, and [[Designed Task Switching Leverages Dopamine Boosts for ADHD Motivation]] sides with engineered novelty. Do I train the tolerance or engineer around it?

## Why It Matters

This decides how I design every recurring routine — the weekly review, the daily reset, the vault maintenance cadence. I have abandoned systems before for exactly the reason the monotony note describes, so getting this wrong has a track record.

## What I Currently Think

They are probably reconcilable on timescale: tolerance for boredom *inside* a single work block, novelty *across* a system's lifecycle. The SoT does not currently say so, and until it does I apply whichever framing I read most recently. This is the only `contradicts` edge emitted from that SoT, so the graph already knows it is unsettled.

## What Would Settle It

Add the timescale distinction to [[SoT - ADHD Management Protocols]] §7.2 as an explicit scope sentence, or run it as an experiment: hold one routine deliberately monotonous for four weeks and log whether adherence decays. The experiment is better evidence but the scope sentence is ten minutes.
