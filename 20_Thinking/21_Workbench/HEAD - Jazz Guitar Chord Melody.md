## Jazz Guitar Chord Melody: The Joe Pass Approach

### The Core Principle

What Joe Pass does is three instruments at once: a bassist (root notes), a pianist (chords), and a singer (melody). The secret is you don't learn all three simultaneously. You layer them one at a time, starting embarrassingly simple, then combining.

---

### The 5 Separate Skills (in Practice order)

#### 1. Melody Only—Know the Tune Cold

You can't ornament what you can't sing. Learn the melody of Autumn Leaves as single notes, like a singer would hum it.

- Key of G minor (or Bb major—same thing) is the most common guitar-friendly key
- Play it in one position (around the 5th–8th frets is comfortable)
- Aim to play it from memory, not reading—your fingers need to _know_ where the tune goes

> Immediate action: Find the melody in a single position. If you don't have it already, search "Autumn Leaves melody guitar tab" or use a Real Book. Play _only_ the melody line, nothing else, at a tempo where you make zero mistakes. Even if that's painfully slow.

---

#### 2. Root Notes Only—Internalise the Harmony as a Bassist Would

The chord progression of Autumn Leaves is beautifully logical—it moves almost entirely in 4ths (the cycle of 4ths), which is the most fundamental movement in jazz:

A section: Cm7 → F7 → Bb△7 → Eb△7 → Am7b5 → D7 → Gm6 (or Gm△7)

That's: ii → V → I → IV → ii° → V → i … in Bb major resolving to G minor.

- Play just the root note of each chord, one per bar, on the low E and A strings
- This teaches your hands where the harmony _lives_ on the neck

> Immediate action: Play the root-note bass line through the whole form. One note per bar. Get the sound of that cycle-of-4ths movement into your ear.

---

#### 3. Basic Chord Shells—The Minimum Viable Harmony

Forget full barre chords. Joe Pass mostly used 3-note voicings (shells). You only need two shapes to start:

- For major 7 and dominant 7 chords: Root on the 6th or 5th string + the 3rd + the 7th (on adjacent strings)
- For minor 7 chords: Same idea, but the 3rd is flatted

These are sometimes called guide tone voicings—just root, 3rd, 7th. Three strings. That's it.

> Immediate action: Learn the Cm7, F7, Bb△7, Eb△7, Am7b5, D7, Gm chord shells. Strum through the progression with _just_ these. One strum per bar. It'll sound surprisingly sophisticated for three notes.

---

#### 4. Melody + Bass Note Together—The First "Joe Pass moment"

Now combine skills 1 and 2:

- Play the melody note on a higher string
- Simultaneously play the root of the current chord on a lower string
- That's it. Two notes at a time. It already sounds like chord melody.

This is where the magic starts. Your ear hears the harmony implied by the root and the melody creates the movement.

> Immediate action: Take just the first 4 bars. Play the melody on the top strings and the bass root underneath. Don't try the whole tune—just 4 bars until it feels natural.

---

#### 5. Fill in the Middle—Add Guide Tones between Melody and Bass

This is where it becomes a true chord melody arrangement:

- You have the bass note (bottom)
- You have the melody note (top)
- Now slot in the 3rd and/or 7th of the chord in between

You won't always have room for both—sometimes the melody note _is_ the 3rd or 7th, which is a gift because you need fewer notes. Joe Pass often played just 3 notes total and it sounded full.

> Immediate action: Again, just 4 bars. For each melody note, ask: "Can I squeeze the 3rd or 7th of this chord between the bass and the melody?" If yes, add it. If it's a stretch, leave it as just melody + bass. Imperfect is fine.

---

### The Reassembly Path

|Week focus|What you're doing|
|---|---|
|Week 1|Melody from memory + root bass line separately|
|Week 2|Chord shells through the whole form + melody/bass combo on the A section|
|Week 3|Melody/bass combo on the whole tune|
|Week 4|Start filling in guide tones, 4 bars at a time|
|Ongoing|Add rhythmic variation, passing tones, voice leading refinements|

---

### Why This Works (the Joe Pass philosophy)

Joe Pass himself said he thought of chord melody as "playing the melody and harmonising each note." He didn't think in terms of chord shapes—he thought melody-first, then dressed each melody note with the harmony it needed. By building from melody → bass → fill, you're learning it the way he conceptualised it.

The trap most people fall into is trying to learn a full written-out arrangement (all five layers at once). That's like learning to juggle by starting with five balls.
