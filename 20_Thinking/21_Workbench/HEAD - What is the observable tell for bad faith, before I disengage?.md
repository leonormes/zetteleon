## The Question

Principle 2 of [[SoT - Principles for Peaceful Productivity]] says disengage immediately from people who prioritise winning over understanding. [[Dismissing People Who Disagree Costs You Your Best Error-Detectors]] says a disagreeing mind is external test coverage and dismissal deletes the test suite. The principle scopes itself to *bad faith*, so the two are compatible — but only if I can identify bad faith in the moment. What is the observable tell?

## Why It Matters

This is the load-bearing assumption of a principle I actually apply. If the scoping test does not exist, then in practice Principle 2 licenses me to disengage from anyone whose objection is uncomfortable, which is precisely the failure the error-detector claim warns about.

## What I Currently Think

The most promising tell is whether they can restate my position accurately when asked — a steelman test. Someone optimising for winning typically cannot or will not. It has the advantage of being a single question I can ask out loud rather than an inference about motive, and inferring motive is exactly what [[Mind-Reading Fallacy and Projection]] says I am bad at.

## What Would Settle It

Try the steelman question in three real disagreements and record whether the answer predicted how the conversation went. If it holds, write it as a claim and add it to Principle 2 as its scoping test. Until then, treat Principle 2 as unscoped and default to staying in the conversation.
