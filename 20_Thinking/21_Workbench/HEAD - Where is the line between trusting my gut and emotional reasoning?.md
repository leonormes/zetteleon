## The Question

[[ADHD Emotional Reasoning]] defines the distortion where a temporary emotional state is read as an objective fact. But intuition is also a real and valuable signal. At what point does 'trusting my gut' become emotional reasoning — and how do I tell, in the moment, rather than in hindsight? Related: how do I validate the *experience* of an emotion as real without validating its *content* as factual?

## Why It Matters

This governs how I respond to every strong reaction at work — an architecture decision that feels wrong, a review comment that lands badly. Read it as data and I act on noise; dismiss it and I lose a genuine signal. The boundary is blurrier with ADHD because the emotion is louder, which makes it feel more true.

## What I Currently Think

The most promising test I have is whether the feeling generates a *falsifiable prediction*. 'This design will break under load' is intuition — checkable. 'They think I am incompetent' is emotional reasoning — it predicts nothing I can test, and [[Mind-Reading Fallacy and Projection]] explains why it feels certain anyway. That reframes the question from feeling-vs-fact to predictive-vs-not.

## What Would Settle It

Log ten strong reactions over two weeks with the prediction each one implies, then check which ones came true. If the falsifiability test sorts them cleanly, write it up as a claim and add it to [[ADHD Emotional Reasoning]]. If it does not sort them, the test is wrong.
