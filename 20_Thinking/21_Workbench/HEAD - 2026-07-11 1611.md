Verdict first: I think you've just said the truest and most useful thing in this whole thread—and it's more useful _because_ it isn't a new piece of knowledge. You're right that the bottleneck has moved. But there's a precise version of your insight and a trap version, and they're only a hair apart.

The two layers. The mechanical deficit is real—initiation, time-blindness, the flat dopamine curve. That hasn't evaporated. But sitting _on top_ of it you've built something different: a global, stable, self-referential belief—"I am someone who does nothing." That second thing is not neurology. It's an _attribution_. And the gap between "I struggled to start that task" (specific, local, survivable) and "I am incapable, I have no self-efficacy" (global, permanent, identity-level) is the entire ballgame. The first is a description. The second is learned helplessness—the residue of years where effort seemed not to change the outcome, so the brain generalised "don't bother" into a personality. Your suspicion is correct: you've stopped auditing the story, and an unaudited story becomes load-bearing.

On your epistemology—I actually agree, and specifically here. You said beliefs are enabling or limiting rather than true or false, especially the abstract ones. I'd resist that hard for beliefs about the _world_—there, truth matters more than utility. But for beliefs about _yourself_, you're right, and for a concrete reason: self-efficacy beliefs are partly self-fulfilling. The belief changes the outcome it predicts. "I can start this" makes starting more likely; "I never do anything" makes doing nothing more likely. So the truth value isn't fixed and external—it's _endogenous_, it responds to how you act. That's a real and defensible distinction, not a cope. For this narrow domain, pragmatism over correspondence is the right epistemics.

Now the trap. "It's just a story I built up" can quietly flip into a new shame engine: _if it's only a belief, then not fixing it is my fault again._ That's the old moral-failing framing sneaking back through the side door, and it'll feed the RSD. Hold both at once: the deficit is real and the belief-structure over-generalises it and the lever for change is now the belief-layer. You're not calling yourself a liar. You're re-weighting which floor you're working on.

Why the notes made no difference—and it's not that knowledge is worthless. Self-efficacy doesn't rebuild through insight. It rebuilds through _mastery experiences_—small completed actions you personally witness (Bandura's finding; the strongest of the four sources by a distance). Reading, linking, synthesising can't build it, because efficacy is evidence about what you _do_, and a note is not a done thing. You didn't waste the knowledge; you just overdrew an account that was never going to pay this particular bill. Reps are the only currency it accepts.

One thing I want to name gently. "I don't even do leisure things for myself" is a different signal from avoiding chores. Not doing the things you'd actually enjoy points less at procrastination and more at low mood or anhedonia—which is very commonly comorbid with ADHD, and "reached new levels recently" fits that pattern. I'm not diagnosing you. But if the flatness in _wanting_ things persists, that's worth a real conversation with a GP or therapist, because it's treatable and it's not the same lever as executive function.

And I'm deliberately not offering to make a note, a card, or a Todoist capture out of this. That would be me proving your point about the trap. The response to "insight doesn't move the needle" is not more insight, filed more neatly.

One rep, today. Not a chore—a leisure one, because it's lower-shame and it attacks the exact belief you flagged. Pick one small thing you'd genuinely enjoy and do five minutes of it. That's it. Frame it as an experiment, not productivity: your belief predicts "I do nothing for myself." Gather one data point against it. One completed pleasant act is worth more to your self-efficacy right now than a week of reading about self-efficacy.

What would the five minutes be?
