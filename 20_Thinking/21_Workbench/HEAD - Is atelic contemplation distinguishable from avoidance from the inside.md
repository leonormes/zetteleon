## The Question

[[Intellectual Activity Belongs to the Atelic Sphere]] says an afternoon of thinking that produces nothing has not failed, because there was nothing it was supposed to produce. [[ADHD Overthinking is a Substitute for Action]] says an afternoon of thinking that produces nothing is a substitute for the thing I was avoiding. Externally the two episodes are identical. Is there any signal available *in the moment* that tells them apart, or can I only ever classify them in hindsight — at which point the classification is doing no work?

## Why It Matters

This is the whole reason I wanted the atelic material in the vault. I spend a lot of time thinking and periodically decide it amounted to nothing. If the atelic account is right, that judgement is a category error I keep making. If the ADHD account is right, the atelic account is the most sophisticated permission structure I have ever built for myself, and importing it into the vault makes things worse rather than better. Both readings are live and I cannot currently tell which one I am in.

## What I Currently Think

The nearest existing thread — [[HEAD - How do I tell productive solitude from isolation, and detachment from disengagement]] — proposes *chosen and bounded* versus *default and open-ended* as the discriminator, and that transfers here cleanly enough to be worth testing. A second candidate, sharper: atelic thinking has an **object** it is about; avoidant thinking has a **subject** it is about not-doing. If I can name what the thinking was *about* and it is not the task I was dodging, that is weak evidence for atelic. If I cannot name the object at all, that is strong evidence for avoidance.

I lean towards this being a real distinction that is genuinely available in the moment but that I have never had a prompt to check.

## What Would Settle It

Two weeks of a two-field log at the end of any stretch of undirected thinking: (1) what was it about — one noun phrase, or "cannot name it"; (2) what task was outstanding at the time. If "cannot name it" correlates with an outstanding avoided task at well above chance, the object test discriminates and I should adopt it. If it does not correlate, the distinction is not introspectable and I should stop trying to make it in the moment and judge only in retrospect.
