## The Archery Shot Process

The archery shot process consists of 10 sequential steps, which are divided into two distinct phases. Each step is built from the ground up and must be completed in order.

### Phase 1: Technique Structure

This phase focuses on the drawing sequence and setting up the body for full draw to achieve maximum stability and consistency.

1. Stance - Setting a stable base for maximal stability. The angle and positioning of the feet are crucial.
2. Posture - Engaging the core, positioning the hips and torso to transfer stability from the stance through the trunk.
3. Setup - Setting the hands onto the bow, including the hook onto the string and the pressure point into the bow grip.
4. Raise - Raising the bow while setting the bow-side shoulder and internally rotating the bow arm.
5. Pre-draw - Aligning the shoulders with rotation through the core; acts as a checking step for posture, front shoulder position, and shoulder alignment.
6. Loading - Drawing the bow using scapular retractors and shoulder extensors to achieve full alignment and correct back tension.
7. Anchor - The final reference for the full draw position and the point of maximal consistency for an aiming reference point.

### Phase 2: Shot Execution

This phase is the action of shooting the bow, focusing on the dynamic forces within the body required for accuracy.

1. Expansion - Maintaining fluidity and continuation of tension throughout the drawing sequence. The archer activates the clicker here for precise control.
2. Release - Releasing the string to shoot the bow; a natural reaction if previous steps are done correctly.
3. Follow-through - The dynamic reaction of the body after release, indicating the tension maintained prior to the shot.

## Casey Kaufhold's Recurve Form Steps

### 1. Stance [00:00:25]

- Square Stance: Stand with feet exactly in line and shoulder-width apart.
- Weight Distribution: Place weight slightly on the balls of the feet, avoiding leaning too far back onto the heels or forward onto the toes.

### 2. Hook [00:01:02]

- Finger Placement: Hook the string on the first knuckle of three fingers.
- Tension: Focus the most pressure on the middle finger (roughly 40%, with 30% on the remaining fingers).
- Relaxation: Keep the pinky relaxed and neutral. The thumb should rest just below the shelf. Maintain a neutral wrist.

### 3. Grip [00:01:58]

- Hand Placement: Drive tension into the pit of the hand towards the target.
- Relaxed Fingers: Curl the fingers in without squeezing or touching the bow.
- Thumb Position: Relax the thumb downwards with slight tension into the riser, avoiding a heavy grip.

### 4. Draw [00:03:08]

- Preparation: Ensure your head is straight, posture is good, and you are looking at the target.
- Shoulder Set: Raise the bow straight up and set the front shoulder down at the peak of the draw.
- Movement: Bring the string to your face—do not move your head forward to meet the string. Move the whole drawing shoulder as a unit, leading with the elbow and drawing tension into the scapula. Bring the sight down onto the target from above.

### 5. Anchor [00:05:07]

- Placement: Settle the hand to the jaw. Ensure the finger and shelf are on the same level, feeling both simultaneously.

### 6. Expansion and Extension [00:07:28]

- Back Half (Expansion): Raise the draw elbow slightly while continuing a rounding motion with the scapula and lifting slightly with the pec muscle.
- Front Half (Extension): Extend the bow shoulder continuously towards the target. Think of this as a two-way fluid motion (pushing and pulling simultaneously).

### 7. Release and Follow-through [00:09:16]

- The Release: Treat the release as an involuntary response to the clicker. Relax the fingers and let the shot go off naturally.
- Back Tension: If back tension is correct, the release hand will come straight back to the area between the ear and neck.
- Bow Hand: Relax the bow hand entirely so the bow jumps and falls naturally, avoiding any torquing or gripping.
