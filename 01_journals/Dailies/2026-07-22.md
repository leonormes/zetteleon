## Archery Session Plan: Grouping Focus

**1. The Round Choice: Warwick (40/30)**
* **Why:** A Warwick is 4 dozen arrows (2 dozen at 40, 2 dozen at 30). When you are trying to rewrite muscle memory and focus on grouping, mental fatigue sets in long before physical fatigue. Keeping the arrow count slightly lower ensures you can maintain 100% conscious focus on your physical execution for every single shot without getting sloppy.

**2. The Golden Rule: "Ignore the Gold"**
* **The Trap:** Remember that a bad shot can hit the bullseye by accident. If you chase the gold, you will subconsciously tweak your form on every shot, which destroys grouping.
* **The Rule:** During your sighters, get your sight roughly calibrated so you are hitting the boss safely. Once the round starts, **do not touch your sight**. If you shoot a beautifully executed end and all six arrows are touching each other in the red 7-ring, *do not move your sight to fix it*. Celebrate the group. 

**3. The Focal Point: The Brick Wall**
To shoot tight groups, you need to isolate your mind on *one* physical sensation. Focus entirely on the **Push and Pull**.
* **During the Draw:** Treat your bow hand like it's pressing against a solid brick wall. Direct that pressure exactly at the center of the target.
* **The Diagnostic Check (Follow-through):** Your feedback for success is not where the arrow lands. Your feedback is your bow arm. The moment the arrow releases, did your bow arm jump slightly *straight forward* toward the target? If it collapsed, went limp, or swung left/right, commit to holding the "brick wall" firmer on the next shot.

**4. The 30-Yard Shift: Pass/Fail Tracking**
When you move to the shorter 30-yard distance for the final two dozen arrows, use a **Data-Driven Progress Tracking** mindset.
* Grade your last 24 arrows on a strict **Pass/Fail** metric based purely on your execution.
* **Pass:** "I maintained continuous push-pull tension and my bow arm reacted straight forward."
* **Fail:** "I went passive, relaxed my bow hand, or plucked the string."
* Aim to get a 20/24 "Pass" rate.
