Why you're doing a PKM

- [[I am iterating on creating a thinking system]] → [[Purpose of my PKM system]]: you want to use writing as an aid to thinking. You see yourself as good at deep, associative thinking but hindered by ADHD in making that thinking practically valuable—externalising it into a system is meant to capitalise on the strength while compensating for the deficit. You call it "ironic… Catch 22" that ADHD is both the reason you need the system and the thing that makes building it hard.
- [[Nihilism vs Constructed Meaning]] is explicitly cited as the theoretical foundation: there's no inherent meaning, but that's a creative opportunity rather than a cause for despair—personal knowledge systems require active meaning-making, and for you specifically this validates interest-based engagement and flexible purpose (ADHD-relevant: it explains motivation fluctuations rather than treating them as failure).
- [[Externalize Memory Aggressively (cognitive offloading)]]: "Your mind generates; your system stores." The PKM exists to free working memory so the generative part of your mind isn't burdened with retention.
- [[SoT - The Extended Mind]]: the deepest framing. The vault isn't a filing cabinet, it's a prosthetic prefrontal cortex—literally part of the cognitive system (Extended Mind Thesis / Parity Principle), not an aid outside it. For an ADHD brain specifically, it converts what would otherwise be cyclical thinking (spinning in place, losing state) into cumulative thinking—"you start having conversations with your past self."

What you think knowledge is for

- [[Knowledge Emerges Through Application and Experience]]: knowledge isn't information stored—it's formed when you apply information to solve problems, make decisions, or generate insight. Abstract data becomes "practical wisdom" only through use.
- [[Deep Processing is the Core of Zettelkasten]] / [[The Processing Is the Hard Part]]: collecting is not the work—it's a seductive trap (the "collector's fallacy") that feels like progress while nothing is understood. The actual value is in paraphrasing and linking, because forcing a fuzzy feeling/understanding into precise words is where real cognitive work happens. You wrote: "That processing only counts if it happens in my own head… a pile of well-organised links I didn't struggle to write is just a second inbox."
- [[Zettelkasten System Essence]]: the system exists in your mental processes, not in the notes. You invoke Luhmann explicitly—the archive wasn't the thinking, the linking was, one card at a time. Graph size/sophistication is not the goal and isn't even evidence of the goal ("the sophistication is a bug not a feature").
- [[PKM as Process vs Product]]: you deliberately reject the "personal wiki" model (polished, final-conclusion, hides-the-thinking) in favour of a process-oriented system that captures the messy journey—questions, false starts, evolving understanding. The value sits in the network the summary rests on, not the summary itself.

The through-line: knowledge, for you, isn't a possession to accumulate—it's a byproduct of the effort of thinking, made durable and cumulative through externalisation so an ADHD working memory doesn't have to hold it all at once. The PKM's job is to be that extended, prosthetic part of your mind, not a library about your mind.
